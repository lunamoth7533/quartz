# Thalamus and hypothalamus

## Definition

The thalamus is a relay and integration structure that sits between sensory pathways and cortex and participates in cortical loops. The hypothalamus is a small structure with broad regulatory roles in endocrine, autonomic and behavioural function.

## How it works

**Relay.** Sensory pathways terminate in thalamus before reaching cortex; in the visual system the lateral geniculate nucleus carries the signal to striate cortex. [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-pathway|Visual Processing: Cortical Pathways]] Thalamic lesions can therefore mimic cortical deficits.

**Hypothalamic organisation.** The hypothalamus is described in longitudinal zones; the periventricular zone contains the arcuate and paraventricular nuclei and is central to neuroendocrine and autonomic control. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-zones|Hypothalamus]] Its nuclei map onto distinct regulatory functions such as feeding, thermoregulation and biological timing. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-nuclei|Hypothalamus]] [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-timing|Hypothalamus]]

**Endocrine output.** The hypothalamus produces releasing and inhibiting factors for the anterior pituitary and projects to the posterior pituitary, which is why the axis is described as a loop with feedback. [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-connection|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-feedback|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]]

## Evidence and status

Anatomy and hormonal control are well established in animals and humans; functional assignment of individual nuclei relies heavily on animal lesion and stimulation work. [[F119 Neuroscience Online pituitary control#^f119-feedback|Hypothalamic control of pituitary hormone]]

> [!info]- In depth: the relay that participates, and the coordinator that times
> **Thalamic relay and participation.** The thalamus receives sensory and motor information and projects to cortex, and it also receives massive return projections from cortex; because of that reciprocal arrangement, thalamic nuclei are described as participating in cortical loops rather than merely passing signals through. Relay nuclei are distinguished by their cortical targets, which is why naming the nucleus is more informative than naming 'the thalamus'. [[F05 OpenStax The central nervous system#^f05-parts|The Central Nervous System]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]]
>
> **Gating as a function.** Because thalamic activity determines whether cortical areas are engaged, the structure is described as gating the flow of information to cortex; arousal state and attentional state both change that gating, which is the mechanistic connection between this structure and the sleep-wake and modulation literature. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]] [[P23040802 Marder 2012 Neuromodulation#^p23040802-state|Neuromodulation]]
>
> **Hypothalamic zones and nuclei.** The hypothalamus is organised into zones containing named nuclei with distinct functions, and it coordinates endocrine, autonomic and behavioural outputs from a small amount of tissue. It contains the releasing-hormone neurons that drive the pituitary, the suprachiasmatic nucleus that times daily rhythms, and nuclei that control temperature, feeding and arousal. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-zones|Hypothalamus]] [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-nuclei|Hypothalamus]]
>
> **Timing and coupling.** Because the same structure contains the circadian clock and the hormonal command neurons, it is the anatomical place where timing and endocrine control are coupled - which is why rhythm disruption and hormonal measures appear together in clinical research. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-timing|Hypothalamus]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]]
>
> **Reading rule.** 'Thalamus' and 'hypothalamus' are collection labels covering nuclei with different connections and functions; a mechanism claim should name the nucleus and the output it controls. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-limit|Hypothalamus]]

> [!info]- Further depth: the relay, the regulator and the feedback loop
> Sensory pathways converge on thalamic relays before cortex - the three-neuron chain ends with a thalamic neuron projecting to the postcentral gyrus for discriminative touch and proprioception - so the thalamus gates and routes information rather than merely passing it on. The hypothalamus links the nervous system to endocrine output, producing releasing and inhibiting hormones for the anterior pituitary and sending neural projections to the posterior pituitary, which releases oxytocin and antidiuretic hormone made in the hypothalamus. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-connection|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-lobes|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]]
>
> Hormone release is regulated by feedback from target-gland products back to hypothalamus and pituitary, which is why the axis is a loop rather than a chain - and why a single blood level samples a pulsatile signal shaped by binding proteins and tissue-level effects the textbook presentation omits. [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-feedback|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-limit|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-limit|The Adrenal Glands]]
>
> **Relay is active, and the return projections prove it.** Every major thalamic nucleus receives reciprocal cortex-to-thalamus projections that usually outnumber its feedforward input, so the thalamus is better described as a participant in cortical loops than as a gateway. State-dependent gating follows: during sleep, thalamic cells shift into burst modes that block pass-through traffic, which is the cellular form of the observation that sensory signals fail to reach cortex effectively in deep sleep. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]
>
> **The hypothalamus is small tissue with system-level reach.** Its nuclei command the pituitary through releasing factors, the autonomic system through descending projections, and behaviour through integrated drives; the suprachiasmatic nucleus sets the timing for all of it. That concentration of command over three output systems in one small structure is why hypothalamic damage produces combinations - endocrine, autonomic, thermoregulatory, circadian - rather than one deficit. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-nuclei|Hypothalamus]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-connection|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The neuroendocrine domain is the hypothalamus's applied literature - the HPA axis, circadian timing and autonomic output all begin at its nuclei - while the sleep and neuromodulation literatures pass through its thalamic neighbour; this one note therefore underwrites claims in at least three other domains, which is why its links run outward rather than summarising them. ## Uncertainties

- Thalamic function is best described as participation in cortico-thalamic loops, which makes simple relay metaphors misleading.
