# Meta-analysis and review limits

**Definition.** A systematic review pools studies through explicit rules, which makes it efficient and also makes its conclusions depend on the studies and choices that went into it. [[F22 Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions#^f22-effects|Cochrane Handbook]]

## Supported claims

- Review production has grown faster than the underlying evidence, and many published reviews are redundant or overlapping. [[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-volume|P27620683]]; [[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-redundancy|P27620683]]
- Reviews are often produced by authors with industry ties, and the essay reports that results tend to align with sponsor interests. [[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-conflict|P27620683]]
- Misleading conclusions can be inherited from flawed included studies or created by review-level analysis choices that ignore bias. [[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-misleading|P27620683]]
- Cochrane methods require an explicit certainty rating for each important outcome and advise against coding results as significant or non-significant. [[F22 Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions#^f22-certainty|F22]]; [[F22 Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions#^f22-thresholds|F22]]
- The complex-trauma meta-analysis in this library reports some indications of publication bias in its own evidence base and lower effects in childhood-trauma samples. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD]]
- A systematic review inherits the biases of its included studies and adds selection and interpretation choices, so it is a method rather than a seal. [[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-caution-use|AI synthesis: P27620683]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Pooled estimates describe the studies that met the inclusion criteria; they do not measure the studies that were never published or never run, and certainty ratings route around that gap rather than filling it.

> [!info]- Reference: what synthesis can and cannot do
> **What meta-analysis adds.** Pooling increases precision and makes the spread of effects visible, and it forces explicit decisions about which studies answer the same question. [[F22 Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions#^f22-effects|Cochrane Handbook]] [[F129 Cochrane Handbook Chapter 10#^f129-fixed-effect|Cochrane Handbook chapter 10]]
>
> **Reporting.** PRISMA 2020 specifies what a systematic review should report, including search strategy, selection process, synthesis and certainty assessment, and recommends a flow diagram documenting the selection path. [[P33782057 PRISMA 2020#^p33782057-purpose|PRISMA 2020]] [[P33782057 PRISMA 2020#^p33782057-flow|PRISMA 2020]]
>
> **Certainty.** GRADE rates certainty separately from recommendation strength and can rate evidence down for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-domains|GRADE]]
>
> **Indirect comparisons.** Network meta-analysis compares treatments never tested head to head, but validity depends on the transitivity assumption that study populations and settings are similar enough. [[P27913917 Network meta-analysis introduction#^p27913917-indirect|Network meta-analysis]] [[P27913917 Network meta-analysis introduction#^p27913917-assumption|Network meta-analysis]] Rankings carry uncertainty and should be read with the confidence in the evidence. [[P27913917 Network meta-analysis introduction#^p27913917-interpretation|Network meta-analysis]]
>
> **Aggregation limits.** An average across heterogeneous studies can conceal that subgroups differ, which is why pooled estimates do not transfer automatically to an individual or a service context.
>
> **Pooling carries two separable assumptions.** Clinical comparability - populations, interventions, comparators and outcomes close enough to answer one question - is a review-design judgement, made before any statistical step runs. The statistical model then describes how the pooled estimate treats variation: a fixed-effect (common-effect) model assumes every study estimates the same quantity; a random-effects model assumes a distribution of related effects across studies. Neither model substitutes for the other, and neither repairs a review that has pooled clinically incomparable studies. Heterogeneity statistics flag when the common-effect assumption is strained; they do not by themselves say whether the differences matter, which study is right, or whether a random-effects estimate is more meaningful. [[F129 Cochrane Handbook Chapter 10#^f129-fixed-effect|Common-effect assumptions]] [[F129 Cochrane Handbook Chapter 10#^f129-rand-effects|Random-effects assumptions]]
>
> **Publication bias is the meta-analysis's structural vulnerability.** Pooling published studies pools a filtered sample; funnel-plot asymmetry is one signal of that filter, but it is not specific evidence for publication bias - other mechanisms (small-study effects, methodological heterogeneity between small and large studies) can produce the same pattern, and the Cochrane Handbook treats asymmetry as an indicator requiring investigation rather than a verdict. Detection is partial and correction is model-dependent. The review's certainty about its own estimate should be read alongside its bias assessment, which is why GRADE-style certainty ratings integrate risk of bias, imprecision, inconsistency, indirectness and publication bias rather than trusting the pooled estimate alone. [[F129 Cochrane Handbook Chapter 10#^f129-pub-bias|Funnel-plot interpretation]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-domains|GRADE certainty domains]] [[Replication and publication bias]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The bipolar psychotherapy and ADHD medication articles in the condition domains are applied instances: their effect sizes and certainty statements are meta-analytic products whose interpretation follows exactly the rules this article describes. [[Bipolar psychotherapy evidence]] [[ADHD medication evidence]]
>
> **What to read first when a review's conclusion is quoted.** The inclusion criteria, the search date, the number of studies and participants behind each pooled estimate, and the certainty rating. Those four things determine how much the quoted conclusion is entitled to say; the abstract's one-sentence summary does not carry them. [[P33782057 PRISMA 2020#^p33782057-items|PRISMA 2020]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]] [[F22 Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions#^f22-effects|Cochrane Handbook]]
>
> **Effect-size and reporting conventions are the article's shared vocabulary.** The effect-size and uncertainty material in the methods domain is what lets two studies be pooled at all - a review that pools incommensurable effect measures has already made a mistake before any statistical step runs. [[Effect sizes and uncertainty]] [[Reading a study and matching populations]]
