# White matter and tracts

## Definition

White matter is composed of myelinated axons grouped into tracts that connect cortical areas to each other, to subcortical structures and to the periphery.

## How it works

**Tissue logic.** Grey matter is dominated by cell bodies and local processing, white matter by long-range connections, though the distinction describes the dominant tissue rather than an absolute rule. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure]]

**Conduction.** Myelination raises conduction velocity by restricting current to nodes, so tract integrity affects timing relationships between regions. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]

**Disconnection.** Because tracts carry information between areas, damage can produce deficits that look like a lost function when the area itself is intact - the logic behind disconnection syndromes. [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-model|Language]] [[F54 Neuroscience Online nervous system overview#^f54-sections|Overview of the Nervous System]]

**Development.** Myelination continues into adulthood and differs by tract, so white-matter development is one basis for changing capabilities across development. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]]

## Evidence and status

Tract anatomy is established by dissection and tracer work in animals; human estimates use diffusion imaging, which measures indirect properties of tissue and carries modelling assumptions. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]]

> [!info]- In depth: what connects to what, and how confident the answer is
> **Composition.** White matter is made of myelinated axons, oligodendrocytes and supporting glia, and it is organised into tracts grouped by origin, destination and crossing pattern: projection fibres connecting cortex with subcortical targets, commissural fibres crossing the midline, and association fibres connecting cortical areas within a hemisphere. This classification is anatomical and predictive: knowing the group predicts where a lesion's effects appear. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure and Function]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]
>
> **How tracts are established.** Tracer methods in animals establish the direction of a connection; staining and dissection establish gross organisation; diffusion-based imaging estimates orientation and continuity in humans. The three methods differ in what they can support, which is why human connectional claims are described as estimates subject to method-specific biases rather than as wiring diagrams. [[Neuroanatomical methods]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-mri|Brain imaging]]
>
> **Conduction and timing.** Myelination raises conduction velocity and reduces the membrane area that must be repolarised, so tracts differ in speed as well as in route; timing differences between tracts are part of how signals converge. That makes white matter a timing structure as much as a routing structure. [[Myelin and saltatory conduction]] [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]]
>
> **Development and clinical relevance.** Myelination continues after synapse numbers peak, with different tracts maturing on different schedules, which is one reason age at injury changes outcome. Where myelin is attacked - as in multiple sclerosis - the resulting deficits follow the tracts involved rather than a single functional system. [[Synaptic pruning and myelination]] [[Demyelination and multiple sclerosis]] [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-course|Multiple Sclerosis]]
>
> **Reading rule.** 'Connectivity' is used for at least three different things: anatomical axons, statistical correlation between regions, and effective influence in a model. Only the first is a tract, and the others must carry their method label. [[White matter and tracts]] [[Structural versus functional measures]]

> [!info]- Further depth: long-range wiring, myelin and inference from diffusion
> The sheaths that give white matter its appearance increase conduction speed along the axons they enclose, so tissue colour, histology and function are linked. Ascending and descending tracts occupy characteristic positions in the spinal cord, which is why the level and pattern of a deficit carries localising information, and the sensory pathway's three-neuron chain with a midline crossing explains why some lesions produce contralateral deficits. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Spinal Cord]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]]
>
> Diffusion-based tractography estimates fibre orientation from water diffusion and is limited at crossings and in complex geometry, and like other imaging methods it inherits analytic flexibility and power constraints. Connectivity descriptions are also degenerate - different parameters can produce similar outputs - so a tract or connectome constrains function rather than determining it. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]] [[P23040802 Marder 2012 Neuromodulation#^p23040802-degeneracy|Neuromodulation]]
>
> **Three tract families, three lesion geometries.** Projection fibres connect cortex with subcortical and spinal targets, so their damage produces long-tract deficits below the lesion; commissural fibres join the hemispheres, so their damage produces disconnection between hemispheres; association fibres join areas within a hemisphere, so their damage produces network-level disconnections without primary sensory or motor loss. Predicting which geometry a lesion will produce is the practical payoff of the classification. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure and Function]] [[F54 Neuroscience Online nervous system overview#^f54-sections|Nervous System Overview]]
>
> **What diffusion imaging can and cannot say.** Diffusion-based methods estimate the preferred orientation of water movement, from which tractography reconstructs plausible continuations; the reconstructions resolve major bundles well, are biased at crossings and by parameter choices, and do not establish direction. Human connectional claims therefore remain estimates with method-specific uncertainty - stronger than guesses, weaker than the tracer literature that anchors them in animals. [[Neuroanatomical methods]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-mri|Brain imaging]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Neurology's disconnection syndromes and developmental research's myelination timelines are this article's two applied faces: one reads adult lesions through tract geometry, the other reads changing capabilities through tract maturation, and both inherit the method cautions stated here.
