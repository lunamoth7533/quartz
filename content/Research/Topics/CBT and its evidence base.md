# CBT and its evidence base

**Definition.** Cognitive behavioural therapy is a structured, skills-based approach that targets the relationship between thoughts, behaviour and mood. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

## Supported claims

- CBT with pharmacotherapy was more effective than pharmacotherapy alone for ADHD core symptoms in adults. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]
- Adjunctive manualized psychotherapy was linked to lower bipolar recurrence than control. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]
- Retention was higher for family or conjoint therapy and brief psychoeducation. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-retention|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

> [!info]- In depth: what cognitive behavioural therapy is, and how its effects are established
> **The approach.** Psychotherapy approaches are grouped by orientation - psychodynamic, behavioural, cognitive and humanistic - and the types-of-treatment overview distinguishes what each targets; behavioural therapies apply conditioning principles directly, including exposure-based methods developed for fear and avoidance. CBT combines the cognitive and behavioural strands, which is why it appears across several condition domains in this library. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-psychotherapy|Types of Treatment]] [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-behavioural|Types of Treatment]]
>
> **The mechanism claim borrowed from learning research.** Exposure-based methods rest on the extinction literature: extinction is new learning that competes with the original fear memory and can return through spontaneous recovery, reinstatement or renewal, so treatment design includes context and relapse planning rather than assuming erasure. [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-newlearning|Fear extinction]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-translation|Fear extinction]]
>
> **What the strongest syntheses report.** The Cochrane review of CBT for adults with ADHD found that adding CBT to medication beat medication alone on clinician-rated and self-rated core symptoms, and that CBT beat a waiting list on self-reported symptoms - with certainty low or very low for most comparisons because trials were small and outcome measures heterogeneous, and with estimates that depended on the comparator (large against a waiting list, not statistically significant against supportive psychotherapy). [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]]
>
> **The bipolar literature shows a different shape.** There, adding manualised psychotherapy to medication was associated with lower recurrence, and CBT was linked to stabilising depressive symptoms with family and interpersonal therapy pointing the same way less certainly. The outcome that moves differs by condition, which is why 'CBT works' is not a finding. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-symptoms|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Name the condition, the comparator, the rater and the outcome; then check the certainty rating. Guideline documents sit on top of that evidence and are service documents rather than measurements. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI appraisal: Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-framing|Bipolar disorder CG185]]

> [!info]- Reference: what CBT is, and how its effects are established
> **Model.** Cognitive behavioural approaches hold that thoughts, behaviour and feelings interact, and that changing the first two changes the third. Skill acquisition and practice between sessions are central. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-psychotherapy|Types of Treatment]]
>
> **Behavioural inheritance.** Exposure-based methods derive from conditioning research and use extinction logic deliberately: repeated contact without the feared outcome builds competing learning. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-behavioural|Types of Treatment]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-newlearning|Fear extinction]]
>
> **Evidence.** Effects are established through randomised trials with waitlist or active comparators. In the adult-ADHD trials reviewed by Cochrane, estimates depended on the comparator - large against waiting list, not statistically significant against supportive psychotherapy - and certainty was mostly low or very low. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]]
>
> **Delivery matters.** Format, therapist training, dose and adherence all influence outcomes, which is why guideline recommendations specify both method and delivery. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-longterm|Bipolar disorder CG185]]
>
> **Limits.** Blinding participants to a psychotherapy is impossible, so expectancy and allegiance effects are structural features of the design rather than artefacts that can be removed.

## Limitation or common misconception

Evidence is protocol- and population-specific: 'CBT' covers many manuals and delivery formats.
