# Heterogeneity and presentations in ADHD

## Definition

ADHD is defined by patterns of inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity that cause impairment, with presentations distinguished by which features dominate. Within any presentation, cognitive profiles differ substantially.

## How it works

**Independent components.** Timing, inhibitory control and delay-related impairments behave as dissociable neuropsychological components in the same sample, rather than as one core deficit. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-finding|Beyond the dual pathway model]] [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-design|Beyond the dual pathway model]]

**Consequences for models.** A single-deficit account cannot explain why people with the same diagnosis differ in which tasks they fail. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-implication|Beyond the dual pathway model]]

**Consequences for treatment.** Because profiles differ, average treatment effects describe groups; matching interventions to profiles is an open research question rather than an established practice.

**Developmental change.** Symptom form changes with age, so heterogeneity partly reflects developmental stage rather than fixed subtype. [[F86 NIMH ADHD#^f86-lifespan|Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder]]

## Evidence and status

Heterogeneity is well established in cognitive and clinical studies; subclassification schemes are proposed but not validated for individual prediction.

> [!info]- In depth: what varies, why one account fails, and how to read a group average
> **Presentations describe symptoms, not mechanisms.** The diagnostic presentations - predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive and combined - record which symptom family dominates. NIMH defines ADHD as a developmental pattern of inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity that interferes with functioning or development, and notes that symptom form can change across childhood, adolescence and adulthood while difficulties persist. Two people with the same presentation can therefore differ completely in which tasks they find hard. [[F86 NIMH ADHD#^f86-definition|ADHD]] [[F86 NIMH ADHD#^f86-lifespan|ADHD]]
>
> **Dissociable components inside one sample.** The strongest single piece of evidence here comes from a study in which children with ADHD, their siblings and non-ADHD controls completed nine tasks tapping inhibitory control, delay aversion and temporal processing. Temporal processing, inhibitory control and delay-related deficits behaved as independent neuropsychological components, and the ADHD group differed from controls on all three. The authors read that pattern as support for heterogeneity rather than for a single core deficit, which is why the paper argues for moving past the dual-pathway model instead of replacing it with another single cause. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-design|Beyond the dual pathway model]] [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-finding|Beyond the dual pathway model]] [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-implication|Beyond the dual pathway model]]
>
> **Why one-deficit accounts keep failing.** A single-deficit account predicts that people with the diagnosis share a common impairment and differ mainly in severity. If the components dissociate, the same diagnosis can arise through different routes, and a group difference on any one task will be carried by a subgroup - which is the pattern that makes cognitive findings in this area hard to replicate. The HiTOP programme makes a parallel argument at the level of classification: categorical taxonomies suffer from arbitrary thresholds, unclear boundaries, frequent co-occurrence, within-disorder heterogeneity and diagnostic instability, and its response is to organise syndromes into dimensional spectra as a research effort rather than a finished clinical system. [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]] [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-structure|HiTOP]] [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-purpose|HiTOP]]
>
> **Lifespan variation is part of the picture.** Estimated adult prevalence is around 2.5%, and up to 70% of people with childhood-onset ADHD continue to have impairing symptoms as adults, sometimes without meeting full criteria. The same review lists late-onset ADHD, emotional dysregulation as a candidate core symptom, the definition of functional impairment and the absence of objective diagnostic measures among the field's open questions - so heterogeneity across the lifespan is an unresolved research problem rather than a footnote. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-base|ADHD in adults: evidence base]] [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults: evidence base]]
>
> **What heterogeneity does to a treatment claim.** Because profiles differ, an average effect answers a question about a group, not about an individual. The Cochrane review of cognitive behavioural interventions in adults with ADHD rated certainty low or very low for most comparisons, in part because trials were small and outcome measures heterogeneous, and its estimates depended on the comparator - large against a waiting list, not statistically significant against supportive therapy. Naming the outcome, the comparator, the rater and the sample is therefore part of reading any claim in this domain. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]]
>
> **Consensus as orientation, not as data.** The World Federation consensus statement curates 208 empirically supported assertions about ADHD, drawn from studies with more than 2000 participants or from meta-analyses meeting comparable thresholds. It is a synthesis of a moving literature produced by judgement, so it maps where agreement sits without replacing current primary evidence on a specific question.
