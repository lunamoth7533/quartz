# Receptor families and second messengers

## Definition

Receptors are the decision point of chemical signalling. Ionotropic receptors are ligand-gated ion channels that produce fast, brief changes in membrane potential. Metabotropic receptors are G-protein coupled and act through intracellular messengers, producing slower and often longer-lasting changes.

## How it works

**Fast path.** A transmitter binds an ionotropic receptor, a channel opens, and ions flow. Glutamate receptors that depolarise and GABA-A receptors that hyperpolarise are the standard cortical examples. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]

**Slow path.** Metabotropic receptors activate second messengers that can modify ion channels, change excitability, or alter gene transcription, which is how a brief signal can produce a lasting change in a cell's behaviour. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]

**Consequence.** Because the same transmitter binds different receptor families in different places, transmitter identity does not determine the sign of the effect. Dopamine's D1-type and D2-type receptors can have opposing actions. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]

**Pharmacological leverage.** Most psychoactive drugs are receptor or transporter ligands, and benzodiazepines illustrate indirect action: they enhance GABA-A receptor function rather than activating it directly. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-mechanism|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]]

## Evidence and status

Receptor pharmacology is quantitatively established with binding and functional assays. Translating receptor occupancy into clinical effect remains difficult, as antipsychotic evidence shows: D2 blockade underlies both response and motor and endocrine side effects, implying a narrow window. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]

> [!info]- In depth: the two receptor logics and what follows from amplification
> **Fast and slow paths.** Ionotropic receptors are ligand-gated ion channels: binding opens a pore, ions flow, and the effect is brief and local. Metabotropic receptors are G-protein coupled and act through intracellular messengers that can modify ion channels, change excitability, or alter gene transcription, which is how a brief signal can produce a lasting change in a cell's behaviour. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **Amplification and its consequences.** A single metabotropic receptor can activate many effector molecules, so the response to one transmitter molecule is not limited to one channel opening. Amplification explains why metabotropic effects can be large, slow and long-lasting, and why they are harder to characterise quantitatively than a channel current - the relationship between occupancy and effect passes through several intracellular steps. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **Receptor identity decides the sign.** Because the same transmitter binds different receptor families in different places, transmitter identity does not determine the sign of the effect: dopamine's D1-type and D2-type receptors can have opposing actions. Every statement about a transmitter's function is therefore implicitly a statement about which receptors are present in the tissue being discussed. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **Subtype selectivity is graded, not absolute.** Most psychoactive drugs bind several receptor subtypes with different affinities, so 'selective' describes a preference rather than a guarantee. Off-target binding is the rule rather than the exception, and it is a common explanation for side effects that do not follow from the intended target. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-offtarget|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]
>
> **Pharmacological leverage, with the occupancy caveat.** Benzodiazepines illustrate indirect action: they enhance GABA-A receptor function rather than activating it directly, so their effect scales with endogenous transmitter release. More generally, receptor occupancy does not map linearly onto clinical effect - exposure, target engagement in tissue, downstream adaptation and the outcome measured all intervene, which is why binding affinities cannot be read as effect sizes. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-mechanism|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]] [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|How do antidepressants work]]

> [!info]- Further depth: how one transmitter produces different effects
> Ionotropic receptors are ligand-gated channels that change membrane potential within milliseconds, while metabotropic receptors act through second messengers that can modify ion channels and gene transcription over seconds to hours - which is how one molecule can both depolarise a cell immediately and change its excitability later. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> Multiple receptor subtypes exist for each transmitter and the same subtypes appear in different tissues, so a drug's effects are rarely single and selectivity across subtypes matters more than selectivity across transmitters. Presynaptic receptors detect transmitter in the cleft and adjust release, and postsynaptic machinery changes with activity, so an effect depends on the current state of the synapse as well as on concentration. ## Uncertainties

- Receptor subtype selectivity of available drugs is often incomplete, so off-target effects are the rule rather than the exception. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-offtarget|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]
- Individual differences in receptor abundance and genetics are not yet usable to predict response. [[P29844615 Schaid 2018 Fine-mapping#^p29844615-limits|Fine-mapping]]
