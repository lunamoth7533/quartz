# Histamine signalling

## Definition

Histamine is both a neurotransmitter and a peripheral signalling molecule involved in gut, skin and immune function. In the brain its neurons are few and their projections are widespread, which makes it a neuromodulatory system.

## How it works

**Source.** Brain histaminergic neurons are located exclusively in the tuberomamillary nucleus of the posterior hypothalamus and project throughout the central nervous system. [[P18626069 Haas 2008 Histamine in the nervous system#^p18626069-source|Histamine in the nervous system]] **Timing.** They are active during waking and contribute to wakefulness and attention, which is why histamine is discussed mainly in the context of arousal and sleep-wake regulation. [[P18626069 Haas 2008 Histamine in the nervous system#^p18626069-waking|Histamine in the nervous system]]

**Receptors.** H1 and H2 receptor actions are largely excitatory, while H3 receptors act as inhibitory auto- and heteroreceptors that restrain release. The same transmitter therefore both drives and limits its own signalling depending on receptor. [[P18626069 Haas 2008 Histamine in the nervous system#^p18626069-receptors|Histamine in the nervous system]]

## Evidence and status

Anatomy and firing patterns are established in animals; the receptor division is established pharmacologically. The relevance of histamine to psychiatric conditions is indirect, largely through sedation and weight effects of drugs that block H1 receptors, which is a side-effect signal rather than a mechanism of illness. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-offtarget|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]

> [!info]- In depth: a wakefulness system discovered through its pharmacology
> **Histamine as a transmitter.** Histamine is presented as a recognised neurotransmitter in the nervous system alongside its better-known roles elsewhere in the body, which is why the same drug can have central and peripheral effects that seem unrelated. Its neuronal source is a small population of cells in the posterior hypothalamus that projects widely across the brain, giving one nucleus access to the whole cortex. [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-histamine|Biogenic Amines]]
>
> **Wide projection, broad effect.** Because that population fires most during waking and quietest during sleep, and because its projections reach cortical, thalamic and hypothalamic targets, histaminergic transmission is described as part of the ascending arousal system. This is an anatomical argument about arousal rather than a claim that histamine is the cause of waking. [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-dopamine|Biogenic Amines]] [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]
>
> **Three receptor types with different jobs.** H1 receptors mediate the classical peripheral effects associated with allergy and are also present centrally; H2 receptors are best known for gastric acid secretion; H3 receptors sit presynaptically and control histamine release. Drugs that cross the blood-brain barrier and block H1 receptors produce sedation as a predictable consequence, which is why first- and second-generation antihistamines differ mainly in brain penetration rather than in receptor selectivity. [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-transport|Blood Brain Barrier]] [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How do medicines work]]
>
> **Clinical leverage from the same anatomy.** Because histaminergic activity is highest in waking, drugs that block it impair alertness; conversely, drugs that increase histaminergic or related arousal signalling were pursued as wake-promoting agents. Both directions illustrate the same point - the system contributes to arousal level, so its manipulation moves alertness rather than targeting a disease process. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Peripheral claims (allergy, gastric acid) and central claims (arousal, cognition) about histamine should not be transferred to one another; they involve overlapping receptors but different tissues, barriers and endpoints. [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-limit|Biogenic Amines]]
>
> **A small population with a large job.** The tuberomamillary nucleus contains the brain's only histaminergic neurons, and their firing follows the sleep-wake cycle: most active in waking, quietest in sleep. Because their axons reach cortex, thalamus and hypothalamus, one small cell group adjusts the background state of most of the forebrain - the defining arrangement of a neuromodulator, in which the message is the state rather than a specific instruction to one target. [[P18626069 Haas 2008 Histamine in the nervous system#^p18626069-source|Histamine in the nervous system]]
>
> **Why the H3 receptor is the system's own brake.** H3 receptors sit presynaptically on histaminergic terminals and on terminals of other transmitters, so histamine release partly regulates itself: more release activates more H3-mediated restraint. That autoreceptor loop is why H3 antagonism raises histaminergic tone and why the receptor has been a wake-promoting drug target, and it illustrates the general principle that transmitter systems carry their own negative feedback. [[P18626069 Haas 2008 Histamine in the nervous system#^p18626069-receptors|Histamine in the nervous system]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Pharmacology's oldest antihistamine lesson is this system's anatomy: first-generation H1 antagonists cross into the brain and produce sedation, which is the clinical fingerprint of removing one arousal input. The histamine case is the cleanest bridge in this library between a transmitter's circuit role and a familiar drug effect. [[Neuromodulation and circuit state]] [[Circadian rhythms and sleep]]

## Uncertainties

- Human histaminergic activity is not directly measurable outside research settings.
- The therapeutic and adverse consequences of histamine receptor blockade are better characterised than histamine's role in psychiatric conditions themselves.
