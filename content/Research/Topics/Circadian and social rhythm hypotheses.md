# Circadian and social rhythm hypotheses

## Definition

The social zeitgeber theory proposes that life events trigger mood episodes by disrupting social routines, which then disrupt biological circadian rhythms. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-theory|The social zeitgeber theory]]

## How it works

**Mechanism.** Regular social cues entrain circadian timing; disruption of those cues is proposed to destabilise sleep-wake and other rhythms that influence mood. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-theory|The social zeitgeber theory]]

**Clock biology.** The molecular clock is a transcriptional feedback loop, and clocks are distributed across tissues and coordinated centrally, which is the biological machinery the hypothesis invokes. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-tissue|Circadian clock architecture]]

**Evidence status.** The review that evaluates the theory reports limited direct support and considers an alternative internal-pacemaker account, so circadian disruption is a plausible contributing process rather than an established cause. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-evaluation|The social zeitgeber theory]] [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-status|The social zeitgeber theory]]

**Clinical form.** Structured routines and sleep regularity are components of psychosocial approaches, which is where the hypothesis has practical expression. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-components|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]]

## Evidence and status

Observational evidence links rhythm disruption to episode onset; intervention studies on rhythm-focused therapy show benefits with modest effect sizes and heterogeneous designs.

> [!info]- In depth: the hypothesis, the clock it invokes, and the state of its support
> **The proposal.** Social zeitgeber theory proposes that life events trigger mood episodes by disrupting social routines, which in turn disrupt biological circadian rhythms. Regular social cues entrain circadian timing; when they are lost - through shift work, travel, bereavement or a change in routine - sleep-wake timing and the rhythms coupled to it are proposed to destabilise in a way that raises episode risk. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-theory|The social zeitgeber theory]]
>
> **The evidence is weaker than the theory's currency suggests.** The review that evaluates the model reports that direct support is limited and considers an alternative internal-pacemaker account in which the rhythm disturbance is not primarily a social-entrainment failure. Its conclusion treats circadian disruption as a plausible contributing process rather than an established cause of mood episodes. The direction-of-effect problem is structural: disturbed sleep can be an early symptom of an episode, a consequence of one, or a trigger, and observational designs rarely separate the three. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-evaluation|The social zeitgeber theory]] [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-status|The social zeitgeber theory]] [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-limit|The social zeitgeber theory]]
>
> **The clock biology the hypothesis invokes.** The core molecular clock is a transcriptional-translational feedback loop in which clock proteins repress their own transcription over roughly 24 hours. Most tissues contain their own clocks, coordinated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus, so circadian organisation is distributed rather than located in one structure, and clock output influences metabolism, physiology and behaviour - which is why disrupted timing has consequences beyond sleep. That architecture is described primarily from animal models. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-tissue|Circadian clock architecture]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-outputs|Circadian clock architecture]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-limit|Circadian clock architecture]]
>
> **How sleep timing is actually regulated.** Sleep timing reflects the interaction of circadian rhythms with a homeostatic sleep drive: how long the person has been awake and what the body clock expects. Sleep cycles through rapid-eye-movement and non-REM stages with different associated activity patterns, so 'sleep disruption' is not one variable - timing, duration, continuity and stage composition are distinct measurements. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]] [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]
>
> **Where the hypothesis has practical expression.** Structured routines and sleep regularity are components of manualised psychological approaches in bipolar disorder. A systematic review and network meta-analysis of 39 randomised trials in outpatients found that adding psychotherapy to medication was associated with lower recurrence, and its component analysis linked psychoeducation with supervised skills practice delivered in family or group sessions to fewer recurrences than the same content delivered individually. Component analyses estimate average contributions across heterogeneous protocols; they do not identify which ingredient produced one person's improvement, and the authors temper their conclusions for heterogeneity in populations, duration and follow-up. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-components|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-limit|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-caution-ingredients|AI appraisal: Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Keep three claims apart: that circadian biology exists and is distributed, that rhythm disruption is associated with episodes, and that stabilising rhythms prevents episodes. The first is well established, the second is supported with unresolved direction, and the third is what the intervention literature tries to test. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]
