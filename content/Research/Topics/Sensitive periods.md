# Sensitive periods

## Definition

A sensitive (or critical) period is a developmental window during which experience has unusually strong and lasting effects on circuit organisation. Outside the window, the same experience has less structural effect.

## How it works

**Trigger.** In primary visual cortex, activity-dependent development is triggered by functional maturation of local inhibitory connections, including a late-developing subset of interneurons. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]]

**Closure.** Structural consolidation of competing inputs involves proteolytic reorganisation of the extracellular matrix that occurs during the critical period. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-consolidation|Critical period plasticity]]

**Reactivation.** The review argues that reactivating the process could support recovery of function in conditions such as amblyopia, framed as a research strategy rather than a treatment. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-reactivation|Critical period plasticity]]

**Timing across systems.** Different functions have different windows, and development is overlapping rather than strictly staged, which is why 'the' sensitive period is a simplification. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]]

## Evidence and status

Visual-cortex sensitive periods are firmly established in animals and supported by clinical observation of amblyopia treatment windows; evidence in humans for other functions is more variable and design-dependent. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-limit|Critical period plasticity]]

> [!info]- In depth: windows with machinery rather than convenient ages
> **The phenomenon.** Different systems mature on different schedules, which is why age of onset and sensitive windows differ across functions, and why an intervention at one age works differently at another. The schedule is a property of the developing system rather than of the calendar. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]]
>
> **The machinery, in the best-studied case.** In primary visual cortex, activity-dependent development is triggered by functional maturation of local inhibitory connections including a late-developing interneuron subset; structural consolidation of competing inputs involves proteolytic reorganisation of the extracellular matrix during the critical period; and the review argues that reactivating the process could support recovery of function, framed explicitly as a research strategy. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]] [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-consolidation|Critical period plasticity]] [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-reactivation|Critical period plasticity]]
>
> **Why the evidence base is narrow.** The critical-period findings come largely from visual-cortex work in animals, and the review is explicit that reactivation is a proposal; the human developmental literature describes typical development and generalises across samples. Extending a visual-cortex window to language, social development or clinical intervention is an inference. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-limit|Critical period plasticity]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-limit|The basics of brain development]]
>
> **Experience is an input, not a script.** Early experience interacts with genetically guided development, so variation in outcome is the expected case and a missed window does not determine a trajectory in the way the metaphor suggests. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]]
>
> **Reading rule.** State the function, the species, the measured marker and whether the claim is about mechanism or about intervention; the second does not follow from the first without a trial. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-limit|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]]
>
> **What the window's opening and closing actually are.** On the inhibitory-trigger account, the window opens when local inhibitory circuits mature enough to sharpen the activity patterns that drive refinement, and it closes when structural consolidation - extracellular-matrix reorganisation around the refined contacts - stabilises the outcome. Both ends are mechanisms with molecular handles, which is why "critical period" names a manipulable biological process rather than an age on a chart. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]] [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-consolidation|Critical period plasticity]]
>
> **Why human extension needs separate evidence.** The machinery was worked out in visual cortex of animals; human development certainly shows schedule-dependent learning, but identifying which human windows have which machinery - and whether they can be reopened - requires human measurement that mostly does not yet exist. Claims that a human sensitive period "closed" at a given age are therefore usually inferences from behaviour, not measurements of the underlying process. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-limit|Critical period plasticity]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-limit|The basics of brain development]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The sensitive-period logic is the developmental argument the condition domains reach for first - autism's early-emerging differences, ADHD's developmental definition - and it is also the argument most often over-extended; this note's scope discipline is what keeps those domain uses honest. ## Uncertainties

- Claims about 'reopening' sensitive periods in humans are not clinically established and should not be read as intervention guidance.
