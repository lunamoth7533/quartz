# Language

## Definition

Language is a structured system for communication, described through phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics and pragmatics. Its neural organisation is distributed and only partly localised.

## How it works

**Structure.** The components of language are separable in principle and dissociable in aphasia, which is why linguistic description and lesion evidence developed together. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-components|Language]]

**Development.** Language develops through a sequence of stages with wide variation in timing, and the section treats the timetable as descriptive rather than fixed. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-development|Language]]

**Localisation history.** Broca's 1861 work localised a language function to a frontal region; the classical Wernicke model connected receptive and expressive centres by a pathway. [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-history|Language]] [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-model|Language]]

**Modern qualification.** The classical model is historically decisive but incomplete: language functions are distributed, and individual lesions do not map cleanly to syndromes. [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-limits|Language]]

## Evidence and status

Lesion-deficit associations are well established; fine-grained functional localisation from imaging is less stable across individuals and tasks. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]]

> [!info]- In depth: components, development and a localisation story with limits
> **The components.** Language is described through phonemes, morphemes, syntax, semantics and pragmatics, which is why 'language ability' is not one measurement: a person can have intact vocabulary and impaired pragmatics, or the reverse. Development is presented as a sequence of stages with substantial variation in timing. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-components|Language]] [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-development|Language]]
>
> **The classical localisation.** Broca's 1861 work localised a language function to a frontal region, and the Wernicke model connects a receptive and an expressive centre through a pathway. That model was historically decisive but is now presented as incomplete: language functions are distributed, and modern work describes networks rather than two centres. [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-history|Language]] [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-model|Language]] [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-limits|Language]]
>
> **Language and thought remain an open question.** The relationship between language and thought is presented as a long-running question with evidence on both sides - so claims that language determines thought, or that it is irrelevant to it, both outrun the sources. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-thought|Language]]
>
> **Association cortex supplies the framework.** Association areas link information from primary and unimodal regions, and anterior association cortex supports executive processing; language networks are a special case of that organisation rather than a separate principle. [[F109 Neuroscience Online association and executive processing#^f109-areas|Association and Executive Processing]]
>
> **Method caveat.** Language imaging inherits the analytic flexibility and power problems documented for neuroimaging, so a group difference in activation during a language task is a sample-level description; lesion evidence and network descriptions remain the stronger basis for localisation claims. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]] [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-limits|Language]]

The language-and-thought relationship remains a long-running question with evidence on both sides. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-components|Language]] [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-development|Language]] [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-history|Language]] [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-limits|Language]] [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-thought|Language]]
>
> **Why aphasia was the original evidence.** The classical localisation model - production separated from comprehension, lesion patterns producing distinct syndromes - came from the correlation between specific lesions and specific language losses. That evidence established language's partial separability; the modern qualifications are that lesions compare coarse regions with fine functions, that recovery redistributes function, and that imaging in intact people shows widely distributed networks rather than two boxes. The classical model survives as a first-order map with its exceptions named. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language]]
>
> **What development shows about structure.** Children acquire the components in overlapping stages with wide timing variation, and late-talking children frequently catch up - facts that constrain any claim equating a timetable with a mechanism. The developmental sequence is also evidence of dissociation: components can develop unevenly, which supports treating phonology, syntax and semantics as separable systems rather than facets of one capacity. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Neuroanatomy supplies the lesion method and the fibre pathways; psychology supplies the constructs that lesions are scored against - the classical language findings are the historical case where the two domains produced one result neither could have produced alone.
