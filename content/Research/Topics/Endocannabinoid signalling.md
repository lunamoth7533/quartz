# Endocannabinoid signalling

## Definition

Endocannabinoids are lipid signalling molecules synthesised on demand rather than stored in vesicles. They couple postsynaptic activity to presynaptic release probability, which makes them one of the clearest departures from the standard synaptic model.

## How it works

**On demand.** Because endocannabinoids are made when needed rather than packaged, their release is a property of the producing cell's activity rather than of a vesicle pool. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-synthesis|Endogenous cannabinoid system]] **Retrograde action.** They typically travel from the postsynaptic cell back to presynaptic CB1 receptors, where they reduce transmitter release - short-term depression of the synapse that generated them. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-retrograde|Endogenous cannabinoid system]]

**System scope.** The system operates across brain and body, with roles in pain, appetite, stress responses and immune signalling, which is why its pharmacology is unusually broad. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-scope|Endogenous cannabinoid system]]

## Evidence and status

The synthetic pathway and retrograde mechanism are established in reduced preparations with direct recording and pharmacology. Human evidence comes largely from pharmacological challenge and from observational studies, which are weaker for causal inference.

> [!info]- In depth: a signalling system that runs backwards
> **Synthesis on demand.** Endocannabinoids are lipids made and released from the postsynaptic cell when it is active, rather than stored in vesicles and released by calcium-triggered fusion in the presynaptic terminal. That anatomical reversal is the defining feature of the system: the signal travels from the receiving cell back to the sending cell, which makes it a feedback mechanism by construction. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-synthesis|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]] [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-retrograde|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]
>
> **Receptors and where they sit.** CB1 receptors are the main neuronal subtype and are found predominantly on presynaptic terminals, where their activation reduces transmitter release; CB2 receptors are concentrated outside the nervous system on immune cells, which is why their pharmacology is discussed separately from the psychotropic effects associated with the system. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-retrograde|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]] [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-scope|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]
>
> **What the mechanism does to a circuit.** Because the signal is triggered by postsynaptic activity and acts presynaptically, it produces short- and long-term depression of specific inputs - the synapses that were recently driving the cell become weaker. This is a selective, activity-dependent gain control rather than a general dampening, and it is one of the clearest examples of retrograde signalling in the mammalian brain. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-retrograde|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]
>
> **Why the pharmacology is hard.** The same receptor system participates in appetite, pain, memory, movement and mood circuits, so a systemic drug inevitably hits several functions at once, and the effects depend on which inputs are active. That is the mechanistic reason therapeutic separation in this system has been difficult rather than a failure of effort. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-scope|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]
>
> **What cannot be concluded.** Evidence that endocannabinoids modulate a circuit in a preparation does not establish that a cannabis product or a dietary compound has a corresponding clinical effect, and the source used here is a pharmacology review of the endogenous system rather than a trial of any product. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-limit|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]
>
> **The two best-studied messengers.** The endocannabinoid literature centres on two lipids: 2-arachidonoyl glycerol and anandamide. They are made and degraded by distinct enzymatic pathways, which is the structural reason a single "endocannabinoid" label hides several different signalling kinetics - one messenger's lifetime and spread are not another's, and experiments that manipulate synthesis or degradation enzymes can separate them. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-synthesis|An Introduction to the Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]
>
> **The retrograde loop, stated mechanically.** Postsynaptic depolarisation and activity raise endocannabinoid production; the lipids travel backward across the synapse and activate presynaptic CB1 receptors, which suppress transmitter release. Depolarisation-induced suppression of inhibition (and of excitation) is the standard experimental demonstration, and because the messenger can reach neighbouring terminals, the suppression can be heterosynaptic - inputs near the active one are damped too. That makes the system a local, activity-gated gain control rather than a point-to-point channel. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-retrograde|An Introduction to the Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]
>
> **A note on scope in this library.** The review this article rests on is also an introduction to perturbation of the system in schizophrenia research; that clinical-connection literature is a research programme, not an established account of any illness mechanism, and nothing in it supports claims about cannabis products' effects in people. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-scope|An Introduction to the Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Pharmacology meets this system at its enzymes and receptors, and the same broad distribution that makes the system scientifically interesting is what makes systemic drugs hit appetite, pain, mood and memory at once - the clearest neurochemistry-level illustration of why target breadth predicts side-effect breadth.

## Uncertainties

- Because the system is distributed and involved in pain, appetite and immunity, findings in one domain should not be generalised to others.
- Human dosing and long-term effects of cannabinoid exposure are outside this library's scope; the note covers mechanism and research only.
