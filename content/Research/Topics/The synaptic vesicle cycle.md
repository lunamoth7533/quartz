# The synaptic vesicle cycle

## Definition

The synaptic vesicle cycle is the sequence by which a presynaptic terminal packages transmitter into vesicles, releases it on calcium entry, and retrieves membrane to refill the pool. It is the machinery behind quantal transmission.

## How it works

**Calcium trigger.** An arriving spike opens voltage-gated calcium channels, calcium enters the terminal, and that entry is the key trigger for release - established by classical experiments at the skeletal neuromuscular junction. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-calcium|Transmitter release]] **Quantal release.** Transmitter is released in discrete units, which is the evidence that vesicles are the unit of release. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-quanta|Transmitter release]] **Pools.** Vesicles cycle through reserve, recycling and readily releasable pools, and how full those pools are sets how much a second spike can release - the mechanism underlying short-term facilitation and depression. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-facilitation|Synaptic plasticity]] [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-requirements|Transmitter release]]

**Retrieval and reloading.** After fusion, membrane is retrieved and vesicles are refilled by transporters, which means transmitter supply and vesicle supply are separate constraints. [[F108 Neuroscience Online acetylcholine#^f108-inactivation|Acetylcholine transmission]]

## Evidence and status

The cycle is established from electrophysiology, electron microscopy and molecular perturbation, largely in neuromuscular junction and slice preparations. Molecular components are well characterised; the relative importance of each pool differs by synapse type, so 'the' vesicle cycle is a composite description.

> [!info]- In depth: the machinery that converts a spike into a chemical message
> **Quantal release.** Transmission occurs in quantal units, which is the evidence base for the vesicle-release model: the postsynaptic response comes in multiples of a smallest unit, consistent with the fusion of individual transmitter-filled vesicles. Quantal size and release probability together determine the amplitude of a synaptic response, and they are separately regulated. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-quanta|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]]
>
> **Calcium as the trigger.** Calcium entry into the presynaptic terminal is a key step in chemical transmission, established in classic experiments at the skeletal neuromuscular junction. Release depends on both calcium availability and the state of the release machinery, so the same action potential can produce different amounts of transmitter at different moments - the observation that makes short-term plasticity possible. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-calcium|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-requirements|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]]
>
> **From arrival to fusion.** The arriving action potential opens voltage-gated calcium channels in the terminal, vesicles fuse with the presynaptic membrane, transmitter diffuses across the cleft and binds postsynaptic receptors. The cycle closes when membrane is retrieved and vesicles are refilled, which is why transmitter synthesis and reuptake are part of synaptic function rather than separate housekeeping. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-release|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F108 Neuroscience Online acetylcholine#^f108-inactivation|Acetylcholine]]
>
> **Pools and availability.** Because only a fraction of vesicles is available for immediate release while the rest must be mobilised, a sustained train of spikes draws down the accessible pool faster than it refills - the mechanism behind short-term depression. Synaptic strength therefore depends on recent history as much as on the number of release sites. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-facilitation|Synaptic Plasticity]] [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-requirements|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]]
>
> **Where the account is anchored.** The classical experiments were performed at the neuromuscular junction and in similar large, accessible preparations, which is why the mechanism is described with unusual confidence at that synapse and by analogy elsewhere. The chapter-level caveat is that release mechanisms are generalised from those preparations. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-limit|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-limit|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **Release probability is the second variable.** Quantal size - the postsynaptic response to one vesicle - and release probability - the chance that an arriving spike releases any given vesicle - are jointly enough to predict a synapse's output, and they are regulated separately. Two contacts on the same cell can share transmitter and receptor types yet differ twenty-fold in probability, which is why synapse identity does not fix synaptic strength. That separability is also what plasticity exploits: short-term facilitation raises probability through residual calcium, and depression lowers it by draining the readily releasable pool. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-quanta|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-facilitation|Synaptic Plasticity]]
>
> **Pools are a budget, not a warehouse.** The reserve pool holds most vesicles; the readily releasable pool is the small fraction docked and fusion-ready. A train of spikes spends the releasable pool first, and recovery depends on mobilisation from the reserve and on membrane retrieval and refilling - steps that take longer than the inter-spike interval during fast firing. This budget view explains why depression is strongest at high-probability synapses (they spend their stock quickly) and weakest at low-probability ones, and why a synapse's response to a burst reflects its recent spending history. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-requirements|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-facilitation|Synaptic Plasticity]]
>
> **Why the classical preparation earned the confidence.** The neuromuscular junction is large, accessible and electrically quiet, so quantal events can be recorded individually and calcium entry manipulated directly. The mechanism earned at that synapse is described with unusual confidence there; at central synapses the same machinery is inferred from smaller, faster and harder-to-access contacts, which is why pool sizes and probabilities measured at one synapse are not transplanted to another without evidence. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-limit|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Pharmacology reaches this machinery directly: drugs that change vesicular filling, release or recycling shift what every downstream receptor sees, which is why the pharmacology domain treats the presynaptic terminal as a target class in its own right alongside the receptors themselves. ## Uncertainties

- Vesicle pool sizes and release probabilities differ by synapse, so findings from one preparation do not generalise to all synapses.
- Human presynaptic function is inferred from indirect measures, including imaging and pharmacology, rather than recorded directly.
