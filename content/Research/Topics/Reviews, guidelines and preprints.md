# Reviews, guidelines and preprints

**Definition.** Reviews, consensus statements and clinical guidelines summarize bodies of evidence, which makes them efficient entry points and one layer removed from the primary data. [[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-scope|World Federation ADHD consensus statement]]

## Supported claims

- The CANMAT and ISBD summary translates evidence into first-, second- and third-line recommendations across illness phases. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]
- The ADHD consensus statement curates 208 assertions using stated inclusion rules. [[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-methods|World Federation ADHD consensus statement]]
- Consensus statements reflect judgment about a changing literature; treatment questions should also check newer primary studies. [[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-caution-consensus|AI synthesis: World Federation ADHD consensus statement]]

> [!info]- In depth: four document types with four kinds of authority
> **Reviews.** A systematic review is a method: PRISMA specifies what such a review should report so readers can assess what was done, including a flow diagram documenting how records were identified, screened, excluded and included. Reporting completeness governs auditability, not quality - a review can be fully reported and still rest on biased studies. [[P33782057 PRISMA 2020#^p33782057-purpose|PRISMA 2020]] [[P33782057 PRISMA 2020#^p33782057-items|PRISMA 2020]] [[P33782057 PRISMA 2020#^p33782057-flow|PRISMA 2020]] [[P33782057 PRISMA 2020#^p33782057-limit|PRISMA 2020]]
>
> **Certainty is graded separately from the recommendation.** GRADE separates the certainty of evidence from the strength of a recommendation, so a strong recommendation can rest on low-certainty evidence where values and context justify it, and certainty is rated down for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. Reading a guideline means reading both columns. [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-domains|GRADE]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-use|GRADE]]
>
> **Guidelines are service documents built on an evidence review.** National guidance such as the NICE bipolar guideline ties graded recommendations to the evidence review its committee used, and the CANMAT and ISBD guidance translates efficacy, safety and tolerability evidence into first-, second- and third-line recommendations by phase of illness. Both reflect a service context as well as a literature, and neither transfers automatically to another health system or to an individual. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-framing|Bipolar disorder CG185]] [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]
>
> **Consensus statements are judgement, however well-catalogued.** The World Federation ADHD consensus statement curates 208 assertions drawn from studies above a size threshold and was read by hundreds of endorsers; its own caution is that consensus reflects judgement about a literature that keeps changing, so treatment questions should be checked against newer primary studies and guidelines. [[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-scope|ADHD consensus statement]] [[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-methods|ADHD consensus statement]] [[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-caution-consensus|AI appraisal: ADHD consensus statement]]
>
> **Preprints and single papers.** A preprint has a permanent identifier and no peer review; a journal article has usually undergone peer review, though peer review varies in rigour and some journal items (editorials, commentaries, conference abstracts) do not undergo full review. The standing rule for either is the same here: identify the design, the access level actually checked, and whether the claim quoted matches the anchored statement. [[F22 Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions#^f22-caution-authority|AI appraisal: Cochrane Handbook]] [[P33954258 Munafo 2017 Reproducible science#^p33954258-practices|A manifesto for reproducible science]]

> [!info]- Reference: how authority differs across document types
> **Systematic reviews.** A systematic review follows an explicit protocol and search strategy; where those are missing, the review is closer to a long narrative article. [[P33782057 PRISMA 2020#^p33782057-items|PRISMA 2020]]
>
> **Guidelines.** Guidelines add value judgements to evidence - cost, feasibility, equity - which is why GRADE separates certainty from recommendation strength. [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]] Recommendations therefore reflect a service context as well as a literature. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-framing|Bipolar disorder CG185]]
>
> **Preprints.** Preprints make work visible before peer review; they are useful for currency and hazardous for settled claims, since many are revised or never published. [[P33954258 Munafo 2017 Reproducible science#^p33954258-practices|A manifesto for reproducible science]]
>
> **Reviews of reviews.** Reviews of the same question can disagree because of inclusion criteria, outcome selection and synthesis method, so reading the methods section is not optional. [[F129 Cochrane Handbook Chapter 10#^f129-overview|Cochrane Handbook chapter 10]]
>
> **Practical order.** Prefer preregistered, protocol-driven syntheses with certainty ratings; treat narrative reviews and preprints as orientation rather than as the final word.

> [!info]- Further depth: four document types with different authority
> PRISMA specifies what a systematic review should report, including a flow diagram documenting identification, screening, exclusion and inclusion, and governs auditability rather than quality. GRADE separates certainty from recommendation strength, so a strong recommendation can rest on low-certainty evidence where values and context justify it. Guidelines tie graded recommendations to an evidence review and to a service context - the CANMAT/ISBD product is phase-specific and carries no individual dose guidance - and consensus statements curate an evolving literature by documented thresholds rather than producing new data.
