# Nervous system divisions

**Definition.** The nervous system is divided anatomically into central and peripheral parts and functionally into sensory input, integration and motor output. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-divisions|Basic Structure and Function of the Nervous System]]

## Supported claims

- The central division is the brain and spinal cord; the peripheral division carries signals to and from the rest of the body. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-divisions|Basic Structure and Function of the Nervous System]]
- Autonomic function splits into sympathetic and parasympathetic branches that often oppose each other while cooperating. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-autonomic|Basic Structure and Function of the Nervous System]]

> [!info]- In depth: the vocabulary the rest of the domain depends on
> **The primary division.** The nervous system is described by a central division (brain and spinal cord) and a peripheral division, and functionally through sensory input, integration and motor output. The division is organisational: central and peripheral tissue differ in their glia, barriers, regenerative capacity and disease patterns, which is why the same pathological process behaves differently in each. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-divisions|Nervous System Structure and Function]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]]
>
> **The functional division and its motor arm.** The somatic and autonomic motor divisions differ in their targets and in whether they are subject to voluntary control; the autonomic division is further split into sympathetic and parasympathetic branches that cooperate as well as oppose, with the enteric system described as a largely self-organising peripheral network. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-autonomic|Nervous System Structure and Function]] [[F31 OpenStax The peripheral nervous system#^f31-enteric|The Peripheral Nervous System]]
>
> **Divisions predict deficits.** Because the adult central nervous system has limited regenerative capacity while peripheral axons can regrow, a transection produces very different prognoses depending on which side of the division it occurs; the vocabulary is therefore clinical as well as descriptive. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure and Function]]
>
> **Reading rule.** When a source says 'peripheral' or 'autonomic', the useful follow-up is which subdivision, which transmitters and which targets, because the functional consequences are carried by those specifics rather than by the broad label. [[F32 OpenStax Divisions of the autonomic nervous system#^f32-limit|Divisions of the Autonomic Nervous System]]

> [!info]- Reference: the division scheme and its limits
> **Central and peripheral.** The central nervous system is the brain and spinal cord, lying in the cranial cavity and vertebral canal; the peripheral nervous system is everything else, which is why it is described by exclusion rather than by a boundary. [[F54 Neuroscience Online nervous system overview#^f54-divisions|Overview of the Nervous System]] [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-divisions|Nervous System Structure]]
>
> **Functional division.** Sensory input, integration and motor output describe the flow of information through either division, and this functional description coexists with the anatomical one. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-divisions|Nervous System Structure]]
>
> **Peripheral subdivisions.** The peripheral system includes twelve cranial nerves and the spinal nerves with their plexuses, and it carries somatic and autonomic fibres alongside the largely self-organising enteric nervous system. [[F31 OpenStax The peripheral nervous system#^f31-cranial|The Peripheral Nervous System]] [[F31 OpenStax The peripheral nervous system#^f31-enteric|The Peripheral Nervous System]]
>
> **Autonomic branches.** Sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions differ in outflow and signalling, and most organs receive both, so regulation reflects their balance rather than the action of one branch. [[F32 OpenStax Divisions of the autonomic nervous system#^f32-cooperation|Divisions of the Autonomic Nervous System]]
>
> **Why the scheme is a teaching device.** Real circuits cross these boundaries continuously, and the divisions are most useful for organising anatomy, not for predicting function. [[F05 OpenStax The central nervous system#^f05-networks|The Central Nervous System]]
>
> **The divisions are diagnostic categories as much as anatomy.** Central-versus-peripheral changes the disease list (demyelinating disease versus Guillain-Barre-type neuropathies), the regenerative prognosis, and the cellular players (oligodendrocytes versus Schwann cells); somatic-versus-autonomic changes which symptoms are reportable versus which are measurable only indirectly. The label a finding falls under therefore determines which textbook continues the story - which is why the division system is load-bearing across the whole domain. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-divisions|Nervous System Structure and Function]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]]
>
> **The enteric system is the boundary-stretching case.** A largely self-organising peripheral network with its own transmitters and reflex circuits, communicating with the central nervous system but not controlled by it in the usual sense, the enteric system is the reason the classical divisions are described as a framework with important exceptions rather than a partition. [[F31 OpenStax The peripheral nervous system#^f31-enteric|The Peripheral Nervous System]] [[Microbiome-gut-brain axis]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The neuroendocrine domain's autonomic-measurement article reads the output arm of these divisions from outside the body, and the interoception article describes the afferent arm into the insula; the divisions note supplies the map both assume. [[Autonomic measurement and heart rate variability]] [[Interoception and autonomic pathways]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Anatomical divisions are teaching aids; function crosses them constantly.
