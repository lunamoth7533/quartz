# Personality models

## Definition

Personality models describe enduring patterns of thinking, feeling and behaving. Trait approaches measure these as continuous dimensions; typological approaches assign categories, which the evidence supports less well.

## How it works

**Trait dimensions.** Contemporary models summarise personality with a few broad dimensions derived from covariation among many descriptors, which is why personality is normally reported as a profile rather than a type. [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-traits|What Is Personality?]]

**Situational debate.** The consistency of behaviour across situations is limited, and the field resolved this by treating traits as tendencies that interact with context rather than as fixed dispositions. [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-debate|What Is Personality?]] The situational emphasis in social psychology is the same argument from another direction. [[F46 OpenStax Psychology 2e social psychology#^f46-situational|What Is Social Psychology?]]

**Clinical use.** Personality constructs appear in clinical classification as patterns of inner experience and behaviour that deviate markedly from expectations and are inflexible; dimensional models are increasingly used in research. [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|Borderline Personality Disorder]]

**Measurement.** Self-report personality measures are vulnerable to reference-group effects and to invariance problems when compared across cultures. [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-practice|Measurement invariance]]

## Evidence and status

Trait structure replicates across cultures; the number of dimensions and the boundary with psychopathology remain debated, which is the same disagreement that appears in the classification articles.

> [!info]- In depth: traits, categories and the hybrid that clinical practice uses
> **Traits are dimensional.** Historical perspectives on personality are traced from temperament ideas through trait and psychodynamic traditions, and trait approaches describe personality through dimensions rather than types, which is why modern work uses continuous scores. Enduring disagreements about consistency across situations remain live and are the same question raised by dimensional accounts of psychopathology. [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-historical|Personality]] [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-traits|Personality]] [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-debate|Personality]]
>
> **Clinical categories persist for practical reasons.** Borderline personality disorder is described through long-standing instability in mood, self-image and relationships with marked sensitivity to rejection and abandonment; features often emerge in adolescence or early adulthood and diagnosis is possible when criteria are met, with evidence-based psychotherapies as the primary approach and medication for specific symptoms rather than the disorder. The clinical label sits on a dimensional construct. [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|Borderline Personality Disorder]] [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-onset|Borderline Personality Disorder]] [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-treatment|Borderline Personality Disorder]] [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-limit|Borderline Personality Disorder]]
>
> **The dimensional critique is mainstream here.** Categorical taxonomies suffer from arbitrary thresholds, unclear boundaries, frequent co-occurrence, within-disorder heterogeneity and diagnostic instability, and the proposed response is to organise covarying syndromes into dimensional spectra as a research effort. Personality pathology is where that critique is least contested. [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]] [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-structure|HiTOP]]
>
> **Measurement before comparison.** Comparing trait or pathology scores across groups or time assumes invariance; absent invariance changes what a difference means, and reliability is not validity - an instrument can produce consistent scores that consistently miss the construct. [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-practice|Measurement invariance]] [[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-caution-score|AI appraisal: Developing and validating scales]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Cultural expectations shape what counts as disordered, so criteria are not culture-free, and harm or dysfunction is part of the definition rather than an add-on. A trait score is a position in a distribution, not a verdict. [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-cultural|Psychological Disorders]] [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-harm|Psychological Disorders]]

> [!info]- Further depth: dimensions, categories and the clinical hybrid
> Trait approaches describe personality through dimensions rather than types, and the situation-consistency debate in that literature is the same question dimensional psychopathology raises. Clinical categories persist for practical reasons: borderline personality disorder is described through long-standing instability in mood, self-image and relationships with marked rejection sensitivity, emerges in adolescence or early adulthood, and is treated primarily with evidence-based psychotherapies - a categorical label on a dimensional construct. The HiTOP critique supplies the reason the hybrid is contested, and invariance testing is the prerequisite for any group comparison. [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-traits|Personality]] [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-debate|Personality]] [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|Borderline Personality Disorder]] [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]] [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-practice|Measurement invariance]]
>
> **What a trait score promises, precisely.** A dimension score locates a person in a population distribution and predicts average tendencies with modest accuracy; it does not describe internal causation, and its predictive value decays as the outcome moves away from the samples used to derive it. Rank order is the defensible claim - people's relative standing is fairly stable in adulthood - while cross-situational consistency of specific behaviours is limited, which is why traits interact with situations rather than overriding them. [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-traits|What Is Personality?]] [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-debate|What Is Personality?]]
>
> **The clinical category on a dimensional base.** Borderline personality disorder is the worked example: a categorical diagnosis whose criteria describe a dimensional construct at its extreme, with psychotherapies as the evidence-based primary approach and medication reserved for specific symptoms. The hybrid is not a compromise pending better taxonomy; it is what practice looks like when categories carry service and communication functions while dimensions carry measurement. [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-treatment|Borderline Personality Disorder]] [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-structure|HiTOP]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Measurement invariance is this article's load-bearing methods import: cross-cultural and cross-time comparisons of personality scores stand or fall on invariance testing, and the clinical-domain dimensional critiques are the same measurement argument extended to psychopathology.
