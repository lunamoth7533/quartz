# Efficacy versus tolerability

**Definition.** Efficacy is how well a treatment reduces the target problem under study; tolerability is the burden and side effects it creates. [[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-tolerability|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]

## Supported claims

- Dose-effect analyses treat efficacy and discontinuation due to adverse events as separate outcomes, with dose-dependent dropout for amphetamines and methylphenidate. [[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-tolerability|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]
- In adults, atomoxetine and guanfacine had lower acceptability than placebo in a component network meta-analysis. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-acceptability|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]
- Amphetamines were less well tolerated than placebo in both children and adults in an earlier network meta-analysis. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-tolerability|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Trials measure tolerability mainly through dropout, which undercounts bothersome effects that people endure rather than report.
