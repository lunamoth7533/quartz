# MRI versus EEG

**Definition.** Structural MRI images anatomy with high spatial resolution while EEG records summed electrical activity with high temporal resolution. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

## Supported claims

- The overview treats imaging and EEG as different modalities measuring different things. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]
- Conventional structural MRI shows anatomy; functional MRI measures activity-related signal change rather than electrical activity itself. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-caution-modality|AI synthesis: Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]
- Structural findings such as group cortical thickness differences describe anatomy, not circuit dynamics. [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

> [!info]- In depth: space and time are different currencies
> **Complementary physics.** MRI reconstructs anatomy and tissue properties from nuclear magnetic resonance signals with millimetre spatial resolution but resolution-limited time; EEG records electric fields generated by synchronised neuronal activity, with millisecond timing but spatial ambiguity caused by volume conduction and the inverse problem. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-mri|Brain imaging]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-eeg|Brain imaging]]
>
> **What each can answer.** Structural questions (where is the lesion, what tissue is it) go to MRI; temporal questions (when does the abnormal activity occur, how does it evolve during a seizure) go to EEG. Combining them is how questions about structure-function relationships are addressed in clinical practice. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-management|Epilepsy]]
>
> **Neither measures neurons directly.** MRI contrast reflects tissue properties; EEG reflects summed fields. Claims that a technique shows 'brain activity' should specify the signal's physical basis and its inference distance to neuronal firing. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-caution-modality|AI synthesis: Brain imaging]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Match the modality to the question and state the inference; a mismatch produces a finding that cannot bear the interpretation placed on it. [[Structural versus functional measures]]

> [!info]- Reference: two modalities with different physics and different inference
> **Signals.** Structural MRI images anatomy from magnetic properties of tissue; functional MRI tracks blood flow and oxygenation as a haemodynamic proxy for activity; EEG records summed electrical activity with millisecond resolution but limited spatial precision. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-mri|Brain imaging]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-fmri|Brain imaging]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-eeg|Brain imaging]]
>
> **What each can answer.** MRI is suited to structure and slow functional change; EEG is suited to timing and to oscillatory activity, which is why epilepsy evaluation relies on electrophysiology. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]]
>
> **Comparability.** Because the modalities measure different quantities, agreement is informative and disagreement is not automatically an error. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-caution-modality|AI appraisal: Brain imaging]]
>
> **Analysis dependence.** Both require preprocessing choices that affect results, and reproducibility in imaging research is a documented concern. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]]
>
> **Why the two are combined in practice.** Epilepsy care is the worked example: MRI looks for the structural lesion, EEG times and characterises the abnormal discharges, and neither alone answers both questions. The combination pattern generalises - space and time are different currencies, and most clinical and research questions need an explicit statement of which currency the claim is made in. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-management|Epilepsy]] [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]]
>
> **The inference distance differs in kind, not just degree.** MRI contrast is one step removed from tissue properties; EEG is several steps from single-neuron firing, through summed post-synaptic currents, volume conduction through tissue and skull, and an inverse problem with no unique solution. Both distances are manageable when named; both produce over-reading when unnamed. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-caution-modality|AI appraisal: Brain imaging]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The research-methods domain treats this as an operationalisation problem: a modality measures an operationalisation of a construct, and modality disagreement is often a disagreement between operationalisations rather than an error. [[Measurement validity and reliability]] [[Structural versus functional measures]]
>
> **Costs, settings and patient factors are selection criteria too.** EEG is portable, quiet, contraindication-free and long-recordable; MRI is expensive, loud, slow and excludes some implants and severe claustrophobia; CT trades resolution for speed and availability in acute settings. A modality-choice claim that omits these practical dimensions is incomplete for clinical purposes, because the best-answerable-question test runs through feasibility as well as through physics. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]
>
> **Modality choice interacts with the population.** MRI excludes or constrains some implanted devices and severe claustrophobia; EEG tolerates movement better and can be used in young children and in prolonged monitoring, but its spatial ambiguity limits localisation claims. Those population-level constraints are part of the answer to "which test should be used", not a footnote to it. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Spatial and temporal resolution trade off, so choosing a modality commits you to the kind of question you can answer.
