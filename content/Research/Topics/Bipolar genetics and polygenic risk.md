# Bipolar genetics and polygenic risk

**Definition.** Genomic studies of bipolar disorder find many common variants of small effect, alongside differences related to ascertainment and subtype. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|Bipolar disorder genomics]]

## Supported claims

- A multi-ancestry analysis of 158,036 cases and 2.8 million controls identified 298 loci and 36 credible genes. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-loci|Bipolar disorder genomics]]
- Genetic architecture differed by ascertainment strategy and bipolar subtype. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|Bipolar disorder genomics]]
- Risk architecture is statistical and does not predict an individual's course. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-caution-prediction|AI synthesis: Bipolar disorder genomics]]

> [!info]- In depth: a large, polygenic architecture and the boundaries of prediction
> **What the largest analysis found.** A multi-ancestry meta-analysis identified 298 genome-wide significant loci and 36 credible genes - a fourfold increase over previous findings - plus one ancestry-specific association in the East Asian cohort. Cell-type analyses implicated GABAergic interneurons and medium spiny neurons, and common and rare variant signals converged on the same genes. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-loci|Genomics of bipolar disorder]] [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-cells|Genomics of bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Ascertainment and subtype change the architecture.** The authors report differences in genetic architecture depending on the source of patient ascertainment and on bipolar subtype, type I or type II - so samples defined differently are not interchangeable, and a finding in one cannot be read straight across to the other. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|Genomics of bipolar disorder]]
>
> **High heritability does not mean a known mechanism.** The study notes heritability of 60-80% while stating that most underlying determinants remained unknown before the analysis, which is the standard shape of complex-trait genetics: substantial inherited contribution, distributed across many loci of small effect. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-limit|Genomics of bipolar disorder]] [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-heritability|10 years of GWAS discovery]]
>
> **Prediction is a different claim.** Risk architecture is statistical and polygenic; it describes populations and does not predict an individual's course or justify personal genetic prediction. Turning a score into a decision requires calibration in a comparable population, evidence of decision benefit, and a pathway that does not yet exist for most conditions in this vault. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-caution-prediction|AI appraisal: Genomics of bipolar disorder]] [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-caution-individual|AI appraisal: 10 years of GWAS discovery]] [[F68 NHGRI Polygenic risk scores#^f68-limits|Polygenic risk scores]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Name the sample, the ancestry composition, the subtype definition and the claim type. Genetic architecture claims from one ascertainment scheme are hypotheses for another. [[P29844615 Schaid 2018 Fine-mapping#^p29844615-limits|Fine-mapping]]

> [!info]- Reference: architecture and what a polygenic score can support here
> **Common variants.** Large association studies in bipolar disorder implicate many common variants of small effect, consistent with the polygenic architecture found across psychiatric conditions. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-loci|Genomics of bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Shared and specific.** Genetic correlations with other psychiatric conditions are substantial, which is a statement about overlapping architecture rather than a claim that the conditions are the same. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|Genomics of bipolar disorder]]
>
> **From loci to mechanism.** Association signals mark regions; fine-mapping and functional annotation are required before a variant-to-gene claim is warranted. [[P29844615 Schaid 2018 Fine-mapping#^p29844615-limits|Fine-mapping]]
>
> **Scores.** Polygenic scores index liability at group level and depend on the discovery sample's ancestry; they are not diagnostic and are limited for individual prediction. [[F68 NHGRI Polygenic risk scores#^f68-limits|Polygenic risk scores]]
>
> **Clinical boundary.** Genetic findings inform aetiological research and counselling for rare high-penetrance syndromes, not routine diagnosis of common forms. [[F67 NHGRI GWAS fact sheet#^f67-meaning|GWAS fact sheet]]
>
> **What a locus finding actually is.** An association signal in a genome-wide study is measured at marker variants, and because DNA is inherited in blocks, a signal at one marker implicates a whole neighbourhood of co-inherited variants rather than one culprit. Narrowing that neighbourhood to a credible set of genes is a separate statistical step with its own uncertainty, which is why the study reports credible genes rather than proved disease genes, and why the same analysis names cell types - GABAergic interneurons and medium spiny neurons - by asking where the implicated genes are active rather than by observing those cells directly. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-loci|Genomics of bipolar disorder]] [[P29844615 Schaid 2018 Fine-mapping#^p29844615-problem|Fine-mapping]] [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-cells|Genomics of bipolar disorder]]
>
> **How a polygenic score is built and why it travels badly.** A score aggregates the trait-associated variants a study found into one summary of genetic liability, so it inherits the discovery sample's choices: who was counted as a case, which ancestry contributed, and how much of the architecture that sample could see. A score built in one ancestry mixture or ascertainment scheme can mis-rank risk in another, which is why interpretation requires calibration in a comparable population rather than a raw number. [[F68 NHGRI Polygenic risk scores#^f68-definition|Polygenic risk scores]] [[F68 NHGRI Polygenic risk scores#^f68-limits|Polygenic risk scores]]
>
> **Why genetic sharing is not clinical identity.** Genetic correlation means the variants that raise risk for one condition also shift risk for another more often than chance predicts. That is a statement about overlapping liability measured across samples; it does not say the conditions share symptoms, course or treatment response, and two people with the same score can have entirely different lives. The useful reading is aetiological: shared architecture points research towards common biology, while diagnosis and care remain at the level of the person. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|Genomics of bipolar disorder]] [[Association versus individual prediction]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** This article sits between the genetics domain, which owns the methods, and the bipolar domain, which owns the clinical construct: a genetics claim becomes a bipolar claim only through the diagnostic definitions of the sample, which is why ascertainment differences change the architecture.

## Limitation or common misconception

Risk architecture is not determinism, and genetic findings do not define the diagnosis.
