# Spinal cord

## Definition

The spinal cord is a segmented structure carrying sensory information to the brain and motor commands away from it, and it contains the circuitry for reflexes.

## How it works

**Segments and dermatomes.** Each segment innervates a dermatome, and nerve roots exit in relation to vertebrae differently in the cervical region than lower down, which is why clinical level estimates follow specific rules. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-segments|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]

**Horns.** Sensory afferents enter dorsally and motor neurons leave ventrally, giving the dorsal and ventral horns their functional associations. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-horns|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]

**Tracts.** Ascending and descending tracts occupy characteristic positions, so the level and pattern of a deficit localise the lesion. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]

**Reflexes.** Reflex arcs are the basic units of motor output, with sensory input, integration and a motor response that can occur with minimal cortical involvement. [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-reflex|Motor Responses]]

## Evidence and status

Spinal anatomy is established and clinically validated by examination findings; tract positions are consistent enough to support localisation in most cases.

> [!info]- In depth: segments, horns and the two long systems
> **Segmental organisation.** The spinal cord is organised into segments corresponding to the level at which their roots enter or leave, and into horns: dorsal structures receive sensory input, ventral structures contain motor neurons, and intermediate zones carry interneurons and autonomic preganglionic cells. Segment and horn together locate a function with unusual precision. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-segments|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-horns|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]
>
> **Two long systems.** Ascending tracts carry sensory modalities that are segregated by type and crossing point - the dorsal column system crosses near the brainstem while the spinothalamic system crosses near its entry level - and descending tracts carry motor commands in lateral and medial groups. The crossing points explain the classical dissociation between deficits on one side of the body and the other, which is why the crossing rules are clinically load-bearing. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-descending|Motor Responses]]
>
> **Local circuits do real work.** Spinal circuits generate reflexes, coordinate alternating movements and modulate their own excitability, so the cord is a processing structure rather than a cable of tracts. Motor neuron pools integrate descending command, sensory feedback and intrinsic excitability, which is why the same command produces different movements depending on the state of the limb. [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-reflex|Motor Responses]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-neuron|Nervous Tissue]]
>
> **Clinical mapping.** Because sensory and motor territories are arranged segmentally, the level of a lesion can be estimated from the pattern of preserved and impaired regions - the reasoning that localisation uses, with its known caveats. [[History and localisation]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-limit|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]

> [!info]- Further depth: segments, horns and what a level means
> The cord is organised into segments each innervating a dermatome, and nerve roots exit in relation to the vertebrae differently in the cervical region than lower down, which is why a clinical level and a radiological level can differ by a segment. Cross-sections show sensory afferents entering dorsally and motor neurons leaving ventrally, and ascending and descending tracts occupy characteristic positions - the pattern that lets a clinician distinguish a cord lesion from a root or peripheral lesion. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-segments|Spinal Cord]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-horns|Spinal Cord]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Spinal Cord]]
>
> Some processing happens in the cord and brain stem before information reaches cortex, which is why reflexes are testable and autonomic control has local components, and why a spinal lesion produces a pattern of preserved and lost function that maps onto segmental and tract organisation rather than a single symptom. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-brainstem|Central Processing]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-limit|Spinal Cord]]
>
> **The crossing rules earn their place.** The dorsal-column system crosses near the brainstem while the spinothalamic system crosses within a segment or two of entry, so a hemisection produces loss of fine touch and proprioception below the lesion on the same side and loss of pain and temperature on the opposite side - the classical dissociated pattern. Being able to derive that pattern from the crossing points, rather than memorising it, is what makes the localisation rules usable at unusual levels and incomplete lesions. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]]
>
> **Reflex testing reads the segmental machinery.** Because reflex arcs close within the cord at a known segment, an absent or exaggerated reflex localises to that segment's circuitry or to the descending control over it. The reflex exam is therefore a map of segmental function layered over the tract exam - one reads the long systems, the other reads the local circuits - and the two together are what make spinal-level diagnosis precise. [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-reflex|Motor Responses]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The neuroendocrine domain meets the cord at its autonomic preganglionic cells - sympathetic outflow leaves at thoracic and lumbar levels, parasympathetic at cranial and sacral levels - so autonomic findings in systemic illness carry segmental logic too, not only motor and sensory findings.
