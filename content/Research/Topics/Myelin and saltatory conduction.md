# Myelin and saltatory conduction

## Definition

Myelin is a multi-layered membrane wrap formed around axons by oligodendrocytes in the central nervous system and Schwann cells in the periphery. It segments the axon into insulated internodes separated by bare nodes of Ranvier.

## How it works

**Speed by insulation.** Myelin reduces current leakage across the internodal membrane, so the depolarisation produced by a spike travels further along the inside of the axon before decaying. Voltage-gated channels are concentrated at nodes, so the spike regenerates at successive nodes rather than continuously; this jumping is saltatory conduction. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]

**Geometry.** Conduction velocity rises with axon diameter and depends on internode length and nodal structure, so myelination changes timing relationships between regions rather than simply making everything faster.

**Development.** Myelination continues well beyond infancy and differs by tract, with association tracts maturing later than primary pathways. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]]

## Evidence and status

The biophysics of saltatory conduction are established directly from single-fibre recordings. Developmental timelines in humans come from imaging studies, which measure proxies for myelin rather than myelin itself. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-sequence|Normal development of brain circuits]]

> [!info]- In depth: insulation as a design trade-off
> **What myelin is.** In some axons glial cells form a myelin sheath that insulates the axon and increases conduction speed, interrupted at intervals by the nodes of Ranvier where the axonal membrane is exposed. Central myelin is produced by oligodendrocytes and peripheral myelin by Schwann cells, and the two differ in how many axons each cell serves - a structural difference with consequences for injury and repair. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]
>
> **Why it speeds conduction.** The sheath raises membrane resistance and lowers capacitance along the internode, so the local current produced by an action potential spreads further and faster before it must be regenerated. Regeneration occurs only at the nodes, where sodium channels cluster; the spike therefore appears to jump from node to node, which is the meaning of saltatory conduction. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] [[F03 OpenStax The action potential#^f03-signals|The Action Potential]]
>
> **The trade-offs.** Speed scales with axon diameter, but so does volume, so the alternative to myelin is simply a much thicker axon - which is what invertebrates with giant fibres do. Myelin buys speed in a compact package and reduces the membrane area that must be repolarised per unit length, which is part of why conduction is metabolically cheaper along myelinated fibres. The cost is a system with more components that can fail, and a longer developmental timeline since myelination continues after synapse numbers peak. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]]
>
> **Timing as a functional variable.** Because conduction delay depends on distance and on myelination, myelin sets the relative arrival times of signals converging on a target. Two pathways of different lengths can be tuned to arrive together by differences in myelination, which is why myelin is best understood as shaping temporal structure rather than merely accelerating conduction. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-receptors|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]
>
> **When myelin is attacked.** Multiple sclerosis is described as an autoimmune process in which the immune system attacks central myelin, disrupting signalling; the typical course begins relapsing-remitting and may later become progressive, and diagnosis combines history, examination, imaging and laboratory findings. Because imaging changes resembling MS can appear without the disease, the diagnosis is explicitly a synthesis rather than a scan result. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-mechanism|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-course|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-diagnosis|Multiple Sclerosis]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Conduction velocity and 'connectivity' are different quantities. A myelinated pathway can be fast and sparse, and a slow unmyelinated one can carry more precise timing over short distances; neither is more connected in any general sense. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-limit|Multiple Sclerosis]]

> [!info]- Further depth: insulation, speed and the trade-off
> Glial cells form a myelin sheath that insulates the axon and increases conduction speed, interrupted by the nodes of Ranvier, so the action potential effectively jumps between nodes rather than propagating continuously along the membrane. Speed is bought with space and metabolic cost, and conduction velocity becomes a design parameter that differs between pathways - which is why demyelination slows or blocks conduction without changing the axon's message. Myelination continues past the period when synapse numbers peak, so conduction properties keep changing after the wiring diagram is largely set. ## Uncertainties

- Imaging estimates of myelination are indirect, and different sequences give different answers.
- Remyelination in the human central nervous system occurs but is variable, and why it fails in some lesions remains an open question. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-course|Multiple Sclerosis]]
