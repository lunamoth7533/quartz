# Chemical and electrical synapses

## Definition

Neurons connect in two ways. At chemical synapses the presynaptic cell releases transmitter onto receptors on the postsynaptic cell. At electrical synapses, gap junctions directly couple the cytoplasm of the two cells. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-types|Communication Between Neurons]]

## How it works

**Chemical transmission.** The spike opens calcium channels, vesicles fuse, transmitter crosses the cleft and binds receptors whose identity determines whether the effect is depolarising or hyperpolarising. This introduces a delay of roughly a millisecond and allows amplification, modulation and plasticity at the contact. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-release|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] **Electrical transmission.** Gap junctions allow current to pass directly, producing fast, bidirectional and largely stereotyped coupling. They are much less common than chemical synapses, but they matter for synchronising activity in specific circuits. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-electrical|Cells of the Nervous System]]

## Evidence and status

Both modes are established by direct electrical recording and anatomy. The functional weight of electrical coupling in mammalian cortex is the subject of ongoing work; its clearest roles are in systems where synchrony is required. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-electrical|Cells of the Nervous System]]

> [!info]- In depth: two transmission mechanisms and why the difference matters
> **The chemical synapse as a sequence.** A arriving spike opens voltage-gated calcium channels in the presynaptic terminal; calcium entry triggers fusion of transmitter-filled vesicles with the membrane; transmitter diffuses across the cleft and binds postsynaptic receptors; the postsynaptic effect then depends on receptor identity rather than on the transmitter alone. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-types|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-release|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **The evidence that made the vesicle model standard.** Transmission occurs in quantal units, and release depends jointly on calcium availability and on the state of the release machinery, so the same action potential can release different amounts of transmitter at different moments. Those two observations - quantal amplitude and calcium-dependent probability - are the empirical backbone of the vesicle-release account, and they came largely from classical recordings at the skeletal neuromuscular junction. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-quanta|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-calcium|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-requirements|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]]
>
> **Receptor identity decides sign and speed.** The same glutamate molecule depolarises adult postsynaptic cells through ionotropic receptors, while metabotropic receptors act through second messengers that can modify channels and gene transcription on slower timescales. That is why 'excitatory' and 'inhibitory' are properties of the synapse, not of the molecule. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **The electrical synapse.** Gap-junction channels connect cytoplasm directly and are much faster than chemical transmission, and much less common in the mammalian nervous system. Because the signal is electrical and bidirectional in most gap junctions, electrical synapses synchronise coupled cells and support fast, stereotyped circuits rather than the flexible routing that chemical transmission allows. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-electrical|Cells of the Nervous System]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-types|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **Comparative properties worth keeping straight.** Chemical transmission adds a delay of roughly a millisecond, converts the signal from electrical to chemical and back, and gains amplification, receptor-dependent sign, and rich modulation. Electrical transmission avoids the delay and preserves timing, but offers little gain and, in most cases, little sign control. The two are not alternatives competing for the same job; they are used by circuits selected for speed, synchrony, flexibility or durability respectively. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-types|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-electrical|Cells of the Nervous System]]
>
> **Where the textbook account stops.** The chapters describe the canonical division - chemical with receptor-dependent effects, electrical with gap junctions - from preparations that made each mechanism visible. They do not cover every signalling role: transmitter release can be graded rather than spike-triggered in some cells, and gap junctions can be gated and asymmetric. The division is a starting taxonomy, not a complete inventory. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-limit|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-caution-summation|AI synthesis: Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **Where the millisecond goes.** The chemical synapse's delay is the price of its machinery: calcium entry, vesicle fusion, diffusion across the cleft and receptor binding each take part of the roughly one millisecond between presynaptic spike and postsynaptic effect. The same machinery that costs time buys everything chemical transmission is chosen for - amplification from one vesicle to many receptor openings, receptor-dependent sign, and modulation by anything that changes release or receptor state. Electrical coupling skips those steps, passing current through gap junctions directly, which is why it preserves timing and synchrony at the cost of gain and sign flexibility. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-release|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-electrical|Cells of the Nervous System]]
>
> **Mixed contacts are the honest picture.** The canonical division - chemical here, electrical there - is a teaching simplification; the same pair of neurons can carry both an excitatory contact and a gap junction, so a spike can deliver a fast electrical nudge followed by a slower chemical signal. Their interaction, rather than a partition of labour, is the general case in circuits where both are found. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-types|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-electrical|Cells of the Nervous System]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Pharmacology's target list is mostly a chemical-synapse list - release machinery, transporters, receptors - because electrical junctions offer few molecular handles; the division therefore explains where drug action is possible as well as where signalling is fast. ## Uncertainties

- How much electrical coupling contributes to human cognition is not established; most evidence is from animal preparations and specific cell types.
- The same pair of neurons can be connected chemically and electrically, so the two modes interact rather than partition the system.
