# Stress response and the HPA axis

**Definition.** Acute stress mobilises the fast sympathetic branch and the slower HPA axis, which ends in cortisol release. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-axis|What Is Stress?]]

## Supported claims

- The stress response begins with appraisal: the same demand can be experienced as threat or challenge. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-appraisal|What Is Stress?]]
- Cortisol mobilises energy and modulates multiple systems rather than acting as a single stress molecule. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-axis|What Is Stress?]]
- Acute stress responses are adaptive, while chronic activation is associated with wear on regulation, mood and health. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-chronic|What Is Stress?]]

> [!info]- In depth: the axis, its mediators and its feedback
> **The loop.** Cortisol secretion follows ACTH from the pituitary, which follows hypothalamic CRH, and corticosteroid levels feed back on the hypothalamus and pituitary to limit further release - which is why the axis is described as a loop rather than a chain. The adrenal cortex is organised into zones with different outputs, with glucocorticoids such as cortisol from the fasciculata and the medulla releasing adrenaline and noradrenaline as part of the sympathetic response. [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-drive|The Adrenal Glands]] [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-cortex|The Adrenal Glands]] [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-medulla|The Adrenal Glands]]
>
> **Fast and slow branches work together.** The sympathetic response is fast and the HPA axis is slower, ending in cortisol release that mobilises energy and modulates many systems; acute stress responses are adaptive and chronic activation is associated with wear on regulation, mood and health. Appraisal shapes the response - the same demand can be experienced as threat or challenge - so physiology and interpretation are part of one system. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-axis|What is stress?]] [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-chronic|What is stress?]] [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-appraisal|What is stress?]]
>
> **What glucocorticoids do is context-dependent.** A long-standing dispute concerns whether glucocorticoids enable, stimulate or suppress the stress response, and one influential classification proposes permissive, suppressive, stimulatory and preparative actions depending on context and timing - so no single summary such as 'stress hormone' captures the role. [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-contested|Glucocorticoids and stress]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|Glucocorticoids and stress]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-timing|Glucocorticoids and stress]]
>
> **Measurement is not the mechanism.** Cortisol measures depend on time of day, pulsatility, binding proteins and the compartment sampled, and the allostasis framework describes cumulative cost rather than a single value. This is why the HPA-axis material in this library treats measures as constructs with confounds rather than as readouts of stress. [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-allostasis|Stress mediators]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-load|Stress mediators]] [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-limit|The Adrenal Glands]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-limit|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]]

> [!info]- Reference: the axis, its mediators and its feedback
> **Anatomy of the axis.** Corticotropin-releasing hormone from the hypothalamus drives adrenocorticotropic hormone from the anterior pituitary, which drives cortisol release from the adrenal cortex; cortisol then feeds back on hypothalamus and pituitary. [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-drive|The Adrenal Glands]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-feedback|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]]
>
> **Two speeds.** The sympathetic response is fast and neural; the HPA axis is slower and hormonal, which is why the two are described as complementary rather than redundant. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-axis|What is Stress]] The adrenal medulla links them by releasing catecholamines as part of sympathetic activation. [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-medulla|The Adrenal Glands]]
>
> **What glucocorticoids do is contested.** One tradition treats glucocorticoids as enabling or stimulating the stress response; another treats them as suppressing it to prevent overactivation. The review proposes classifying their actions as permissive, suppressive, stimulatory or preparative depending on context and timing. [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-contested|Glucocorticoids and stress]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|Glucocorticoids and stress]] That classification is a proposal, not a settled taxonomy. [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-timing|Glucocorticoids and stress]]
>
> **From acute to chronic.** Acute responses are adaptive; chronic activation is associated with wear on regulation and health, which is the everyday version of the allostatic-load argument. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-chronic|What is Stress]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-load|Stress mediators]]
>
> **Measurement caveat.** Cortisol is pulsatile and affected by time of day, sleep, meals and recent stressors, so single measurements are weak indices of chronic axis activity.
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The HPA axis is the physiological route the neuroendocrine-neuroimmune domain uses to connect stress to immune and metabolic signalling, so a stress claim in any clinical-psychiatry or neurodevelopmental article has to state which axis is being measured and how. ## Limitation or common misconception

Cortisol is not a synonym for stress: it follows a daily rhythm and responds to many inputs.
