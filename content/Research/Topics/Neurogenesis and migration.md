# Neurogenesis and migration

## Definition

Neurogenesis is the production of new neurons from progenitor cells; migration is their movement from birthplace to final position. Both are early developmental processes that shape the adult architecture of the brain.

## How it works

**Sequence.** Development runs through proliferation, migration and differentiation, with these processes overlapping across regions rather than completing in sequence. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]]

**Neural tube.** The neural plate folds into the neural tube, which becomes the central nervous system, with the ventricular system deriving from its hollow centre. [[F29 OpenStax Embryologic perspective#^f29-tube|The Embryologic Perspective]] Regional identity is established through primary and secondary vesicles. [[F29 OpenStax Embryologic perspective#^f29-vesicles|The Embryologic Perspective]]

**Adult neurogenesis.** Where adult neurogenesis persists it is regionally restricted, and evidence in humans is methodologically difficult; claims about its role in mood or memory should be read as hypotheses with contested measurement. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-limit|The basics of brain development]]

## Evidence and status

Embryonic development is established in animals and human tissue; adult human neurogenesis remains an area of active methodological dispute.

> [!info]- In depth: building a brain in overlapping stages
> **The sequence, with overlap.** Brain development is described as overlapping processes - neurogenesis, migration, differentiation, synaptogenesis, pruning and myelination - that overlap in time across regions rather than completing in sequence, which is why structure-function relations shift with age. A claim that assigns one process to one age is therefore an approximation. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]]
>
> **The ordered version.** The circuit-development review describes proliferation, migration, differentiation, synapse formation and experience-dependent refinement as an ordered sequence, which is the classical account the overlap description qualifies. Both are useful: the sequence names the processes, and the overlap describes when they happen. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-sequence|Normal development of brain circuits]]
>
> **Migration and specification.** Migrating cells acquire regional identity under the influence of signalling gradients, and their eventual phenotype depends on both intrinsic programme and local signals, which is the mechanistic basis for describing development as guided rather than determined. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]] [[F29 OpenStax Embryologic perspective#^f29-limit|Embryologic Perspective]]
>
> **Activity refines what is built.** Overproduced synapses and connections are subsequently eliminated, so normal development involves loss as well as growth, and in visual cortex the onset of activity-dependent refinement is triggered by maturation of local inhibitory connections. Building and pruning are one process. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-pruning|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Name the process, the region and the species, because timing data from one system do not transfer to another and developmental windows differ across functions. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-limit|The basics of brain development]]
>
> **Where cortical neurons come from, and why order matters.** Cortical projection neurons are generated in the ventricular and subventricular zones and migrate outward along radial glia, so the deepest cortical layers are populated by the earliest-generated cells and superficial layers by later ones - the inside-out pattern. The pattern is why a disruption timed early versus late produces structurally different outcomes, and why migration is described as bearing the burden of building the cortex's columnar order. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]] [[Cortical layers and columns]]
>
> **Regional identity is assigned before arrival.** The neural tube's vesicle stage establishes broad territories, and signalling gradients within them assign finer identity; migrating cells both carry intrinsic programmes and respond to local signals, so their eventual phenotype is negotiated rather than pre-specified. That is the mechanistic reading of "guided, not determined", and it is why the same developmental programme produces strikingly different regional structures. [[F29 OpenStax Embryologic perspective#^f29-vesicles|The Embryologic Perspective]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The developmental-timing literature in the neurology domain consumes this sequence directly: perinatal injuries are interpreted through what is migrating and differentiating at the time of injury, which is why the same injury at different gestational ages produces different patterns. [[Cerebrovascular disease]] [[Neurodegeneration and cognitive syndromes]]
>
> **Progenitors are the population that proliferation acts on.** Early in development, ventricular-zone progenitors expand their own pool through symmetric division, then switch to asymmetric divisions that produce neurons while maintaining the progenitor layer; the switch timing is part of what sets how many neurons a region ends up with. This is the second reason the overlap account matters: the proliferative phase does not merely happen before migration, it coexists with early migration and its duration is itself regulated. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]] [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-sequence|Normal development of brain circuits]]
