# Therapy models overview

## Definition

Psychotherapy models differ in what they treat as the mechanism of change: behaviour and its consequences, cognitions, relationships and attachment, or narrative and meaning.

## How it works

**Behavioural and cognitive.** Behavioural approaches apply conditioning principles, including exposure; cognitive approaches target appraisals and beliefs. The two are frequently combined. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-psychotherapy|Types of Treatment]] [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-behavioural|Types of Treatment]]

**Psychodynamic and humanistic.** Psychodynamic approaches work with recurring relational patterns and unconscious processes, while humanistic approaches emphasise the therapeutic relationship and self-actualisation; both have been studied less extensively in randomised trials than CBT.

**Combination.** Medication and psychotherapy are often combined, and the section notes that combined treatment is common rather than exceptional. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-biomedical|Types of Treatment]]

**Evidence design.** Trials compare against waitlist, treatment-as-usual or another active therapy; the comparator determines how large an effect looks, so 'works' is always relative to a specified alternative.

## Evidence and status

Evidence is uneven across orientations and conditions, with the strongest trial base for cognitive-behavioural and behavioural methods in specific conditions. Comparator choice changes the apparent size of an effect, and certainty can stay low even where a difference is statistically significant. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]]

> [!info]- In depth: mechanisms claimed, comparators used, and what the trials support
> **Each model claims a mechanism.** Behavioural approaches apply conditioning and exposure; cognitive approaches target appraisals and beliefs; the two are usually combined. Psychodynamic approaches work with recurring relational and unconscious patterns; humanistic approaches emphasise the therapeutic relationship and self-actualisation; systemic approaches work with relationships rather than individuals. Naming the mechanism is what makes a model testable, because it specifies what should change first. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-psychotherapy|Types of Treatment]] [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-behavioural|Types of Treatment]]
>
> **Comparators decide how effects look.** Trials compare against waiting list, treatment as usual or another active therapy, and the choice determines the apparent size of an effect. In the adult-ADHD trials reviewed by Cochrane, gains over waiting list were large while the comparison against supportive psychotherapy was not statistically significant, and certainty was mostly low or very low. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]
>
> **Non-specific factors are part of the mechanism problem.** Alliance, expectancy and allegiance contribute to outcomes in all orientations, which is why attributing change to a specific technique requires designs that hold those factors constant; most trials do not. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI synthesis: CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]
>
> **Combination is the norm.** Medication and psychotherapy are frequently combined in practice, and the section used here notes that combination rather than exclusivity is typical; combination trials answer a different question from monotherapy trials. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-biomedical|Types of Treatment]] [[Trial endpoints, benefit and harms]]
>
> **Reading rule.** A psychotherapy claim needs the comparator, the outcome and the rater, plus the horizon; without them 'therapy works' and 'therapy does not work' can both be supported by the same trial. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

> [!info]- Further depth: what the orientations target and how effects are established
> Psychotherapy approaches are grouped by orientation - psychodynamic, behavioural, cognitive and humanistic - and the textbook distinguishes what each targets, with behavioural therapies applying conditioning principles directly and biomedical treatments described in parallel, noting that psychotherapy and medication are frequently combined rather than chosen exclusively. The comparison in this library's strongest synthesis is instructive about method: adding manualised psychotherapy to medication was associated with lower recurrence in bipolar disorder and the component analysis linked psychoeducation with supervised skills practice to fewer recurrences, while component analyses estimate average contributions across heterogeneous protocols. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-psychotherapy|Types of Treatment]] [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-biomedical|Types of Treatment]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-caution-ingredients|AI appraisal: Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Dismantling and additive designs are how mechanisms get tested.** Comparing a full package against a version with one component removed tests whether that component carries unique effect; most therapy trials do not do this, so component claims in therapy - like exposure being the active ingredient of CBT for a given condition - rest on a thinner design base than the package-level evidence. The honest statement is usually that a package works and that a mechanism is plausible and partially tested. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]] [[Trial endpoints, benefit and harms]]
>
> **Equivalence findings deserve the same scrutiny as superiority findings.** When two active therapies show no significant difference, the interpretation depends on power, horizon and outcome - the same absence-of-evidence discipline the pharmacology articles apply. Non-inferiority is a design claim that must be designed for, not a conclusion available by default from a null contrast. [[Effect sizes and uncertainty]] [[Short-term versus long-term outcomes]]
>

## Uncertainties

- Common factors such as alliance and expectancy contribute to outcomes in all orientations, which complicates attributing effects to specific techniques.
