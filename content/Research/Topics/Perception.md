# Perception

## Definition

Sensation is detection of a stimulus by a sensory receptor; perception is the organisation and interpretation of that information into a coherent scene. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-sensation|Sensation versus Perception]]

## How it works

**Thresholds.** Absolute thresholds describe the minimum stimulus that can be detected, and signal detection methods separate sensory sensitivity from the decision criterion a person uses. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-absolute|Sensation versus Perception]]

**Construction.** Illusions demonstrate that the same input supports different interpretations depending on context and expectation, so perception is inference rather than recording. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-illusions|Sensation versus Perception]]

**Cortical processing.** Visual cortex is the best-mapped example: thalamic relay to striate cortex, with orientation, direction and motion properties emerging in successive areas. [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-pathway|Visual Processing: Cortical Pathways]] [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-properties|Visual Processing: Cortical Pathways]]

**Somatosensory detail.** Discriminative touch and proprioception run in a separate channel from pain and temperature, which is why they can dissociate clinically. [[F103 Neuroscience Online somatosensory systems#^f103-division|Somatosensory systems]]

## Evidence and status

Psychophysics is among the most quantitatively precise areas of psychology; the mapping from perceptual performance to neural mechanism still requires models. [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-common|Predictive coding algorithms]]

> [!info]- In depth: from sensation to an interpreted scene
> **The distinction.** Sensation is the detection of a stimulus by a sensory receptor, while perception is the organisation and interpretation of that information; detection thresholds describe the minimum stimulus that can be noticed with signal detection theory separating sensitivity from response bias. The distinction matters because a perceptual report combines both stages. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-sensation|Sensation versus Perception]] [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-absolute|Sensation versus Perception]]
>
> **Perception is constructive.** Illusions are used to show that perception is constructive: the same sensory input supports different interpreted scenes depending on context and expectation. That is the empirical basis for treating perception as inference rather than as a passive readout. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-illusions|Sensation versus Perception]]
>
> **The cortical stages.** Visual signals reach cortex through the lateral geniculate nucleus and arrive first in striate cortex, and cortical neurons are described by properties such as orientation, direction and movement sensitivity - the hierarchical account of how features are extracted. Somatosensory processing divides into a discriminative-touch and proprioceptive channel and a separate pathway, showing that the principle generalises across modalities. [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-pathway|Visual Cortex]] [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-properties|Visual Cortex]] [[F103 Neuroscience Online somatosensory systems#^f103-division|Somatosensory Systems]]
>
> **The inferential version of the story.** Predictive-coding algorithms all fit a generative model to sensory data, sharing the idea that perception involves inference about hidden causes; they differ substantially in their generative models and optimisation methods, so evidence for one is not evidence for another. [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-common|Predictive coding algorithms]] [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-difference|Predictive coding algorithms]]
>
> **Effect sizes put perception findings in perspective.** With large samples a significant difference can accompany a trivial one, and effect-size conventions are heuristics that ignore measurement accuracy and population diversity - so a perceptual threshold difference should be translated into the outcome's own units before it is interpreted. [[P23997866 Sullivan 2012 Using effect size#^p23997866-significance|Using effect size]] [[P23997866 Sullivan 2012 Using effect size#^p23997866-magnitude|Using effect size]]
>
> **Why the constructive account is not a metaphor.** The same retinal input supports different scenes under different contexts, and illusions are stable, repeatable instances of that fact. The constructive claim is that perception combines input with assumptions the system carries - about lighting, distance, object constancy - and that the assumptions are normally so well matched to the environment that their contribution only shows when the environment is arranged to violate them. Signal detection adds the second half: even at the sensory edge, a response reflects both the observer's sensitivity and their willingness to call an ambiguous event a signal, so thresholds are properties of a decision process, not of an eye alone. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception]]
>
> **What bottom-up and top-down actually divide.** Bottom-up descriptions cover the chain from receptor to cortex; top-down descriptions cover the effects of expectation, context and attention on what is perceived. The division is analytical rather than anatomical - real perception runs both at once - but it earns its keep in interpretation: an effect of expectation on perception is a top-down claim that needs its own evidence, and cannot be read off a sensory pathway diagram. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Computational neuroscience's predictive-processing account is the formal version of the constructive claim - perception as inference combining prior and input - and the Bayesian article develops what that would require and what would count as testing it. [[Bayesian inference and predictive processing]] [[Attention]]

## Connections

Perception is the input side of [[Attention]], and it is where the computational accounts in [[Bayesian inference and predictive processing]] are usually tested.

## Uncertainties

- Whether perception should be described as inference is a modelling claim; the behavioural regularities themselves are better established.
