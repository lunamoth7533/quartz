# Short-term versus long-term outcomes

**Definition.** Trial evidence for a treatment usually covers weeks, while long-term questions about function, stability and safety rest on weaker designs. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]

## Supported claims

- An ADHD network meta-analysis found insufficient data at 26 and 52 weeks and called for research on long-term effects. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]
- A 2025 adult ADHD review keeps long-term treatment effects among its open questions. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults review]]
- A 2025 adult intervention synthesis reports that medications were not efficacious on additional outcomes such as quality of life. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Extending short-term results to years is an assumption, and observational long-term data brings confounding with it.
