# Short-term versus long-term outcomes

**Definition.** Trial evidence for a treatment usually covers weeks, while long-term questions about function, stability and safety rest on weaker designs. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]

## Supported claims

- An ADHD network meta-analysis found insufficient data at 26 and 52 weeks and called for research on long-term effects. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]
- A 2025 adult ADHD review keeps long-term treatment effects among its open questions. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults review]]
- A 2025 adult intervention synthesis reports that medications were not efficacious on additional outcomes such as quality of life. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]

In depth: why the time horizon changes the claim

**The short-term base is real but narrow.** Randomised evidence for symptom reduction over weeks is well established across several conditions in this library, and the trials were powered for exactly that question. The same reviews state their limits without prompting: insufficient data at 26 and 52 weeks for ADHD medications, with the authors calling for urgent research on long-term effects. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-limit|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

**What longer horizons change.** A different timescale changes the outcome that matters - function, participation, quality of life, educational and occupational trajectories - and those outcomes are not measured by symptom scales. The ADHD intervention synthesis reports that medications were not efficacious on additional outcomes such as quality of life even at 12 weeks, which is an outcome question rather than a duration question. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-domains|International Classification of Functioning]]

**Open questions are themselves evidence.** The adult ADHD evidence review lists long-term treatment effects among its open questions alongside late-onset ADHD, emotional dysregulation and the definition of functional impairment, which is the honest form of a statement that a literature cannot yet answer a question. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults: evidence base]]

**Absence of evidence is not equivalence.** When longer-term comparisons are missing, the correct reading is that no average benefit has been demonstrated at that horizon; it is not evidence that treatment stops working, and the same logic applies in the other direction. The distinction is what keeps a reference account from converting a gap into a verdict. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-caution-absence|AI appraisal: Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

**Reading rule.** For any treatment claim, name the outcome, the horizon and the population, then check whether the study measured all three. Time horizon is one of the most common places where a correct short-term result becomes an incorrect long-term claim. [[F22 Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions#^f22-effects|Cochrane Handbook]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Extending short-term results to years is an assumption, and observational long-term data brings confounding with it.

> **What stays open.** The 2025 adult review keeps long-term treatment effects and non-pharmacological interventions among its explicitly open questions. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults evidence base]]
>
> **Additional outcomes.** The 2025 adult synthesis reports that medications were not efficacious on additional outcomes such as quality of life, which shows that horizon and outcome are separate questions. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]
>
> **Certainty tooling.** Network meta-analysis borrows strength through shared comparators and depends on the transitivity assumption, while GRADE formalises how much confidence a body of evidence warrants - both are tools for stating what a short-horizon result does not cover. [[P27913917 Network meta-analysis introduction#^p27913917-assumption|Network meta-analysis: an introduction for clinicians]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]]
>
> **Why long trials are hard.** Extending short-term results to years is an assumption, and observational long-term data carries the confounding that comes with it; see [[Observational designs]] and [[Causality and counterfactuals]].
>
> **Why the horizon narrows the outcome, not just the duration.** Short trials measure what changes in weeks: symptom scales under specific raters. Function, participation, education, employment and quality of life move over months to years and are measured differently, so the long-term question is not "does the effect last" but "does the thing being measured still matter at that timescale". The 2025 adult synthesis finding that medications were not efficacious on quality-of-life outcomes is exactly this gap made visible, not a contradiction of the symptom findings. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-acceptability|Comparative interventions for ADHD]] [[Functional outcomes and measurement]]
>
> **What the open-questions lists are telling you.** When a 2025 evidence review lists long-term treatment effects among its open questions alongside late-onset ADHD and emotional dysregulation, it is marking where the field's own confidence ends. Those lists are produced by authors who read the whole literature; treating them as homework for the reader rather than as evidence about where certainty stops is the misreading they exist to prevent. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults review]]
