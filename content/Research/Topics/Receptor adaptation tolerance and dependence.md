# Receptor adaptation, tolerance and dependence

## Definition

Tolerance is reduced effect after repeated exposure; dependence is a state in which cessation produces withdrawal; withdrawal is the syndrome following reduction or discontinuation. They overlap but are distinct.

## How it works

**Partial tolerance.** Tolerance develops for some effects of a drug and not others, which is why a person can tolerate one action while another intensifies. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-tolerance|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]]

**Receptor level.** Desensitisation, phosphorylation, uncoupling, internalisation and post-endocytic trafficking are among the mechanisms studied in opioid pharmacology. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-desensitization|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]] [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-mechanism|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]]

**Clinical example.** Benzodiazepines show tolerance, dependence and withdrawal together with adverse effects such as sedation and falls, which is why duration of use is a clinical decision. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-risks|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]]

**Adaptation is not always harmful.** Some adaptive changes are the mechanism of therapeutic effect over weeks, which is why 'adaptation' and 'tolerance' should not be equated. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-plasticity|How do antidepressants work?]]

## Evidence and status

Receptor-level adaptation is well characterised in cell and animal systems; predicting an individual's tolerance or withdrawal from mechanism alone is not reliable.

> [!info]- In depth: what repeated exposure changes, at the receptor and at the person
> **Tolerance is a reduced effect after repeated exposure.** It develops for some effects of a drug and not others, which is why the pattern matters: a person can become tolerant to one action of an opioid while remaining sensitive to another, and the same literature examines receptor-level processes, especially desensitisation, that may contribute. Those processes include phosphorylation, uncoupling, internalisation and post-endocytic trafficking, and they differ in how reversible they are. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-tolerance|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]] [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-desensitization|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]] [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-mechanism|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]]
>
> **Dependence and withdrawal are distinct from tolerance.** Benzodiazepine pharmacology describes tolerance, dependence and withdrawal together with adverse effects such as sedation and falls in older patients - a cluster that reflects both receptor adaptation and the clinical context of long-term use. Dependence is a physiological state; withdrawal is the syndrome produced when the drug is reduced; neither is a synonym for addiction. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-risks|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]]
>
> **Adaptation happens at every level of the synapse.** Receptor number and composition can change over minutes to hours, and changes in gene expression and structure persist for days or longer; the plasticity literature organises these timescales separately because they have different mechanisms and different evidence bases. A tolerance claim that does not name the timescale is untestable. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-lasting|Synaptic Plasticity]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-mechanism|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **Withdrawal can be a causal factor in other clinical problems.** The opioid and benzodiazepine reviews both treat discontinuation as an effect in its own right rather than as a failure of willpower, which is why abrupt cessation is a clinical question rather than a personal one. The dependence literature also supplies the terminology that separates physiological adaptation from behavioural patterns of use. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-tolerance|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]] [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-risks|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Name the effect that is tolerant, the timescale, and the population in which the finding was obtained. This library contains no tapering or substitution guidance, and the existence of a receptor mechanism does not license individual predictions. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-mechanism|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]]
>
> **The three terms answer different questions.** Tolerance answers "does the same exposure still produce the same effect"; dependence answers "does the system now require the drug to function normally"; withdrawal answers "what happens when exposure stops". They travel together often enough to be conflated, but they come apart: tolerance to sedation can coexist with unchanged tolerance to other effects, dependence can develop without misuse, and withdrawal syndromes differ in duration and severity across drug classes. Precision here is safety-relevant, not pedantic. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-tolerance|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]]
>
> **The receptor-level mechanisms are a menu, not one switch.** Desensitisation, phosphorylation, uncoupling from second messengers, internalisation and altered post-endocytic trafficking each change signalling differently and on different timescales, so "the receptor adapted" is a family of claims. Which mechanism dominates varies by receptor and tissue, which is one reason tolerance develops for some effects of a drug and not others. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-mechanism|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]]
>
> **Why antidepressant adaptation complicates the picture.** The same word covers the receptor and transporter changes that develop over weeks of antidepressant use - changes that are the leading candidates for the therapeutically relevant effect, not an adverse outcome. Adaptation is therefore neither good nor bad by definition; whether it helps or costs depends on which system adapts, to what, and against what baseline. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|How do antidepressants work?]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The benzodiazepine case is where pharmacology, clinical psychiatry and neurochemistry meet on this topic: tolerance, dependence and withdrawal are documented together with sedation and falls, and duration-of-use questions follow from the biology rather than from policy preferences. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology]] [[Drug classes and mechanisms overview]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Receptor adaptation in the pharmacology domain supplies the molecular substrate that the clinical-psychiatry domain uses when it discusses discontinuation syndromes and treatment-emergent effects, and that the neurochemistry domain uses when it describes homeostatic compensation. The inference chain from receptor-level adaptation to clinical discontinuation runs through at least two domains, and the methods domain's causal-inference rules govern whether the chain can be read in the stated direction.
