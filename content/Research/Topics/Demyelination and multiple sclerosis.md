# Demyelination and multiple sclerosis

## Definition

Multiple sclerosis is an immune-mediated condition in which central myelin is attacked, disrupting conduction. It is the commonest demyelinating disease of the central nervous system in young adults. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-mechanism|Multiple Sclerosis]]

## How it works

**Conduction.** Myelin restricts current to nodes of Ranvier, so demyelination slows or blocks conduction while leaving the axon intact. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] Symptoms therefore depend on which tracts are affected and vary over time.

**Course.** Relapsing-remitting disease is the most common initial course, with progressive courses distinguished by accumulating disability between relapses. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-course|Multiple Sclerosis]]

**Diagnosis.** Diagnosis combines history, examination, imaging and laboratory findings, and imaging changes resembling MS can occur without the disease, which is why criteria require dissemination in time and space rather than a single scan. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-diagnosis|Multiple Sclerosis]]

**Management.** Care includes relapse treatment, disease-modifying therapy and symptom management across bladder, bowel, fatigue, mobility and cognitive domains. [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-care|Multiple Sclerosis NG220]] [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-symptoms|Multiple Sclerosis NG220]]

## Evidence and status

Disease-modifying treatments have randomised evidence for relapse reduction; long-term disability outcomes are harder to establish and are studied with observational designs. [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-context|Multiple Sclerosis NG220]]

> [!info]- In depth: a myelin target, two courses, and a diagnosis built from several findings
> **The mechanism.** Multiple sclerosis is described as an autoimmune process in which the immune system attacks myelin in the central nervous system, disrupting signalling. The functional consequence follows from myelin's role: in some axons glial cells form a myelin sheath that insulates the axon and increases conduction speed, interrupted by the nodes of Ranvier, so demyelination slows or blocks conduction along the same pathway rather than changing the tissue's output. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-mechanism|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]
>
> **Two courses, and what they change.** The overview distinguishes relapsing-remitting from progressive courses and notes that most people start with relapsing-remitting disease. Course matters for the evidence base as well as for prognosis: relapses are countable events that trials can measure, while progression is a trajectory that requires different endpoints. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-course|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-limit|Multiple Sclerosis]]
>
> **Diagnosis is a composite, and the imaging is not specific.** Diagnosis combines history, examination, imaging and laboratory findings, and imaging changes resembling MS can appear without the disease. That is the localisation logic again: no single finding establishes the diagnosis, which is why the combination and its timing are specified rather than any one test. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-diagnosis|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-purpose|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]
>
> **Care is broader than relapse treatment.** National guidance covers coordination of care, information and support alongside treatment of relapses and disease-modifying therapy, and it addresses symptom management - bladder, bowel, fatigue, mobility and cognitive concerns - as part of standard care rather than as afterthoughts. As with other guidance products, recommendations are tied to an evidence review and to the funded service context. [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-care|Multiple sclerosis NG220]] [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-symptoms|Multiple sclerosis NG220]] [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-context|Multiple sclerosis NG220]]
>
> **Where the barrier literature connects.** The blood-brain barrier is an active research area rather than a settled mechanism, and one review is organised around unanswered questions including how the barrier changes across regions, ages and disease states. Claims that a treatment acts by "opening" or "protecting" the barrier should therefore be read as research questions. [[P32211826 Profaci 2020 Blood-brain barrier#^p32211826-open|The blood-brain barrier]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-limit|Blood-brain barrier]]
>
> **Dissemination in time and space is the criteria logic.** Because scans can show changes resembling multiple sclerosis without the disease, diagnosis requires lesions disseminated in space (multiple CNS locations) and time (separated in date), combined with clinical picture and laboratory support - the criteria structure exists to prevent a single scan from carrying the diagnosis. That logic is a clinical instance of the methods domain's specificity-over-sensitivity discipline for serious diagnoses. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-diagnosis|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-care|Multiple Sclerosis NG220]]
>
> **Symptom management is a large share of care.** The guideline's management spans relapse treatment, disease-modifying therapy and symptom work across fatigue, bladder, bowel, mobility and cognition; fatigue in particular is common, disabling and only partly understood mechanistically, which makes it a standing reminder that a disease's dominant daily burden may not be its primary lesion.
