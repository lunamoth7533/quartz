# Double empathy

## Definition

The double empathy account proposes that difficulties in understanding arise between people with different social expectations and styles, rather than residing in one group. It predicts better mutual understanding within, than across, groups.

## How it works

**Prediction.** If misunderstanding is relational, information transfer should be more efficient within a group than across groups in both directions.

**Supporting observation.** Autistic adults report managing self-presentation and compensating for expectations placed on them, with costs in effort and self-concept - consistent with a relational rather than individual locus. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-strategies|Social camouflaging]] [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-consequence|Social camouflaging]]

**Research-design implication.** Studies that measure autistic people against non-autistic norms will find differences by construction, so designs should include both directions. [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-outcomes|Autistic adults' views on early interventions]]

**Language.** The account is a reason to describe interaction differences rather than to attribute a social deficit to one party.

## Evidence and status

The account is supported by information-transfer and rapport studies and by qualitative evidence; it is a reframing with growing empirical support rather than a complete theory of social cognition.

> [!info]- In depth: a relational hypothesis and the evidence that bears on it
> **The prediction.** If misunderstanding arises between people with different social expectations and styles rather than residing in one group, then information transfer should be more efficient within a group than across groups, and it should be inefficient in both directions rather than only one. That prediction is what makes the account testable rather than merely polite: it can fail if cross-group understanding turns out to be asymmetric. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-strategies|Social camouflaging]]
>
> **The supporting observations available here.** Autistic adults report managing self-presentation and compensating for expectations placed on them, with costs in effort and self-concept - a pattern consistent with a relational rather than individual locus. In reviewing early interventions, autistic adults emphasised respecting authentic autistic communication, changing environments, reducing demands and supporting the wider community, and they described negative consequences from being taught to hide autistic characteristics. These are reported views and experiences rather than measurements of mutual understanding, so they motivate the account without settling it. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-consequence|Social camouflaging]] [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-support|Autistic adults' views on early interventions]] [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-masking|Autistic adults' views on early interventions]]
>
> **The design implication is the sharpest part of the account.** Studies that measure autistic people against non-autistic norms will find differences by construction, so designs should include both directions of the comparison. The same logic applies to instruments: comparing scores across groups assumes measurement invariance, and where that has not been tested the comparison inherits an unknown amount of measurement artefact. [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-definition|Measurement invariance]] [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-practice|Measurement invariance]]
>
> **Co-occurrence and single-condition framings.** Autism and ADHD frequently co-occur, sharing some features while remaining distinct categories; the earlier diagnostic system excluded a dual diagnosis and the revision allows comorbid ADHD with autism. Co-occurring presentations show more severe social and adaptive difficulties and may fit single-condition treatments less well, which is another reason descriptions should be relational and context-aware rather than deficit-centred. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-definition|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]] [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-history|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]] [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-clinical|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]
>
> **Limits.** Most of the evidence concerns autistic adults in Western settings, and bidirectional effects in other cultural and linguistic contexts are less studied. The review of adult views is also retrospective and methodologically varied, so recall and selection shape what was reported. The account is best described as a well-motivated reframing with growing support, not as a complete theory of social cognition. [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-limit|Autistic adults' views on early interventions]] [[P41993846 Fernandez 2026 Quality-of-life assessment in autistic adults#^p41993846-context|Quality-of-life assessment in autistic adults]]
>
> **Therapy and education follow from the framing.** If misunderstanding is bidirectional, the remedial responsibility does not sit with one party: what changes is the communication environment, the expectations placed on the autistic participant, and the training offered to the non-autistic side. That is why co-located intervention research in this area increasingly frames outcomes around joint or environmental adjustments rather than around teaching autistic people to mask more effectively - and why the camouflaging literature's reported costs in effort, fatigue and self-concept are direct evidence about what the deficit-framed approach was already doing to participants. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-strategies|Social camouflaging]] [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-consequence|Social camouflaging]]
>
> **Not a substitute for a theory of mechanism.** A relational account tells you where to look for the difficulty and how to describe it; it does not by itself specify the cognitive or neural differences that produce it. The two framings can coexist - a mechanism-level account explains what differs, a relational account explains how it plays out between people - and conflating them into one claim ("autistic people cannot understand others") misstates both.
