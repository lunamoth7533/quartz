# Bipolar I episodes and course

**Definition.** Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania, with depressive episodes common across the lifetime course. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar Disorder]]

## Supported claims

- The diagnosis is defined by mania; a depressive episode is common but not required. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar Disorder]]
- Clinical care considers acute symptoms and long-term stability together. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar Disorder]]
- Guideline recommendations are organised by illness phase. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

> [!info]- In depth: episodes, the lifetime frame and what assessment has to cover
> **The defining episode.** Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania; depressive episodes are common but not required for the diagnosis, and the clinical picture combines episodes with a lifetime course so that treatment planning considers acute symptoms and long-term stability together. That structure is why an assessment cannot be an episode inventory alone. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]] [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Episode-level description is standardised.** ICD-11 gives separate descriptions for depressive, manic, mixed and hypomanic episodes before the disorder categories, with bipolar type I disorder numbered 6A60 and each entry stating essential features, boundaries and course qualifiers. The separation of episode from disorder means a claim about one does not automatically describe the other. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-use|ICD-11 CDDR]]
>
> **Assessment covers risk and co-occurring conditions.** National guidance sets out assessment expectations for people with suspected or diagnosed bipolar disorder, including risk assessment and attention to coexisting conditions, and recommends long-term pharmacological management with structured psychological approaches, with explicit cautions for particular medicines in pregnancy and for younger people. The assessment is a structured process rather than a checklist for the presenting episode. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-assessment|Bipolar disorder CG185]] [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-longterm|Bipolar disorder CG185]]
>
> **Course and treatment evidence are phase-specific.** Because the guideline evidence is organised by phase, a statement about treatment applies to the phase in which it was tested; the CANMAT and ISBD synthesis translates evidence into phase-specific lines of recommendation and carries no individual dosing guidance. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]] [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-caution-individual|AI appraisal: CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Name the episode, the phase, the course pattern and the source of the diagnostic criteria. Longitudinal course is the part of this picture that a single assessment cannot supply, and retrospective recall is an imperfect substitute. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-limit|Bipolar disorder CG185]] [[P18509902 Shiffman 2008 Ecological momentary assessment#^p18509902-rationale|Ecological momentary assessment]]

> [!info]- Reference: episodes, course and the longitudinal frame
> **Definition by mania.** Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania; depressive episodes are common but not required for the diagnosis. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Episode versus course.** The clinical picture combines episodes with a lifetime course, so acute treatment and long-term management answer different questions. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Episode descriptions in ICD-11.** The manual gives separate descriptions for depressive, manic, mixed and hypomanic episodes before the disorder categories, which is why episode-level description is separable from diagnosis. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]]
>
> **Guideline framing.** UK guidance sets expectations for assessment, including risk and coexisting conditions, and recommends long-term management with both pharmacological and structured psychological components. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-assessment|Bipolar disorder CG185]] [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-longterm|Bipolar disorder CG185]]
>
> **Uncertainty.** How to define recovery, and how much subthreshold symptoms matter for outcome, remain debated, which is why course descriptions rely on longitudinal cohorts rather than single assessments.

## Limitation or common misconception

Descriptions of course are population averages; individual trajectories, triggers and comorbidities vary.
