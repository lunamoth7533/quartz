# Circadian rhythms and sleep

**Definition.** Sleep timing is regulated by circadian rhythms interacting with homeostatic sleep drive. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]

## Supported claims

- Sleep cycles through REM and non-REM stages with different patterns of brain activity. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]
- Sleep regulation combines a circadian clock with homeostatic pressure, so timing and duration are separate questions. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]

> [!info]- In depth: two regulatory systems, one measurable output
> **The two-system account.** Sleep timing is regulated by circadian rhythms interacting with homeostatic sleep drive: how long you have been awake and what your body clock expects. The two systems can be dissociated experimentally and clinically - a person can be sleepy at the wrong circadian phase, or have a normal clock and accumulated sleep debt - which is why timing and duration are separate measurements. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]
>
> **Architecture across the night.** Sleep cycles through rapid-eye-movement and non-REM stages distinguished by brain-wave patterns, with slow-wave sleep early in the night and longer REM periods later, so a night's composition matters as much as its length. [[F98 OpenStax Psychology 2e stages of sleep#^f98-stages|Stages of Sleep]] [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]
>
> **The clock machinery.** The core clock is a transcriptional-translational feedback loop in which clock proteins repress their own transcription over roughly 24 hours; most tissues contain their own clocks coordinated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus, so circadian organisation is distributed rather than located in one structure, and clock output influences metabolism, physiology and behaviour. The molecular account comes primarily from animal models. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-tissue|Circadian clock architecture]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-outputs|Circadian clock architecture]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-limit|Circadian clock architecture]]
>
> **Why misalignment has wide effects.** Because clock output reaches metabolism, physiology and behaviour, disrupted timing has consequences beyond sleep - which is the mechanistic basis of the social-rhythm and shift-work literatures, and the reason timing is studied in mood disorders. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-outputs|Circadian clock architecture]] [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-theory|The social zeitgeber theory]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Specify which system is being claimed - circadian phase, homeostatic drive, architecture or duration - and which measure supports it. A single night's sleep report contains none of them separately. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-caution-ninds|AI appraisal: Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]

> [!info]- Reference: clocks, sleep pressure and mood
> **Molecular clock.** The core clock is a transcriptional-translational feedback loop in which clock proteins repress their own transcription over roughly 24 hours. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]]
>
> **Distributed timing.** Most tissues contain their own clocks coordinated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus, so circadian organisation is distributed rather than located in one structure. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-tissue|Circadian clock architecture]] Clock output influences metabolism and physiology broadly, which is why disrupted timing has effects beyond sleep. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-outputs|Circadian clock architecture]]
>
> **Sleep pressure versus timing.** Sleep is regulated by circadian timing interacting with homeostatic sleep drive - how long you have been awake - and the two systems are dissociable. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain basics: understanding sleep]] Adenosine accumulation is one component of the pressure signal. [[F98 OpenStax Psychology 2e stages of sleep#^f98-stages|Stages of Sleep]]
>
> **Mood link, stated carefully.** The social zeitgeber theory proposes that life events trigger episodes by disrupting social routines and thereby circadian rhythms; the review that evaluates it reports limited direct support and considers an internal pacemaker alternative. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-theory|The social zeitgeber theory]] [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-evaluation|The social zeitgeber theory]] Circadian disruption is therefore a plausible contributing process, not an established cause. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-status|The social zeitgeber theory]]
>
> **Sleep architecture.** Sleep cycles through non-REM and REM stages with different brain-wave patterns, and deprivation affects attention and mood. [[F98 OpenStax Psychology 2e stages of sleep#^f98-rem|Stages of Sleep]] [[F98 OpenStax Psychology 2e stages of sleep#^f98-deprivation|Stages of Sleep]]
>
> **The clock is a molecular machine, not a metaphor.** In clock-containing cells, clock proteins accumulate, enter the nucleus and repress the transcription of their own genes; the protein decays, repression lifts, and the cycle restarts with a period of roughly a day. That transcriptional-translational feedback loop is the mechanism behind "the body clock", and because the loop runs in most tissues, peripheral organs keep their own local time, coordinated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus through neural and hormonal signals. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]]
>
> **Light is the principal entraining input.** Specialised retinal projections reach the suprachiasmatic nucleus and pull the molecular clocks toward the light-dark cycle, which is why irregular light exposure - screens at night, shift work, travel - shifts the clock rather than merely overriding sleepiness. The same pathway is why "sleep hygiene" advice is really advice about light and timing inputs to a measurable mechanism. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-outputs|Circadian clock architecture]]
>
> **Rhythm disruption and mood: the honest state of the link.** The social zeitgeber theory proposes that life events destabilise social routines and thereby circadian rhythm stability, contributing to mood episodes; the evaluating review reports limited direct support for the full theory while noting the plausibility of circadian mechanisms. The defensible connection is that rhythm stability is measurable, disrupted in some conditions, and treatment-relevant as a stability target - not that rhythm disruption causes episodes in any individual. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-evaluation|The social zeitgeber theory]] [[Psychoeducation and social rhythms]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Neuroendocrinology is the natural partner domain: cortisol's daily rhythm, body-temperature cycles and sleep-stage physiology are all clock outputs, so a claim about "circadian effects" in any of them inherits the two-system logic spelled out here. [[Stress response and the HPA axis]] [[Circadian clock biology]]

## Limitation or common misconception

The two-process description is a model of average behaviour; shift work, illness, medication and light exposure complicate it.
