# Phase-based trauma therapy evidence

**Definition.** Phase-based trauma therapy sequences stabilization before trauma processing; non-phase-based approaches begin processing earlier or integrate it. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-comparison|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]

## Supported claims

- Across most outcomes there were no significant differences between phase-based and non-phase-based interventions. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-comparison|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]
- Multi-phase interventions did better on PTSD symptoms, and phase, multi-phase and exposure designs did better on affect regulation. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-outcomes|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]
- Non-phase-based and non-exposure-based approaches can be as effective as structured approaches in many contexts, while some outcomes may still benefit from structure. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-conclusion|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]
- The comparison is between packaged protocols rather than a randomized test of sequencing within one protocol. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-caution-sequence|AI synthesis: Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]

> [!info]- In depth: what the sequencing debate actually compared
> **The comparison.** Across most outcomes the planned contrasts - phase versus non-phase, multi-phase versus single-phase, and exposure versus non-exposure - showed no significant differences. Multi-phase interventions had superior effects on PTSD symptoms, and phase-based, multi-phase and exposure interventions showed greater improvement on the self-organization domain of affect regulation. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-comparison|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]] [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-outcomes|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]
>
> **The authors' reading.** In many situations, approaches without a stabilisation phase or formal exposure performed comparably, while selected outcomes still looked better under structured designs. That is a statement about average differences between packaged protocols rather than a demonstration that sequencing does not matter. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-conclusion|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]
>
> **What the design cannot answer.** The comparison is between packaged protocols, not a randomised test of sequencing inside one protocol, so the clinical question of how to order components for a particular person stays open. Meta-analytic limits compound the ambiguity: the review is limited by the small number of available trials, a single time point and heterogeneity in outcome measures - limitations the authors state themselves. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-caution-sequence|AI appraisal: Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]] [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-limit|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]
>
> **The related pooled evidence.** Psychotherapy for complex trauma showed significant pooled effects on PTSD, depression, anxiety and dissociation outcomes, with effects slightly smaller at follow-up and no longer significant for anxiety and dissociation, and with moderators including childhood trauma and risk of bias. Complex trauma exposure in those trials is not identical to a formal ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-followup|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-caution-category|AI appraisal: Psychological interventions for CPTSD]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Separate a null contrast between protocols, a positive finding on one outcome, and a claim about how treatment should be sequenced. Only the last is the clinical question, and the evidence here does not settle it. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-limit|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-limit|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]

> [!info]- Reference: the sequencing debate in its own terms
> **The proposal.** Phase-based treatment proposes stabilisation and skills before trauma-focused work, on the reasoning that severe dysregulation makes exposure unsafe or ineffective.
>
> **Evidence.** A systematic review of phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy reported that outcomes were broadly comparable between approaches, which does not support a requirement for stabilisation as a precondition. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-outcomes|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]
>
> **Guideline framing.** Current guideline products describe trauma-focused therapy as central for PTSD while acknowledging that sequencing may be adapted for particular presentations. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]]
>
> **What the debate is about.** The question is whether sequencing changes outcomes, not whether stabilisation has any value; skills can be useful without being a gate.
>
> **Boundary.** This is a research summary; it contains no instruction for any individual's treatment and no trauma-exposure guidance. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-caution-sequence|AI appraisal: Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]

> [!info]- Further depth: what the sequencing comparison did and did not test
> Across most planned contrasts - phase versus non-phase, multi-phase versus single-phase, exposure versus non-exposure - there were no significant differences, while multi-phase interventions had superior effects on PTSD symptoms and structured designs showed greater improvement on affect regulation. The authors read the evidence as showing that approaches without a stabilisation phase or formal exposure often performed comparably. The comparison is between packaged protocols rather than a test of sequencing inside one protocol, and the review is limited by few trials, a single time point and heterogeneous outcome measures. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-comparison|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]] [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-outcomes|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]] [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-conclusion|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]] [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-limit|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Phase sequencing is a clinical-psychiatry reasoning claim built on the methods domain's evidence-hierarchy conventions - which phase comes first is argued from trial design, endpoint choice and comorbidity logic rather than from pharmacology, so reading it without the methods material strips the reasoning away. ## Limitation or common misconception

The trial base is small, with one analysis time point and heterogeneous outcome measures, as the authors state.
