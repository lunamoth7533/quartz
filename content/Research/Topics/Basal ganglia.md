# Basal ganglia

## Definition

The basal ganglia are a set of forebrain nuclei - caudate, putamen, nucleus accumbens and globus pallidus, with related midbrain and diencephalic structures - that modulate cortical output rather than generating movement directly.

## How it works

**Components.** The corpus striatum and its associated structures are described anatomically first, with nomenclature tables, because the terminology differs across traditions. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-structures|Basal ganglia]]

**Pathways.** Signals are processed through direct and indirect pathways whose relative activity shapes cortical output, which is why the system is described as modulating rather than driving movement. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal ganglia]]

**Clinical mapping.** Dysfunction produces extrapyramidal signs such as resting tremor, chorea and dystonia, distinguished by pattern rather than by single symptom. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-dyskinesias|Disorders of the Motor System]]

**Dopamine link.** Dopamine projections to striatum are central to this system, which is why dopamine-blocking drugs produce motor side effects and why Parkinson's disease involves dopamine neuron loss. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]] [[F76 MedlinePlus Parkinson's disease#^f76-mechanism|Parkinson's Disease]]

## Evidence and status

The pathway model is a well-established framework that has been refined; clinical signs are mapped onto it with reasonable success, but individual variability is substantial. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-disorders|Basal ganglia]]

> [!info]- In depth: the nuclei, the circuit and why the simple model is incomplete
> **The parts, and where they sit.** The basal ganglia are forebrain nuclei: the **striatum** (caudate nucleus, putamen and nucleus accumbens), the **globus pallidus** divided into an external segment (GPe) and an internal segment (GPi), the **subthalamic nucleus** (STN) in the diencephalon, and the midbrain **substantia nigra** with its compact part (SNc, which supplies dopamine) and reticular part (SNr, which acts with GPi as a basal ganglia output nucleus). Nomenclature differs across traditions, which is why the chapter presents structure and terminology first. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-structures|Basal ganglia]]
>
> **Why it is a circuit and not a motor centre.** The system receives input mainly from cortex and thalamus into the striatum, processes it through the pallidal and nigral nuclei, and returns its output to cortex through the thalamus, so it modulates cortical output rather than generating movement directly. That loop anatomy is the reason basal ganglia damage changes the initiation, scaling and selection of movements instead of paralysing them. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal ganglia]] [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-descending|Motor Responses]]
>
> **The classical direct pathway, with its signs.** Striatal neurons of the direct pathway inhibit GPi/SNr, and GPi/SNr inhibit the thalamus. Two inhibitory links in series therefore produce net facilitation: striatal activity (driven by cortical input and by dopamine at D1-type receptors) reduces the tonic inhibition of thalamus, which **releases** thalamocortical activity. This is the sense in which basal ganglia output is described as acting by disinhibition rather than by excitation. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal ganglia]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **The classical indirect pathway, with its signs.** Striatal neurons of the indirect pathway inhibit GPe, GPe inhibits STN, and STN excites GPi/SNr. The extra excitatory step means the indirect pathway **increases** GPi/SNr output and therefore suppresses thalamus. In the textbook model the two pathways have opposing effects on the same output, and dopamine at D2-type receptors reduces indirect-pathway signalling, so dopamine shifts the balance towards facilitation. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal ganglia]]
>
> **Loop domains beyond movement.** The same circuit architecture is repeated across functional territories: sensorimotor loops through putamen, associative loops through caudate, and limbic loops through nucleus accumbens and ventral pallidum, distinguished by which cortical areas supply the input and which thalamic nuclei receive the output. That is why basal ganglia dysfunction produces cognitive and motivational changes alongside movement signs, and why the anatomy is described in loops rather than in one pathway. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-structures|Basal ganglia]] [[F109 Neuroscience Online association and executive processing#^f109-areas|Association and Executive Processing]]
>
> **Clinical mapping.** Dysfunction produces extrapyramidal signs - resting tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, chorea, dystonia - distinguished by pattern rather than by any single symptom. Loss of dopamine neurons in the substantia nigra compacta is the characteristic pathology of Parkinson's disease, and dopamine-blocking drugs produce motor side effects through the same receptor population, which is the clearest clinical evidence that the pathway account is tracking something real. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[F76 MedlinePlus Parkinson's disease#^f76-mechanism|Parkinson's Disease]] [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]
>
> **Where the simple rate model breaks down.** The textbook version - two pathways with opposed signs, one output rate, dopamine as a balance knob - is a framework that has been refined rather than a complete description. Recordings show patterned, temporally structured activity rather than a single rate; the hyperdirect pathway from cortex to STN provides a fast route that bypasses the striatum; GPe and STN form a recurrent network rather than a one-way chain; and different cell types within striatum have different connectivity. The chapter's own caveat is that the pathway model is well established as a framework and that clinical signs are mapped onto it with reasonable success while individual variability remains substantial. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal ganglia]] [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-disorders|Basal ganglia]] [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-limit|Basal ganglia]] [[P23040802 Marder 2012 Neuromodulation#^p23040802-degeneracy|Neuromodulation]]
>
> **Reading rule.** A sentence that attributes a symptom to 'the basal ganglia' without naming the loop, the pathway and the sign is as uninformative as attributing a symptom to 'the cortex'. The useful level of description here is: which territory, which pathway, which output nucleus. [[P22722855 Churchland 2012 Neural population dynamics#^p22722855-claim|Neural population dynamics]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The neuroanatomy of the basal ganglia supplies the circuit substrate that the pharmacology domain uses when it describes dopamine-receptor drug effects on movement, and that the clinical-psychiatry domain uses when it discusses extrapyramidal side effects of antipsychotic medication. Anatomy-to-clinical inference runs through pharmacology in between, and reading them together makes that two-step chain explicit. [[Antipsychotic mechanisms]] [[Movement disorders]]

## Uncertainties

- The classical direct/indirect model does not capture all basal ganglia functions, including non-motor roles in learning and motivation.
