# Dopamine signalling

**Definition.** Dopamine is a catecholamine transmitter with roles in movement, motivation, reward processing and cognitive control depending on the pathway. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-transmitters|Cells of the Nervous System]]

## Supported claims

- Dopamine receptor subtypes can have opposite effects: D1 receptors are excitatory while D2-type receptors are inhibitory. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]
- In bipolar research, elevated D2/3 availability accompanies mania while increased dopamine transporter levels accompany depression, with inconsistent findings elsewhere. [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-state|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]
- Both dopamine agonists and antidopaminergics have improved bipolar depressive symptoms in pharmacological studies. [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-pharmacology|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Dopamine is not one signal: pathways, receptor subtypes and brain regions do different jobs with the same molecule.
