# Trial endpoints, benefit and harms

## Definition

An endpoint is the outcome a trial is designed to measure. Primary endpoints determine the main conclusion; secondary and safety endpoints supply context. Endpoint choice encodes a judgement about what matters.

## How it works

**Endpoint families.** Symptom scales, functional measures, quality of life and adverse-event discontinuation are different endpoints, and improvement in one does not imply improvement in another. [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-domains|International Classification of Functioning]]

**Duration.** Short-term symptom trials dominate the evidence base; long-term questions about function and safety usually rest on observational data with different limitations. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

**Rater.** Self-report, clinician rating and informant rating produce systematically different estimates, which is why trials report them separately. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

**Certainty.** Guidelines rate evidence per outcome, so a recommendation can be strong for one outcome and weak for another. [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]]

**Non-significance.** A trial that fails to detect a difference has not shown equivalence, and absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-caution-absence|AI appraisal: Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

## Evidence and status

Endpoint and rater effects are well documented in the meta-analytic literature; their interpretation requires knowing the decision the trial was designed to support.

> [!info]- In depth: choosing what counts, and reading benefit beside harm
> **Endpoints are choices.** A trial's primary endpoint decides what counts as success, and different choices answer different questions: a symptom scale measures symptoms, a functioning instrument measures activity and participation, and a quality-of-life measure measures something else again. WHODAS 2.0, for example, covers cognition, mobility, self-care, getting along, life activities and participation - and a functioning score summarises reported activity rather than symptom severity. [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-domains|International Classification of Functioning]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-caution-impairment|AI appraisal: International Classification of Functioning]]
>
> **Rater and setting define the measurement.** In the ADHD medication literature every drug beat placebo on clinicians' ratings in children while only two did on teachers' ratings, which is a change in result produced by a change in informant rather than in treatment. Any benefit claim should therefore name who rated it. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI appraisal: Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]]
>
> **Harms are part of the effect.** Amphetamines were less well tolerated than placebo in both age groups and dose-related discontinuation rose above study-specific ranges for amphetamines and methylphenidate, while the antipsychotic literature shows benefit and side effects sharing one mechanism. Tolerability is not a footnote to efficacy; it is the other half of the same question. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-tolerability|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-tolerability|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]] [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]
>
> **Certainty and recommendation are separate outputs.** GRADE separates the certainty of evidence from the strength of a recommendation, and certainty is rated down for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. A strong recommendation on low-certainty evidence is legitimate where values and context justify it - which is exactly why the two must be read together. [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-domains|GRADE]]
>
> **Reading rule.** For each benefit claim, find the paired harm claim and the certainty grade, and check whether the endpoint was chosen before or after the analysis. A trial report that supplies all three is readable; one that supplies a headline is not. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-acceptability|Comparative interventions for ADHD]] [[P23997866 Sullivan 2012 Using effect size#^p23997866-example|Using effect size]]

> [!info]- Further depth: endpoint choice, rater and certainty
> Endpoint choice decides what counts as success - a symptom scale, a functioning instrument or a quality-of-life measure answer different questions - and rater effects change results within the same trials: all drugs beat placebo on clinicians' ratings in children while only two did on teachers' ratings. Harms belong to the same question: amphetamines were less well tolerated than placebo in both age groups and dose-related discontinuation rose above study-specific ranges. GRADE separates certainty from recommendation strength, rating certainty down for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias.
