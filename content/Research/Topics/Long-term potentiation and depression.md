# Long-term potentiation and depression

**Definition.** Long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD) are activity-dependent changes in synaptic strength that persist beyond the stimulation that induced them. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-definition|Long-term potentiation and long-term depression]]

## Supported claims

- LTP is defined as a persistent increase in synaptic strength and LTD as the complementary decrease; both are treated as cellular models of learning-related change. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-definition|P21779718]]
- The properties that matter for a memory mechanism are longevity, input specificity and associativity, so one synapse can change without its neighbours changing. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-properties|P21779718]]
- Induction polarity depends on calcium entry and on the balance of kinases and phosphatases; persistent activation recruits gene expression and new protein synthesis. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-mechanism|P21779718]]
- Spike timing within roughly 50 milliseconds sets the direction: presynaptic-then-postsynaptic favours LTP and the reverse order favours LTD. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-timing|P21779718]]
- Metaplasticity means previous activity changes how a synapse responds to later plasticity protocols. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-metaplasticity|P21779718]]
- Human non-invasive stimulation produces LTP-like or LTD-like changes, but the review says their defining features still need to be shown, so human learning claims outrun the evidence. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|P21779718]]
- The most detailed mechanisms come from reduced animal preparations, so a synaptic change is evidence about a mechanism rather than proof about a person's learning. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-caution-inference|AI synthesis: P21779718]]

> [!info]- In depth: the cellular model, its rules, and how far the model reaches
> **The definitions and their properties.** Long-term potentiation is an activity-dependent, persistent increase in synaptic strength, and long-term depression is its complementary decrease. Three properties are treated as defining: the change outlasts the inducing stimulus, it is specific to inputs that were active rather than to the whole cell, and it is associative, requiring coincident activity in the relevant presynaptic and postsynaptic elements. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-definition|Long-term potentiation and depression]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-properties|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **The induction switch.** Whether a synapse potentiates or depresses follows from calcium entry and the resulting balance of kinase and phosphatase activity: modest, brief calcium favours depression while larger, sustained calcium favours potentiation. Enduring forms require gene expression and protein synthesis as well, which is what distinguishes a persistent change from a transient facilitation. The review therefore treats early and late phases as mechanistically different, not as one process at two intensities. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-mechanism|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **The timing rule.** Presynaptic spiking followed by postsynaptic spiking within roughly fifty milliseconds favours potentiation, while the reverse order favours depression. That ordering rule is why spike-timing protocols are used as a laboratory tool for writing changes into specific pathways, and it is also the cleanest evidence that the direction of change is information about coincidence timing rather than about activity quantity. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-timing|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **Metaplasticity.** Previous activity changes how a synapse responds to later plasticity - the same protocol can potentiate a naive synapse and depress one that has recently been activated. Metaplasticity is the reason a plasticity result is meaningless without its history, and it complicates any attempt to read 'how much plasticity' off a single measurement. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-metaplasticity|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **What the model is and is not.** Mechanisms come mainly from reduced preparations - slices and animals - and human non-invasive protocols produce changes described as LTP-like that have not demonstrated the defining features of the cellular phenomenon. The review's own caution is the important one: LTP and LTD are the dominant cellular model of learning-related change, not a measurement of learning, and a synaptic change in a slice constrains but does not determine what a behaving animal or person learns. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Long-term potentiation and depression]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-caution-inference|AI synthesis: Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Ask three questions of any plasticity claim: which preparation, which protocol, and which timescale. Answers that specify only 'plasticity' hide all three. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-limit|Long-term potentiation and depression]]

> [!info]- Reference: the cellular model of lasting synaptic change
> **Definition.** Long-term potentiation (LTP) is an activity-dependent, persistent increase in synaptic strength; long-term depression (LTD) is its complement. LTP is long-lasting, input-specific and associative in the classical hippocampal preparations. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-definition|Long-term potentiation and depression]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-properties|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **Induction rule.** The polarity of change follows postsynaptic calcium entry and the balance of kinase and phosphatase activity, and durable forms require protein synthesis. Spike timing matters: presynaptic-then-postsynaptic pairing within tens of milliseconds favours LTP, the reverse order favours LTD. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-mechanism|Long-term potentiation and depression]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-timing|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **Metaplasticity.** Previous activity changes how a synapse responds to later plasticity, so the same stimulation protocol can produce different outcomes depending on history. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-metaplasticity|Long-term potentiation and depression]] This is one reason a simple 'strengthening = learning' reading fails.
>
> **Evidence boundary.** Mechanism work is largely from reduced animal preparations, and human non-invasive measures of 'LTP-like' effects are not measurements of LTP in a person. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Long-term potentiation and depression]] The model is the dominant cellular account of learning-related change; it is not itself a measurement of learning. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-caution-inference|AI appraisal: Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **Systems context.** Neuromodulators reconfigure which synapses are plastic at a given moment, so plasticity rules established in a slice may not predict behaviour in an intact, modulated circuit. [[P23040802 Marder 2012 Neuromodulation#^p23040802-state|Neuromodulation]]
>

## Limitation or common misconception

The evidence base for mechanisms is largely animal tissue; LTP and LTD are models of learning-related change, not measurements of memory in a person.
