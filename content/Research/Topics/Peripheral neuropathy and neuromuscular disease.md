# Peripheral neuropathy and neuromuscular disease

## Definition

Peripheral nerve disorders affect nerves outside the brain and spinal cord, and include a large group of conditions with different causes. Neuromuscular disease affects the junction or muscle itself.

## How it works

**Causes.** Diabetes, infections, nutritional deficiencies, toxins, trauma and inherited conditions are named causes, and many present first through symptoms rather than an identified cause. [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-causes|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-list|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]]

**Pattern.** Symptoms such as tingling, numbness and pain typically begin in the hands and feet, reflecting the length-dependent vulnerability of long axons. [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-symptoms|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] Myelin biology explains why long or heavily myelinated fibres are affected first. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]

**Neuromuscular junction.** The junction is the site where transmitter release, receptor binding and muscle activation meet, and the classical experiments on release were done there. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-calcium|Transmitter release]]

## Evidence and status

Classification and typical patterns are established; identifying a specific cause requires laboratory and electrophysiological investigation, which is beyond this reference note's scope.

> [!info]- In depth: a heterogeneous group with a shared exam logic
> **The size of the category.** Peripheral nerve disorders are a large group affecting the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord with over a hundred recognised types - which is why the clinical task is pattern recognition within a category rather than diagnosis by name. [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-list|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-limit|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]]
>
> **Symptoms and their distribution.** Symptoms described include weakness, aching, and tingling, numbness or pain that typically begins in the hands and feet, and causes include diabetes, infections, nutritional deficiencies, toxins, trauma and inherited conditions, with many illustrated by symptoms first. The distal, symmetric pattern is a localising clue; a focal pattern points elsewhere. [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-symptoms|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-causes|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]]
>
> **Where the lesion is, conceptually.** Myelin insulates axons and increases conduction speed, interrupted by nodes of Ranvier, so a demyelinating process slows conduction and an axonal process reduces signal amplitude - the distinction the nerve-conduction literature is built on. Classical experiments at the skeletal neuromuscular junction established the transmission mechanism at the other end of the pathway. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-calcium|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]]
>
> **Disorders map to circuits.** The motor-disorders literature maps signs to the circuits that produce them, so weakness, sensory loss and reflex change are interpreted anatomically rather than as separate complaints. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-mapping|Motor Disorders]] [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-dyskinesias|Motor Disorders]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Name the distribution, the fibre type implicated and the time course; then state what test would separate the candidates. This library's consumer-level source aggregates the group without ranking causes by frequency, so it supports pattern description rather than workup guidance. [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-limit|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]
>
> **Length-dependence is the diagnostic workhorse.** Because the longest axons are most vulnerable to metabolic and toxic injury, a stocking-and-glove sensory pattern - symptoms longest nerves feel first - is the classic presentation of a length-dependent peripheral neuropathy, and departures from it (asymmetry, proximal involvement, rapid progression) push the differential away from the metabolic causes toward inflammatory, compressive or inherited motor-sensory subtypes. Pattern is the evidence; the pattern follows from axon biology. [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-symptoms|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]
>
> **Nerve conduction studies localise the lesion's level and kind.** By stimulating and recording along a nerve, conduction studies separate axonal from demyelinating processes - reduced amplitude versus slowed conduction - and localise lesions to root, plexus, nerve or terminal. That separation matters because the differential and the prognosis differ: demyelinating processes often respond to immunotherapy, axonal ones follow their metabolic or toxic cause. [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-causes|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The neuromuscular junction is where pharmacology meets this territory: transmitter release, receptor binding and muscle activation are each drug-targetable steps, which is how this article connects to the vesicle-cycle and receptor articles without duplicating them. [[The synaptic vesicle cycle]] [[Receptor families and second messengers]]
>
> **Autonomic involvement is part of the peripheral story.** Small-fibre neuropathies can affect autonomic fibres - producing orthostatic intolerance, sweating and gut changes - with normal routine conduction studies, because conduction tests assess large myelinated fibres. That gap between symptom and test is a measurement limitation of the standard battery rather than a sign the symptoms are not peripheral, and it connects the neuropathy differential to the autonomic-measurement material directly.
