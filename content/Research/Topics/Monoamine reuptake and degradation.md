# Monoamine reuptake and degradation

## Definition

Monoamine signalling is terminated mainly by reuptake into the presynaptic terminal through specific transporters, with enzymatic degradation by monoamine oxidase and catechol-O-methyltransferase as a parallel route. Both routes are drug targets.

## How it works

**Transporters.** Each monoamine has its own transporter, and transporter blockade raises extracellular transmitter concentrations in the short term. This is the mechanism shared by stimulants, many antidepressants and some other agents. [[F12 NIGMS What happens to medicine in your body|What happens to medicine in your body]] [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work|How do medicines work]]

**Enzymatic degradation.** Monoamine oxidase breaks down transmitter intracellularly, so inhibitors raise the pool available for release in addition to affecting synaptic concentrations. [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-classes|Biogenic amines]]

**Delayed clinical effect.** Increasing transmitter availability happens within hours; clinical improvement in depression typically takes weeks. That gap is one of the main reasons monoamine elevation alone is not treated as an adequate mechanistic account. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|How do antidepressants work?]]

**Adaptation.** Repeated transmitter elevation produces receptor and transporter adaptations, which is why tolerance and discontinuation effects occur with several classes. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-tolerance|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]] Benzodiazepines provide a parallel example of dependence developing with sustained action at an inhibitory target. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-risks|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]]

## Evidence and status

Transporter and enzyme pharmacology is established molecularly; clinical outcomes require trials, and effect sizes differ substantially across compounds and indications. Where evidence is summarised by guideline bodies, recommendation strength is tied to the underlying evidence review rather than to the mechanism. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-framing|Bipolar disorder CG185]]

> [!info]- In depth: how transmission is switched off, and why that is a drug target
> **Two clearance routes.** Monoamines are removed from the cleft by reuptake transporters and degraded by enzymes. Reuptake is fast, local and quantitative - the transporter returns transmitter to the terminal for repackaging - while enzymatic degradation by monoamine oxidase and catechol-O-methyltransferase handles transmitter that escapes and provides the main metabolic products used as clinical markers. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-receptor|Cells of the Nervous System]] [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-classes|Biogenic Amines]]
>
> **Transporters are structurally specific.** Each monoamine has its own uptake transporter with a characteristic distribution, which is why drugs can be relatively selective for the serotonin transporter, the noradrenaline transporter or the dopamine transporter, and why selectivity is a matter of degree rather than a binary property. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-receptor|Cells of the Nervous System]]
>
> **Reuptake inhibition versus release.** Keeping transmitter active in the cleft for longer is a different mechanism from increasing release: inhibitors act on the transporter, releasers act on vesicular stores and reverse transport. The two produce different concentration dynamics and different side-effect profiles, which is why they are discussed as separate pharmacological classes rather than as variants of one. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-receptor|Cells of the Nervous System]] [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-agonism|How do medicines work]]
>
> **Why kinetics matter clinically.** Blocking reuptake changes availability within hours, while clinical change in depression typically appears over weeks; that dissociation is one of the main arguments against a simple replacement account of antidepressant action. The persistence of the same biochemical effect over a much longer clinical timescale is evidence that downstream adaptation, not the acute change itself, mediates the outcome. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|How do antidepressants work]] [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-plasticity|How do antidepressants work]]
>
> **Where the system shows up elsewhere.** The same transporters are the target of stimulant medications used in ADHD, where the mechanism is increased synaptic availability in prefrontal circuits. Naming the transporter and the brain region is what separates a mechanism claim from a category claim, and it is the level at which the treatment literature operates. [[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-mechanisms|Prefrontal catecholamine mechanisms]] [[Stimulant and non-stimulant mechanisms]]
>
> **Two enzymes, different locations.** Monoamine oxidase works intracellularly on transmitter that has been reuptaken or is free in the terminal, while catechol-O-methyltransferase works extracellularly and peripherally. The split matters for interpretation: enzyme inhibitors change both the stored pool available for release and, for COMT, the transmitter surviving in the cleft and circulation - so "raises monoamine signalling" aggregates different sites of action that trials and imaging cannot always separate. [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-classes|Biogenic Amine Neurotransmitters]]
>
> **Why transporter occupancy is not a clinical dial.** Imaging can estimate what fraction of transporters a drug occupies, and two drugs with similar occupancy can differ in effect; the chain from occupancy through network-level change to symptom change has links that are measured separately and imperfectly. This is the clearest pharmacology-level illustration of why a mechanism claim needs the outcome level named. [[F12 NIGMS What happens to medicine in your body#^f12-adme|What happens to medicine in your body]]
>
> **What the adaptations do to the story.** Repeated elevation of extracellular monoamines produces receptor-scale and transporter-scale adaptations over days to weeks - the same timescale on which antidepressant clinical effects appear. That alignment is why adaptation, not acute elevation, is the leading candidate for the therapeutically relevant change, and why discontinuation effects follow when the adapted system is suddenly unblocked. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-mechanism|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** This note is the shared mechanism floor for two clinical literatures - stimulant treatment in ADHD and antidepressant treatment in depression - and the evidence articles in both condition domains exist precisely because mechanism and clinical effect must be verified separately. ## Uncertainties

- Occupancy of a transporter measured by imaging does not translate directly into clinical effect.
- Individual differences in metabolism change exposure to the same dose, which is why monitoring exists for some drugs and not others. [[F18 FDA Narrow therapeutic index drugs#^f18-nti|Narrow therapeutic index drugs]]
