# Axon guidance and synaptogenesis

## Definition

Axon guidance is the process by which a growing axon navigates to its target using molecular cues; synaptogenesis is the formation of synaptic connections once it arrives.

## How it works

**Cues.** Axons are guided by attractive and repulsive cues in the environment, and conserved families - netrins, Slits, semaphorins and ephrins - carry those signals. [[P12471249 Dickson 2002 Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance#^p12471249-cues|Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance]] **Growth cone.** Cues act by regulating cytoskeletal dynamics in the growth cone, through signalling pathways that remain incompletely understood. [[P12471249 Dickson 2002 Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance#^p12471249-growthcone|Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance]]

**Specificity.** Regulatory mechanisms ensure the right axon responds to the right cue at the right time, which is how a modest number of guidance factors generates complex wiring. [[P12471249 Dickson 2002 Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance#^p12471249-precision|Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance]] Receptor regulation, including trafficking and modification, is a major source of that context sensitivity. [[P33706917 Korb 2021 Axon guidance signalling#^p33706917-regulation|Axon guidance signalling]]

**Synapse formation.** Once contact is made, adhesion and receptor maturation proceed in a defined sequence, and the molecular composition of the junction changes during development. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-receptors|Synapse formation and elimination]]

## Evidence and status

Guidance molecules and their receptors are established in genetic and biochemical work, largely in invertebrate and vertebrate model organisms; human relevance is inferred, with disease associations as supporting context. [[P33706917 Korb 2021 Axon guidance signalling#^p33706917-disease|Axon guidance signalling]]

> [!info]- In depth: wiring by molecular cues, refined by activity
> **Guidance.** Growing axons follow chemical gradients and surface cues to their targets; families of guidance molecules act through receptors, and how those receptors are regulated and trafficked shapes whether a growth cone is attracted or repelled, so the same cue can be interpreted differently in different contexts. The mapping examples in the genetics-in-neurology literature show how identifying such molecules changed understanding of specific disorders. [[P33706917 Korb 2021 Axon guidance signalling#^p33706917-regulation|Axon guidance signalling]] [[P33706917 Korb 2021 Axon guidance signalling#^p33706917-context|Axon guidance signalling]] [[F117 Neuroscience Online genetics and neuronal disease#^f117-disease|Genetics and Neuronal Disease]]
>
> **Synapse formation.** Once axons reach targets, synapses form and initially supernumerary connections are refined by activity-dependent competition and trophic support; the neuromuscular junction illustrates the outcome: fewer inputs per fibre, each stronger. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-competition|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]
>
> **Receptor composition changes with age.** The same synapse has different molecular properties at different developmental stages, which is why developmental findings do not transfer automatically to adults. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-receptors|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Distinguish guidance (molecular, developmental) from refinement (activity-dependent) from maintenance (adult); the three have different evidence bases. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-limit|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]
>
> **Four conserved families, one combinatorial code.** Netrins, Slits, semaphorins and ephrins each include attractants and repellents, act at short and long range, and are deployed in gradients and boundaries along the pathway; a growing axon reads the combination through its receptor complement rather than any single cue. Combinatorial coding is how a limited molecular vocabulary specifies the brain's wiring complexity, and it predicts that disrupting one cue shifts only the axons that depend on it - which is what the disorder-genetics examples show. [[P12471249 Dickson 2002 Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance#^p12471249-cues|Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance]] [[P33706917 Korb 2021 Axon guidance signalling#^p33706917-context|Axon guidance signalling]]
>
> **The growth cone is the decision-maker.** Guidance signals act by remodelling the cytoskeleton of the growth cone at the axon's tip, so the sign of a cue - attraction or repulsion - depends on the cone's current receptor set and internal state, not on the molecule alone. Receptor trafficking and modification are major sources of that context sensitivity, which is why the same cue can attract at one stage and repel at another. [[P12471249 Dickson 2002 Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance#^p12471249-growthcone|Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance]] [[P33706917 Korb 2021 Axon guidance signalling#^p33706917-regulation|Axon guidance signalling]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The neurobiology domain's trophic-competition article takes over where guidance ends: molecular cues deliver an axon to its target, and activity plus trophic support decide which of the arriving contacts survive - a clean division of labour between the genetics-guidance phase and the activity-refinement phase of wiring. [[Neurotrophic support and cell death]] [[Circuit development and homeostasis]]
>
> **From contact to working synapse.** When a growth cone reaches its target, the handshake proceeds through recognisable stages: adhesion stabilises the contact, pre- and postsynaptic specialisations assemble reciprocally, and the receptor composition of the junction continues to change after it is functional. That maturation sequence is why an early synapse is not simply a small adult synapse, and why developmental electrophysiology shows silent synapses that are anatomically present but not yet functional in the same way. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-receptors|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The axon-guidance and synaptogenesis material in the genetics-neurodevelopment domain supplies the mechanism that the autism and ADHD condition domains reach for when they describe wiring differences; a molecular-guidance claim and a clinical wiring claim are connected only insofar as the same cellular process is being described, so reading them together makes the inference chain explicit rather than implied.
