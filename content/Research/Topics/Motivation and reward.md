# Motivation and reward

## Definition

Motivation concerns the initiation and persistence of goal-directed behaviour. Reward describes outcomes that increase the likelihood of the behaviour that produced them, together with the subjective experience of wanting and liking.

## How it works

**Theories.** Motivational accounts include drive reduction, arousal theory and expectancy-value models in which beliefs about capability and outcome value predict effort. [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-theories|Motivation]] [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-efficacy|Motivation]]

**Reinforcement.** Operant consequences shape behaviour through reinforcement and punishment, with schedules producing characteristic persistence. [[F39 OpenStax Psychology 2e operant conditioning#^f39-consequences|Operant Conditioning]]

**Neural basis.** Reward-related learning is linked to dopamine prediction-error signals, with the important caveat that dopamine carries additional and heterogeneous signals. [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-rpe|Reward prediction error]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]]

**Social motives.** Belonging and achievement function as organised motivations rather than separate drives, which matters when motivation is described clinically. [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-social|Motivation]]

## Evidence and status

Behavioural effects of reinforcement are robust; translating reward constructs into measurements of anhedonia or apathy in clinical populations is much less settled.

> [!info]- In depth: motives, reinforcement and the reward system underneath
> **The psychological traditions.** Motivation is described through several traditions - instinct and drive theories, arousal theory and expectancy accounts - with self-efficacy and goal-setting research linking beliefs about capability to persistence and performance, and social motives such as belonging and achievement treated as organised patterns rather than separate drives. The traditions differ in what they treat as the explanatory unit. [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-theories|Motivation]] [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-efficacy|Motivation]] [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-social|Motivation]]
>
> **The behavioural mechanism.** Operant conditioning changes behaviour through consequences: reinforcement increases and punishment decreases the likelihood of a behaviour. That is the learning-level account of why a rewarding outcome shapes what a person does next. [[F39 OpenStax Psychology 2e operant conditioning#^f39-consequences|Operant Conditioning]] [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-operant|Conditioning and Learning]]
>
> **The neural candidate and its limits.** Midbrain dopamine neurons respond to reward-prediction error, which maps onto reinforcement learning's teaching signal; but dopamine carries additional signals including salience and movement-related information, and different populations carry different signals, so 'reward' is an approximation rather than a complete description. [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-rpe|Reward prediction error]] [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-scope|Reward prediction error]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]]
>
> **The pharmacology shows the same structure.** Drugs can flood reward circuitry with dopamine, producing effects much larger or faster than natural rewards, and repeated exposure changes circuits involved in reward, stress and self-control - while response varies with the drug, route, amount, genetics and environment, so no single pathway explains all substance-use problems. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-reward|Drugs and the Brain]] [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-adaptation|Drugs and the Brain]] [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-variation|Drugs and the Brain]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Separate what a person reports wanting, what a behaviour reveals they value, and what a dopamine measurement shows. The three are related but are measured differently, and a claim that treats one as a proxy for another has lost a level. [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-caution|What does dopamine mean?]]

> [!info]- Further depth: reported motives, revealed value and the dopamine question
> Motivation research runs from drive and arousal theories to expectancy accounts, with self-efficacy and goal-setting linking beliefs about capability to persistence, and social motives such as belonging treated as organised patterns. Operant consequences explain how outcomes shape behaviour, and dopamine reward-prediction error is the candidate neural teaching signal - with the caveats that dopamine carries salience and movement signals too and that population identity matters. Keeping reported wanting, revealed value and the neural measurement separate is the discipline this literature requires. [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-theories|Motivation]] [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-efficacy|Motivation]] [[F39 OpenStax Psychology 2e operant conditioning#^f39-consequences|Operant Conditioning]] [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-scope|Reward prediction error]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-heterogeneity|What does dopamine mean?]]
>
> **Expectancy-value is the bridge construct.** Beliefs about capability and beliefs about outcome value jointly predict effort and persistence, which is why expectancy-value models organise the motivation literature and why interventions that change only one arm - capability beliefs or outcome value - often underperform. The construct is also where motivation meets clinical presentation: apathy, anhedonia and amotivation are describable as changes in the expectancy or value arms, which is more precise than a global "motivation deficit". [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-efficacy|Motivation]]
>
> **Wanting, liking and learning come apart.** The dopamine literature separates the appetitive pull of a cue from the pleasure of consumption and from the learning signal itself; they co-occur in healthy cases, which hides their separability, but pharmacology and addiction research split them cleanly. That split is why "reward" is not one thing in this library: a reward-prediction error is a learning signal, and treating it as a happiness meter is a category error the primary literature itself warns against. [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-rpe|Reward prediction error]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Substance-use research is the applied case where this article's distinctions do clinical work: drugs act on the learning signal and the pull rather than on pleasure alone, which explains patterns that a hedonic account misses.
