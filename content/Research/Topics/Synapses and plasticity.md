# Synapses and plasticity

**Definition.** At a chemical synapse the electrical signal becomes transmitter release and then a signal in the target cell; electrical synapses also exist. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-types|Communication Between Neurons]]

## Supported claims

- Transmitter is released by calcium-dependent vesicle fusion and cleared by degradation or by neuronal or glial reuptake. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-release|Communication Between Neurons]]
- The postsynaptic effect depends on the receptor rather than the transmitter alone. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]
- Metabotropic signalling can modify ion channels and gene transcription, which the textbook links cautiously to stronger connections and possibly to learning and memory. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]

> [!info]- In depth: the chain from vesicle release to lasting change
> **The base state.** Transmission begins when calcium entry triggers vesicle fusion, continues through receptor binding and postsynaptic current, and ends with clearance of transmitter from the cleft. Each step is a place where the strength of the connection can be changed, which is why 'synaptic strength' is a summary of several distinct variables rather than one quantity. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-calcium|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-release|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-receptor|Cells of the Nervous System]]
>
> **Three timescales of change.** Release probability varies over hundreds of milliseconds (short-term plasticity); receptor number and composition can change over minutes to hours; and changes in gene expression and structure persist for days or longer. A single word - plasticity - covers all three, and specifying the timescale is what makes a plasticity claim testable. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-facilitation|Synaptic Plasticity]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-mechanism|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **The associative rule.** The defining property of the Hebbian family is coincidence detection: change depends on the relative timing of pre- and postsynaptic activity rather than on activity magnitude alone, so a synapse that is active when its target cell is depolarised is the one that changes. That specificity is what allows a network to store patterns rather than merely to get stronger. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-properties|Long-term potentiation and depression]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-timing|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
>
> **Structure follows function, sometimes.** Spine cytoskeletal elements are responsible for the shape changes observed in some forms of plasticity, so in those cases a functional change is accompanied by an anatomical one. The converse - inferring function from anatomy alone - does not follow, which is why spine density is a suggestive rather than decisive measure. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-spines|Organization of Cell Types]]
>
> **Where the chain is inferred rather than demonstrated.** Slice and animal preparations establish mechanism; human non-invasive measures produce LTP-like changes without demonstrating the defining features; and behaviour is related to plasticity by inference. Keeping the three levels separate prevents the most common error in this area, which is to treat a slice result as an explanation of learning. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Long-term potentiation and depression]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-caution-inference|AI synthesis: Long-term potentiation and depression]]

> [!info]- Reference: from vesicle release to lasting change
> **The release step.** At a chemical synapse the arriving spike opens voltage-gated calcium channels; calcium entry into the terminal is the key trigger for transmitter release. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-calcium|Transmitter release]] Release occurs in quantal units, which is the classical evidence that transmitter is packaged and released in discrete events. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-quanta|Transmitter release]]
>
> **Receptor logic.** What a transmitter does is decided by the receptor, not by the molecule. Ionotropic receptors open an ion channel directly and act on a millisecond timescale; metabotropic receptors act through second messengers and can modify channels, excitability or transcription over seconds to hours. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **Short-term versus long-term change.** Short-term plasticity works through residual calcium and vesicle availability: facilitation makes a second spike release more transmitter, and depression makes it release less, depending on the synapse's release probability. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-facilitation|Synaptic plasticity]] Lasting change additionally requires second-messenger cascades and, for durable forms, new protein synthesis - the territory covered in [[Long-term potentiation and depression]]. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-lasting|Synaptic plasticity]]
>
> **Developmental context.** Synapses are not only modified but created and removed: during development, activity-dependent competition and trophic support decide which inputs survive. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse formation and elimination]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-competition|Synapse formation and elimination]] Adult plasticity and developmental refinement therefore use overlapping machinery to answer different questions.
>
> **Structural correlates.** Dendritic spine shape changes are one visible correlate of plasticity, linking the molecular story to something that can be imaged. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-spines|Cell types]] The inference from spine morphology to 'learning' remains a correlate-level claim.
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Synaptic plasticity is the cellular substrate the neuroanatomy, genetics-neurodevelopment and computational-brain-theories domains each describe at their own scale - developmental pruning, learning rules and lasting change all reduce to this material when they are made concrete. [[Synaptic pruning and myelination]] [[Long-term potentiation and depression]]
