# Neurons and glia

**Definition.** The nervous system is built from neurons that carry electrical and chemical signals and glia that support, insulate and modulate them. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-neuron|Nervous Tissue]]

## Supported claims

- A prototypical neuron receives input at dendrites, has a cell body with the nucleus, and sends output along an axon to terminals. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-neuron|Nervous Tissue]]
- Glial cells provide scaffolding, insulation, nutrient transport and immune mediation, at a roughly 1:1 ratio to neurons. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]]

> [!info]- In depth: division of labour in nervous tissue
> **The neuronal plan.** A prototypical neuron has dendrites that serve as input sites, a cell body containing the nucleus, and an axon that carries output to terminals. The plan is caricature rather than rule: some neurons lack axons, some have no spines, and dendritic and axonal compartments carry different proteins and organelles - differences that decide what a cell can compute and how far it can be modulated. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-neuron|Nervous Tissue]] [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-compartments|Organization of Cell Types]]
>
> **Structural specialisations with functional consequences.** Dendrites branch extensively and often carry spines that increase contact surface; spines contain cytoskeletal elements responsible for shape changes seen in some forms of plasticity, so structural plasticity and synaptic plasticity share machinery. Reading a dendritic tree is a rough map of how much input a cell expects and where it accepts it. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-dendrites|Organization of Cell Types]] [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-spines|Organization of Cell Types]]
>
> **The glial complement.** Glia provide scaffolding, insulation, nutrient transport, waste handling and immune mediation, and current estimates put the ratio at roughly one glial cell per neuron rather than the far larger ratios of older literature. Astrocytes influence the extracellular environment and barrier properties, oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells produce myelin, microglia survey the parenchyma continuously, and ependymal cells line the ventricular system. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]] [[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-surveillance|Microglial surveillance]]
>
> **Why the 1:1 ratio matters.** Numeric claims about glia are used rhetorically to argue that neurons are a minority of the tissue and that glia must therefore be doing more than support. The corrected ratio removes the arithmetic premise while leaving the substantive point intact: glial cells are required for neurons to work, and several populations respond to activity rather than merely tolerating it. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-support|Blood Brain Barrier]]
>
> **Where the division becomes misleading.** Treating 'neuron' and 'glia' as two kinds of cell obscures the specialised subtypes within each - pyramidal cells, interneurons, motoneurons; protoplasmic and fibrous astrocytes; surveillant, reactive and disease-associated microglia. Functional claims should name the subtype where the evidence does, and treat family-level statements as generalisations. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-compartments|Organization of Cell Types]] [[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-limit|Microglial surveillance]]

> [!info]- Reference: the cell types and what each one does
> **Neurons.** A neuron is a cell specialised for receiving, integrating and transmitting signals. Four compartments carry most of that work. Dendrites branch to collect input and are often studded with spines; the soma houses the nucleus and most protein synthesis; the axon carries a signal away from the soma and can extend a metre or more; and the presynaptic terminal converts an electrical signal into chemical release. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-dendrites|Cell types]] Dendritic spines are not decoration: they contain cytoskeletal elements responsible for the shape changes seen in some forms of plasticity, which is one reason spine density is treated as a structural correlate of learning rather than a synonym for it. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-spines|Cell types]]
>
> **Glia.** Glial populations are functionally distinct. Astrocytes contact synapses and blood vessels and help regulate the extracellular environment and barrier properties; oligodendrocytes myelinate central axons; microglia survey the parenchyma continuously and respond to damage; ependymal cells line the ventricles. The textbook summary that glia provide scaffolding, insulation, nutrient transport and immune mediation is a fair high-level description, but each of those verbs belongs to a different cell type with its own signalling. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-support|Blood Brain Barrier]]
>
> **The 'resting' glia correction.** Microglia in uninjured cortex are not quiescent. Two-photon imaging showed them continually extending and retracting processes to survey their surroundings, and barrier disruption switched them within minutes from surveillance to shielding the affected site. [[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-surveillance|Microglial surveillance]] [[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-response|Microglial surveillance]] Any account that treats glia as passive support is describing an older textbook picture.
>
> **Why the ratio claim needs care.** Neuron-to-glia ratios vary by region and species, and the frequently quoted 'roughly 1:1' figure is an average, not a constant. Repeating it as if it held everywhere is a small error that becomes large when it is used to argue that glia cannot be doing much.

> [!info]- Reference: how the parts cooperate
> The compartments of a neuron are functionally coupled: transmitter release depends on calcium entry at the terminal, which depends on a spike arriving, which depends on summed dendritic input reaching threshold at the axon initial segment. [[F51 OpenStax Function of nervous tissue#^f51-summation|Function of Nervous Tissue]] Glia sit inside that loop rather than beside it, since astrocytes influence extracellular ion and transmitter concentrations that determine how effectively a synapse transmits. [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-transport|Blood Brain Barrier]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Cell types are the biological substrate the neuroanatomy and neuroendocrine-neuroimmune domains describe at tissue scale - microglia here are the cells whose immune signalling that domain describes, and the excitatory/inhibitory cells here are the ones whose balance other domains discuss. ## Limitation or common misconception

Textbook diagrams show clean arrows; real cells have thousands of inputs and glia that respond on their own timescales.
