# Pharmacodynamics and receptors

**Definition.** Pharmacodynamics describes what a drug does to the body, usually through receptors, enzymes, transporters or ion channels. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How Do Medicines Work?]]

## Supported claims

- Drug targets include receptors, enzymes, transporters and ion channels. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How Do Medicines Work?]]
- Agonists mimic or enhance a signal while antagonists block it, and one target can appear in many tissues. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-agonism|How Do Medicines Work?]]
- Nicotinic receptors depolarise while muscarinic receptors can do either, depending on subtype. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-cholinergic|Communication Between Neurons]]

> [!info]- In depth: the four ways a drug can act at a receptor, and what follows
> **The action categories.** Agonists mimic or enhance the endogenous signal; partial agonists produce a submaximal response even at full occupancy; antagonists block without producing a response; and allosteric modulators change how the receptor responds to the endogenous ligand. The categories predict different behaviour at different levels of endogenous transmitter, which is the practical reason they are distinguished. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-agonism|How do medicines work]] [[Receptor families and second messengers]]
>
> **State dependence follows from modulation.** Benzodiazepines enhance GABA-A receptor function rather than activating it directly, so their effect scales with how much endogenous GABA is present - which is why their effects vary with arousal state and dose. Generalising from a modulator to a direct agonist misstates the mechanism. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-mechanism|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]] [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-effects|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]]
>
> **Off-target binding shapes the profile.** Most psychoactive drugs bind several receptor families with different affinities, so side effects often follow from targets other than the intended one; antipsychotic motor and metabolic effects, for example, are attributed across dopaminergic, serotonergic, histaminergic, cholinergic and adrenergic binding rather than to D2 blockade alone. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-offtarget|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]
>
> **Reading rule.** A pharmacodynamic claim should name the receptor, the mode of action and the endogenous context; the same molecule can be an agonist at one target and an antagonist at another. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-limits|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]

> [!info]- Reference: targets, binding and the gap to clinical effect
> **Targets.** Pharmacodynamics describes what a drug does to the body, usually by acting on receptors, enzymes, transporters or channels. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How do medicines work]]
>
> **Agonism and antagonism.** Agonists mimic or enhance a signal, antagonists block it, and the same target appears in many tissues, which is why specificity matters. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-agonism|How do medicines work]]
>
> **Occupancy and effect.** Receptor occupancy is measurable but does not map directly onto clinical effect. For the dopamine-blocking antipsychotics, response and several side effects occupy overlapping ranges of D2 blockade, which is why the field describes a window rather than a target to maximise; the effects themselves arise in different pathways, so occupancy names the receptor event rather than the clinical consequence. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-prolactin|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]]
>
> **Off-target effects.** Binding at serotonergic, histaminergic, cholinergic and adrenergic receptors contributes to sedation, weight gain and dysphoria, which is why side-effect profiles differ between drugs of the same class. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-offtarget|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]
>
> **Adaptation.** Repeated action at a target produces receptor-level changes, including desensitisation and trafficking, which is one basis for tolerance. [[P25566076 Allouche 2014 Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance#^p25566076-mechanism|Opioid receptor desensitization and tolerance]]
>
> **What each action category predicts.** The four modes of action at a receptor carry different predictions: agonists produce the endogenous effect scaled by occupancy; partial agonists cap the response below the endogenous maximum and can reduce it when endogenous tone is high; antagonists shift the effect toward whatever endogenous signalling exists; allosteric modulators change the receptor's response to its own transmitter. Predicting a drug's effect therefore requires knowing the mode, the endogenous tone and the receptor distribution - three facts, not one drug name. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-agonism|How Do Medicines Work?]]
>
> **Why state dependence is not a footnote.** Benzodiazepines potentiate GABA-A receptors rather than opening the channel themselves, so their effect scales with endogenous GABA: the same dose does more where inhibitory tone is high and less where it is low. This is the general pattern for modulators, and it is why the same medicine can look weak in one state and strong in another, and why "how much drug" is never the whole exposure question for drugs that modulate rather than drive. [[Pharmacodynamics and receptors]] [[Receptor adaptation tolerance and dependence|Receptor adaptation, tolerance and dependence]]
>
> **The occupancy-effect gap, named once and kept.** Receptor occupancy is the most measurable link in the chain from dose to outcome, and the antipsychotic case shows it is not the clinical link: response and side effects occupy overlapping ranges of D2 blockade, so occupancy predicts where effects become possible, not which effects a person gets. Every chain beyond occupancy - circuit state, adaptation, placebo context, measurement - is studied separately, and mechanism-only claims skip those links silently. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Neurochemistry supplies the endogenous signalling that pharmacodynamics modulates: a receptor account without its transmitter's state is half a mechanism, which is why the two domains' articles are written as complements rather than summaries of each other. ## Limitation or common misconception

Receptor language is precise about molecules and imprecise about experience, because downstream effects depend on circuit state and time course.
