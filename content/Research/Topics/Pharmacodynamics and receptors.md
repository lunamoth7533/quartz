# Pharmacodynamics and receptors

**Definition.** Pharmacodynamics describes what a drug does to the body, usually through receptors, enzymes, transporters or ion channels. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How Do Medicines Work?]]

## Supported claims

- Drug targets include receptors, enzymes, transporters and ion channels. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How Do Medicines Work?]]
- Agonists mimic or enhance a signal while antagonists block it, and one target can appear in many tissues. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-agonism|How Do Medicines Work?]]
- Nicotinic receptors depolarise while muscarinic receptors can do either, depending on subtype. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-cholinergic|Communication Between Neurons]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Receptor language is precise about molecules and imprecise about experience, because downstream effects depend on circuit state and time course.
