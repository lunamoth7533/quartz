# CBT for adult ADHD

**Definition.** Cognitive behavioural approaches for adult ADHD target practical skills, planning and the thought patterns that interact with attention difficulties. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

## Supported claims

- CBT with medication was more effective than medication alone for core symptoms in a Cochrane review. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]
- The authors rated the evidence low-quality, limited by short follow-up, heterogeneous outcomes and geography. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]
- Secondary outcomes such as self-reported depression and anxiety improved in places, while quality-of-life results varied. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-secondary|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

> [!info]- In depth: skills-based therapy for adults, and the certainty attached to it
> **What the intervention is.** CBT for adult ADHD is a skills-based approach that addresses the practical and cognitive consequences of attentional and executive difficulties - planning, task initiation, time management and the beliefs that accumulate around repeated failure. In the trials it is usually delivered as an adjunct, which is why the pooled results are about adding therapy to medication. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-nonpharm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]
>
> **The pooled results, with their comparator.** Adding CBT to medication beat medication alone on clinician-rated and self-rated core symptoms, and CBT also beat a waiting list on self-reported symptoms. The estimate against supportive psychotherapy was not statistically significant, which is the comparator that controls best for attention and contact time. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]]
>
> **Certainty and rater effects.** Certainty was low or very low for most comparisons because trials were small and outcome measures heterogeneous, and outcome depended on the rater - clinician-reported and self-reported results differed. In the broader component synthesis, psychological components were better than placebo only on clinician-reported measures. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI appraisal: Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-nonpharm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]
>
> **Secondary outcomes.** Some secondary outcomes improved, including self-reported depression and anxiety, while quality-of-life results varied - a reminder that a symptom-scale result and a life-quality result are separate claims. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-secondary|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Report the comparator, the rater and the certainty; a component analysis of average effects does not show that an intervention cannot help an individual, and missing evidence for an outcome is not evidence of no benefit. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-caution-absence|AI appraisal: Comparative interventions for ADHD]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-domains|International Classification of Functioning]]

> [!info]- Reference: skills-based therapy in adult ADHD
> **Target and design.** Cognitive behavioural protocols for adult ADHD work on practical skills, planning and the thought patterns that interact with attention difficulties, and they are tested in randomised trials against control conditions or as an addition to medication. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]
>
> **Comparator matters.** In the Cochrane review the estimates depended on the comparator: large against waiting list and not statistically significant against supportive psychotherapy, which is why 'does CBT work' is under-specified without naming the alternative. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]
>
> **Rater matters.** Clinician-reported and self-reported outcomes can disagree, so a benefit claim should name who rated the outcome. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI synthesis: CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]
>
> **Wider adult evidence.** The 2025 adult synthesis found non-pharmacological interventions better than placebo only on clinician-reported measures, and medications were not efficacious on additional outcomes such as quality of life. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-nonpharm|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]
>
> **Framework.** The treatment model itself - thoughts, behaviour and feelings interacting, with practice between sessions - comes from the general psychotherapy literature. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-psychotherapy|Types of Treatment]] [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-behavioural|Types of Treatment]]
>
> **Limits.** Short follow-up, heterogeneous outcome measures and restricted geography leave durability and functional gain unresolved. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

> [!info]- Further depth: a skills-based adjunct and its comparators
> Adult CBT addresses the practical and cognitive consequences of attentional difficulties - planning, initiation, time management and the beliefs that accumulate around repeated failure - and in the trials it is delivered alongside medication rather than instead of it. Adding CBT to medication beat medication alone on clinician- and self-rated core symptoms, and it beat a waiting list on self-reported symptoms, while the comparison against supportive psychotherapy was not statistically significant; secondary outcomes including self-reported depression and anxiety improved, and quality-of-life results varied. Certainty was low or very low because trials were small and outcome measures heterogeneous. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-secondary|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-nonpharm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The measurement of what CBT changes for adult ADHD is a functioning-and-outcomes question, so the endpoint chosen matters as much as the effect size - an instrument that does not index real-world function is measuring something else, which is the measurement-validity issue the methods domain treats as a first-order constraint. ## Limitation or common misconception

Low-certainty, short-term evidence with varied outcome measures leaves durability and functional gains unresolved.
