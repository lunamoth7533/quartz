# Seizures and epilepsy

## Definition

A seizure is an episode of abnormal excessive brain activity. Epilepsy, per the ILAE 2014 practical definition, is a disease of the brain defined by any of: at least two unprovoked (or reflex) seizures more than 24 hours apart; one unprovoked (or reflex) seizure with a recurrence risk over the next 10 years similar to the general two-seizure recurrence risk (at least 60%); or a diagnosis of an epilepsy syndrome. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-definition|Epilepsy]] [[F130 Fisher 2014 ILAE epilepsy definition#^f130-criteria|Fisher 2014 ILAE]]

## How it works

**Causes and triggers.** Causes include head injury, stroke, infection, genetic factors and metabolic disturbance, and the same seizure type can arise from different causes. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-triggers|Epilepsy]] Regional excitability is central, which is why excitation-inhibition ideas appear in both basic and clinical accounts. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-inhibitory|Amino acid transmitters]]

**Evaluation.** First seizure assessment involves specialist referral, history, examination and investigations that include EEG and imaging where indicated. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]]

**Treatment.** Antiseizure medication is chosen by seizure type and syndrome, with surgery or other interventions considered for specific situations; the guideline also lists research recommendations where evidence is insufficient. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-treatment|Epilepsies NG217]] [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-research|Epilepsies NG217]]

**Overlap with psychiatric practice.** A medication history matters when neurological and psychiatric care overlap. General interaction categories explain why one treatment can alter another's exposure or effects; whether a particular drug changes seizure risk requires its specific evidence and prescribing information. A class label alone does not establish that risk or an individual's response. [[F19 FDA Drug interactions what you should know#^f19-types|Drug interaction categories]] [[Drug classes and mechanisms overview]]

## Evidence and status

Seizure classification and drug efficacy by seizure type are established; prognosis after a first seizure is variable and depends on cause and findings. This note is reference material, not guidance for any individual.

> [!info]- In depth: abnormal activity, its triggers, and how a diagnosis is assembled
> **The definition.** Epilepsy is described as a brain disorder characterised by an enduring predisposition to generate epileptic seizures. The ILAE 2014 practical definition operationalises that predisposition through three routes: two or more unprovoked seizures more than 24 hours apart; one unprovoked seizure with a ≥60% ten-year recurrence risk; or a diagnosed epilepsy syndrome. That is why a single seizure with low recurrence risk is not the same clinical problem as epilepsy, while a single seizure with a documented high recurrence risk or a diagnosed syndrome can be. [[F130 Fisher 2014 ILAE epilepsy definition#^f130-criteria|ILAE practical definition]]
>
> **What is associated with seizures.** The listed conditions and events include head injury, stroke, infection, genetic factors and metabolic causes - a heterogeneous list that is precisely why assessment looks for a cause rather than assuming one. Treatment is described as medication first for many people, with surgery, devices and dietary approaches used in specific situations; the page links out rather than advising individuals. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-triggers|Epilepsy]] [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-management|Epilepsy]]
>
> **The diagnostic pathway is staged.** National guidance covers referral and diagnostic pathways, including the role of specialist assessment after a first suspected seizure, and it recommends specific antiseizure medicines by seizure type and syndrome and sets out when surgery or other interventions are considered. The organising concept is syndrome: the same event pattern in different syndromes implies different treatment logic. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]]
>
> **EEG's role and its limits.** EEG measures the brain's electrical activity through scalp electrodes, reporting frequency and amplitude with millisecond timing; its spatial precision is limited compared with structural imaging, but EEG can contribute to localising epileptiform activity and seizure onset in clinical evaluation, particularly when combined with video monitoring or invasive recording. It is a functional rather than structural measure, which is why it complements rather than replaces imaging. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-eeg|Brain Imaging]] [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological diagnostic tests]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Name the event type, the syndrome if known, the investigations performed and the treatment phase being discussed. Seizure classification drives drug choice in the guidance, so a claim that names no seizure type has lost the detail that makes it actionable. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]
>
> **An event, a predisposition and a syndrome are different levels.** A seizure is an event, whereas epilepsy denotes an enduring predisposition operationalised by the ILAE definition. A first unprovoked seizure can satisfy that definition in specified high-risk circumstances; a syndrome diagnosis is another route. Classification and assessment therefore cannot be reduced to counting events. An acute symptomatic seizure prompts investigation of the provoking condition, while longer-term decisions also depend on the underlying disease, recurrence risk and the person's circumstances. The guideline organizes assessment and treatment by seizure type and syndrome rather than prescribing one response to every event. [[F130 Fisher 2014 ILAE epilepsy definition#^f130-criteria|ILAE practical definition]] [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|NICE epilepsy guidance]]
>
> **The excitation-inhibition link is real and specific.** Seizures are disruptions of the balance between excitatory and inhibitory signalling, and many antiseizure drugs act by enhancing inhibition (potentiating GABA) or reducing excitation (blocking sodium channels or glutamate receptors) - the clinical territory where the basic E/I framework earns its keep. The caveat from the autism literature travels here: balance language is meaningful at the circuit level with named mechanisms, not as a general explanation of any condition. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-inhibitory|Amino acid transmitters]] [[Excitation and inhibition balance]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Pharmacology meets epilepsy at shared mechanisms: psychotropics that lower seizure threshold and antiseizure drugs with psychiatric effects are the practical case for the interaction article's dynamic-interaction category.
