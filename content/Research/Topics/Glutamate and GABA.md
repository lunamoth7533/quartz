# Glutamate and GABA

**Definition.** Glutamate and GABA are the main excitatory and inhibitory amino-acid transmitters of the adult central nervous system. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]

## Supported claims

- Glutamate receptors depolarise adult postsynaptic cells while glycine and GABA receptors hyperpolarise them. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]
- Autism risk-gene expression was enriched in excitatory and inhibitory lineages, consistent with multiple imbalance paths. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing study]]

> [!info]- In depth: the two-channel account, its synthesis cycle and its limits
> **Who does what.** Excitatory transmission producing EPSPs is mediated largely by glutamate; inhibitory transmission is mediated primarily by GABA in the brain and glycine in the spinal cord. Because GABA is itself a glutamate metabolite, the two channels are metabolically linked rather than independent systems, and the enzymes that interconvert them are a pharmacological target. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-excitatory|Amino Acid Transmitters]] [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-inhibitory|Amino Acid Transmitters]]
>
> **Recycling between cell types.** Glutamate released at synapses is cleared by transporters and re-supplied to neurons largely through the glutamine cycle, in which a glial enzyme converts it to glutamine that neurons take up and convert back. This is the clearest case in the transmitter world where the recycling step is a shared tissue function rather than a presynaptic property, and it explains why astrocytic function shows up in excitatory transmission claims. [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-support|Blood Brain Barrier]] [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-requirements|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]]
>
> **Receptor families with opposite kinetics.** Glutamate acts through ionotropic receptors that depolarise and through metabotropic receptors that modulate; GABA acts through ionotropic receptors that hyperpolarise and metabotropic receptors that act more slowly. The result is that each transmitter can produce fast and slow effects in the same tissue, and drugs that target one family leave the other intact. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]
>
> **From molecules to a balance claim.** Because the same transmitter can act at receptors of opposite sign and because inhibitory contacts differ in where they land, an E/I statement requires a level of description: single cell, local circuit or network. The chapter's own caveat is that the canonical excitatory/inhibitory division is a teaching frame that does not cover every signalling role of these amino acids. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-spatial|Amino Acid Transmitters]] [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-limit|Amino Acid Transmitters]]
>
> **Where pharmacology sits.** Benzodiazepines act as positive modulators at GABA-A receptors, enhancing inhibitory signalling rather than activating the receptor directly, whereas most psychoactive drugs are receptor or transporter ligands. The distinction between an agonist and an allosteric modulator is not cosmetic: the modulator's effect depends on how much endogenous transmitter is present, which is why its effects vary with state. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-mechanism|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]] [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How do medicines work]]

> [!info]- Reference: the two-channel account and its metabolic coupling
> **The pair.** Glutamate carries most fast excitatory transmission and GABA most fast inhibitory transmission in the brain; glycine carries inhibition in the spinal cord. Together they set the moment-to-moment excitatory/inhibitory state of a circuit. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-excitatory|Amino acid transmitters]] [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-inhibitory|Amino acid transmitters]]
>
> **Metabolic coupling.** Glutamate is the precursor of GABA and the two are linked through the tricarboxylic acid cycle, so manipulating one pathway can change the other. [[P12467378 Petroff 2002 GABA and glutamate#^p12467378-metabolism|GABA and glutamate]] GABA synthesis depends on two isoforms of its rate-controlling enzyme, and human brain has unusually high concentrations of two GABA metabolites with anticonvulsant properties [[P12467378 Petroff 2002 GABA and glutamate#^p12467378-metabolism|GABA and glutamate]] [[P12467378 Petroff 2002 GABA and glutamate#^p12467378-human|GABA and glutamate]] - one of several ways in which human GABA metabolism differs from the standard animal model.
>
> **Spatial logic.** Inhibitory synapses are placed to control specific inputs and the site of generation of the spike, not simply to subtract excitation globally. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-spatial|Amino acid transmitters]]
>
> **Receptor pharmacology.** Both transmitters act on ionotropic and metabotropic receptors, and the metabotropic receptors are current drug targets, which is why the pair matters far beyond electrophysiology. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]] Benzodiazepines, for example, enhance GABA-A receptor function rather than activating the receptor directly. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-mechanism|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]]

> [!info]- Further depth: the excitatory/inhibitory pair as one system
> Excitatory transmission producing EPSPs is mediated largely by glutamate, while inhibitory transmission is mediated primarily by glycine in the spinal cord and by GABA, a glutamate metabolite, in the brain - and the two are linked metabolically through the tricarboxylic acid cycle, which is why they are treated as one system. Cortical excitability is framed as a balance between the two, and human GABA metabolism is distinctive, with metabolites such as homocarnosine occurring at unusually high concentration and having anticonvulsant properties. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-excitatory|Amino Acid Transmitters]] [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-inhibitory|Amino Acid Transmitters]] [[P12467378 Petroff 2002 GABA and glutamate#^p12467378-metabolism|GABA and glutamate]] [[P12467378 Petroff 2002 GABA and glutamate#^p12467378-human|GABA and glutamate]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Excitatory/inhibitory balance is the physiological substrate that the neuroanatomy and computational-brain-theories domains use when they describe network arithmetic; a change in glutamate/GABA ratio at the cellular level can only be translated into a network-level claim once the four E/I-balance meanings described elsewhere are kept distinct. ## Limitation or common misconception

Excitation and inhibition are often described in broad terms; local measurement is difficult and findings do not transfer across conditions.
