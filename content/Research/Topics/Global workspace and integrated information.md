# Global workspace and integrated information

## Definition

Global workspace theory holds that information becomes conscious when it is broadcast widely across the brain. Integrated information theory starts from properties of experience and proposes that consciousness corresponds to irreducible causal structure.

## How it works

**Global workspace.** Access to a shared workspace makes information available to many systems, and this availability is what the theory identifies with conscious access. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-claim|Integrated information theory]]

**Integrated information.** IIT 3.0 begins with phenomenological axioms and translates them into postulates, defining quantities intended to measure irreducible causal structure. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-axioms|Integrated information theory]] [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-phi|Integrated information theory]]

**Contrasting commitments.** Function-based theories locate consciousness in what a system does; causal-structure theories locate it in how the system is wired, making claims such as feedforward systems not being conscious. [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-target|The unfolding argument]]

**Critique.** The unfolding argument uses computability results to claim causal-structure theories are either false or unfalsifiable, because behaviour can be produced by structures that differ in consciousness-relevant ways. [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-argument|The unfolding argument]]

## Evidence and status

Both theories generate predictions and remain under active test; neither is established, and the disagreement is partly about what would count as a decisive experiment. [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-debate|The unfolding argument]]

> [!info]- In depth: two theories with committed claims, and a critique aimed at both
> **Integrated information theory's structure.** IIT begins with phenomenological axioms about experience and translates them into postulates, and it defines quantities - notably integrated information - intended to measure how much a system's causal structure contributes to its own state. It is a mathematical theory with committed claims, including that feedforward systems cannot be conscious. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-axioms|Integrated information theory]] [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-phi|Integrated information theory]] [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-claim|Integrated information theory]]
>
> **The unfolding argument.** A theoretical critique targets theories that identify consciousness with causal structure, such as IIT and recurrent-processing accounts, and uses results from computability theory to argue such theories are either false or unfalsifiable. It is a contribution to an ongoing debate rather than a finding that settles which theory is correct. [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-target|The unfolding argument]] [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-argument|The unfolding argument]] [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-debate|The unfolding argument]] [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-limit|The unfolding argument]]
>
> **The comparison problem is general.** A framework that accommodates many theories can be difficult to falsify in specific cases, and the same breadth critique applies to predictive-processing accounts; the consciousness debate is a sharp case of a problem that recurs across this domain. [[P20068583 Friston 2010 Free-energy principle#^p20068583-status|The free-energy principle]] [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-plural|Predictive coding algorithms]]
>
> **What would count as progress.** Theories with committed claims make risky predictions that can exclude them; where theories share predictions, the discriminating experiment is the one that distinguishes them rather than the one that confirms either. That is the standard the model-comparison literature sets for behavioural models. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-limit|Integrated information theory]] [[P31769410 Wilson 2019 Computational modelling rules#^p31769410-identifiability|Computational modelling rules]]

> [!info]- Further depth: committed claims and the falsifiability critique
> IIT begins from phenomenological axioms and translates them into postulates, defines integrated information as a measure of how much a system's causal structure contributes to its own state, and commits to claims such as that feedforward systems cannot be conscious. A critique using computability results argues that theories identifying consciousness with causal structure are either false or unfalsifiable, and it is a contribution to an ongoing debate rather than a settlement. Where theories share predictions, the informative study is the one that excludes at least one account. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-axioms|Integrated information theory]] [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-phi|Integrated information theory]] [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-claim|Integrated information theory]] [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-argument|The unfolding argument]] [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-debate|The unfolding argument]]
>
> **Where the theories genuinely disagree.** The disagreement is not about data but about what would count as the explanandum: global-workspace-style theories explain conscious access - why some content becomes available to report, memory and control - while causal-structure theories aim at consciousness itself as a property of organised matter, whatever it does. The unfolding argument presses the structural side with a computability result: behaviour alone cannot fix the causal structure, so either the structural theory reaches beyond evidence or its claims are not testable against behaviour. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-axioms|Integrated information theory]] [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-argument|The unfolding argument]]
>
> **Why both remain useful in a reference library.** Workspace ideas organise findings about attention, report and cognitive access; structural ideas organise questions about what physical organisation could support experience. A reader benefits from both as organising frameworks while noting that neither currently delivers a clinical or diagnostic instrument, and that measures proposed by each are theory-laden in exactly the way the comparison article describes. [[Theories of consciousness compared]] [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-phi|Integrated information theory]]
