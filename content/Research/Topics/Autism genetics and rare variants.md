# Autism genetics and rare variants

**Definition.** Autism genetics includes rare variants of larger effect and common variants of small effect, converging on genes involved in development and neuronal communication. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-genes|Autism exome sequencing study]]

## Supported claims

- A 35,584-sample exome study identified 102 risk genes, mostly expressed early in brain development. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-genes|Autism exome sequencing study]]
- Forty-nine genes were enriched in developmental delay and 53 in autism, with phenotypic differences. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-subgroups|Autism exome sequencing study]]
- Risk-gene expression was enriched in excitatory and inhibitory lineages, consistent with multiple imbalance paths. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing study]]
- Finding a risk gene does not determine a person's traits or support needs. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-caution-risk|AI synthesis: Autism exome sequencing study]]

> [!info]- In depth: rare variation, convergence on development and the individual gap
> **What the exome analysis found.** The analysis identified 102 risk genes at a false discovery rate of 0.1 or less; most act on gene regulation or neuronal communication and are expressed early in development, and risk-gene expression was enriched in excitatory and inhibitory lineages, which the authors call consistent with multiple paths to an imbalance. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-genes|Autism exome sequencing]] [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing]]
>
> **Ascertainment shapes the gene list.** Forty-nine genes were enriched in people ascertained for severe developmental delay and 53 in people ascertained for autism, with phenotypic differences between the groups - so the same underlying architecture surfaces differently depending on who is studied. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-subgroups|Autism exome sequencing]]
>
> **What kind of claim this is.** It is a genetic association study using rare de novo and case-control variation that estimates population-level risk contributions; the library's own caution notes that finding a risk gene does not determine an individual's traits, strengths or support needs. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-limit|Autism exome sequencing]] [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-caution-risk|AI appraisal: Autism exome sequencing]]
>
> **Common variation contributes too.** Complex traits are highly polygenic and credible signals require very large samples, so rare-variant findings describe one part of the architecture; the two literatures converge on genes and pathways rather than on individual prediction. [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-polygenic|10 years of GWAS discovery]] [[F67 NHGRI GWAS fact sheet#^f67-scale|GWAS fact sheet]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Name the variant class, the ascertainment scheme and the level of claim. A risk-gene list is a statement about a sample's genetics, not a description of an autistic person. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-caution-risk|AI appraisal: Autism exome sequencing]] [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-caution-prediction|AI appraisal: Genomics of bipolar disorder]]

> [!info]- Reference: common and rare contributions side by side
> **Two architectures.** Autism genetics involves both common variants of small effect, detectable through association studies, and rare variants of larger effect, including de novo mutations detectable through sequencing. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing]]
>
> **What exome studies show.** Large sequencing studies implicate genes enriched for synaptic and regulatory functions, and identify a substantial set of risk genes, while noting that most individual cases are not explained by a single variant. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-genes|Autism exome sequencing]]
>
> **Interpretation boundaries.** Finding a risk variant is not a diagnosis, a prognosis or a mechanism: variants vary in penetrance, and the same variant can appear in people with different presentations. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-limit|Autism exome sequencing]] [[P29844615 Schaid 2018 Fine-mapping#^p29844615-limits|Fine-mapping]]
>
> **Common-variant contribution.** Where family and twin designs suggest partly shared genetic influence across conditions, the mechanism is not a shared single gene but overlapping polygenic architecture. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-shared|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]
>
> **Practical framing.** Genetics research in autism is best read as identifying biological pathways and improving understanding of heterogeneity - not as establishing that any individual's autism was caused by a specific gene. [[F66 NHGRI Genomics fact sheet#^f66-applications|Genomics fact sheet]]
>
> **Penetrance and variable expression are the rule, not a footnote.** A risk variant raises the probability of a particular developmental profile without fixing it: carriers of the same variant differ in whether they meet diagnostic criteria at all, in language level, in co-occurring conditions and in support needs. That is why a risk-gene list characterises a sample's biology while saying almost nothing about any carrier's presentation, and why genetic findings feed research stratification and counselling conversations rather than diagnostic checklists. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-limit|Autism exome sequencing]] [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-caution-risk|AI appraisal: Autism exome sequencing]]
>
> **Why de novo and inherited variants answer different questions.** De novo variants - present in the child but neither parent - are visible in sequencing as striking, recent events whose effects can be large precisely because they are rare; inherited variants contribute the small-effect, common-variant architecture that association studies detect. The two together explain why family recurrence patterns, twin heritability and exome gene lists describe the same population from different angles without any one of them accounting for most individual cases. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing]] [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-polygenic|10 years of GWAS discovery]]
>
> **Convergence on regulation and communication is a pathway claim.** The genes found across studies cluster less by protein family than by function: regulation of gene expression and neuronal communication appear repeatedly, and expression is enriched in cell lineages that produce both excitatory and inhibitory neurons. That pattern motivates circuit-level hypotheses - including balance hypotheses tested elsewhere in this library - without identifying which alteration in which circuit produces which features for which person. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing]] [[Excitation and inhibition balance]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The genetics domain supplies the designs and the condition domain supplies the constructs: an exome finding becomes autism-relevant only through how the sample was ascertained, which is why the same gene list splits into developmental-delay-enriched and autism-enriched subsets when ascertainment differs. ## Limitation or common misconception

Genetic findings describe population-level risk contributions, and pathway stories need convergent evidence.
