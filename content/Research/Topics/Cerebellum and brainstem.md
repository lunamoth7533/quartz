# Cerebellum and brainstem

## Definition

The cerebellum is a highly structured cortical structure attached to the brain stem, and the brain stem contains the cranial nerve nuclei, long ascending and descending tracts, and the core autonomic and arousal systems.

## How it works

**Cerebellar cortex.** Three layers - granule cell, Purkinje cell and molecular - with massive convergence: each parallel fibre contacts many Purkinje cells and each Purkinje cell receives enormous input. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-layers|Cerebellum]] [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-circuit|Cerebellum]]

**Learning.** Climbing-fibre and parallel-fibre input support forms of plasticity that develop over days, which is why the cerebellum is treated as a learning structure as well as a coordinator. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-learning|Cerebellum]]

**Brain stem.** The brain stem carries cranial nerve nuclei and long tracts, and central autonomic control originates in interconnected brain-stem and hypothalamic cell groups that also connect with somatic, endocrine and limbic circuitry. [[F63 Neuroscience Online central autonomic control#^f63-network|Central Control of the Autonomic Nervous System]] [[F31 OpenStax The peripheral nervous system#^f31-cranial|The Peripheral Nervous System]]

## Evidence and status

Cerebellar microcircuitry is among the best-described in neuroscience, largely from animal physiology; brain-stem anatomy is established from clinical and anatomical correlation. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]

> [!info]- In depth: a layered computer and a column of nuclei
> **Cerebellar architecture.** The cerebellum has a stereotyped layered organisation - molecular, Purkinje and granular layers - with a single output cell type, the Purkinje cell, and two main input systems: mossy fibres relayed through granule cells and climbing fibres that wrap Purkinje cells directly. That regularity, with a huge number of very similar units, is why it is described as a computational structure rather than a collection of nuclei. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-layers|Cerebellum]] [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-circuit|Cerebellum]]
>
> **What it computes.** The classical function is error-based adjustment of ongoing movement: climbing-fibre input carries a teaching signal, and the circuit adjusts gain accordingly, which is why cerebellar learning is described as supervised rather than reinforcement-based. Beyond movement, the same architecture appears in vestibular, oculomotor and cognitive loops, so the structure is best described by its computation rather than by a single output domain. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-learning|Cerebellum]] [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-scope|Dopamine reward prediction error coding]]
>
> **Brainstem organisation.** The brainstem contains cranial nerve nuclei, long ascending and descending tracts, and the nuclei that generate and gate arousal and autonomic outflow. Its organising principle is vertical: functions are arranged along the neuraxis from spinal cord to midbrain rather than in one map, which is why small lesions can produce combinations of cranial nerve, long-tract and autonomic findings. [[F05 OpenStax The central nervous system#^f05-parts|The Central Nervous System]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]
>
> **Clinical translation.** Because the brainstem carries both nuclei and tracts in a small volume, its lesions produce crossed and mixed patterns that localisation reasoning uses to infer level. This is the clearest example in the domain where anatomical knowledge directly constrains clinical inference. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[Differential reasoning in neurology]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Cerebellar and brainstem claims should distinguish the structure's intrinsic computation from the function of the loop it participates in; a finding about one loop does not transfer to others. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-limit|Cerebellum]]

> [!info]- Further depth: two structures, two logics, one exam page
> The cerebellar cortex has three layers - granule cell, Purkinje cell and molecular - with Purkinje cells forming a single-cell-thick layer and projecting inhibitory output to deep nuclei; that anatomical regularity with massive input convergence is why the cerebellum became a model system for circuit computation. The brain stem carries long tracts between cord and forebrain and houses cranial nerve nuclei, respiratory and cardiovascular centres and the ascending arousal system, with reflexes and autonomic integrations happening there before information reaches cortex. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-layers|Cerebellum]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-brainstem|Central Processing]]
>
> The exam tests coordination separately from motor strength and localises cranial nerve findings to brain-stem levels, so the two structures appear in the same examination domains while subserving unrelated functions. Introductory diagrams simplify: parallel channels, feedback and convergence are not represented. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-limit|Central Processing]]
>
> **The uniform microcircuit is the theoretical clue.** The cerebellum repeats the same three-layer module with the same two input systems across a huge number of units, which suggests a single computation applied to many domains rather than many specialisations in one structure. The error-based learning account - climbing-fibre signals as teaching input that adjusts Purkinje output - fits that shape, and it explains why the same architecture serves eye movements, vestibular calibration, gait and, in imaging work, cognitive sequences. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-circuit|Cerebellum]] [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-learning|Cerebellum]]
>
> **Brainstem lesions localise by combination.** Because cranial nerve nuclei sit alongside long tracts in a small volume, a brainstem lesion produces crossed patterns - cranial nerve findings on one side, body findings on the other - whose combination identifies the level. That combination logic is the brainstem's signature contribution to neurological localisation, and it is why brainstem anatomy is taught vertical-column-first. [[F05 OpenStax The central nervous system#^f05-parts|The Central Nervous System]] [[Differential reasoning in neurology]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The central autonomic network that this article shares with the interoception article puts the brainstem inside the neuroendocrine domain's regulation stories - arousal, visceral control and hypothalamic coupling all run through brainstem cell groups - so brainstem anatomy is common infrastructure for three domains rather than a neurology monopoly.
