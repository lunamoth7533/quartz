# Acetylcholine signalling

**Definition.** Acetylcholine mediates signalling at neuromuscular junctions, in autonomic ganglia and in the brain, where it contributes to arousal, memory and cognition. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-transmitters|Cells of the Nervous System]]

## Supported claims

- The chapter associates acetylcholine with muscle action and memory. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-transmitters|Cells of the Nervous System]]
- Cholinergic synapses include the neuromuscular junction; nicotinic receptors depolarise while muscarinic receptors can depolarise or hyperpolarise depending on subtype. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-cholinergic|Communication Between Neurons]]

## How it works

**Where it acts.** Acetylcholine is released at the neuromuscular junction, at autonomic ganglia, at parasympathetic target organs and by central pathways. The best-characterised central projection runs from the nucleus basalis of Meynert in the basal forebrain to the neocortex and limbic structures, which is why cholinergic signalling is discussed in the context of cortical activation and attention rather than only in the periphery. [[F108 Neuroscience Online acetylcholine#^f108-pathways|Acetylcholine]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-cholinergic|Communication Between Neurons]]

**Two receptor families decide the sign and the timescale.** Nicotinic receptors are ionotropic and depolarise their target directly, which is why they dominate at the neuromuscular junction and at autonomic ganglia where speed matters. Muscarinic receptors are metabotropic and act through second messengers, producing slower and longer-lasting changes in excitability and cellular state, including at cardiac and glandular targets. At the neuromuscular junction nicotinic receptors turn a motoneuron spike into muscle contraction in milliseconds; in the cortex, cholinergic terminals from the nucleus basalis adjust the excitability of whole networks over seconds to minutes through muscarinic receptors. The molecule is the same; the receptor families, circuits and questions are not, so an acetylcholine claim that does not name its pathway is under-specified. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-cholinergic|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]

**Clearance by enzyme, not reuptake.** Most small-molecule transmitters are cleared by reuptake; acetylcholine is cut apart in the cleft by acetylcholinesterase, and the choline fragment is carried back for re-synthesis. Transmission therefore ends on the enzyme's timescale, the fast local cycle lets cholinergic synapses sustain high firing rates, and precursor recovery constrains how much transmitter can be released again - which is also why enzyme inhibition raises acetylcholine everywhere the enzyme sits rather than in one circuit. [[F108 Neuroscience Online acetylcholine#^f108-inactivation|Acetylcholine]]

**The Alzheimer's thread, kept honest.** Degeneration of the basal forebrain cholinergic projection is one of the pathologies associated with Alzheimer's disease, which motivated cholinergic drug development. The chapter frames this as a rationale for a treatment avenue, not a demonstration that the degeneration is the disease or that enhancement reverses it; it does not make acetylcholine "the memory transmitter" or generalise to other conditions, and the drug classes that act here also illustrate the trade-off between central and peripheral cholinergic effects. [[F108 Neuroscience Online acetylcholine#^f108-alzheimer|Acetylcholine]] [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How do medicines work]]

**Reading rule.** A claim about acetylcholine and memory should specify the pathway, the receptor family and the preparation; a claim about a drug should specify whether it acts on the receptor, on the enzyme or on precursor supply, because those three produce different effect profiles. [[F108 Neuroscience Online acetylcholine#^f108-limit|Acetylcholine]] [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-caution-framing|AI synthesis: Cells of the Nervous System]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Pharmacology's anticholinergic side-effect catalogue - dry mouth, constipation, cognitive blunting - is the peripheral-and-central muscarinic distribution showing up as drug effects, which is why drug-class articles track anticholinergic burden alongside their primary mechanisms. ## Limitation or common misconception

The same molecule serves motor output and cognitive regulation in different circuits, so single-purpose intuition misleads.
