# Microbiome-gut-brain axis

## Definition

The microbiota-gut-brain axis describes signalling between gut microorganisms and the brain. It is a research area with several plausible communication routes and limited causal evidence in humans.

## How it works

**Routes.** The review lists immune signalling, tryptophan metabolism, the vagus nerve and the enteric nervous system as communication channels. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-routes|The Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis]]

**Metabolites.** Microbial products such as short-chain fatty acids are candidates for mediating effects, and their measurement is part of the evidence base. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-metabolites|The Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis]]

**Early life.** Microbial colonisation occurs early and is influenced by factors including mode of birth, feeding and antibiotics, which is why developmental timing is a focus. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-status|The Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis]]

## Evidence and status

Preclinical evidence is substantial; human evidence is largely correlational, and the field's own reviews emphasise unresolved causal direction. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-status|The Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis]]

> [!info]- In depth: routes of communication, candidate mediators and the causal question
> **The routes.** The review describes several communication routes between gut microbiota and brain: immune signalling, tryptophan metabolism, the vagus nerve and the enteric nervous system. Because the routes are multiple and partly redundant, a finding that microbiota composition correlates with a brain measure cannot by itself identify which route carries the effect. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-routes|The microbiota-gut-brain axis]]
>
> **The mediators are candidates.** Microbial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids are treated as candidate mediators rather than established transmitters of effect, which places the mechanistic claim one step short of demonstration. A candidate mediator has to be shown to change, to reach the relevant compartment, and to produce the effect when manipulated. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-metabolites|The microbiota-gut-brain axis]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-transport|Blood-brain barrier]]
>
> **The state of the field.** The authors present the field as rapidly growing and emphasise that many findings are correlational, with causal direction often unresolved; the access for this record was abstract-level and the causal claims it discusses are largely preclinical. That combination - a plausible mechanism and a mostly correlational human literature - is the honest summary. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-status|The microbiota-gut-brain axis]] [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-limit|The microbiota-gut-brain axis]]
>
> **Why the immune route is the best-specified arm.** Peripheral immune signals do reach the brain and produce sickness behaviour through cytokine signalling, altered tryptophan metabolism and vagal transmission, and the barrier itself is selective rather than open - gases diffuse freely while ions, nutrients and larger molecules depend on transporters. That makes inflammation a mechanistically concrete route for microbiota effects without making it the only one. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-mechanism|Inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-tight|Blood-brain barrier]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Separate composition differences, candidate mediators, mechanistic routes and clinical effects, and note the species the evidence came from. The microbiome literature is the clearest case in this library where the popular claim runs several steps ahead of the checked evidence. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-status|The microbiota-gut-brain axis]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-mediators|Stress mediators]]
>
> **The causal ladder the field itself uses.** The literature's strongest designs are germ-free and colonisation studies in animals, which establish that microbiota are necessary or sufficient for specific effects; human studies are mostly correlational, with intervention trials (probiotics, diet) still small and heterogeneous. Reading the human literature by the animal ladder's standards is the discipline this review's own caution implies: association in people, causation in mice, and a gap between them that is being filled study by study rather than jumped. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-status|The Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis]] [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-routes|The Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** This note shares its causal-discipline problem with the cytokines article in the same domain - both fields run from strong animal mechanisms to weak human correlations - and with the methods domain's translational-validity article, which names the assumptions each cross-species step requires. [[Cytokines and inflammation in psychiatric conditions]] [[Translational validity]]
>
> **What the human evidence can support today.** Human studies support three modest statements: microbiota composition differs between some clinical groups and controls; diet shifts composition measurably; and small intervention trials show mixed, mostly short-term effects on mood or behaviour. None of these establishes that altering microbiota treats a psychiatric condition, and the composition differences themselves are confounded by medication, diet and illness state - the same confounding structure that the cytokines literature had to work through. [[P31460832 Cryan 2019 Microbiota-gut-brain axis#^p31460832-metabolites|The Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis]]
>
> **Stress physiology connects the axis to the neuroendocrine domain.** Chronic stress reshapes allostatic regulation through the HPA axis and sympathetic output, and both feed back into gut motility, permeability and microbial composition - which is why the allostatic article and the stress-mediator literature belong in the same reading set. [[Allostasis and chronic stress]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-mediators|Stress mediators]]

## Connections

This axis is a second route by which peripheral state reaches the brain, alongside [[Neuroimmune interactions and microglia]], and it belongs with the allostatic framework in [[Allostasis and chronic stress]].

## Uncertainties

- Diet, medication and illness alter both microbiome and behaviour, so observational associations are difficult to interpret.
