# Cytokines and inflammation in psychiatric conditions

## Definition

Cytokines are immune signalling molecules that influence the central nervous system as well as peripheral immune function. The hypothesis under discussion is that inflammatory signalling contributes to psychiatric symptoms in some people, not that inflammation explains these conditions generally.

## How it works

**From immune signal to symptoms.** Peripheral immune signals reach the brain and produce sickness behaviour - changed activity, appetite, sleep and mood - and prolonged signalling is proposed to sustain depressive symptoms in vulnerable individuals. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-sickness|From inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-depression|From inflammation to sickness and depression]]

**Subset framing.** Reviews argue that inflammation is relevant to a subgroup identifiable by markers, rather than to depression as a whole. [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-subset|The role of inflammation in depression]]

**Bipolar evidence.** Meta-analytic findings report elevated TNF-alpha and soluble receptor levels in bipolar disorder, which describe group differences rather than a cause or a test. [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-finding|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]] [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-caution|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]]

**Treatment directions.** Anti-inflammatory strategies are discussed as experimental approaches requiring patient selection, not as established practice. [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-treatment|The role of inflammation in depression]]

## Evidence and status

Group differences in circulating markers are reproducible in some conditions; individual-level prediction and causal direction are not established, and markers are affected by sleep, infection, medication, and body composition.

> [!info]- In depth: the immune-to-brain route, and the three claims it is used to support
> **The physiology.** Peripheral immune signals reach the brain through routes that include cytokine signalling, altered tryptophan metabolism and vagal transmission, and they produce sickness behaviour - a coordinated set of changes in activity, appetite, sleep and mood. The brain's interface is selective rather than open: brain endothelial cells are joined by continuous tight junctions, and ions, nutrients and larger molecules depend on specific transporters, which is how a peripheral signal can act on the central nervous system without free access. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-mechanism|Inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-sickness|Inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-tight|Blood-brain barrier]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-transport|Blood-brain barrier]]
>
> **The clinical hypothesis and its scope.** Prolonged inflammatory signalling is proposed to contribute to depressive symptoms in vulnerable individuals, and the depression literature argues for targeting a marker-identified subset rather than treating inflammation as a general mechanism, framing inflammatory responses as potentially adaptive in short bursts with costs when sustained. Anti-inflammatory strategies are discussed as experimental directions requiring targeting and patient selection. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-depression|Inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-subset|The role of inflammation in depression]] [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-adaptive|The role of inflammation in depression]] [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-treatment|The role of inflammation in depression]]
>
> **What the psychiatric marker literature shows.** A systematic review pooled studies of endogenous cytokine concentrations in bipolar disorder across affective states and reported elevated TNF-alpha, soluble TNF receptor type 1 and soluble interleukin-2 receptor in the included studies. Circulating markers are affected by infection, sleep, medication, body composition and illness state, so group differences are not usable individual tests, and the meta-analytic averages do not identify a cause. [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-finding|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]] [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-caution|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Where this sits in research frameworks.** In RDoC terms, immune and arousal/regulatory processes are units and systems to be studied across conditions rather than the property of one diagnosis, which is why the same marker literature is cited in several disorder domains in this library. [[F121 NIMH RDoC#^f121-matrix|RDoC]] [[F122 NIMH RDoC constructs#^f122-domains|RDoC constructs]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Separate marker findings, mechanistic models and treatment claims. The first is empirical, the second is a proposal, and the third is experimental and requires that a person belongs to the relevant subset - a determination no cross-sectional group average can make. [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-limit|The role of inflammation in depression]]
>
> **What a marker difference can and cannot support.** Elevated group-level cytokine concentrations survive meta-analysis in bipolar disorder and subgroup analyses in depression, and they support exactly that: a group-level difference in a peripheral marker. The chain from marker to mechanism requires direction (cause or consequence), specificity (which signalling), and timing (state or trait), and the sources themselves state that medication, body composition, sleep and infection confound the marker measurements. [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-finding|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]] [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-subset|The role of inflammation in depression]]
>
> **Why the subset framing is the field's honesty move.** Early inflammation-in-depression claims failed as population claims; the current framing - a subgroup identifiable by elevated markers, with experimental medicine evidence that inflammatory challenges can produce depressive-like changes - is narrower and better supported. Anti-inflammatory treatment directions follow the same narrowing: they are investigated for marker-defined subgroups, not offered as general antidepressants. [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-treatment|The role of inflammation in depression]]
>
