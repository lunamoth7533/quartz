# Genes, environment and polygenic risk

**Definition.** Genes are expressed in interaction with environment and experience, and common conditions are influenced by many variants of small individual effect. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-expression|Human Genetics]]

## Supported claims

- Heritability estimates describe variation within a population, not how strongly genes determine one person. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-heritability|Human Genetics]]
- Genotype is a set of instructions read differently in different conditions rather than a fixed destiny. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-expression|Human Genetics]]
- Bipolar genomics reports 298 associated loci and 36 credible genes, most with individually small effects. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-loci|Bipolar disorder genomics]]

> [!info]- In depth: what gene-environment language is actually measuring
> **The heritability starting point.** Heritability estimates describe how much of the variation in a population is associated with genetic differences rather than how strongly genes determine one individual, and genes are expressed in interaction with environment and experience. That pair of statements rules out both genetic determinism and the opposite claim that genes do not matter. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-heritability|Human Genetics]] [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-expression|Human Genetics]]
>
> **Polygenic risk is a population statistic.** A polygenic risk score aggregates many small variant associations into one number intended to summarise genetic liability; scores are population statistics built from specific study samples, so their meaning can shift across ancestry groups and study designs, and possible uses such as stratification or screening are framed as areas under investigation. [[F68 NHGRI Polygenic risk scores#^f68-definition|Polygenic risk scores]] [[F68 NHGRI Polygenic risk scores#^f68-limits|Polygenic risk scores]] [[F68 NHGRI Polygenic risk scores#^f68-use|Polygenic risk scores]]
>
> **Environment is not a residual.** Environmental exposures include the social and material conditions in which a person lives, and social conditions shape stress exposure and health outcomes rather than serving as noise around a genetic signal. That is a design implication: a study that treats environment as an error term cannot estimate its contribution. [[F65 OpenStax Stress and illness#^f65-social|Stress and Illness]] [[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-argument|Stigma as a fundamental cause of health inequalities]]
>
> **Gene-by-environment claims need specific designs.** Detecting interaction requires either measured exposures with adequate variation or designs that exploit natural experiments, and the field's methodological literature emphasises preregistration, larger samples and reporting standards because flexible analyses of interaction are prone to false positives. [[P33954258 Munafo 2017 Reproducible science#^p33954258-practices|A manifesto for reproducible science]] [[P16060722 Ioannidis 2005 Why most findings are false#^p16060722-probability|Why most published research findings are false]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Separate heritability, genetic correlation, polygenic score and interaction; each is estimated differently, and only a design that measured the environment can support an environmental claim. [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-caution-individual|AI appraisal: 10 years of GWAS discovery]] [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-limit|Human Genetics]]

> [!info]- Reference: how genetic contribution is actually measured
> **Two vocabularies.** Genetics studies single genes and their variants; genomics studies the whole set of genetic material, which changes the questions that can be asked. [[F66 NHGRI Genomics fact sheet#^f66-scope|Genomics fact sheet]]
>
> **Common variants.** Genome-wide association studies compare variants across large samples for statistical association with a trait, without assuming which gene is involved. [[F67 NHGRI GWAS fact sheet#^f67-design|GWAS fact sheet]] Most individual variant effects on complex traits are small, so credible signals require very large samples and replication. [[F67 NHGRI GWAS fact sheet#^f67-scale|GWAS fact sheet]]
>
> **Aggregate scores.** Polygenic scores aggregate many small associations into one number summarising genetic liability. They are population statistics built from particular samples, so their meaning can shift across ancestry groups and designs. [[F68 NHGRI Polygenic risk scores#^f68-definition|Polygenic risk scores]] [[F68 NHGRI Polygenic risk scores#^f68-limits|Polygenic risk scores]]
>
> **From signal to mechanism.** A signal marks a region in linkage disequilibrium; identifying which variant acts requires fine-mapping plus functional work, and fine-mapping narrows candidates without establishing mechanism. [[P29844615 Schaid 2018 Fine-mapping#^p29844615-problem|Fine-mapping]] [[P29844615 Schaid 2018 Fine-mapping#^p29844615-limits|Fine-mapping]]
>
> **Environment.** Early experience interacts with genetically guided development, and epigenomic marks change how cells use DNA without altering sequence. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]] [[F69 NHGRI Epigenomics fact sheet#^f69-definition|Epigenomics fact sheet]]
>
> **What heritability is not.** Heritability describes the share of variation in a population associated with genetic differences; it is not a statement about an individual or about immutability. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-heritability|Human genetics]]
>
> **What a genome-wide association study does, mechanically.** It genotypes hundreds of thousands to millions of marker variants across very large case-control or quantitative-trait cohorts and tests each for association, under significance thresholds made stringent precisely because so many tests are run. The threshold protects against false positives, not against systematic biases: population stratification, ascertainment and measurement differences each leave signatures that can mimic signal, which is why replication in independent samples and careful matching of cases and controls are part of the design rather than an afterthought. [[F67 NHGRI GWAS fact sheet#^f67-design|GWAS fact sheet]] [[F67 NHGRI GWAS fact sheet#^f67-scale|GWAS fact sheet]]
>
> **From marker to mechanism is two more steps.** An associated marker names a region because of co-inheritance with nearby variants; fine-mapping narrows the credible set using the region's inheritance structure, and functional work then asks what the remaining candidates actually do. Most associated variants sit outside protein-coding sequence, so the usual outcome is a regulatory hypothesis about where and when a gene is used - a weaker, slower kind of knowledge than 'the gene for X', and the honest one. [[P29844615 Schaid 2018 Fine-mapping#^p29844615-problem|Fine-mapping]] [[P29844615 Schaid 2018 Fine-mapping#^p29844615-limits|Fine-mapping]]
>
> **Where environmental claims earn their keep.** A measured exposure, adequate variation in it, and a design that separates its association with genotype from its effect are the entry requirements for any gene-by-environment statement. Social conditions - stigma, material insecurity, stress exposure - are exposures with distributions, not residual noise; studies that measure them can estimate their contribution, and studies that do not cannot. [[F65 OpenStax Stress and illness#^f65-social|Stress and Illness]] [[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-argument|Stigma as a fundamental cause of health inequalities]] [[Gene-environment interplay and epigenetics]]
>

## Limitation or common misconception

Polygenic scores aggregate population-level risk and do not predict an individual's future.
