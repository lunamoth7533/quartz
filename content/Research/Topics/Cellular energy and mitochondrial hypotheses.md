# Cellular energy and mitochondrial hypotheses

## Definition

Cellular energy hypotheses propose that altered mitochondrial function or energy metabolism contributes to mood episodes, based on findings from peripheral tissue studies, genetics and post-mortem work.

## How it works

**Cellular basis.** Restoring ion gradients after signalling consumes most of the brain's energy budget, so neurons are unusually dependent on mitochondrial function. [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-transport|Blood Brain Barrier]]

**Evidence types.** Findings include altered mitochondrial enzyme activity or gene expression in peripheral samples and suggestive genetic overlaps; each is indirect with respect to the living brain.

**Lithium's uncertain mechanism.** Lithium's actions include signalling effects that remain incompletely understood, which is one reason interest in cellular-level mechanisms persists. [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-signalling|Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics]] [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-unknown|Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics]]

**General cellular caveat.** Mitochondrial dysfunction is studied in many conditions, so a shared finding is weak evidence of a specific mechanism for any one of them. [[P20877280 Nestler 2010 Animal models#^p20877280-construct|Animal models]]

## Evidence and status

Peripheral and post-mortem findings are consistent enough to sustain research programmes and too indirect to establish mechanism; this note treats the hypothesis as unresolved.

> [!info]- In depth: what the hypothesis claims, and what the evidence can carry
> **Why neurons are unusually dependent on energy metabolism.** Restoring ion gradients after signalling consumes most of the brain's energy budget, and cerebral metabolism is coupled to transport across a selective barrier rather than to bulk flow - the barrier is selective rather than absolute, with gases diffusing freely while ions, nutrients and larger molecules depend on specific transporters. That coupling is the physiological reason a disorder of cellular energy could matter for neuronal signalling, and also the reason peripheral measurements are a long way from the relevant compartment. [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-transport|Blood-brain barrier]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-tight|Blood-brain barrier]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-limit|Blood-brain barrier]]
>
> **The claim in its current form.** Cellular energy hypotheses propose that altered mitochondrial function or energy metabolism contributes to mood episodes in bipolar disorder. Support comes from several indirect sources: altered mitochondrial enzyme activity or gene expression in peripheral samples, post-mortem tissue work, and suggestive overlaps in genetic studies. Each of these measures something other than mitochondrial function in the living brain of the person described, and the review literature is explicit that cellular and animal models should be judged by whether their biology matches the human mechanism - construct validity, which the model literature calls the hardest and most important criterion. [[P20877280 Nestler 2010 Animal models#^p20877280-construct|Animal models]] [[P20877280 Nestler 2010 Animal models#^p20877280-validity|Animal models]] [[P20877280 Nestler 2010 Animal models#^p20877280-limit|Animal models]]
>
> **Lithium keeps the question open without answering it.** Lithium's actions include signalling effects at named nodes such as GSK3, CREB and Na+/K+ ATPase, usually enhancing basal and inhibiting stimulated activity. The mechanism that prevents recurrences remains only partially understood: distinct hypotheses have not been conclusively supported or rejected, research is limited by the absence of suitable animal models, and pharmacogenetic findings remain limited by small samples. A drug that acts on cellular signalling and works clinically is a reason to keep studying the pathway, not evidence that the pathway explains the disorder. [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-signalling|Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics]] [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-unknown|Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics]] [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-pharmacogenetics|Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics]]
>
> **Shared findings are weak evidence of a specific mechanism.** Mitochondrial dysfunction is studied in many conditions, so a finding common across them does not identify what is distinctive about any one of them. The same logic applies to genetic overlap: an association shared between disorders constrains the specificity of any explanation built on it. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-cells|Genomics of bipolar disorder]]
>
> **Confounds that the available designs cannot separate.** Illness course, medication exposure and health behaviours all differ between people with bipolar disorder and comparison groups, and the largest bipolar cortical imaging analysis found that commonly prescribed drugs, including lithium, antiepileptics and antipsychotics, were associated with cortical measures even after accounting for multiple medications, with longer illness duration associated with reduced cortical thickness. The same caution applies with more force to peripheral biomarkers, which respond to sleep, activity, diet and treatment. Cross-sectional group differences in such markers describe samples; they do not date the process or establish direction. [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-medication|ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder]] [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-course|ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder]] [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-caution-groups|AI appraisal: ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder]]
>
> **How to read a cellular-energy claim.** Separate (a) a measured difference in a peripheral or post-mortem sample, (b) an inference that mitochondrial function in the brain differs, and (c) a causal role in mood episodes. The first is sometimes demonstrable, the second is an inference with cross-tissue assumptions, and the third is not established. [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-translation|10 years of GWAS discovery]]

## Uncertainties

- Peripheral measures are not proxies for brain energetics, and reverse causation from illness or treatment is plausible.
