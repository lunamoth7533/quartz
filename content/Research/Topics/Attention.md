# Attention

## Definition

Attention is the selection of some information for further processing at the expense of other information. It has several forms - selective, sustained, divided - that are related but not identical.

## How it works

**Selection and capacity.** Limits on processing appear as interference between concurrent tasks and as costs of switching between them, which is why divided attention degrades performance. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-absolute|Sensation versus Perception]]

**Sustained attention.** Vigilance declines over time, and the rate of decline depends on task demands, reward structure and arousal. [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-theories|Motivation]]

**Neural basis.** Attention involves posterior association cortex and prefrontal control regions, with modulatory input from noradrenaline and acetylcholine shaping the gain of processing. [[F109 Neuroscience Online association and executive processing#^f109-areas|Association and executive processing]] [[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-networks|Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD]] [[F108 Neuroscience Online acetylcholine#^f108-pathways|Acetylcholine transmission]]

**Frameworks.** Computational accounts treat attention as precision weighting of prediction errors, a description that connects attention to learning and perception. [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-common|Predictive coding algorithms]]

## Evidence and status

Behavioural attention effects are robust; how many separable systems exist and whether they map onto distinct networks remains debated. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]]

> [!info]- In depth: selection, its limits and the control systems behind it
> **Detection limits.** Detection thresholds describe the minimum stimulus that can be noticed, and signal detection theory separates sensitivity from response bias - which is the measurement foundation for attention research, because a 'miss' can reflect either the sensory limit or the criterion the person applied. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-absolute|Sensation versus Perception]]
>
> **Control sits in association cortex.** Association areas are grouped into anterior, posterior and limbic regions, each linking information from primary and unimodal sensory or motor areas, and anterior association cortex including prefrontal regions is linked with executive processing, which the source treats as a set of interacting functions rather than one faculty. Attention is therefore a family of control operations rather than a single spotlight. [[F109 Neuroscience Online association and executive processing#^f109-areas|Association and Executive Processing]] [[F109 Neuroscience Online association and executive processing#^f109-executive|Association and Executive Processing]]
>
> **The arousal and cholinergic story.** Prefrontal function depends on noradrenaline and dopamine released according to arousal state, with an inverted-U relationship to performance, and long-axon cholinergic pathways including the nucleus basalis projection to cortex provide a separate modulatory route. That is why alertness, motivation and attention are difficult to separate experimentally. [[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-networks|Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD]] [[F108 Neuroscience Online acetylcholine#^f108-pathways|Acetylcholine]]
>
> **Constructive perception constrains attention claims.** Predictive-coding algorithms share the idea that perception involves inference about hidden causes, and such accounts predict that what is attended changes what is perceived rather than only what is reported - a claim that requires a specific algorithm to test. [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-common|Predictive coding algorithms]] [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-difference|Predictive coding algorithms]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Name the task, the manipulation, the outcome measure and the control condition; in this literature a difference in performance is often a difference in strategy or criterion rather than in capacity. [[F09 Noba Research designs#^f09-correlation|Research Designs]] [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]]

>
> **Selection has an early and a late version, and the debate is empirical.** Whether unattended input is filtered early in processing or processed semantically before being dropped was settled neither way: findings depend on task and load, which itself supports the family-of-operations framing. The practical residue is that attention claims must specify what selection acts on - location, feature, object or stream - because the operations dissociate. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-absolute|Sensation versus Perception]]
>
> **Vigilance decline is a measurement model.** The vigilance decrement - declining detection over sustained task time - depends on task demands and reward structure, which makes it partly a motivational effect rather than a pure resource drain. That is why vigilance claims in clinical contexts name the task and the reinforcement conditions, and why "poor sustained attention" from one task does not automatically generalise. >
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** Neuroanatomy supplies the control circuitry - association cortex plus prefrontal modulation - and the cholinergic and catecholamine systems supply its state; the precision-weighting account from predictive coding is the formal proposal that ties selection to perception, and it remains at the algorithmic-proposal level of evidence in this library.
