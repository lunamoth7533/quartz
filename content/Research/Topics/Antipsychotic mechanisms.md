# Antipsychotic mechanisms

## Definition

Antipsychotic drugs reduce psychotic symptoms. Across the dopamine-blocking drugs that dominated the class when the 2020 review by Kaar and colleagues surveyed it, in vitro, preclinical, clinical and imaging work identifies blockade of the dopamine D2 receptor as the mechanism shared by clinical response and by several characteristic side effects. The class is no longer mechanism-uniform: in September 2024 the FDA approved a fixed combination of xanomeline, a muscarinic agonist, with trospium, a peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist, for schizophrenia in adults; the approved label states that xanomeline's mechanism in schizophrenia is unclear and that its efficacy is thought to follow from M1/M4 muscarinic agonist activity, with no direct D2 blockade.

## How it works

**One receptor, several pathways.** The D2 receptor is the same molecule wherever it sits, but its blockade produces different effects in different pathways, which is why an anatomical claim has to name the pathway rather than the receptor alone. In the striatum, where the source review locates the blockade behind clinical response, the same blockade produces drug-induced movement effects through the motor circuitry of the basal ganglia. Endocrine effects run through a separate pathway: dopamine reaching the anterior pituitary tonically inhibits prolactin release, so blockade there removes an inhibition and raises prolactin. Prolactin elevation and its consequences are therefore expected from the same pharmacology without the striatum being their explanation. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal Ganglia]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-prolactin|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]]

**Therapeutic window.** Because clinical response and several side effects share the target, antipsychotic prescribing is described in terms of a therapeutic window for D2 blockade rather than a dose raised until symptoms stop. The window is a statement about occupancy in the dopamine-blocking drugs, and it is what makes benefit and harm hard to separate by pushing on dose alone. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]

**Partial agonism is the same target with different intrinsic activity.** D2/3 partial agonists produce submaximal signalling at the same receptor rather than acting somewhere else, and the review places them inside this framework rather than treating them as a different mechanism. That distinction is a difference in what binding does, not in where it happens. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]

**A second mechanism now in the class.** The 2024 muscarinic combination is not a dopamine-blocking drug. Its approved prescribing information states that xanomeline's mechanism of action in schizophrenia is unclear but that efficacy is thought to follow from agonist activity at M1 and M4 muscarinic acetylcholine receptors in the central nervous system, with trospium antagonising muscarinic receptors mainly in peripheral tissues. A class-wide claim that antipsychotics work by blocking D2 therefore has to carry a date and a subclass. [[F127 FDA prescribing information COBENFY#^f127-mechanism|COBENFY prescribing information]] [[F126 FDA Drug Trials Snapshot COBENFY#^f126-approval|Drug Trials Snapshot: COBENFY]]

**Off-target binding shapes side-effect profiles.** Binding at serotonergic, histaminergic, cholinergic and adrenergic receptors contributes to effects such as weight gain, sedation and dysphoria, which is why agents in the class do not share one side-effect profile. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-offtarget|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]

**A serotonergic drug outside both accounts.** The class also contains a drug approved for a different indication on a different target: pimavanserin is indicated for hallucinations and delusions associated with Parkinson's disease psychosis, and its approved label describes inverse agonist and antagonist activity at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors with no appreciable affinity for dopaminergic (including D2) or muscarinic receptors. Its presence is why antipsychotic mechanism claims are best made about named drugs or named subclasses rather than about one historical class definition. [[F128 DailyMed label NUPLAZID pimavanserin#^f128-indication|NUPLAZID approved label]] [[F128 DailyMed label NUPLAZID pimavanserin#^f128-mechanism|NUPLAZID approved label]]

**Limits.** Antipsychotics are ineffective for some patients, and side effects drive non-adherence in others, so mechanism does not translate into uniform benefit. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-limits|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]

## Evidence and status

The D2 mechanism is among the best-supported in psychopharmacology, and dopamine-blocking drugs produce predictable extrapyramidal effects. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Disorders of the Motor System]]

> [!info]- In depth: what the shared mechanism covers, and what it does not
> **What the source establishes.** The review's own summary of its position is that D2 blockade is the key shared mechanism of the dopamine-blocking class, that it accounts for clinical response and for endocrine and motor side effects, and that this is the reason the field talks about a therapeutic window. That is a claim about a class of drugs and about a receptor, and its support comes from the convergence of the evidence types the review surveys. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]
>
> **Reading the same mechanism across pathways.** A mechanism claim becomes an anatomical claim only when a pathway is named. The same D2 blockade can produce a clinical effect through striatal transmission, movement effects through basal-ganglia motor circuitry and prolactin changes through pituitary disinhibition, so one receptor supports several distinguishable clinical signatures, and a person can show one without the others. Which of them a given patient develops is not delivered by the receptor account. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal Ganglia]] [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-prolactin|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]]
>
> **Time scope and class scope.** The review was published in 2020 and its treatment of mechanism is a synthesis of work to that point, including coverage of muscarinic and other non-dopaminergic strategies as approaches under development. Because the field has since approved a muscarinic treatment, the correct reading is a time-scoped one: D2 blockade is the mechanism of the dopamine-blocking subclass as established in that literature, not a definition of antipsychotic action. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-limit|Antipsychotics mechanisms]] [[F127 FDA prescribing information COBENFY#^f127-mechanism|COBENFY prescribing information]]
>
> **Where the mechanism stops explaining.** The same review notes that antipsychotics are ineffective for some patients and that response varies, so mechanism does not translate into uniform benefit - which rules out the reading in which the target explains the illness. Negative and cognitive symptom domains are where a D2 story has least purchase, and the review treats them as separate problems with separate candidate approaches. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-limits|Antipsychotics mechanisms]] [[F83 NIMH Schizophrenia#^f83-features|Schizophrenia]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Separate four statements that are often merged: the class-level claim that this subclass of drugs acts at D2 receptors; the pathway-level claim about which circuit produces which effect; the agent-level claim about what else a particular molecule binds; and the person-level claim about how an individual will respond. The first is well supported, the second is supported pathway by pathway, the third differs between agents, and the fourth is not delivered by occupancy alone. No ranking of the four in terms of predictive strength is asserted here, because the sources do not measure one. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-offtarget|Antipsychotics mechanisms]] [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-limits|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]
>
> **Cross-domain connection (curation).** The pharmacological framing here depends on the methods-domain distinction between association and individual prediction: a receptor-occupancy account explains what a drug does at the target, but does not by itself forecast response for one person, which is why prescription guidance lives in the clinical-psychiatry domain.
