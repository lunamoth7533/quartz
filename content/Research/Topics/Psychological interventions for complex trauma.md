# Psychological interventions for complex trauma

**Definition.** This topic covers whether psychological therapies help adults with complex trauma presentations, separately from which structure they use. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]

## Supported claims

- Pooled psychotherapy effects in a 2025 meta-analysis were significant for PTSD, depression, anxiety and dissociation outcomes. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]
- Effects decreased slightly at follow-up but remained significant except for anxiety and dissociation. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-followup|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]
- Participants with childhood trauma showed lower effects, risk of bias moderated results, and the authors report some indications of publication bias. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]
- Complex trauma exposure in these trials is not the same category as a formal ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-caution-category|AI synthesis: Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]

> [!info]- In depth: pooled effects, moderators and the boundaries of the category
> **The pooled result.** Effects of psychotherapy compared with control were significant for PTSD (k = 27), depression (k = 23), anxiety (k = 13) and dissociation (k = 7) outcomes. At follow-up the effect sizes decreased slightly but remained significant except for anxiety and dissociation symptoms. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-followup|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]]
>
> **Who responded less well.** Participants with childhood trauma showed lower effects than those with other trauma types, risk of bias and the proportion of female participants moderated results, and the authors report some indications of publication bias. Moderators of this kind are the most informative part of a synthesis because they describe where the average came from. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-limit|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]]
>
> **The category mismatch.** Complex trauma exposure in these trials is not the same category as a formal ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis, so group averages should not be read as individual prognosis; the diagnostic construct is instrument-dependent, with the ITQ developed as a self-report measure whose properties must be established in each new population. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-caution-category|AI appraisal: Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-development|International Trauma Questionnaire]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-limit|International Trauma Questionnaire]]
>
> **Sequencing is a separate question.** The phase-versus-non-phase comparison found no significant differences across most planned contrasts, with selected outcomes better under structured designs, and the comparison was between packaged protocols rather than a test of sequencing. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-comparison|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]] [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-caution-sequence|AI appraisal: Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Report the outcome, the follow-up and the population definition; then check whether the trials measured a formal diagnosis or a trauma-exposure category. The distinction changes what the average describes. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-caution-boundary|AI appraisal: ICD-11 PTSD proposals review]]

> [!info]- Reference: pooled therapy effects and what moderates them
> **Pooled effects.** In a 2025 meta-analysis, psychological interventions improved PTSD, depression, anxiety and dissociation outcomes in complex trauma presentations. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]
>
> **Durability.** Effects decreased slightly at follow-up but remained significant except for anxiety and dissociation, so the durability claim is outcome-specific rather than uniform. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-followup|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]
>
> **Moderators.** Participants with childhood trauma showed lower effects, risk of bias moderated results, and the authors report some indications of publication bias, which is the pattern that makes pooled effects fragile to interpret. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]
>
> **Category caution.** Trial exposure categories are not the same thing as an ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis, and the questionnaire used to measure the construct has its own documented limits. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-caution-category|AI synthesis: Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-limits|International Trauma Questionnaire]]
>
> **Mechanistic background.** Extinction-based models supply the learning account behind exposure components, while [[Phase-based trauma therapy evidence]] covers the sequencing debate and [[Memory and trauma]] covers the memory side.

## Limitation or common misconception

Heterogeneous samples and moderator findings mean the pooled effect describes an average, not a prognosis for one person.
