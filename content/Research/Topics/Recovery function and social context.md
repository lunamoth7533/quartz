# Recovery, function and social context

## Definition

Recovery in this literature includes symptom reduction but also functioning, participation and quality of life. Social conditions - safety, housing, income, discrimination - shape both exposure to trauma and the resources available afterwards.

## How it works

**Functioning framework.** The ICF describes functioning through activities, participation and environmental factors, which is why symptom scores alone understate or overstate change. [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-framework|International Classification of Functioning]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-domains|International Classification of Functioning]]

**Structural causes.** Stigma operates as a fundamental cause of inequality through constrained access to resources, which makes it part of the clinical picture. [[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-argument|Stigma as a fundamental cause of health inequalities]]

**Service context.** Guideline products describe care pathways and support needs, and their recommendations reflect service capacity as well as evidence. [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-care|Multiple sclerosis NG220]]

**Measurement.** Functioning instruments are general rather than trauma-specific, so their results describe participation rather than symptom severity. [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-caution-impairment|AI appraisal: International Classification of Functioning]]

## Evidence and status

Functioning and participation are established outcome domains; the evidence linking specific trauma treatments to functional gains is thinner than for symptom outcomes.

> [!info]- In depth: what recovery includes beyond symptoms
> **The functioning framework.** The WHO's International Classification of Functioning describes functioning through body functions, activities, participation and environmental factors, and was endorsed by Member States in 2001 as the international standard for describing and measuring health and disability. Its linked generic instrument, WHODAS 2.0, covers six domains - cognition, mobility, self-care, getting along, life activities and participation - in 36-item and 12-item versions with cross-cultural, reliability and validity field testing. Using it means describing participation and activity rather than symptom severity. [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-framework|International Classification of Functioning]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-standard|International Classification of Functioning]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-domains|International Classification of Functioning]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-instrument|International Classification of Functioning]]
>
> **What such a score is not.** A functioning score summarises reported activity and participation; it is not a direct measure of symptom severity, capacity or potential, and it should not be read as a verdict about a person. How functioning is operationalised, scored and interpreted depends on the instrument, the informant and the study design, so two studies of 'functioning' can be measuring different things. [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-limit|International Classification of Functioning]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-caution-impairment|AI appraisal: International Classification of Functioning]]
>
> **Structural causes belong in the picture.** Stigma functions as a fundamental cause of health inequality because it constrains access to resources such as employment, housing and healthcare, and the framework distinguishes structural, interpersonal and internalised forms, each linked to measurable outcomes. The implication is that interventions targeting only individual behaviour miss mechanisms operating at the level of law, policy and institutions. For trauma-related conditions, that places safety, housing, income and discrimination among the conditions that shape recovery rather than in the background. [[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-argument|Stigma as a fundamental cause of health inequalities]] [[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-levels|Stigma as a fundamental cause of health inequalities]] [[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-implication|Stigma as a fundamental cause of health inequalities]]
>
> **Service context shapes what guidelines can recommend.** Guideline products describe care pathways covering information, support, relapse and symptom management as standard care rather than afterthoughts, with recommendations tied to an evidence review and to the funded service context; that is why they are service-level documents rather than sources of individual treatment advice. Availability of the recommended components varies between systems, and a recommendation is not a measurement of an individual's outcome. [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-care|Multiple sclerosis NG220]] [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-symptoms|Multiple sclerosis NG220]] [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-context|Multiple sclerosis NG220]] [[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-limit|Multiple sclerosis NG220]]
>
> **Where the evidence is thinner.** Functioning and participation are established outcome domains, but the evidence linking specific trauma treatments to functional gains is thinner than for symptom outcomes: the CPTSD psychotherapy meta-analysis pooled PTSD, depression, anxiety and dissociation symptoms and reported that effect sizes decreased slightly at follow-up while remaining significant except for anxiety and dissociation, with moderators including childhood trauma and risk of bias. Functioning and quality of life were not the primary pooled outcomes. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-followup|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-caution-category|AI appraisal: Psychological interventions for CPTSD]]
>
> **Reading rule.** Separate symptom change, functional change and social conditions. Recovery definitions vary between clinical, research and lived-experience perspectives, and outcome selection reflects that disagreement rather than resolving it. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-limit|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]]
