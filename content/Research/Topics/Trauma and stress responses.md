# Trauma and stress responses

**Definition.** Trauma responses are the physiological and psychological consequences of overwhelming events, shaped by appraisal, context and prior experience. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

## Supported claims

- Acute stress mobilisation is adaptive; chronic activation is associated with wear on regulation, mood and health. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-chronic|What Is Stress?]]
- ICD-11 and DSM-5 classify trauma-related disorders differently, and current definitions are symptom-based. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]; [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

> [!info]- In depth: appraisal, physiology and the classification step
> **Appraisal comes first.** Stress responses depend on appraisal: the same demand can be experienced as a threat or a challenge, which changes the physiological response. That is why a stressor cannot be defined independently of the person's interpretation, and why exposure measures in trauma research record the event rather than the response. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-appraisal|What is stress?]]
>
> **Physiology runs on two timescales.** The sympathetic response is fast and the HPA axis is slower, ending in cortisol release that mobilises energy and modulates many systems; acute responses are adaptive and chronic activation is associated with wear on regulation, mood and health. Cortisol secretion follows ACTH from the pituitary which follows hypothalamic CRH, with corticosteroid feedback limiting further release. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-axis|What is stress?]] [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-chronic|What is stress?]] [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-drive|The Adrenal Glands]]
>
> **The mediators are not simply 'stress hormones'.** A long-standing dispute concerns whether glucocorticoids enable, stimulate or suppress the stress response, and one influential classification proposes permissive, suppressive, stimulatory and preparative actions depending on context and timing. Allostatic load describes the cumulative cost of repeated or poorly managed responses with the brain as both regulator and target. [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-contested|Glucocorticoids and stress]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|Glucocorticoids and stress]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-load|Stress mediators]]
>
> **Immune signalling is part of the response.** Peripheral immune signals reach the brain and produce sickness behaviour - coordinated changes in activity, appetite, sleep and mood - and prolonged inflammatory signalling is proposed to contribute to depressive symptoms in vulnerable individuals. The causal claim is a model rather than an established mechanism. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-sickness|Inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-depression|Inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-limit|Inflammation to sickness and depression]]
>
> **The classification step is separate.** ICD-11 and DSM-5 classify trauma-related disorders differently, and current definitions are symptom-based rather than exposure-based, so a stress response and a diagnosis are different objects. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|Complex PTSD: history and definitions]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|Complex PTSD: history and definitions]]

> [!info]- Reference: appraisal, physiology and the classification step
> **Appraisal first.** Stress responses depend on how an event is appraised and on prior experience, which is why the same exposure produces different responses in different people. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-appraisal|What Is Stress?]]
>
> **Acute versus chronic.** Acute mobilisation is adaptive; sustained activation is associated with wear on regulation, mood and physical health, which is the distinction between a response and a burden. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-chronic|What Is Stress?]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-load|Stress mediators]]
>
> **Endocrine detail.** Glucocorticoid actions are permissive, suppressive, stimulatory and preparative depending on context and timing, so 'stress hormone' is not a single-direction description. [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|Glucocorticoids and stress]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-contested|Glucocorticoids and stress]]
>
> **Immune side.** Inflammatory signalling produces sickness behaviour and is one route by which stress physiology connects to mood and behaviour. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-sickness|Inflammation to sickness and depression]]
>
> **Classification step.** Only some responses become disorders: ICD-11 and DSM-5 classify trauma-related conditions differently, current definitions are symptom-based, and exposure to a traumatic event is necessary but not sufficient for a diagnosis. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-exposure-required|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Exposure to traumatic events is common and does not imply a disorder.
