# Does trauma therapy need a stabilization phase before processing?

**Question.** Clinical guidance has long recommended stabilization before trauma-focused processing. Does the comparative evidence support requiring that sequence?

**Origin.** AI-generated starter question, written for this library. It does not represent anyone's beliefs or history.

## Competing explanations

**Sequencing protects the vulnerable.** Stabilization is intended to build regulation and safety before confronting traumatic material, reducing dropout and destabilization. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-comparison|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]

**The comparison does not support a rule.** Across most outcomes, phase-based and non-phase-based approaches did not clearly differ, though the trial base is small and heterogeneous. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-conclusion|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]; [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-limit|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]

**Individual differences may matter more than protocol order.** Baseline regulation and dissociation may moderate who benefits from which sequence, but current samples are too small to test this robustly. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-dso|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

## Evidence that would decide

- Trials that randomize the sequence of the same components rather than comparing packaged protocols.
- Baseline moderator analyses for dissociation, affect regulation and complexity of trauma history.
- Harm and dropout reporting, since both explanations predict specific patterns of attrition.

## Cautions

Do not read the uncertainty as a licence to change clinical practice in either direction; it is a call for better evidence.
