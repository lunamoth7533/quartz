## Reported findings (checked full text)

The authors identify limitations in existing quality-of-life measures for autistic adults with lower support needs - content validity, measurement invariance and cultural or linguistic adaptation - and do not establish that no validated instruments exist.
^p41993846-gap

Existing tools may overestimate quality of life and under-detect vulnerability, and content validity suffers when measured domains do not match lived experience.
^p41993846-validity

Self-report and proxy report carry different limitations, so both should be considered alongside objective indicators.
^p41993846-report

Stigma is described as a consistent negative predictor, and contextual and intersectional factors shape participation and life trajectories.
^p41993846-context

## Scope as reported

A mini-review synthesising conceptual, psychometric and representativeness issues rather than a new empirical study. It highlights emerging challenges in adult quality-of-life measurement and is recent (2026), so its claims describe the current state of a developing literature.
^p41993846-limit

## Analyst cautions (AI synthesis)

These paragraphs are this library's own appraisal, not statements from the review.

A measurement gap is not evidence that no support works; it constrains what current studies can detect and argues for outcome measures designed with autistic adults rather than for them.
