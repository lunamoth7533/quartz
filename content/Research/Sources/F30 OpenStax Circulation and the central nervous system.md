# Circulation and the Central Nervous System

## Key points (as published)

The central nervous system is described as having a privileged circulatory arrangement: blood supply is extensive, tightly regulated and separated from neural tissue by a selective barrier.
^f30-privilege

Cerebrospinal fluid is produced in the ventricles, circulates through the ventricular system and the subarachnoid space, and is reabsorbed, so obstruction changes pressure and function.
^f30-ventricles

Because neural tissue depends on continuous perfusion and has limited storage, interrupted supply produces focal deficits; the section frames stroke in exactly those vascular terms.
^f30-stroke

## Scope and limitations

An introductory textbook section on circulation. It describes anatomy and general principles rather than the molecular regulation of barrier permeability.
^f30-limit
