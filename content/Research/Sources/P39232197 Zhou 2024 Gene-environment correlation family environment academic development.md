# Gene-environment correlation: the role of family environment in academic development

## Reported findings (checked full text)

In 5,151 children from the Twins Early Development Study, family-environment measures consistently mediated polygenic-score effects on academic achievement from ages 7 to 16, accounting for up to 34.3% of the total effect; neighbourhood context did not.
^p39232197-finding

With family fixed effects, mediation was observed nearly exclusively between families rather than between siblings, which the authors read as consistent with passive gene-environment correlation or genetic nurture.
^p39232197-passive

Family-environment measures were more robustly linked to noncognitive than cognitive polygenic effects, and the sibling contrast separates between-family mediation from child-specific effects.
^p39232197-twin

## Scope as reported

A twin-and-sibling study of academic achievement in one United Kingdom cohort using polygenic scores. The outcome is achievement rather than a psychiatric condition, and mediation is a decomposition of effects within this sample.
^p39232197-limit
