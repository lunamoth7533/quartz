# Complex PTSD: History and Definitions

**Publisher.** US Department of Veterans Affairs, National Center for PTSD (Sadie E. Larsen)

**Source.** <https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/essentials/complex_ptsd.asp>

**Verification.** Official page opened and checked on 2026-09-24; educational material, not primary research.

## Key points (as published)

ICD-11 and DSM-5 classify trauma-related disorders differently: ICD-11 includes complex PTSD while DSM-5 does not use that separate diagnosis.
^f17-systems

Complex PTSD adds disturbances in self-organization to core PTSD symptoms: problems with affect regulation, a persistently negative self-concept and difficulties in relationships.
^f17-dso

Current definitions are symptom-based; trauma type or duration alone does not determine the diagnosis.
^f17-definition

## Clarification: criteria order and exposure (checked 2026-09-24)

The blocks below were appended during the staged learning-layer build to make the criteria order explicit. The
original note above is unchanged and its reading status remains `queued`.

ICD-11 PTSD is defined by exposure to an extremely threatening or horrific event or series of events, followed
by symptom clusters; the working group selected three essential fear-related components: reliving in the here
and now, avoidance of trauma material, and a heightened sense of current threat.
^f17-core-clusters

Complex PTSD requires that the PTSD criteria are met in addition to the disturbances in self-organization. A
specific trauma type is not required, and prolonged complex trauma is treated as a risk factor rather than a
necessary condition; qualifying exposure remains part of the PTSD criteria.
^f17-exposure-required
