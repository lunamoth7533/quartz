# The Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus

**Publisher.** OpenStax, Anatomy and Physiology 2e, section 17.3

## Key points (as published)

The hypothalamus links the nervous system to endocrine output: it produces releasing and inhibiting hormones for the anterior pituitary and sends neural projections to the posterior pituitary.
^f34-connection

Anterior pituitary hormones include TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, growth hormone and prolactin, while the posterior pituitary releases oxytocin and antidiuretic hormone made in the hypothalamus.
^f34-lobes

Hormone release is regulated by feedback from target-gland products back to the hypothalamus and pituitary, which is why the axis is described as a loop rather than a chain.
^f34-feedback

Secretion of prolactin from the anterior pituitary is inhibited by prolactin-inhibiting hormone, which the section identifies as the neurotransmitter dopamine released from hypothalamic neurons.
^f34-prolactin

## Scope and limitations

An introductory textbook section. It presents the classical axis model, which simplifies pulsatile release and tissue-specific receptor effects.
^f34-limit
