# International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF) and WHODAS 2.0

**Publisher.** World Health Organization · [ICF page](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health) · [WHODAS 2.0 page](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health/who-disability-assessment-schedule)

**Source.** <https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health>

**Verification.** Official WHO pages opened and checked on 2026-09-24. Key points below are verified from the published pages.

## Key points (as published)

ICF is WHO's framework for measuring health and disability, including environmental factors alongside body functions, activities and participation.
^f23-framework

Member States endorsed it in 2001 as the international standard for describing and measuring health and disability.
^f23-standard

WHODAS 2.0 is a generic instrument linked to the ICF, covering six domains: cognition, mobility, self-care, getting along, life activities and participation.
^f23-domains

It is documented for use across diseases and cultures in 36-item and 12-item versions, with cross-cultural, reliability and validity field testing.
^f23-instrument

## Scope and limitations

An official framework and instrument description rather than a study. How functioning is operationalized, scored and interpreted depends on the instrument, the informant and the study design.
^f23-limit

## Analyst cautions (AI synthesis)

These paragraphs are this library's own appraisal, not statements from WHO.

A functioning score summarises reported activity and participation; it is not a direct measure of symptom severity, capacity or potential, and it should not be read as a verdict about a person. This library uses the framework to ask better outcome questions, not to benchmark anyone.
^f23-caution-impairment
