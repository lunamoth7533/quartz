# Higher Cortical Functions: Language

**Publisher.** Neuroscience Online, McGovern Medical School (UTHealth), Section 4 Chapter 8

**Source.** <https://nba.uth.tmc.edu/neuroscience/m/s4/chapter08.html>

**Verification.** text opened and checked on 2026-09-25; retrieval saved as `receipts/source-checks/F110.txt`. Educational or official reference material, not primary research.

## Key points (as published)

Broca's 1861 work localised a language function to a frontal region, and the chapter traces how that claim was made and contested.
^f110-history

The classical Wernicke model connects a receptive and an expressive centre through a pathway, and the chapter shows how lesion locations relate to aphasia types.
^f110-model

The chapter presents the classical model as historically decisive but incomplete: language functions are distributed and individual lesions do not map cleanly to syndromes.
^f110-limits

## Scope and limitations

A textbook chapter on the history and structure of language localisation models.
^f110-limit
