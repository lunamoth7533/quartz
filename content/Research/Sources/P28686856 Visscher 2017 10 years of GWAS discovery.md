# 10 Years of GWAS Discovery: Biology, Function, and Translation

**Authors.** Visscher PM, Wray NR, Zhang Q, Sklar P, McCarthy MI, Brown MA, Yang J. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28686856/)

**Verification.** Free full text read on PubMed Central on 2026-09-24. Blocks are original paraphrases of the review's statements.

## Reported findings (checked full text)

GWAS produced roughly ten thousand robust trait-variant associations across hundreds of traits.
^p28686856-discovery

Complex traits are highly polygenic; individual common-disease odds ratios are often near 1.01.
^p28686856-polygenic

"Missing heritability" narrowed as samples grew: height variants explained more than twenty percent of heritability by 2014.
^p28686856-heritability

Polygenic scores apply discovery-sample variant effects in independent samples, and accuracy depends mainly on discovery sample size.
^p28686856-prs

Power depends on the number of loci, their effect sizes, sample size, genotyping panel and trait measurement.
^p28686856-design

The review covers prediction, causal inference such as Mendelian randomisation, and biological follow-up.
^p28686856-translation

## Scope as reported

A review of the first decade of GWAS, dominated by studies in European-ancestry samples, reporting population-level statistical associations. Individual effect estimates and scores are distributional properties of studied populations, not clinical predictions.
^p28686856-limit

## Analyst cautions (AI synthesis)

These paragraphs are this library's own appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A polygenic score summarises average risks across a sample. Turning it into a statement about one person requires calibration and validation in a comparable population, evidence for decision benefit, and a clinical pathway that does not exist yet for most conditions in this vault.
^p28686856-caution-individual
