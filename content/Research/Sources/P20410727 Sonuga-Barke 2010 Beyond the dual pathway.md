# Beyond the dual pathway model: evidence for the dissociation of timing, inhibitory, and delay-related impairments in attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder

**Authors.** Sonuga-Barke E, Bitsakou P, Thompson M. · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20410727/)

**Published.** 2010.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jaac.2009.12.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2009.12.018) · PMID [20410727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20410727/)

**Verification.** Retrieved as `europepmc-abstract` on 2026-09-25 and checked at the level of the abstract (`receipts/source-checks/P20410727.txt`).

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Children with ADHD, their siblings and non-ADHD controls completed nine tasks tapping inhibitory control, delay aversion and temporal processing.
^p20410727-design

Temporal processing, inhibitory control and delay-related deficits behaved as independent neuropsychological components, with ADHD children differing from controls on all three.
^p20410727-finding

The result supports heterogeneity in ADHD's cognitive profile rather than a single core deficit, which is why the paper proposes moving beyond the dual pathway model.
^p20410727-implication
