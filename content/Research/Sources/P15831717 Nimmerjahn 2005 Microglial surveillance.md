# Resting microglial cells are highly dynamic surveillants of brain parenchyma in vivo

**Authors.** Nimmerjahn A, Kirchhoff F, Helmchen F. · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15831717/)

**Published.** 2005.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1126/science.1110647](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1110647) · PMID [15831717](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15831717/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Two-photon imaging in living cortex showed that microglia in their so-called resting state continually extend and retract processes to survey their surroundings.
^p15831717-surveillance

Blood-brain barrier disruption produced immediate, focal microglial activation, switching behaviour from surveillance to shielding the injured site.
^p15831717-response

The result revised the view that resting microglia are quiescent, which changed how immune surveillance of the brain is described.
^p15831717-revision
