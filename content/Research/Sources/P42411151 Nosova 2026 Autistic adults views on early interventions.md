# The Views of Autistic Adults on Early Autism Interventions

**Authors.** Nosova E, Sturrock A, Humphrey N, Leadbitter K. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411151/)

**Published.** 2026 in Autism: the international journal of research and practice.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613261460931](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613261460931) · PMID [42411151](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411151/) · PMCID [PMC13392167](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13392167/)

**Verification.** Open-access full text read through Europe PMC on 2026-09-24. Blocks are original paraphrases of the review and the studies it summarises.

## Reported findings (checked full text)

A mixed-methods systematic review examined autistic adults' views of early autism interventions across 19 studies.
^p42411151-scope

Autistic adults endorsed outcomes of well-being, adult support and accessibility, and reducing harmful behaviours; they did not endorse interventions aimed at reducing autism traits.
^p42411151-outcomes

They described tailoring support to the individual, respecting authentic autistic communication, changing environments and reducing demands, and supporting the wider community.
^p42411151-support

Negative consequences were described from being taught to hide autistic characteristics, which the review discusses as camouflaging or masking.
^p42411151-masking

The reviewers conclude that early interventions should be autism-affirmative, developmentally appropriate and foster self-determination.
^p42411151-conclusion

## Scope as reported

A systematic review of qualitative and mixed-methods studies. Participants were mostly adults commenting retrospectively on childhood interventions, the included studies used varied methods, and the review is recent (2026), so its findings describe priorities rather than intervention effects.
^p42411151-limit

## Analyst cautions (AI synthesis)

These paragraphs are this library's own appraisal, not statements from the review.

Reported views inform which outcomes matter to autistic adults; they are not measurements of whether a specific intervention works, and the retrospective design means recall and selection shape what was reported.
^p42411151-caution
