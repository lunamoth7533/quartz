## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The paper argues that the probability a claimed finding is true depends on prior odds, study power, the number of tested relationships and bias, not on the p value alone.
^p16060722-probability

It identifies settings where false positives are more likely: small studies, small effects, many tested hypotheses and flexible designs.
^p16060722-conditions

The argument is analytic rather than empirical; its influence comes from reframing how published significance should be interpreted.
^p16060722-influence
