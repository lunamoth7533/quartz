# Module 08 - ADHD

**Lessons.** 6 · **Canvas.** [[Learning Map - ADHD.canvas]] · **Entry point.** [[Learning Hub]]

## What this module is for

Lifespan course, measurement, prefrontal catecholamine mechanisms, medication evidence and what short-term trials can and cannot say about long-term outcomes.

ADHD applies the foundations to a lifespan condition defined by persistent, impairing inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity. The module concentrates on measurement, mechanism and what short-term randomised evidence can actually say.

## Learning objectives

1. Describe the lifespan course and the difference between clinical and community samples.
2. Explain how ADHD is measured, including the role of the rater and the absence of a confirmatory test.
3. Explain the prefrontal catecholamine account and its inverted-U logic.
4. Read the medication syntheses with attention to comparison structure, confidence and outcomes.
5. Distinguish short-term symptom evidence from long-term functional questions.

## Core and advanced branches

**Core sequence**

- Read course and measurement first, then the catecholamine lesson, then the three evidence lessons.
- For every treatment claim, note the outcome, the rater, the time point and the comparison before judging it.

**Advanced branch**

- Compare the 2018, 2025 and 2026 syntheses and explain how their questions differ rather than which is 'best'.
- Design the outcome set you would want in a two-year adult ADHD trial and justify each measure.

## Common confusions

- Group rankings are not prescribing guidance.
- Averages hide who benefits and who does not.
- Quality of life is not automatically improved when core symptoms improve.

## Module assessment

Answer from memory first; the parent lesson holds the supporting detail.

1. Why does the rater matter when judging an ADHD treatment result?
   - *Working answer.* Clinician-rated and self-rated outcomes can disagree, and classroom or workplace informants add another perspective; the rater is part of the measurement.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI synthesis: CBT for ADHD rater caution]]

2. What is the strongest and weakest part of the ADHD medication evidence base?
   - *Working answer.* Short-term randomised comparisons of core symptoms are relatively strong; long-term functional and safety evidence is thin, with insufficient data at 26 and 52 weeks in the 2018 synthesis.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]; [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

3. Explain the inverted-U in one sentence without giving any dosing guidance.
   - *Working answer.* Prefrontal function depends on an intermediate level of catecholamine signalling, so both too little and too much arousal-related signalling are described as impairing performance.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-catecholamines|Prefrontal catecholamine mechanisms]]
