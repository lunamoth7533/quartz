# Module 15 - Clinical Psychiatry and Psychopathology

**Lessons.** 14 · **Entry point.** [[Learning Path]]

## What this module is for

The vocabulary and construct families of clinical psychiatry: how a symptom becomes a syndrome and then a
diagnosis, the categorical-versus-dimensional argument that runs underneath every category in this domain, the
major construct families (mood, anxiety, OCD, psychosis, dissociative, personality and borderline personality
disorder, eating, substance-related), the network and developmental formulations that read a presentation
without a single hidden cause, emotion dysregulation as a dimension that cuts across diagnoses, and suicide and
self-harm read at the level evidence actually supports: group risk, not individual prediction.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish a symptom, a syndrome and a diagnosis, and state what a classification system does and does not
   establish.
2. Explain the case for categorical and for dimensional classification, and name when each framework's
   assumptions hold.
3. Describe the central differential in each major construct family covered here - bipolar boundary, fear versus
   anxiety, reinforcement in OCD, symptom domains in psychosis, instrument-dependence in dissociation, bipolar II
   and complex PTSD and autism in borderline personality disorder, medical risk and comorbidity in eating
   disorders, and the separated constructs of substance use.
4. Read a network or developmental formulation for what mechanism and level it actually claims, and what it
   leaves open.
5. Recognise emotion dysregulation as a dimension reported across ADHD, bipolar disorder, autism and complex
   PTSD rather than a feature owned by one diagnosis.
6. Separate three different questions in any suicide or self-harm claim: raised group risk, individual
   prediction, and intervention effect.

## Core and advanced branches

**Core sequence**

- Read the first two lessons together: they supply the vocabulary (symptom, syndrome, diagnosis) and the
  framework argument (categorical versus dimensional) that every later construct-family lesson assumes.
- For every construct family, find its stated central differential before reading its treatment or mechanism
  claims.

**Advanced branch**

- Compare how the network formulation (lesson 12) and the emotion-dysregulation dimension (lesson 13) each
  argue against a single-diagnosis-owns-this-feature reading, and state what evidence would distinguish a shared
  mechanism from a shared questionnaire.
- Take one construct family's differential (bipolar II versus borderline personality disorder, or the autism-
  eating-disorder overlap) and list what additional study design would sharpen it.

## Common confusions

- A diagnostic label is mistaken for a mechanism, when it is built for communication, planning and research
  eligibility.
- A formulation - network or developmental - is mistaken for proof of a single cause.
- A group-level risk finding, in suicide research or anywhere else in this module, is mistaken for something
  that can sort individuals.
- A score or trait taken during an acute state (a mood episode, starvation, a crisis) is read as a stable
  feature of the person.

## Module assessment

Answer from memory first; the parent lesson holds the supporting detail.

1. Why do arbitrary thresholds and unclear boundaries count as documented costs of categorical classification
   rather than reasons to discard it?
   - *Working answer.* Categories still let services and trials define who is studied and let clinicians share a
     common label; the costs are real but are traded off against those functions, which is why hybrid models add
     dimensional measures rather than abandoning categories.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]]

2. Why can't a suicide risk factor that multiplies relative risk several times over be used to flag one person
   with confidence?
   - *Working answer.* Because most conditions this library covers have a low base rate, a large relative
     increase still leaves absolute risk small for most flagged individuals, and fifty years of research show
     prediction only slightly better than chance.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P27841450 Franklin 2017 Risk factors for suicidal thoughts and behaviors#^p27841450-caution-base-rate|Appraisal: Franklin 2017]]

3. Name two features that discriminate bipolar II disorder from borderline personality disorder better than
   impulsivity does.
   - *Working answer.* Family history and developmental antecedents discriminate better; illness course and how
     mood states are experienced also do, while impulsivity, neuropsychological profile and treatment response
     discriminate poorly.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P31749106 Bayes 2019 Bipolar II versus borderline personality disorder#^p31749106-discriminators|Bayes 2019]]

## Source boundaries

Every lesson names the access level and reading status of its sources where the parent concept note does. Several
lessons rely on meta-analyses or reviews whose own authors flag high heterogeneity, sparse comparisons, or
single cross-sectional samples; where a claim rests on such a source, the lesson says so rather than presenting
a pooled estimate as settled. No lesson in this module gives dosing, titration or individual treatment guidance,
and the suicide and self-harm lesson carries no detail about methods of self-harm or suicide.
