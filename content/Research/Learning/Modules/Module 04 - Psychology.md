# Module 04 - Psychology

**Lessons.** 5 · **Canvas.** [[Learning Map - Psychology.canvas]] · **Entry point.** [[Learning Hub]]

## What this module is for

Attention, learning, memory, emotion regulation and the evidence base for psychological treatment in general.

Psychology covers the processes the condition modules refer to constantly: attention and executive function, learning, memory, emotion regulation and the general evidence base for psychological treatment. It is the bridge between mechanism and clinical research.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain attention and executive function as control processes with narrow laboratory proxies.
2. Describe classical and operant conditioning, and why extinction is new learning rather than erasure.
3. Distinguish encoding, storage and retrieval failures and the reconstructive character of recall.
4. Explain emotion regulation as a set of context-dependent processes, including appraisal.
5. State what a therapy trial measures and the limits of symptom-scale and rater-dependent outcomes.

## Common confusions

- Extinction does not delete a memory; it adds competing learning.
- Confidence is not accuracy in recall.
- 'CBT' is a family of protocols, not a single treatment.

## Module assessment

Answer from memory first; the parent lesson holds the supporting detail.

1. Why does the rater matter in a psychological treatment trial?
   - *Working answer.* Clinician-rated and self-rated outcomes can disagree, and each is subject to different biases, so a benefit claim should name who reported it and how.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI synthesis: CBT for ADHD rater caution]]

2. What does the extinction finding imply for planning behaviour change?
   - *Working answer.* Old associations can return in new contexts or after stress, so plans need ongoing practice and context variation rather than a single 'finished' moment.

3. Give one reason a laboratory attention task may not predict daily functioning.
   - *Working answer.* The task isolates a narrow process under controlled conditions and does not capture motivation, context, strategy or the many demands of daily life.
