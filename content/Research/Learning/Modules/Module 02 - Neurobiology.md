# Module 02 - Neurobiology

**Lessons.** 8 · **Canvas.** [[Learning Map - Neurobiology.canvas]] · **Entry point.** [[Learning Hub]]

## What this module is for

Cells, membranes, spikes, synapses, plasticity, networks and the genetic background that the chemistry and clinical modules build on.

Neurobiology supplies the physical vocabulary for everything else: how cells hold a voltage, how a spike is produced, how synapses change, how circuits are organised, and what genes can and cannot explain. The module deliberately separates the mechanism from the mental vocabulary that later modules use.

## Learning objectives

1. Describe a neuron's parts and the role of myelin, and why grey and white matter are tissue descriptions rather than functions.
2. Explain how ion gradients produce a resting potential and how a threshold produces an all-or-none spike.
3. Distinguish short-term transmission from long-term plasticity, including LTP, LTD and metaplasticity.
4. Describe brain organisation in terms of networks and hubs rather than single structures.
5. Explain what heritability and polygenic findings do and do not license.

## Common confusions

- An all-or-none spike does not mean all-or-none signalling; graded potentials and release rates carry information.
- A thinner cortex or a bigger structure is not automatically 'less' or 'more' function.
- Heritability is a population statistic, not a personal proportion.

## Module assessment

Answer from memory first; the parent lesson holds the supporting detail.

1. Why does an action potential keep its amplitude along an axon while a graded potential fades?
   - *Working answer.* The spike is regenerated by voltage-gated channels along the membrane, so it carries full strength; graded potentials spread passively and decay with distance.
   - *Answer sources.* [[F03 OpenStax The action potential#^f03-signals|The Action Potential]]

2. What is the single most important limitation of slice-based plasticity evidence for human learning?
   - *Working answer.* The preparation is reduced and often non-human; it constrains mechanism but does not show what any person learned, and human proxy measures still need to demonstrate the defining features.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Long-term potentiation and long-term depression]]

3. State one claim that heritability data can support and one it cannot.
   - *Working answer.* It can support statements about how much variation in a studied population is associated with genetic differences; it cannot say how much of one person's trait is genetic.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-heritability|10 Years of GWAS Discovery]]
