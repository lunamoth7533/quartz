# Module 01 - Research Methods and Evidence Literacy

**Lessons.** 11 · **Canvas.** [[Learning Map - Research Methods.canvas]] · **Entry point.** [[Learning Hub]]

## What this module is for

How designs, measurement, statistics and synthesis shape what a study can show. Start here: every later module assumes these tools.

Research literacy is the module that makes the other nine readable. It teaches how to tell a design from a result, how measurement and statistics shape what a study can show, and how to hold a review at the right distance. Nothing later in the curriculum requires previous biology, which is why this module comes first.

## Learning objectives

1. State what a study design can and cannot support, including the difference between association, prediction and causation.
2. Read an effect together with its uncertainty, its absolute and relative forms, and the population it came from.
3. Judge a measurement by its validity, reliability and rater rather than by its familiarity.
4. Explain why a systematic review inherits the biases of its studies and adds its own choices.
5. Describe how genetic inference moves from heritability to polygenic scores without becoming personal prediction.

## Common confusions

- A statistically significant result is not an important result; significance is about compatibility with a model, importance is about magnitude and consequence.
- Reliability is not validity: a consistent measure can consistently miss the target.
- A systematic review is a method with a paper trail, not a verdict.

## Module assessment

Answer from memory first; the parent lesson holds the supporting detail.

1. A trial reports a 40% relative reduction with a 1% absolute reduction. What does each number mean, and which would you put in a summary sentence?
   - *Working answer.* The relative number describes proportional change against baseline risk; the absolute number describes how many people are affected. A useful sentence carries both, plus the baseline risk.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P26952180 Ranganathan 2016 Absolute and relative risk#^p26952180-measures|Absolute and relative risk]]

2. Why can a measure with excellent internal consistency still be unusable for your question?
   - *Working answer.* Reliability concerns consistency; validity concerns whether the scores mean what you need in your population and purpose. High alpha does not supply content or criterion validity.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-validity|Developing and Validating Scales]]

3. Name two ways a meta-analysis can reach a confident conclusion that is still wrong.
   - *Working answer.* It can inherit biased or selectively reported included studies, and it can add review-level choices such as outcome selection, exclusion decisions or ignoring heterogeneity and publication bias.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-misleading|Mass production of systematic reviews]]
