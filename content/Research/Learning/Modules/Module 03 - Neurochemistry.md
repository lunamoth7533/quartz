# Module 03 - Neurochemistry

**Lessons.** 11 · **Canvas.** [[Learning Map - Neurochemistry.canvas]] · **Entry point.** [[Learning Hub]]

## What this module is for

Transmitters, receptors, balance, stress physiology and autonomic regulation, with the chemical-imbalance framing kept as a misconception to reason about rather than a model to teach.

Neurochemistry covers how signals are transmitted, how the same molecule performs different jobs, and how the body regulates itself through stress and autonomic systems. The module keeps the chemical-imbalance framing as a worked misconception rather than a teaching model.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain the synaptic signalling model and where drugs or disease could act on it.
2. Describe at least two transmitters with different jobs in different circuits.
3. Explain excitation and inhibition as a balance at the integration point rather than a global quantity.
4. Describe the HPA axis, the fast sympathetic response and what cortisol does and does not measure.
5. Explain why a single autonomic measurement cannot be read as stress or as one branch's activity.

## Common confusions

- Transmitter names are not moods; the same molecule does different jobs in different circuits.
- Cortisol is not a synonym for stress.
- A heart-rate-variability ratio is not a direct measure of autonomic balance.

## Module assessment

Answer from memory first; the parent lesson holds the supporting detail.

1. Give two ways a drug could change synaptic signalling without changing how much transmitter is released.
   - *Working answer.* It could act on postsynaptic receptors as an agonist or antagonist, or it could block reuptake or degradation to change how long the transmitter stays active.
   - *Answer sources.* [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]

2. Why is 'excitation and inhibition balance' easy to say and hard to measure?
   - *Working answer.* It is a local, dynamic relationship between inputs at a integration point; most measurements are indirect and a difference in one circuit says little about the whole brain.
   - *Answer sources.* [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-caution-summation|AI synthesis: Communication Between Neurons]]

3. What three things would you need before interpreting a single HRV measurement?
   - *Working answer.* Recording length and context, individual factors such as age, posture and breathing, and a comparison value from a comparable population or from the same person over time.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-context|Heart Rate Variability Metrics and Norms]]
