# Module 07 - Bipolar Disorders

**Lessons.** 21 · **Canvas.** [[Learning Map - Bipolar Disorders.canvas]] · **Entry point.** [[Learning Path]]

## What this module is for

Course and phases, treatment evidence by phase, lithium's mechanism and uncertainty, the dopamine hypothesis, genomic findings and imaging limits, mania and its psychotic features, hypomania and the bipolar II course, cyclothymia and the bipolar spectrum, rapid cycling and mixed features, bipolar II's own treatment evidence, the contested antidepressant question, mood monitoring and early warning signs, and the circadian, immune and cellular-energy hypotheses.

Bipolar I and bipolar II are the first condition module. It applies the foundations to two related diagnoses - one defined by mania, the other by hypomania and major depression - each with a lifelong episodic course, where treatment evidence is organised by phase and by type, and where mechanism stories, imaging, genomics and course specifiers each have visible limits.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain how bipolar I is defined by mania and bipolar II by hypomania and major depression, and how each course shapes treatment logic.
2. Describe how phase-specific evidence is assembled and why efficacy, safety and tolerability are weighed together.
3. Explain lithium's multi-pathway action and the difference between mechanism uncertainty and clinical uncertainty.
4. Evaluate the dopamine hypothesis as a research framework rather than a test.
5. Read the genomic and imaging findings at group level without converting them into personal claims.
6. Distinguish bipolar II's own treatment evidence from findings extrapolated from bipolar I, including the contested antidepressant and rapid-cycling questions.
7. Read the circadian, immune and cellular-energy hypotheses for what they actually establish - association or mechanism plausibility - rather than as proven causes.

## Common confusions

- Mechanism uncertainty does not mean the treatment lacks evidence.
- A group cortical difference is not a diagnostic finding.
- Genetic architecture is statistical, not destiny.

## Module assessment

Answer from memory first; the parent lesson holds the supporting detail.

1. Why is treatment evidence presented by phase rather than as one recommendation?
   - *Working answer.* Acute mania, acute depression and maintenance have different evidence bases, goals and risk profiles, so a single pooled recommendation would blend incompatible questions.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

2. What does the component analysis add beyond 'therapy helps'?
   - *Working answer.* It estimates average contributions of protocol elements across trials, which helps generate hypotheses about active ingredients but cannot identify what caused one person's improvement.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-caution-ingredients|Appraisal: adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

3. Restate the ENIGMA cortical finding as a group claim without using causal language.
   - *Working answer.* Across 6503 individuals, the bipolar group showed thinner cortex on average in frontal, temporal and parietal regions, with medication exposure and illness duration associated with the same measures.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings]]
