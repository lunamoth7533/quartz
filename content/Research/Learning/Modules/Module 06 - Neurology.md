# Module 06 - Neurology

**Lessons.** 5 · **Canvas.** [[Learning Map - Neurology.canvas]] · **Entry point.** [[Learning Hub]]

## What this module is for

Organisation of the nervous system, the clinical examination, and how structural and functional measures differ in what they can answer.

Neurology covers the organisation of the nervous system and the measurement tools that appear in the condition modules. Its central lesson is that every measure answers a narrow question, and that clinical examination and imaging are complementary rather than competing.

## Learning objectives

1. Describe the central and peripheral divisions and the sensory, integrative and motor flows.
2. Explain what a neurological examination contributes before technology is ordered.
3. Distinguish structural, functional and electrophysiological measures.
4. Explain why a group-level imaging difference is not an individual test.
5. Interpret a finding using the measure's spatial and temporal resolution.

## Common confusions

- Anatomical divisions are teaching aids; function crosses them.
- 'MRI' names a family of methods, not one measure.
- A robust group difference can be useless for one person.

## Module assessment

Answer from memory first; the parent lesson holds the supporting detail.

1. Why does a clinician order imaging after a focal examination finding rather than before?
   - *Working answer.* History and examination generate a question; imaging is then chosen to answer that specific question, which changes what is ordered and how the result is interpreted.
   - *Answer sources.* [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

2. What makes a group imaging difference unsuitable as a personal test?
   - *Working answer.* It is an average difference with small effect size relative to individual variation, entangled with treatment and illness-duration confounds, and it was never calibrated for individual classification.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings]]

3. Which measure would you use to study the timing of neural activity, and why?
   - *Working answer.* EEG, because it records summed electrical activity with millisecond resolution, whereas structural MRI is anatomical and fMRI follows slower blood-flow-related signals.
   - *Answer sources.* [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-eeg|Brain Imaging (Psychology 2e, section 3.4)]]
