# Module 09 - Autism

**Lessons.** 5 · **Canvas.** [[Learning Map - Autism.canvas]] · **Entry point.** [[Learning Hub]]

## What this module is for

Heterogeneity and support needs, rare-variant genetics and the limits of genetic inference, and how support and services are organised and evaluated.

Autism applies the foundations to a heterogeneous developmental condition where support needs vary widely, genetics includes rare and common contributions, and much of the intervention evidence comes from children. The module is explicit about whose outcomes are being measured.

## Learning objectives

1. Describe autism as a developmental construct with wide variation in features and support needs.
2. Explain how rare variants and common variation contribute to risk, and what a risk gene does not determine.
3. Explain why co-occurrence with ADHD and other conditions matters for assessment and support.
4. Describe how support and services are organised and why local evidence matters.
5. Explain how adult assessment proceeds from screening to a comprehensive, multi-source evaluation, and why a screen is not a diagnosis.
6. Describe environmental and communication accommodations as access design rather than treatment.
7. Identify whose priorities shape outcome measures in autism research.

## Common confusions

- A risk gene does not determine a person's traits or support needs.
- Child intervention findings do not automatically transfer to adults.
- Co-occurrence is not causation and not a lay diagnosis.

## Module assessment

Answer from memory first; the parent lesson holds the supporting detail.

1. Why is 'autism varies widely' a scientifically important statement rather than a platitude?
   - *Working answer.* Because samples, outcomes and support needs differ so much that group averages and single mechanisms have limited reach; it constrains what any study of 'autism' can generalise.

2. What does finding a risk gene license you to say?
   - *Working answer.* That variants in the gene are statistically enriched in the studied samples; it does not classify an individual's variant or determine their traits, strengths or support needs.
   - *Answer sources.* [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-caution-risk|AI synthesis: autism exome sequencing study]]

3. Name two reasons local evidence matters for support questions.
   - *Working answer.* Service availability, funding and eligibility differ by country and region, and outcomes that matter locally may not be measured in international trials.
   - *Answer sources.* [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-limit|NICE CG142]]
