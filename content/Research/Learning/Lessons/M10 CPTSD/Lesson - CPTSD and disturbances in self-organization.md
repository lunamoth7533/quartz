# CPTSD and disturbances in self-organization

**Module.** M10 CPTSD · **Topic.** [[CPTSD and disturbances in self-organization]]

## Why this matters

Complex PTSD is the vault's clearest example of a construct that exists in one classification and not another.
Understanding what the diagnosis adds — and what it does not claim about trauma exposure — is the foundation for
reading the treatment evidence in this module.

## The core model

Start with the ICD-11 PTSD criteria, because complex PTSD is defined as those criteria plus more. The
stress-disorders working group selected three essential fear-related symptom clusters: reliving in the here and
now, avoidance of trauma material, and a heightened sense of current threat.
[[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-core-clusters|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

Complex PTSD is then diagnosed when PTSD criteria are met in addition to disturbances in self-organization.
Qualifying exposure is part of the PTSD criteria, so exposure is necessary but not sufficient: the symptom
pattern, its duration and impairment also matter. The type or duration of trauma is not determinative — a
specific trauma type is not required, and prolonged complex trauma is treated as a risk factor.
[[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-exposure-required|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

The self-organization disturbances cover affect regulation, a persistently negative self-concept and
relational difficulties.
[[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-dso|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

The construct is intended to describe the more complex reactions typical of chronic trauma exposure.
[[P32345416 Cloitre 2020 ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial#^p32345416-aim|ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial]]

Two boundary facts keep the construct precise. Current definitions are symptom-based: trauma type or duration
alone does not determine the diagnosis, and the same exposure can be followed by no disorder, PTSD, or complex
PTSD.
[[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]
And the three domains come from the ICD-11 criteria and the VA overview rather than from the introductory
editorial, which is a provenance note rather than a scientific claim.
[[P32345416 Cloitre 2020 ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial#^p32345416-caution-domains|AI synthesis: ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial]]

In practice the three domains overlap: affect dysregulation, negative self-concept and relational difficulty
feed one another, and instruments operationalize them differently across studies. That is a live measurement
question, not a reason to abandon the construct.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Two people have both experienced prolonged trauma. One meets criteria
for PTSD only; the other has PTSD symptoms plus severe affect dysregulation, a persistently negative
self-concept and difficulty in relationships.

Apply the construct. The difference is not the trauma history, which is similar, but the symptom pattern: the
second person's presentation includes the self-organization disturbances, and the vignette is written so that
both people meet the core PTSD criteria and share a qualifying exposure. The construct says that this pattern
is coherent and clinically meaningful, and the treatment literature in this module is organised around it. What
the construct does not say is that trauma duration caused those domains in that person, or that everyone with
prolonged exposure develops them.

Note the status of the vignette: it illustrates what the DSO domains add, and it is not a completed assessment.
Diagnosis requires the full criteria, a qualified assessment and a person's actual history, and this library
does not diagnose anyone.

## Common confusions

- "Complex PTSD means more trauma." It describes a symptom pattern that is more likely with sustained trauma
  but is not defined by trauma type or duration; qualifying exposure is still required by the PTSD criteria.
- "The three domains are separate scales." They overlap in practice and are operationalized differently across
  instruments.
- "CPTSD replaces PTSD." ICD-11 includes both, with complex PTSD requiring the core clusters plus the
  self-organization disturbances.

## What the sources do not establish

The VA page is an official overview and the editorial is commentary without data; neither establishes
prevalence, prognosis or treatment for an individual, and this vault holds no complex-PTSD-specific biological
study. Nothing here supports self-diagnosis.

## Check yourself

1. What does ICD-11 complex PTSD add to the core PTSD clusters?
2. Name the three self-organization domains.
3. Why does trauma exposure alone not determine the diagnosis?

## Answer notes

1. Disturbances in self-organization.
2. Affect regulation difficulties, a persistently negative self-concept, and relational difficulties.
3. Because the definition is symptom-based: qualifying exposure is necessary, but the symptom pattern,
   duration and impairment have to be met as well, and trauma type or duration alone decides nothing.
