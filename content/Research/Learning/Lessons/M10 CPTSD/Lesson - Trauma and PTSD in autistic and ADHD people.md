# Trauma and PTSD in autistic and ADHD people

**Module.** M10 CPTSD · **Topic.** [[Trauma and PTSD in autistic and ADHD people]]

## Why this matters

This lesson closes the module by putting autism, ADHD, and PTSD or complex PTSD in one frame - the clearest
place to practise this curriculum's recurring rule: before comparing a rate or treatment effect, name the
sample, instrument and classification system, since here those choices move the numbers the most.

## The core model

**Recorded and screening rates diverge in autism.** Diagnosed PTSD in diagnosed autistic samples sits near
general-population estimates, yet screening-tool studies report heavier symptom loads. The gap is itself the
finding: either criteria and services under-recognise trauma in autistic people, or screening over-counts it,
and current designs cannot say which.
[[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-discrepancy|Mansour 2025]]
[[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-caution-underdiagnosis|Appraisal: Mansour 2025]]

**Vulnerability traced forward in time.** In a twin cohort, childhood autistic traits predicted PTSD at 18 even
among children already exposed to trauma - not explained by exposure alone. A separate survey found negative
post-trauma appraisals of alienation, shame and fear more common in autistic adults, statistically carrying part
of their higher severity, though that decomposes cross-sectional data rather than showing a cause.
[[P40133042 Quinton 2025 Autistic traits and later PTSD#^p40133042-exposed|Quinton 2025]]
[[P41454656 Prosser 2026 Trauma appraisals in autistic adults#^p41454656-severity|Prosser 2026]]

**ADHD and PTSD travel together, unevenly.** Diagnosed co-occurrence is more common in females in adult samples,
echoing the earlier ADHD-in-women lesson. In combat-deployed veterans, ADHD was about twice as common among
those with current PTSD.
[[P40252259 Wilson 2025 Sex differences in ADHD and PTSD#^p40252259-age|Wilson 2025]]
[[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-ptsd|Knight 2025]]

**Why assessment is harder.** In those veterans, ADHD self-report measures agreed poorly with each other and
with prior diagnosis - poor concentration can be ADHD, PTSD, or both. Adult autism assessment already lists
neurodevelopmental, mental, physical, communication and sensory explanations in its differential, the same
ground a trauma assessment covers.
[[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-measures|Knight 2025]]
[[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-differential|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]]
What counts as trauma also moves who is counted: autistic adults report more events, including ones like
bullying, that sit outside the formal exposure criterion.
[[P41454656 Prosser 2026 Trauma appraisals in autistic adults#^p41454656-criterion|Prosser 2026]]

**Adapting treatment.** In general PTSD trials, treatment worked as well after multiple traumatic events as
after one, removing a reason to assume heavily-exposed groups benefit less - but those trials were not selected
for autism or ADHD, and adaptations for these groups remain to be validated.
[[P38219762 Hoppen 2024 Single versus multiple trauma treatment#^p38219762-conclusion|Hoppen 2024]]

**Reading rule.** Before comparing rates or effects here, name whether the sample is diagnosed or trait-defined,
which events counted as trauma, which instrument and informant were used, and which classification system
applied.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A hypothetical autistic adult reports shutdowns and poor concentration
after a frightening job loss that might not meet a formal trauma definition. A clinician weighs autism-related
overload, untreated ADHD, or a trauma response.

The person's own sense that the loss was traumatic does not fail a formal check by itself, since such events are
often described as traumatic outside the strict definition. Concentration difficulty overlaps ADHD and PTSD, and
self-report ADHD measures agreed poorly with each other in a trauma-exposed sample, so one questionnaire should
not settle it. No source here has tested a trauma treatment adapted for this combination, so a confident
recommendation would overreach.

The accurate position names the overlap and the assessment gap rather than defaulting to one label.

## Common confusions

- "Low recorded PTSD rates in autistic people mean less impact." Screening finds heavier symptom loads; the
  discrepancy is unresolved, not evidence of lower impact.
- "Overlapping symptoms mean the categories are interchangeable." They remain distinct categories sharing
  surface features, which is why a careful differential matters.
- "General PTSD trials already prove treatment works for autistic or ADHD people." Those trials were not
  selected for either group.

## What the sources do not establish

No source settles whether low recorded prevalence reflects under-recognition, over-inclusive screening, or
both, or whether events outside the formal exposure criterion should be counted. None tests a trauma treatment
adapted specifically for ADHD.

## Answer notes

1. Diagnosed PTSD in diagnosed autistic samples looks close to general-population rates, while screening finds
   heavier symptom loads; current designs cannot say whether that is under-recognition or over-inclusive
   screening.
2. They agreed poorly with each other and with a prior diagnosis, so a symptom like poor concentration could be
   ADHD, PTSD, or both.
3. It removes the assumption that multiply-exposed people automatically benefit less from treatment; it does
   not establish this for autistic or ADHD samples, which those trials did not select for.
