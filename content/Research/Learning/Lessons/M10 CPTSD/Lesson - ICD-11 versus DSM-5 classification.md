# ICD-11 versus DSM-5 classification

**Module.** M10 CPTSD · **Topic.** [[ICD-11 versus DSM-5 classification]]

## Why this matters

Classification is not administrative detail: it decides who enters a study, which changes every number that
follows. This lesson is the module's methodology spine, and it is why the treatment lessons here warn about
sample definitions before they discuss effects.

## The core model

ICD-11 includes complex PTSD as a separate diagnosis; DSM-5 does not use the separate diagnosis.
[[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

A validity review reports support for the proposed three-factor PTSD structure, the six-factor complex PTSD
structure, and the distinction between them.
[[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-structure|ICD-11 PTSD and complex PTSD proposals]]

ICD-11 complex PTSD identifies a group with more multiple and sustained traumas and greater functional
impairment than PTSD alone.
[[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-impairment|ICD-11 PTSD and complex PTSD proposals]]

The review also estimates lower ICD-11 PTSD prevalence in adults than older systems, using DSM-based items,
which the authors flag as a measurement caveat rather than a settled finding.
[[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-prevalence|ICD-11 PTSD and complex PTSD proposals]]

And the boundary between PTSD and complex PTSD remains sensitive to measurement development.
[[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-caution-boundary|AI synthesis: ICD-11 PTSD and complex PTSD proposals]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Two trials both claim to study "complex PTSD". Trial A recruits people
meeting ICD-11 complex PTSD criteria. Trial B recruits adults with PTSD and a history of prolonged trauma,
using DSM-5, where complex PTSD is not a separate diagnosis.

Compare them correctly. The samples are defined by different constructs, so baseline severity, symptom
configuration and functional impairment may differ. Trial B may include people who would not meet ICD-11
complex PTSD criteria and may exclude people who would. The treatment protocols may also be adapted to their
definitions of the problem.

The conclusion is not that one trial is invalid, but that the pooled or compared numbers are about different
populations, and any summary must say so. The coverage matrix records this as a real limit of the current
literature rather than a problem with either study.

## Common confusions

- "Both systems describe the same patients." They define overlapping but different groups.
- "ICD-11 is more severe by definition." Its criteria select a more impaired group on average; that is an
  empirical finding, not a definitional guarantee.
- "Classification debates are academic." They determine who is studied, treated and counted.

## What the sources do not establish

The validity review was written before ICD-11's final release and uses studies testing proposals; the VA page
is an overview. Neither provides a harmonised crosswalk between the systems, and this vault holds no study
comparing matched samples under both.
