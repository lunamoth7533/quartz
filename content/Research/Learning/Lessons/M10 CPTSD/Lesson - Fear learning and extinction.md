# Fear learning and extinction

**Module.** M10 CPTSD · **Topic.** [[Fear learning and extinction]]

## Why this matters

Exposure-based therapy, the psychological approach named first-line in most PTSD guidelines, is built directly
on this laboratory model. Understanding what extinction is - and is not - explains both why the treatment is
expected to work and why a return of fear afterwards is not, by itself, a sign that it failed.

## The core model

**Acquisition.** Classical conditioning pairs a neutral cue with a meaningful stimulus so the cue comes to evoke
a response on its own. In fear conditioning the stimulus is aversive, the conditioned response is measurable in
behaviour and autonomic signals, and the paradigm models how emotional associations form.
[[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-pairing|Classical Conditioning]]

**Extinction is new learning, not erasure.** Extinction reduces the conditioned response through repeated
presentation of the cue without the outcome, and the fact that the response can return is exactly what shows
extinction is new, competing learning rather than deletion of the original memory. Fear can return through
spontaneous recovery over time, reinstatement after a new aversive event, or renewal when the context changes.
[[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-extinction|Classical Conditioning]]
[[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-newlearning|Milad 2012]]

**Neural basis.** Amygdala circuits support threat learning, connected to cortical, thalamic, hypothalamic and
brain-stem structures - a hub rather than a single "fear centre."
[[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-connections|Amygdala]]

**The gap between mechanism and treatment.** Findings on the time windows during which a memory consolidates
and can be changed again on retrieval were expected to improve treatment design, but clinical translation has
been slower than hoped; a review of treatments derived from this research, including pharmacological,
behavioural and device-based augmentation, is explicit that this remains a research pipeline rather than
established care.
[[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-translation|Milad 2012]]
[[P39032727 Maples-Keller 2025 Treatments derived from fear extinction#^p39032727-caution-translation|Appraisal: Maples-Keller 2025]]

**A prospective human link.** In firefighters followed for two years, generalising threat expectancy to stimuli
that only resembled a safety signal predicted later PTSD symptoms after adjusting for baseline symptoms - a
group-level risk marker, not a predictor for a particular person.
[[P38484507 Lommen 2024 Fear generalization predicts PTSD symptoms#^p38484507-prediction|Lommen 2024]]
[[P38484507 Lommen 2024 Fear generalization predicts PTSD symptoms#^p38484507-caution-predictor|Appraisal: Lommen 2024]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A hypothetical client completes exposure-based therapy and feels no fear
of the trigger for months. After a difficult week, the fear returns in full force, and both the client and a
friend conclude "the therapy never really worked."

Apply the mechanism instead of the intuition. Extinction is new, competing learning, not deletion, so a fear
returning later is not evidence against the earlier improvement having been real. The pattern fits the model
precisely: a stressful event can produce reinstatement, and time passing can produce spontaneous recovery, both
recognised features of extinction rather than signs the improvement was fake.

The accurate response matches the mechanism: a return of fear after extinction is a known possibility, which is
why relapse-prevention planning is built into exposure-based treatment rather than treated as an afterthought.

## Common confusions

- "Extinction erases the original fear memory." It creates a new, competing memory that suppresses the
  original, which is why the original can resurface.
- "The amygdala is 'the fear centre' and nothing more." It is a hub connected to several other structures,
  involved in more than one function.
- "Basic-science findings on memory reconsolidation should have already produced new treatments." Clinical
  translation has been explicitly slower than researchers hoped.

## What the sources do not establish

Direct mechanistic evidence here comes largely from animal work, lesion and stimulation studies, and
conditioning in healthy humans, so it constrains what a human mechanism could look like without describing any
person's actual trauma memory. Treatments that augment extinction remain research-stage.

## Answer notes

1. If extinction had erased the association, nothing would be left to return; the fact that fear can come back
   shows the original memory was suppressed by a newer, competing one rather than deleted.
2. Spontaneous recovery over time, reinstatement after a new aversive event, and renewal when the context
   changes.
3. It is a prospective human result but describes a group-level risk marker, not a predictor for one person.
