# Recovery function and social context

**Module.** M10 CPTSD · **Topic.** [[Recovery function and social context]]

## Why this matters

A trial reporting "symptoms improved" has not automatically reported that someone recovered. Function,
participation and social conditions - safety, housing, income, discrimination - are a separate, less-measured
outcome domain, and this lesson gives the framework for noticing when a study has, or has not, addressed it.

## The core model

**The functioning framework.** The WHO's International Classification of Functioning describes functioning
through body functions, activities, participation and environmental factors, the international standard for
measuring health and disability. Its linked instrument, WHODAS 2.0, covers six domains - cognition, mobility,
self-care, getting along, life activities and participation - which is why symptom scores alone can understate
or overstate what actually changed.
[[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-framework|International Classification of Functioning]]
[[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-domains|International Classification of Functioning]]

**What a functioning score is, and is not.** These instruments are general rather than trauma-specific: a score
summarises reported activity and participation, not symptom severity, and should not be read as a verdict about
a person. Results depend on the instrument, informant and design, so two "functioning" studies can measure
different things.
[[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-limit|International Classification of Functioning]]
[[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-caution-impairment|Appraisal: International Classification of Functioning]]

**Structural causes belong in the picture.** Stigma is argued to function as a fundamental cause of health
inequality because it constrains access to employment, housing and healthcare, in structural, interpersonal and
internalised forms - for trauma-related conditions, that places safety, housing, income and discrimination among
the conditions shaping recovery, not in the background.
[[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-argument|Hatzenbuehler 2013]]
[[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-implication|Hatzenbuehler 2013]]

**Service context shapes what guidelines recommend.** Guideline documents generally tie recommendations to both
an evidence review and their funded service context - one such document, for a different condition, illustrates
the pattern: what is recommended reflects service capacity as well as evidence, so such documents are
service-level guidance, not individual advice.
[[F96 NICE Multiple sclerosis NG220#^f96-context|Multiple sclerosis NG220]]

**What the treatment evidence actually measured.** In a meta-analysis of psychological interventions for complex
trauma, symptom effect sizes decreased slightly at follow-up but stayed significant except for anxiety and
dissociation; functioning and quality of life were not the primary pooled outcomes.
[[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Hu 2025]]

**The gap in the record.** In trials of trauma-focused therapy for young people, pooled change in social skills
and functioning was small and not significant, and few trials even measured social outcomes.
[[P39450446 Phillips 2024 Social outcomes of youth trauma therapy#^p39450446-pooled|Phillips 2024]]

**Reading rule.** Separate symptom change, functional change and social conditions; recovery definitions vary
between clinical, research and lived-experience perspectives, and which outcomes a study picks reflects that
disagreement rather than resolving it.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A hypothetical trial reports "trauma symptoms improved significantly
after treatment," and stops there.

Ask whether functioning or participation was measured at all - few real trials even measure social outcomes, so
absence here would not be unusual. Ask which instrument, if any, and whose report, since a functioning score
depends on those choices. Ask whether housing, income or safety were recorded, since those can limit what
recovery is achievable regardless of the symptom score.

The accurate summary distinguishes what was shown: symptom improvement is real and specific; it is not, by
itself, a claim about functioning, participation or social conditions.

## Common confusions

- "A drop in symptom scores means someone has recovered." Functioning is a separate outcome domain, measured
  less consistently.
- "Recovery is a single, agreed endpoint." Definitions vary between clinical, research and lived-experience
  perspectives.
- "Social conditions like housing are background, not treatment." Stigma and structural barriers are argued to
  be a fundamental cause of health inequality, not a side issue.

## What the sources do not establish

The evidence linking specific trauma treatments to functional gains, as opposed to symptom gains, is thinner
than the symptom evidence. No source tests whether directly addressing social conditions changes
trauma-treatment outcomes.

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between what a symptom scale measures and what a functioning instrument measures?
2. Why does the lesson call stigma a "structural" issue rather than only an individual one?
3. What did the youth-therapy trials find about social and functioning outcomes, and what does that gap show?

## Answer notes

1. A symptom scale tracks specific symptoms; a functioning instrument tracks activity and participation, and
   the two are not interchangeable.
2. Stigma is argued to constrain access to employment, housing and healthcare at the level of law, policy and
   institutions, not only through one person's beliefs.
3. Pooled change was small and not significant, and few trials measured it at all - functional outcomes are
   measured far less consistently than symptoms.
