# Monotropism

**Module.** M09 Autism · **Topic.** [[Monotropism]]

## Why this matters

Monotropism reverses the order social cognition and predictive processing accounts assume: instead of starting from social difficulty or perceptual inference, it puts the allocation of attention first and derives everything else, including social and communication features, from how narrowly attention is distributed. It is also unusual among the theories here because autistic people developed it themselves, drawing on first-person experience alongside diagnostic criteria. Reading it well means taking that origin seriously while being just as honest about how little has been tested.

## The core model

The founding paper drew on research, formal diagnostic criteria and autistic first-person accounts, including one author's own, to argue that the social interaction and communication criteria for autism derive from the narrowed interests named in the third criterion. On this account, differences in how attention is distributed come first, and the social and communication features follow from them.
[[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-attention|Murray 2005]] [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-criteria|Murray 2005]]

That is a coherent way to organise the diagnostic picture, but deriving the criteria from a single principle is not evidence that the principle causes the features - a theory can be tidy and still be wrong.

Qualitative work has explored what deep attention feels like from the inside. In interviews with twenty-four autistic adults, descriptions of becoming absorbed in a task closely matched flow, hyperfocus and monotropism, and immersion was described as all-encompassing. Participants valued it as a state of being fully themselves, but also named real costs - exhaustion and neglected self-care - hard to balance with the rest of life.
[[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-immersion|Rapaport 2024]] [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-balance|Rapaport 2024]]

The one laboratory test gives a more cautious picture. Autistic adolescents and their caregivers reported more hyperfocus than comparison families and rated its overall effect modestly positive, but event-related brain measures of hyperfocus and attention capture showed no group difference, and the different ways of measuring attention barely related to each other.
[[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-questionnaires|Dwyer 2025]] [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-lab|Dwyer 2025]] [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-convergence|Dwyer 2025]]

That disagreement between questionnaire and laboratory measures is the theory's central open problem. A concept can feel completely real to the people describing it while still lacking a measure precise enough to confirm or rule it out - and monotropism currently sits closer to a compelling first-person description than to a tested mechanism.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A study reports: "Autistic participants scored higher on a hyperfocus questionnaire, confirming the monotropism theory of autism." Before treating this as confirmation, ask what the theory needs to survive as a scientific claim rather than a redescription. A questionnaire score shows people report more hyperfocus - it does not show attention is organised differently at the level of perception or brain response, and it does not show social and communication features actually follow from that attentional difference rather than arising alongside it. The more accurate statement: self-reported hyperfocus was higher, which is consistent with monotropism but does not confirm its causal claim, especially since questionnaire and laboratory measures of the same construct have been found not to agree.

## Common confusions

- "Monotropism has been proven by brain research." The one laboratory study found no group difference in the neural markers it tested; the theory rests mainly on argument and interview evidence so far.
- "Liking deep focus on an interest is the same as monotropism as a theory of autism." The theory makes a specific causal claim - that attention differences produce the social and communication criteria - stronger than simply enjoying focus.
- "Because autistic people developed it, it needs no further testing." An insider origin makes a theory worth taking seriously; it does not exempt it from needing predictions that could fail.

## What the sources do not establish

The founding paper is a theoretical argument with no systematic literature search and no new data. The interview study had no comparison group and a sample of mostly white, well-educated, employed women who used spoken communication, so it cannot show the immersion described is distinctly autistic. The laboratory study involved only forty adolescents, so its null results are weak evidence either way.

2. Immersion felt all-encompassing and let participants feel fully themselves, but also carried costs including exhaustion and neglected self-care, and was hard to balance with other demands.
