# Autism genetics and rare variants

**Module.** M09 Autism · **Topic.** [[Autism genetics and rare variants]]

## Why this matters

This is the vault's clearest large-scale genetics study, and the clearest place where a real biological finding
gets converted into a false personal claim. The lesson walks the study's design and its limits, with the
heterogeneity of autism held alongside it.

## The core model

The exome sequencing study analysed 35,584 samples, including 11,986 individuals with autism, and identified
102 risk genes at a false discovery rate of 0.1 or less; most act on gene regulation or neuronal development.
[[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-genes|Autism exome sequencing study]]

The genes split by phenotype: 49 were enriched in people ascertained for severe developmental delay and 53 in
people ascertained for autism.
[[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-subgroups|Autism exome sequencing study]]

Risk-gene expression was enriched in excitatory and inhibitory neuronal lineages, which the authors describe
as consistent with multiple imbalance paths rather than one mechanism.
[[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing study]]

The design is a case-control association study of rare de novo and inherited variation, estimating
population-level risk contributions.
[[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-limit|Autism exome sequencing study]]

Two consequences follow. First, the subgroup finding shows that the genetic correlates differ with the
presentation that led to ascertainment, which is a concrete way heterogeneity shows up in the data. Second,
finding a risk gene does not determine a person's traits, strengths or support needs.
[[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-caution-risk|AI synthesis: Autism exome sequencing study]]

The co-occurrence literature adds a related caution: family and twin evidence suggests partly shared
contributions with ADHD.
[[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-shared|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A family has a genetic report listing a variant "in a gene associated
with autism" — one of the 102 risk genes — and asks what it means for their child's development.

Audit the chain in two steps, because they are different claims. Step one is gene-level: across thousands of
people, rare variants in this gene are statistically enriched in autism and developmental-delay samples, with
effects that vary by variant and by sample. Step two is variant-level: whether this particular variant is
classified as risk-causing or pathogenic depends on variant-specific evidence — its frequency in the general
population, segregation in families, and predicted or measured functional effects — which the gene-level study
does not establish. Being located inside a risk gene does not by itself make a variant risk-causing, and most
variants in these genes are not classified.

The honest answer: "the gene is associated with autism risk in studied populations; that does not classify
this variant or determine the child's development, and questions about support are answered by assessment, not
by the report."

## Common confusions

- "A risk gene is a diagnosis." It is a probabilistic association in samples, not a personal outcome.
- "Rare variants explain most autism." The study estimates population-level contributions from rare variation,
  with most of the underlying variation unexplained.
- "Genetic findings make heterogeneity irrelevant." The subgroup findings are a form of heterogeneity.
- "Any variant in a risk gene is the cause." Gene-level enrichment and variant-level classification are
  different claims, and this study supports only the first.

## What the sources do not establish

The genetic source is abstract-level in this vault, and the study does not provide an individual risk model;
the co-occurrence review is narrative and reports cited family findings rather than new data. Nothing here
supports interpreting a personal genetic result, and this vault gives no genetic counselling.

## Answer notes

1. 102 risk genes at FDR 0.1 or less, from a case-control exome analysis of 35,584 samples.
2. That different gene sets were enriched depending on whether samples were ascertained for developmental
   delay or autism.
3. Because the association is statistical and gene-level: it varies by variant and sample, leaves much
   variation unexplained, and does not classify this person's variant or determine their trajectory.
4. Evidence for partly shared familial and genetic contributions with ADHD, which is context for the overlap
   rather than a single-cause explanation.
