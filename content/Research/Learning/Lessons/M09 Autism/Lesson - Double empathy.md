# Double empathy

**Module.** M09 Autism · **Topic.** [[Double empathy]]

## Why this matters

When two people misunderstand each other, it is tempting to locate the problem in one of them. The double empathy account challenges that habit for autism: it proposes that misunderstanding between autistic and non-autistic people is often relational rather than a one-sided deficit, and it turns that proposal into a testable prediction. Reading it well means asking "compared with what, and measured which way?" before accepting a claim about who misunderstands whom.

## The core model

The prediction is precise: if misunderstanding arises between people with different social expectations and styles rather than sitting inside one group, information should transfer more efficiently within a group than across groups, and any difficulty should run in both directions rather than only one. That is what makes the account testable rather than simply a generous re-description - it can fail if cross-group understanding turns out to be lopsided.

Qualitative evidence motivates the idea without settling it. Autistic adults report managing self-presentation and compensating for expectations placed on them, at a cost in effort and self-concept, consistent with the difficulty being relational rather than located entirely in the autistic person.
[[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-strategies|Social camouflaging]] [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-consequence|Social camouflaging]]

The sharpest part of the account is a design rule: a study that only measures autistic people against non-autistic norms will find differences by construction, so a fair test needs both directions built in from the start.

Two recent experiments took up that challenge, with different results. In one, thirty-six four-person groups met for a short task: all-autistic groups matched all-non-autistic groups on overall rapport, and both rated mixed groups lower on enjoyment and friendliness - but not symmetrically. Autistic rapport fell as non-autistic members were added, while non-autistic rapport barely moved.
[[P39989258 Foster 2025 Rapport in same and mixed neurotype groups#^p39989258-groups|Foster 2025]] [[P39989258 Foster 2025 Rapport in same and mixed neurotype groups#^p39989258-asymmetry|Foster 2025]]

A second, larger study asked autistic and non-autistic raters to judge storytellers on video for empathic accuracy. It found no overall advantage for either neurotype judging its own kind, and only a trend toward autistic-to-autistic accuracy being somewhat better - described by the authors as partial support and a caution against treating either neurotype as uniform.
[[P42271534 Rum 2026 Double empathy and empathic accuracy#^p42271534-accuracy|Rum 2026]] [[P42271534 Rum 2026 Double empathy and empathic accuracy#^p42271534-conclusion|Rum 2026]]

Read together, the two studies show the prediction is genuinely at risk of failing rather than automatically confirmed: a short group task found an asymmetric effect, while a video-judgment task found close to none. The account is best treated as a well-motivated reframing with growing but mixed support, not a settled finding.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A headline claims: "New study proves autistic people struggle to understand others." Before accepting it, ask the double-empathy question: did the study also test whether non-autistic participants struggled to understand autistic people, or only the reverse? If only one direction was tested, the study cannot distinguish a one-sided deficit from a two-sided mismatch, because the two-sided explanation was never given a chance to show up. The accurate rewrite is narrower: this study found autistic participants scored lower on understanding non-autistic people, and did not test the reverse direction.

## Common confusions

- "Double empathy means no differences are ever found." Foster 2025 found a real, asymmetric effect; the account predicts bidirectional difficulty, not zero difficulty.
- "The theory replaces mechanism-level accounts like theory of mind." It describes how difficulty plays out between people, not the cognitive or neural difference that produces it.
- "One supportive study settles the question." The 2026 empathic-accuracy study found only a trend, which is why this remains an open, active question.

## What the sources do not establish

Most evidence concerns autistic adults in Western, English-speaking settings, so bidirectional effects elsewhere are largely untested. A null or partial result in a short lab task says little about live, extended interaction either way, and the qualitative camouflaging evidence documents self-report rather than measured mutual understanding.

## Answer notes

1. Understanding should be more efficient within a group than across groups, and any difficulty should appear in both directions rather than only one.
2. All-autistic and all-non-autistic groups had similar rapport, and mixed groups rated lower on enjoyment and friendliness, but autistic rapport fell more as non-autistic members were added while non-autistic rapport barely changed - an asymmetric effect.
3. Because it never measures the reverse direction, so it cannot show whether non-autistic people also struggle to understand autistic people, which is the comparison the account depends on.
