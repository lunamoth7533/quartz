# Adult autism assessment and differential considerations

**Module.** M09 Autism · **Topic.** [[Adult autism assessment and differential considerations]]

## Why this matters

Adult autism assessment is where the library's heterogeneity lesson becomes practical. Many autistic adults were
never assessed as children, screening tools are easy to misread, and the differential list is long. This lesson
teaches what assessment is for and where instruments stop.

## The core model

Assessment is considered when persistent difficulties in social interaction or social communication, or
restricted and repetitive behaviours, co-occur with functional, social or service-contact criteria.
[[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-identification|NICE CG142]]

For adults without a moderate or severe learning disability, the AQ-10 may be used as a screen; a score of six
or more, or clinical judgement, leads to a comprehensive assessment rather than a diagnosis.
[[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-screening|NICE CG142]]

Comprehensive assessment is team-based and draws on informant or documentary evidence of early development and
current behaviour. Additional perspectives and records can help contextualize developmental history where
available; the guideline's suggestion to seek them does not show that adult self-report is inherently
unreliable or that assessment is impossible without an informant.
[[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-comprehensive|NICE CG142]]

It covers core features present since childhood, functioning at home, in education or employment, past and
current physical and mental disorders, and other neurodevelopmental conditions.
[[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-domains|NICE CG142]]

Differential assessment explicitly includes other neurodevelopmental conditions, mental disorders, neurological
and physical disorders, communication difficulties and sensory sensitivities.
[[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-differential|NICE CG142]]
Biological, genetic and neuroimaging tests are not used routinely for diagnosis.
[[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-tests|NICE CG142]]

Two background facts explain why the differential list is long. Autism varies widely between people in features
and support needs, so there is no single presentation to match against.
[[P31949163 Lord 2020 Autism spectrum disorder primer#^p31949163-construct|Autism spectrum disorder primer]]
And ADHD and autism frequently co-occur; the earlier exclusion of a dual diagnosis limited research until the
criteria were revised.
[[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-history|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

The boundary is worth stating plainly: screening can produce both false positives and false negatives, so a
score alone neither confirms nor excludes autism; it opens the door to assessment rather than closing the
question.
[[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-caution|AI synthesis: NICE CG142]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A 34-year-old completes an AQ-10 during a workplace health appointment
and scores 7. The colleague who arranged it says the score "confirms autism".

Trace what the score does and does not do. Seven is above the threshold that NICE recommends using to proceed to
a comprehensive assessment, so the correct next step is assessment, not diagnosis. That assessment would be
team-based, would seek developmental history from a parent or documentary records if available, and would cover
current functioning, mental and physical health, and other neurodevelopmental conditions. It would consider
co-occurring ADHD, anxiety and other explanations rather than ruling them out by the score.

The accurate summary: "the screen indicates that a full assessment is worth offering; the diagnosis depends on
the assessment, and the score alone cannot make or exclude it."

## Common confusions

- "A high screening score means autism." Screens can produce false positives and false negatives; the score
  alone neither confirms nor excludes autism.
- "Assessment is a questionnaire." It is a team-based process combining history, informant information and
  clinical judgement.
- "Finding one explanation ends the assessment." Differential assessment looks for coexisting conditions rather
  than stopping at the first fit.

## What the sources do not establish

The NICE guidance describes services in England and Wales and does not establish eligibility or availability
elsewhere, and it gives no tool that a person can self-administer to reach a diagnosis. This vault holds no
source on the sensitivity and specificity of adult instruments in detail.
