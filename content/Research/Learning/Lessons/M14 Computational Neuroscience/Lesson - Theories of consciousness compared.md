# Theories of consciousness compared

**Module.** M14 Computational Neuroscience and Brain Theories · **Topic.** [[Theories of consciousness compared]]

## Why this matters

This lesson gives a recent worked case of theories tested against each other on purpose, and shows what "progress" looks like where researchers disagree about what a theory must explain.

## The core model

Two families of theory: function-based theories explain consciousness by what a system does - accessing, representing, predicting; causal-structure theories identify it with how the system is organised, independent of function. [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-target|The unfolding argument]]

Each makes committed, exclusionary claims. Integrated information theory, building from axioms about experience, defines a quantity meant to track irreducible causal structure and excludes, for instance, feedforward systems from consciousness; global neuronal workspace theory identifies conscious access with broad, late broadcasting ("ignition") across sensory, parietal and prefrontal networks. Predictions that rule systems out are what makes either theory testable at all. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-axioms|Integrated information theory]]

The critique structuring the whole comparison: an argument from computability theory holds causal-structure theories are either false or unfalsifiable from behaviour alone, since behaviour underdetermines causal structure; comparing theories seriously means examining exclusion criteria, not just predictions. [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-target|The unfolding argument]] Breadth and theory-laden measures are recurring problems too: definitions of the target phenomenon differ, and frameworks broad enough to accommodate many results are correspondingly hard to falsify - the same structural problem seen elsewhere in this module whenever generality is mistaken for support. [[P20068583 Friston 2010 Free-energy principle#^p20068583-status|The free-energy principle]]

A concrete worked case, the adversarial collaboration: because most consciousness measures are theory-laden, ordinary comparisons cannot cleanly separate rival frameworks, so one large collaboration tested integrated information theory and global neuronal workspace theory directly against each other. Proponents of each theory and a neutral team preregistered divergent predictions and what would count as support, before testing 256 participants with three brain-recording methods. Results matched some predictions from each theory while substantially challenging others: the sustained posterior synchrony integrated information theory predicted was largely absent, and so was the broad "ignition" workspace theory predicted at stimulus offset. [[P40307561 Cogitate 2025 Adversarial consciousness#^p40307561-results|Cogitate 2025]] A theory can respond to a failed prediction by restricting its scope or adjusting an assumption, so the honest summary is that key tenets of both were challenged, not that either was eliminated. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-claim|Integrated information theory]]

The first-step problem is still unresolved: a separate structured debate among proponents of five major theories found more disagreement than agreement about what consciousness is, how it is identified, and what a theory must explain. Recording disagreement is not the same as testing who is right. [[P39929381 Mudrik 2025 Theories of consciousness debate#^p39929381-format|Mudrik 2025]] [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-debate|The unfolding argument]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

A science article reports: "the big 2025 brain-imaging study proved global workspace theory beats integrated information theory." Check the claim against what an adversarial collaboration is built to do: both sides preregister diverging predictions, precisely so a result challenging one theory's tenets can be distinguished from a result crowning a winner. Applying that to the actual result - both theories challenged, neither confirmed nor refuted - the accurate headline is "a preregistered test challenged key predictions of both leading theories," not "one theory beat the other."

## Common confusions

- "A theory that survives a challenge unscathed has been proven correct." Surviving one paradigm's predictions is not the same as being established.
- "If a study challenges a theory's prediction, that theory is refuted." A theory can adjust its scope or an auxiliary assumption instead.
- "Since researchers still disagree, no progress has been made." Naming where theorists disagree, and building a study whose predictions diverge enough to test jointly, are themselves progress.

## What the sources do not establish

No theory of consciousness is currently established; the adversarial-collaboration result challenges specific predictions within one visual paradigm, not a decisive resolution, and the structured-debate study documents disagreement rather than resolving it.

## Check yourself

1. What made the 2025 adversarial collaboration different from an ordinary comparison study?
2. What happened to the theories' predictions - confirmation, refutation, or something else?
3. Why does the structured debate count as useful despite not resolving which theory is correct?

## Answer notes

1. Both sides preregistered divergent predictions and what would count as support before data collection, so the design let either theory's tenets fail.
2. Something else: predictions from both theories were substantially challenged, different from confirmation or refutation.
3. Identifying where theorists disagree about what consciousness is, and what a theory must explain, is necessary before any test can adjudicate.
