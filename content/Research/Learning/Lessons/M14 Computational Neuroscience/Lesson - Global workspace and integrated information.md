# Global workspace and integrated information

**Module.** M14 Computational Neuroscience and Brain Theories · **Topic.** [[Global workspace and integrated information]]

## Why this matters

Consciousness theories are the clearest example here of two frameworks disagreeing not about data but about what counts as the thing to be explained. The reading skill - naming what each theory would forbid - generalises to any theoretical dispute in the vault.

## The core model

Global workspace, in outline: information becomes consciously accessible when broadcast widely enough to become available to many other systems at once - report, memory, control - and that wide availability is what the theory identifies with conscious access. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-claim|Integrated information theory]]

Integrated information, in outline: this theory starts from stated axioms about experience and works backward to postulates about physical systems that could have them, defining a quantity meant to measure irreducible causal structure - how much a system's organisation contributes to its own state. It commits to exclusionary claims, including that purely feedforward systems cannot be conscious. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-axioms|Integrated information theory]] [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-claim|Integrated information theory]]

Where the two genuinely disagree: function-based theories like global workspace locate consciousness in what a system does; causal-structure theories like integrated information locate it in how the system is wired. The disagreement is not primarily about data - it is about what should count as needing explanation: conscious access (why content becomes reportable) versus consciousness as a property of organised matter. [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-target|The unfolding argument]]

A pointed critique of the causal-structure approach: an argument from computability theory contends such theories are either false or unfalsifiable, because behaviour alone can be produced by systems whose causal structure differs in ways that should matter - so behavioural evidence cannot, even in principle, fix the structure such a theory depends on. [[P31078047 Doerig 2019 Unfolding argument#^p31078047-target|The unfolding argument]] This is one instance of a problem recurring across the domain: a framework accommodating many theories is difficult to falsify in any one case, the same criticism levelled at overly broad predictive-processing accounts. [[P20068583 Friston 2010 Free-energy principle#^p20068583-status|The free-energy principle]] [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-plural|Predictive coding algorithms]]

What counts as progress: theories making exclusionary predictions can be tested against each other; the informative experiment distinguishes two theories' predictions and rules at least one out, rather than confirming either - the standard model-comparison sets for any behavioural model. [[P24811198 Oizumi 2014 Integrated information theory#^p24811198-limit|Integrated information theory]] [[P31769410 Wilson 2019 Computational modelling rules#^p31769410-identifiability|Computational modelling rules]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

A debate panel is asked, "which theory of consciousness is correct, global workspace or integrated information?" Apply the reading rule instead of picking a side: ask what each theory forbids. If a purely feedforward system behaving indistinguishably from a conscious being would count as conscious under one theory's criteria and not the other's, that difference is only testable with a way to independently establish causal structure, not just behaviour - exactly the gap the causal-structure critique raises. The accurate framing: "these theories disagree about what counts as evidence, so a claim one has 'won' must say what exclusionary prediction was tested, and how, independently of behaviour."

## Common confusions

- "The two theories compete to explain the same phenomenon, so evidence for one counts against the other." They partly target different explananda, so some claims are not even in direct competition.
- "A theory that has generated a lot of research must be well supported." Generating research and surviving a falsifiable test are different achievements; neither is described as established.
- "The unfolding argument proves integrated information theory is false." It critiques testability, not a demonstrated refutation of content.

## What the sources do not establish

Neither theory is established, both continue to generate active research, and no source here provides a measure from either theory ready for clinical or diagnostic use; the unfolding argument is itself a contribution to an ongoing debate, not a result settling which theory is correct.

## Check yourself

1. What does global workspace theory identify with conscious access?
2. Why does the unfolding argument claim causal-structure theories are either false or unfalsifiable?
3. Why are the two theories not simply competing answers to the same question?

## Answer notes

1. Information broadcast widely enough to become available to many other systems at once, such as report, memory and control.
2. Behaviour alone can be produced by systems with different causal structures, so behavioural evidence cannot establish the structure such a theory depends on.
3. They aim at different explananda: conscious access, what becomes reportable, versus consciousness as a property of a system's organisation.
