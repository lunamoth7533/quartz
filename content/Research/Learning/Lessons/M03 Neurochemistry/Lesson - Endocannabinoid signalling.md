# Endocannabinoid signalling

**Module.** M03 Neurochemistry · **Topic.** [[Endocannabinoid signalling]]

## Why this matters

Every transmitter system covered so far sends a signal forward, from the cell that fires to the cell that listens. Endocannabinoids run the signal backwards. Learning this one system is worth the effort because it is the clearest example in this domain of a synapse regulating its own input, and because "endocannabinoid" claims in popular writing routinely confuse this tightly local feedback mechanism with the much broader, unrelated question of cannabis products' effects on a whole person.

## The core model

Endocannabinoids are lipids made and released by the postsynaptic cell - the receiving side of the synapse - when that cell is active, rather than being stored in vesicles and released from the presynaptic terminal the way classical transmitters are. Because the signal starts at the receiving cell and travels backward to the sending cell, retrograde signalling is the defining feature of this system by construction, not an incidental detail. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-synthesis|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]

Two lipids dominate the literature, 2-arachidonoyl glycerol and anandamide, and they are made and broken down by different enzymes. That matters because "the endocannabinoid system" is not one signal with one timing profile; it is at least two messengers with different lifetimes and different reach, which experiments can tell apart by manipulating the enzymes that make or degrade each one specifically. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-synthesis|An Introduction to the Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]

CB1 receptors, the main neuronal type, sit predominantly on presynaptic terminals, and their activation reduces how much transmitter that terminal releases. CB2 receptors are concentrated outside the nervous system on immune cells, which is why their pharmacology is discussed separately from the psychoactive effects associated with the system. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-retrograde|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]

Stated mechanically, the loop runs like this: activity in the postsynaptic cell raises endocannabinoid production, the lipid travels backward across the synapse, and it activates presynaptic CB1 receptors, which suppress further transmitter release from that input - and sometimes from nearby inputs too, since the messenger can diffuse to neighbouring terminals. The recently active synapse quiets itself down. This makes the system a local, selective, activity-triggered gain control rather than either a general dampener or a simple point-to-point channel, and it is one of the clearest demonstrations of retrograde signalling in the mammalian brain. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-retrograde|An Introduction to the Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]

Because this system operates across the brain and body, with roles described in pain, appetite, memory, movement, mood, stress responses and immune signalling, a drug that acts on it systemically is very unlikely to hit only one of those functions. That breadth is the mechanistic reason separating out a single therapeutic effect from this system has proven difficult, rather than a sign that the research has failed. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-scope|The Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A wellness blog cites laboratory findings that endocannabinoids modulate memory circuits in animal brain slices as proof that a particular cannabis-derived product "supports memory health" in people.

Trace the gap between the evidence and the claim. The cited mechanism describes activity-triggered, local retrograde suppression of release at specific synapses in a reduced animal preparation; it is not a claim about any commercial product's effect on human memory, and the review this vault draws on studies the endogenous system's pharmacology, not any product's clinical benefit. Evidence that a mechanism exists in a preparation does not establish that a product delivered to a whole person reproduces that effect, at that site, at a dose that matters.

The accurate statement: "the cited research describes a retrograde signalling mechanism in animal preparations; it does not test or support a claim about any commercial product's effect on human memory."

## Common confusions

- "Endocannabinoids work like a stored, released transmitter." They are synthesised on demand by the postsynaptic cell, not stored in vesicles presynaptically.
- "One 'endocannabinoid system' means one signal." At least two distinct lipid messengers exist with different synthesis, degradation and reach.
- "A mechanism shown in a brain-slice preparation supports a claim about a commercial product in people." It shows the mechanism exists in that preparation; a product's human effect needs its own evidence.

## What the sources do not establish

The synthetic pathway and retrograde mechanism are established in reduced preparations with direct recording and pharmacology; human evidence comes largely from pharmacological challenge and observational studies, which are weaker for causal inference, and nothing here supports specific claims about cannabis products or dietary compounds in people.

## Answer notes

1. The signal is produced by the postsynaptic (receiving) cell and travels backward to act on the presynaptic (sending) terminal, which is the opposite direction from classical transmission and makes it a built-in feedback mechanism.
2. 2-arachidonoyl glycerol and anandamide; they are synthesised and degraded by different enzymes, giving them different lifetimes and spread.
3. Because a systemic drug acting on this system will inevitably affect several of its many functions - pain, appetite, memory, mood - at once, making a single, isolated therapeutic effect hard to achieve.
