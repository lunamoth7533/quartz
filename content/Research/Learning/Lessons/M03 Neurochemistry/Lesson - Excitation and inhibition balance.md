# Excitation and inhibition balance

**Module.** M03 Neurochemistry · **Topic.** [[Excitation and inhibition balance]]

## Why this matters

This is the concept that turns "a chemical is high or low" into "a circuit computes". It is also frequently
abused, so the lesson does double duty: it teaches the integration model and it marks the boundary between a
measurable local relationship and a global metaphor.

## The core model

Whether a neuron fires depends on the summed excitatory and inhibitory influence reaching the point where the
spike begins. Graded potentials summate at the initial segment, where voltage-gated sodium channels are dense.
[[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-initial-segment|Communication Between Neurons]]

Excitatory postsynaptic potentials move the membrane toward threshold; inhibitory postsynaptic potentials move
it away.
[[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-initial-segment|Communication Between Neurons]]

The word "balance" therefore names a local, dynamic relationship, not a brain-wide level. It changes with every
input, and it is expressed in the timing of spikes rather than only in their number.

The clinical literature uses the concept at a different level of abstraction. In autism genetics, risk-gene
expression was enriched in both excitatory and inhibitory lineages, which the authors describe as consistent
with multiple imbalance paths.
[[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing study]]
That is a hypothesis-generating finding about development, not a measurement of balance, and the lessons here
keep the two apart.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A question asks: "How could inhibition change behaviour without
reducing the amplitude of any action potential?"

Answer with the integration model. The spike is all-or-none once it starts, so inhibition changes behaviour by
changing when and whether spikes occur — shifting the membrane away from threshold, narrowing the time window
in which excitatory inputs can summate, and shaping the precise timing of the output. In circuits where the
meaning depends on coincidence between two inputs, a few milliseconds of delay can change downstream effect
entirely while every spike keeps its full amplitude.

That is also why "inhibition" is not the opposite of "activity": it is a control signal that can sharpen,
gate and sculpt network rhythms.

## Common confusions

- "Inhibition means less firing." It often does, and it also controls timing, gain and rhythm.
- "Balance is a static property." It is re-established moment to moment by the inputs present.
- "Imbalance is a diagnosis." It is a research hypothesis that requires local measurement.

## What the sources do not establish

The textbook material describes summation qualitatively; it does not provide the quantitative conductances or
the models needed to compute balance at a real synapse. This vault holds no source that measures
excitation-inhibition balance in humans, so the phrase stays a research framework here.

## Check yourself

1. Where does summation happen, and what does it decide?
2. Describe one way inhibition can change behaviour without changing spike amplitude.
3. Distinguish a measured imbalance from a hypothesised one.

## Answer notes

1. At the initial segment, where it decides whether threshold is crossed and a spike begins.
2. By shifting the membrane potential, narrowing the window for summation, or altering the timing of spikes so
   coincidence-dependent circuits behave differently.
3. A measured imbalance comes from local measures of excitatory and inhibitory influence with a defined
   baseline; a hypothesised one is inferred from indirect findings such as gene expression enrichment.
