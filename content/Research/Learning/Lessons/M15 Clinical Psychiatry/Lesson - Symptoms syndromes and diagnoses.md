# Symptoms syndromes and diagnoses

**Module.** M15 Clinical Psychiatry and Psychopathology · **Topic.** [[Symptoms syndromes and diagnoses]]

## Why this matters

This lesson opens the module because every later lesson leans on the same three-level vocabulary: a symptom, a
syndrome and a diagnosis are different things, built by different amounts of human judgement, and a diagnostic
label was never designed to explain what causes a person's difficulties by itself.

## The core model

A symptom is a reported or observed feature; a syndrome is a characteristic cluster of features that co-occur;
a diagnosis is a named category defined by specified criteria, used for communication, statistics and treatment
planning. Categories are built by selecting features, setting thresholds, and adding duration and impairment
requirements - decisions that encode judgement, not a direct measurement of an underlying disease.
[[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-definition|What Are Psychological Disorders?]]

Defining "disorder" takes more than noticing something unusual. The textbook definition ties a psychological
disorder to clinically significant disturbance in cognition, emotion regulation or behaviour, connected to
dysfunction and distress rather than difference alone, and cultural expectations shape what counts as
disordered - so the same feature can be a problem in one setting and not in another.
[[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-harm|What Are Psychological Disorders?]]
[[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-cultural|What Are Psychological Disorders?]]

A classification manual is a compressed statement about a pattern, not evidence about mechanism. ICD-11's
clinical descriptions number each category and state essential features, boundaries with neighbouring categories
and course qualifiers, written to support reliable clinical identification - and its rules differ from DSM-5-TR
in places, so reading a diagnosis means reading the system it came from.
[[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-purpose|ICD-11 CDDR]]
[[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]]
[[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-use|ICD-11 CDDR]]

Diagnostic labels support communication, service planning and research eligibility; they were not designed to
describe mechanisms or to guide an individual's treatment choice by themselves.
[[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-purpose|HiTOP]] Research frameworks make this separation deliberate: the
NIMH Research Domain Criteria framework organises studies around dimensions of functioning - combining units of
analysis from genes to self-report with functional domains such as negative valence, positive valence and
cognitive systems - explicitly as an approach for mechanism research across conditions, not a diagnostic system
for clinical use.
[[F121 NIMH RDoC#^f121-purpose|RDoC]]
[[F122 NIMH RDoC constructs#^f122-domains|RDoC constructs]]

The reading rule this sets up for the whole module: for any claim in this domain, separate the descriptive
features, the category label and the classification system that defines it, and any mechanistic or treatment
inference. Classifications exist to make communication and research possible; their boundaries are decisions,
and decisions can be revised.
[[F121 NIMH RDoC#^f121-scope|RDoC]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This is an invented exercise about reading a case description, not a real diagnosis.

Imagine a fictional case note reads: "Meets criteria for Diagnosis Z." Rather than treating that as an
explanation, pull it into three layers: the descriptive features actually observed - symptoms, duration,
functional impact; the label and system, since a category's boundaries are specific to whichever manual was
used; and any inference about cause or treatment, checked against whether the note actually supports that
inference or has smuggled it in beside the label. A diagnostic label, on its own, only licenses the first two
layers.

## Common confusions

- "Unusual behaviour is disordered behaviour." The definition requires dysfunction and distress, not statistical
  rarity alone, and cultural context is part of the definition.
- "A diagnostic label explains what caused the difficulty." Labels are built for communication, planning and
  research eligibility, not as mechanism claims.
- "Every classification system draws the same boundaries." ICD-11 and DSM-5-TR differ in places, so a claim
  about prevalence or boundaries has to name the system.

## What the sources do not establish

Diagnostic reliability varies by category and setting, and categorical boundaries are described as imperfect -
a documented property of these systems, not a reason to discard them. None of the sources here establish that a
diagnostic label, by itself, identifies a mechanism or predicts an individual's response to treatment.

## Answer notes

2. Because it defines essential features, boundaries and course qualifiers to support reliable identification,
