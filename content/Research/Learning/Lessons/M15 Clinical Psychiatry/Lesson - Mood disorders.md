# Mood disorders

**Module.** M15 Clinical Psychiatry and Psychopathology · **Topic.** [[Mood disorders]]

## Why this matters

The boundary between unipolar depression and bipolar disorder is the highest-stakes differential in this
construct family, because a depressive presentation that is actually part of bipolar disorder changes what the
evidence base can be applied to. Reading a mood-disorder claim well starts with naming the episode, the disorder
and the phase.

## The core model

A depressive syndrome - low mood or loss of interest with cognitive and physical features - becomes a disorder
category through duration, the count of features present, functional impact, and the exclusion of grief-related,
substance-related and medical explanations. NIMH describes depression as a common but serious mood disorder that
affects feeling, thinking and daily activity, and distinguishes major depressive episodes from persistent
depressive disorder and from bipolar depression because the treatment implications differ.
[[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-scale|Depression]]
[[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-patterns|Depression]]

Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania; depressive episodes are common in it but not required
for the diagnosis. The clinical picture combines discrete episodes with a lifetime course, so acute and
long-term considerations differ.
[[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]]
[[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar disorder]]

Episode and disorder are different levels of description. ICD-11 gives separate descriptions for depressive,
manic, mixed and hypomanic episodes before it describes the disorder categories built from them, so an episode
label describes a period of illness while the disorder label describes the pattern over time; course qualifiers
express recurrence and persistence without changing the category itself.
[[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]]

That separation is why the bipolar boundary matters so much: misclassifying bipolar depression as unipolar
depression is described as the classic costly error in this literature, which is why history-taking for past
elevated or irritable periods is built into mood-disorder assessment.
[[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]]

Treatment evidence is phase-specific rather than pooled. Clinical guidance translates efficacy, safety and
tolerability evidence into separate first-, second- and third-line recommendations for different phases of
bipolar illness, reflecting how the evidence was generated as much as how the categories are drawn.
[[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This is an invented exercise in reading a trial's applicability, not guidance about any real treatment choice.

Imagine a fictional trial reports "Treatment X improved depressive symptoms in adults with major depressive
disorder." Before assuming that finding applies to bipolar depression too, ask the questions this lesson's
distinctions point to: did the trial exclude people with a history of mania or hypomania (if so, its population
is narrower than "depression" in general); is the finding about an episode or about long-term course; and which
phase of illness does the result actually describe. Because treatment evidence in this domain is organised by
phase rather than pooled across unipolar and bipolar depression, a positive unipolar-depression result does not
transfer to bipolar depression without its own trial.

## Common confusions

- "Any period of low mood is a depressive disorder." Duration, symptom count, functional impact and exclusion of
  other causes are all part of the threshold.
- "Depression evidence generalises to bipolar depression." The evidence bases are organised by phase and do not
  pool across unipolar and bipolar presentations.
- "Mania and hypomania are just different words for the same severity." Bipolar I is specifically defined by the
  occurrence of mania, a distinguishable severity level from hypomania.

## What the sources do not establish

Episode definitions are reliably applied in research settings, but the boundaries between categories and the
role of subthreshold presentations remain contested, and whether bipolar spectrum concepts should be widened is
an active, unsettled debate. This lesson contains no guidance about diagnosing or treating any individual.

## Answer notes

1. An episode describes a period of illness; the disorder describes the pattern of episodes over time, expressed
   through course qualifiers rather than a change in category.
2. Misclassifying bipolar depression as unipolar depression, since it changes the treatment implications
   substantially.
3. Because treatment evidence in this domain is organised by phase and does not pool across unipolar and bipolar
   presentations; a trial's exclusion criteria define which population its result actually describes.
