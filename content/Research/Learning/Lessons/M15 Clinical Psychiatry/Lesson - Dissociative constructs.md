# Dissociative constructs

**Module.** M15 Clinical Psychiatry and Psychopathology · **Topic.** [[Dissociative constructs]]

## Why this matters

Dissociation - disruption of normally integrated consciousness, memory, identity or perception - is a symptom
set that turns up in several unrelated presentations, so it is a good case study in why the instrument used to
measure something has to be named before a claim about it can be trusted.

## The core model

Dissociative disorders are described as a group in which these integrated functions break down, and the
professional overview stresses differential diagnosis from trauma- and stressor-related presentations and from
neurological and substance-related causes, because dissociative symptoms are non-specific - they show up in more
than one condition.
[[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-group|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]]
[[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-differential|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]]

A dissociative subtype of PTSD was proposed around depersonalisation and derealisation, symptoms of feeling
detached from oneself or from reality, and supported by statistical clustering analyses in veteran and civilian
samples using the standard basis for claiming a subtype is meaningful: evidence before, during and predicting
outcomes.
[[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-definition|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]]
[[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-validators|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]]
The review proposing it argued the distinction may matter for treatment, while noting evidence on differential
treatment response was still developing.
[[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-implication|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]]
[[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-limit|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]]

This PTSD subtype is a different construct from ICD-11 complex PTSD, which adds its own three domains on top of
core PTSD; keeping the two separate matters, because a finding about the PTSD dissociative subtype should not be
relabelled as a finding about complex PTSD.

Assessment depends heavily on the instrument. Self-report of dissociation correlates only modestly with
interview measures, both can be shaped by the context of assessment, and the International Trauma Questionnaire
- a self-report measure built through item response theory in trauma-exposed samples - is described by its own
authors as an evolving standard whose properties are sample-dependent rather than fixed once and for all.
[[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-development|International Trauma Questionnaire]]
[[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-structure|International Trauma Questionnaire]]
[[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-limits|International Trauma Questionnaire]]
Any claim about how common a dissociative or complex presentation is therefore has to name the instrument and
the sample it came from.
[[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-limit|International Trauma Questionnaire]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This is an invented exercise in reasoning through conflicting figures, not a real dataset.

Suppose two fictional studies report different rates for a dissociative subtype: Study A finds it in 15% of a
sample using a clinician interview, Study B finds it in 35% using a self-report questionnaire. Rather than
splitting the difference or picking the "bigger" number, work through what would explain the gap: self-report and
interview measures of dissociation are described as only modestly correlated, so different instruments are a
plausible full explanation for the entire 20-point gap before any real difference between the samples needs to
be invoked. The next step, not available from either number alone, would be a study using the same instrument in
comparable samples.

## Common confusions

- "The PTSD dissociative subtype and ICD-11 complex PTSD's dissociation are the same thing." They are kept as
  separate constructs in this material precisely so one is not mistaken for the other.
- "A functional-imaging finding is a diagnostic test for dissociation." It is described as an active research
  area showing group patterns, not an individual diagnostic tool.
  [[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-biology|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]]
  [[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-limit|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]]
- "A subtype proposed with strong statistical support is settled." The proposing review itself flagged that
  treatment-response evidence was still developing.

## What the sources do not establish

The dissociative subtype has empirical support but its differential treatment implications remain unsettled, and
functional-imaging patterns are group-level research findings rather than tools that could diagnose one person.
Prevalence figures throughout this area vary with instrument and setting and should not be quoted as fixed.

3. Why can two studies report very different rates of the same construct without either being wrong?

## Answer notes

1. Because dissociative symptoms are non-specific and appear in trauma-related, neurological and
   substance-related presentations alike.
2. Statistical clustering analyses in veteran and civilian samples, argued from evidence before, during and
   predicting outcomes.
3. Because self-report and interview measures of dissociation correlate only modestly, so instrument choice
   alone can produce a large gap.
