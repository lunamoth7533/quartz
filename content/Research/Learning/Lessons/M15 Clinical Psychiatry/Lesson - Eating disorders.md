# Eating disorders

**Module.** M15 Clinical Psychiatry and Psychopathology · **Topic.** [[Eating disorders]]

## Why this matters

Eating disorders carry real medical and suicide risk on top of the psychiatric picture, and they are measurably
tangled up with ADHD and autism. Reading an overlap figure between eating disorders and another condition well
means checking how the second condition was established and whether the person was acutely unwell when assessed.

## The core model

Anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge-eating disorder and avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder make up
the main eating disorders, with a combined lifetime prevalence of 2% to 5%, higher in females.
[[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-scope|Attia 2025]] The body carries much of the risk:
complications include electrolyte disturbance, slow heart rate, hormonal change and bone loss, and anorexia
nervosa carries nearly six times the mortality of same-age peers, a quarter of those deaths by suicide.
[[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-harms|Attia 2025]] Treatment differs by disorder:
family-based treatment helps young people with anorexia nervosa, behavioural therapy helps bulimia nervosa and
binge-eating disorder, and some medications reduce binge episodes - but no medication works for anorexia nervosa
itself.
[[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-treatment|Attia 2025]] These are average trial
effects, not guidance for any individual.
[[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-caution-average|Appraisal: Attia 2025]]

The ADHD overlap runs both ways. People with ADHD have close to four times the odds of any eating disorder
compared with controls, and people with eating disorders have raised odds of ADHD too, strongest in binge-eating
samples.
[[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-risk|Nazar 2016]]
[[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-reverse|Nazar 2016]] The associations were
stronger when ADHD was diagnosed by clinical interview than by self-report, which is a measurement effect worth
remembering whenever two studies disagree.
[[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-method|Nazar 2016]]

The autism overlap shows up on trait measures too: people with anorexia nervosa score well above healthy
controls on autistic-trait measures, and about 29% score above a cut-off on an observational autism assessment.
[[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-traits|Inal-Kaleli 2025]]
[[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-ados|Inal-Kaleli 2025]] Starvation
itself can bring rigidity and social withdrawal, so traits measured during acute illness may overstate how much
autism is really present once someone has recovered.
[[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-caution-starvation|Appraisal: Inal-Kaleli 2025]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This is an invented exercise in reading an overlap claim, not a description of any real person's diagnosis.

Imagine a fictional report: "70% of patients admitted for an eating disorder scored above an autism cut-off on
observation." Before accepting that as a true co-occurrence rate, ask when the observation happened. If everyone
was assessed while acutely unwell and underweight, some of that score plausibly reflects starvation-driven
rigidity and withdrawal rather than a lasting autistic profile - exactly the confound the autistic-traits research
flags. The next question is what "above a cut-off" on an observational schedule means: it is a positive result on
one instrument, not a clinical autism diagnosis, and not the same claim as a diagnostic prevalence figure. A
careful summary would say the finding raises a real question about assessment timing, not that 70% of the group
are autistic.

## Common confusions

- "No medication works for eating disorders." That is true specifically for anorexia nervosa; some medications
  reduce binge episodes in bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder.
- "A raised odds ratio shows which condition causes the other." Cross-sectional odds ratios show the conditions
  travel together, not which came first.
- "A trait score above a cut-off means a diagnosis." An observational schedule score is one measurement, not a
  diagnostic prevalence figure, and acute illness can inflate it.

## What the sources do not establish

Neither the ADHD nor the autism literature here can yet say whether having both conditions changes course or
treatment response, and the autism-trait findings cannot yet separate a lasting profile from a starvation effect
that might resolve with recovery. The eating-disorder review itself summarises trials and meta-analyses rather
than presenting new data.

## Answer notes

1. Anorexia nervosa.
2. Because the two measurement methods may capture ADHD with different accuracy, which is a measurement effect
   rather than evidence the association itself changes.
3. Starvation can produce rigidity and social withdrawal that resemble autistic traits, so scores taken while
