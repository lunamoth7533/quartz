# Network and developmental formulations

**Module.** M15 Clinical Psychiatry and Psychopathology · **Topic.** [[Network and developmental formulations]]

## Why this matters

A formulation answers "why this presentation, for this person, now" in a way a diagnostic label alone cannot.
Two formulations recur across this vault - one that treats symptoms as interacting with each other, and one that
treats development as an accumulating process - and both replace a single hidden cause with a structured account
that still has to name its mechanism and level.

## The core model

The network approach treats symptoms as interacting components rather than as signs of one hidden disorder, so
the disorder is the pattern of interaction itself; it predicts sudden shifts and treats overlap between diagnoses
as shared symptom links rather than as two separate things happening to co-occur.
[[P28127906 Borsboom 2017 Network theory#^p28127906-claim|A network theory of mental disorders]]
[[P28127906 Borsboom 2017 Network theory#^p28127906-consequences|A network theory of mental disorders]]

What this changes in practice is where attention goes: two people with the same number of symptoms can differ in
which symptom-to-symptom links are strongest, and a study can ask which symptom sits most centrally rather than
only which diagnosis applies. That is a way of framing a study, not yet a settled clinical method.

The developmental formulation describes how risk and protective factors accumulate over time. Brain development
proceeds through processes - forming new cells, moving them into place, growing connections and pruning and
insulating them - that overlap in time across brain regions rather than completing in a fixed sequence, so how
structure relates to function keeps shifting with age.
[[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]]
[[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]] Early
experience interacts with genetically guided development, so variation in outcome is the expected result rather
than an exception needing special explanation.
[[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]]

Both formulations make the same demand on a reader: name the mechanism being claimed and the level - symptom
network, developmental process, or something else - at which it operates, rather than letting "formulation"
stand in for an explanation on its own.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This is an invented exercise in reasoning through a formulation, not a case study of any real person.

Imagine five symptoms reported together in a fictional presentation: poor sleep, low energy, irritability,
difficulty concentrating and social withdrawal. A network-style question is: which of these, if it improved
first, would plausibly pull the others down with it? Poor sleep is a reasonable candidate, since it plausibly
feeds energy, concentration and irritability directly. That is a hypothesis worth testing, not a demonstrated
causal chain - a review of depression network studies found that most did not discuss confounding, and none
discussed a phenomenon called positivity, even though the whole approach assumes the links between symptoms are
causal.
[[P36927618 Huang 2023 Causal assumptions in depression networks#^p36927618-assumptions|Huang 2023]]
[[P36927618 Huang 2023 Causal assumptions in depression networks#^p36927618-premise|Huang 2023]] So the honest
next step is to say what evidence - not just plausibility - would distinguish "sleep drives the others" from
"all five share a common upstream cause."

## Common confusions

- "A network diagram shows what causes what." The paradigm assumes causal links between symptoms, and that
  assumption is usually untested in any given study.
- "Network theory has replaced diagnosis." It is described as a research programme with early support, not an
  installed clinical method.
  [[P28127906 Borsboom 2017 Network theory#^p28127906-status|A network theory of mental disorders]]
- "A developmental finding about typical samples tells you about one clinical case." Typical-development
  findings generalise across samples studied, not down to an individual.

## What the sources do not establish

Network theory's own literature states that it is a theoretical proposal with early empirical support, not a
replacement for categorical classification in clinical use.
[[P28127906 Borsboom 2017 Network theory#^p28127906-limit|A network theory of mental disorders]] The causal
assumption behind any fitted symptom network is rarely tested in the studies that use it.
[[P36927618 Huang 2023 Causal assumptions in depression networks#^p36927618-caution-scope|Appraisal: Huang 2023]]
Developmental findings here describe typical development in the samples studied; they do not transfer directly
to a claim about one person, and timing findings from one brain system do not transfer automatically to another.
[[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-limit|The basics of brain development]]

   system does not automatically hold for another.
