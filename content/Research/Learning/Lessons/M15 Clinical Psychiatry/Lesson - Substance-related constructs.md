# Substance-related constructs

**Module.** M15 Clinical Psychiatry and Psychopathology · **Topic.** [[Substance-related constructs]]

## Why this matters

Substance-related constructs sit at the intersection of a clear mechanism story and a strong warning against
over-applying it. Reading this material well means holding the reward mechanism as a real, useful account of
plausibility while refusing to let it predict any one person's outcome. This lesson carries no treatment or
dosing guidance.

## The core model

Addictive drugs can flood reward circuitry with dopamine, driving it more strongly or faster than naturally
occurring rewards do - the starting point for why the same learning machinery that supports everyday adaptive
behaviour can be recruited by a substance.
[[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-reward|Drugs and the Brain]]

Repeated exposure changes circuits involved in reward, stress and self-control together, which is why use can
become compulsive; naming three systems rather than one receptor is a coordinated-change account, not a
chemical-imbalance claim.
[[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-adaptation|Drugs and the Brain]] Compulsive use resembles a shift described
elsewhere in learning research, from behaviour driven by a goal to behaviour driven by habit.
[[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal ganglia]]

The constructs used to describe this are not one thing: use is behaviour, intoxication is a state, and a use
disorder combines impaired control, social impairment, risky use and pharmacological signs such as tolerance and
withdrawal, graded by how many are present. These travel together imperfectly - pharmacological dependence can
exist without social impairment, and problematic use without dependence pharmacology - so a screening or research
question has to name which construct it means.
[[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-adaptation|Drugs and the Brain]]

Response varies with the drug, the route, the amount, genetics and environment, so no single pathway explains
every substance-use problem. That single sentence rules out two tempting stories at once: a moralised account
that ignores mechanism, and a strictly pharmacological one that ignores variation.
[[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-variation|Drugs and the Brain]] It is also worth knowing that "dopamine"
itself is not one clean signal - dopamine neurons carry salience and movement-related information as well as
reward-related signals, and different populations of neurons carry different signals - so the reward story is a
useful approximation of a more complicated system, not a complete description of it.

[[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This is an invented exercise in reading a claim, not advice about any substance or any person.

Suppose a fictional headline says "Substance Y hijacks the brain's reward system." Work through what that
sentence supports and what it does not. It supports plausibility: a substance that strongly engages reward
circuitry gives a mechanistic reason why repeated use could become compulsive for some people. It does not
support a prediction about who becomes dependent, since response is described as varying with the drug, the
route, the amount, genetics and environment together - a list long enough that no single number could summarise
an individual's risk from mechanism alone. The honest rewrite is closer to "Substance Y engages a circuit that
can be recruited into compulsive use in some people, for reasons that are not yet individually predictable."

## Common confusions

- "Dependence is the same as addiction." The constructs are separated on purpose: dependence pharmacology,
  impaired control and social impairment do not always travel together.
- "A reward-circuit mechanism means anyone exposed enough will become dependent." Variation by drug, route,
  amount, genetics and environment is described as the rule, not the exception.
- "Dopamine involvement proves a reward explanation." Dopamine signals also carry salience and movement
  information, so a dopamine finding needs its own population and paradigm named before it is read as reward.

## What the sources do not establish

This material explains why a substance's engagement with reward circuitry is plausible as a mechanism; it does
not predict which exposures become disorders or which approaches work for whom, and it contains no treatment or
dosing guidance. The basal-ganglia habit account is a conceptual bridge between behavioural and neural
description, not a demonstration that any one person's use has shifted from goal-directed to habitual.

## Check yourself

1. Why is "repeated exposure changes reward, stress and self-control circuits" described as a coordinated-change
   account rather than a chemical-imbalance claim?
2. Name two constructs that can occur separately even though they often travel together.
3. Why can't a reward-circuit mechanism alone predict who develops a substance use disorder?

## Answer notes

1. Because it names three interacting systems rather than one signal that is simply too low or too high.
2. Any two of: use, intoxication, dependence pharmacology (tolerance and withdrawal), and social or functional
   impairment.
3. Because response is described as varying by drug, route, amount, genetics and environment together, so
   mechanism alone cannot fix an individual outcome.
