# Interpreting group brain differences

**Module.** M06 Neurology · **Topic.** [[Interpreting group brain differences]]

## Why this matters

Imaging findings are the most quotable and least portable results in psychiatry. This lesson teaches the
three-question discipline — group or individual, causal or descriptive, confounded or clean — using the
largest bipolar imaging analysis in the vault.

## The core model

The ENIGMA analysis compared 6503 individuals and found thinner cortical grey matter bilaterally in frontal,
temporal and parietal regions in bipolar disorder.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

The same analysis found that commonly prescribed drugs, including lithium, antiepileptics and antipsychotics,
were associated with cortical measures.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-medication|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

Longer illness duration was also associated with reduced thickness, while reduced surface area was associated
with a different pattern of variables.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-course|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

The authors describe the analysis as an extensive exploration of potential confounding, which is the honest
status of the design: an observational, cross-sectional comparison among people who differ in treatment
history, illness duration and state.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-limit|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

Group-level averages describe a sample, not a person: they cannot establish causation or diagnose an
individual.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-caution-groups|AI synthesis: ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Write two sentences about this finding: one the study supports, one it
does not.

Supported: "Across 6503 individuals, cortical thickness estimates were lower on average in frontal, temporal
and parietal regions in the bipolar group, with medication exposure and longer illness duration also associated
with the same measures."

Not supported: "Bipolar disorder causes cortical thinning, and this scan can help diagnose it."

The difference is not rhetorical caution. The unsupported sentence adds a causal direction the design cannot
establish, uses an individual claim the group design cannot support, and ignores the medication association —
which is itself one of the most interesting findings, because it means treatment history is entangled with
the measure.

## Common confusions

- "Large sample means individual validity." Large samples sharpen group estimates; they do not create
  individual prediction.
- "Controlling for a variable removes it." Statistical adjustment reduces some confounding; it cannot fix
  unmeasured or poorly measured confounders.
- "A difference in patients means the difference is part of the disorder." Medication, illness duration, age
  and measurement all differ between groups.

## Answer notes

1. Group averages across 6503 individuals; they cannot describe or diagnose one person.
2. Medication exposure and illness duration (also age and state, depending on the analysis).
3. Because treatment is associated with the outcome measure and with the diagnosis, so it can produce or
   attenuate the difference instead of being a nuisance removed from it.
4. A supported sentence keeps the group, the regional pattern and the associations; an unsupported one adds
   cause or individual diagnosis.
