# Sleep disorders

**Module.** M06 Neurology · **Topic.** [[Sleep disorders]]

## Why this matters

"Trouble sleeping" can mean four different problems: difficulty falling asleep, difficulty staying asleep, a
timing mismatch, or an unusual behaviour during sleep. Because the mechanisms and treatments differ across
these, sorting which one is present is the necessary first step before anything else is useful.

## The core model

**The family is organised by which part of sleep is disrupted.** Sleep disorders are grouped by whether the
problem is falling asleep, staying asleep, timing, or unusual behaviour during sleep, covering insomnia,
sleep-related breathing disorders, circadian rhythm disorders, hypersomnias and parasomnias as distinct
categories with different mechanisms. [[F79 MedlinePlus Sleep disorders#^f79-scope|Sleep Disorders]]

**Normal architecture gives abnormality something to be measured against.** Sleep cycles through non-REM and
REM stages distinguished by characteristic brain-wave patterns, and timing is regulated by circadian rhythms
interacting with how long a person has been awake, so a sleep problem can involve amount, timing, internal
architecture, or behaviour during sleep, and these four are assessed differently.
[[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]

**A molecular clock sits underneath timing disorders.** The core circadian clock is a feedback loop in which
clock proteins repress their own production over roughly 24 hours, and most tissues carry their own version
of this clock, coordinated by a central structure, so disrupted timing has consequences well beyond feeling
sleepy at the wrong time.
[[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]]

**Objective measurement is chosen selectively, not applied routinely.** A laboratory sleep study is reserved
for suspected breathing disorders, parasomnias or diagnostic uncertainty, while diaries and longer-term
timing measurement carry most insomnia and circadian assessment, because history plus roughly two weeks of
timing data outperforms a single laboratory night for those presentations.
[[F79 MedlinePlus Sleep disorders#^f79-assessment|Sleep Disorders]]

**Insomnia treatment components have been compared directly.** Across 241 trials in more than 31,000 adults
with chronic insomnia, cognitive restructuring, sleep restriction and stimulus control were associated with
remission, while sleep hygiene education alone was not shown to be essential - consistent with insomnia as
hyperarousal and maladaptive sleep-related behaviour rather than a simple lack of sleep information.
[[P38231522 Furukawa 2024 Components of CBT for insomnia#^p38231522-components|Furukawa 2024]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Someone reports "bad sleep," and on questioning it turns out they
fall asleep quickly and sleep soundly but on a schedule roughly four hours later than their work requires, so
they feel sleepy only on workdays. Sort this by category before assuming it is insomnia. Because sleep onset
and continuity are not the problem, and the complaint tracks a mismatch between the person's internal
schedule and an externally required one, this fits a circadian rhythm problem rather than insomnia, which
would instead involve difficulty falling or staying asleep regardless of when the attempt is made. That
distinction matters because assessment and any relevant intervention differ between the two categories.

## Common confusions

- "Any sleep complaint is insomnia." Insomnia is one category among several; timing, breathing and
  behaviour-during-sleep problems are distinct categories with different mechanisms.
  [[F79 MedlinePlus Sleep disorders#^f79-scope|Sleep Disorders]]
- "A sleep study is needed to diagnose any sleep problem." Diaries and longer-term timing measurement carry
  most insomnia and circadian assessment; laboratory studies are reserved for specific indications.
  [[F79 MedlinePlus Sleep disorders#^f79-assessment|Sleep Disorders]]
- "Better sleep hygiene information is the essential ingredient of insomnia therapy." Component analysis
  found sleep hygiene education was not shown to be essential, while cognitive and behavioural components
  were linked to remission.
  [[P38231522 Furukawa 2024 Components of CBT for insomnia#^p38231522-components|Furukawa 2024]]

## What the sources do not establish

The consumer overview orients readers to categories and assessment without diagnostic criteria, and the
insomnia component estimates are indirect averages across trials rather than a test of any specific person's
likely response, so none of this supports self-diagnosis or an individual treatment choice.
[[F79 MedlinePlus Sleep disorders#^f79-limit|Sleep Disorders]]
