# Functional neurological disorder

**Module.** M06 Neurology · **Topic.** [[Functional neurological disorder]]

## Why this matters

Functional neurological disorder is sometimes misunderstood as a diagnosis of last resort, reached only
once every other test has come back normal. Learning that it is instead a positive diagnosis, based on
specific examination findings, changes how a reader should judge both the diagnosis itself and the treatment
evidence behind it.

## The core model

**The diagnosis rests on positive signs, not on exclusion alone.** Functional neurological disorder involves
genuinely experienced symptoms, such as seizures, abnormal movements, dizziness or cognitive complaints, that
follow recognisable patterns and vary within and between tasks over time; it is diagnosed from these
positive features rather than simply by ruling out other disease.
[[P35430029 Hallett 2022 Functional neurological disorder subtypes and mechanisms#^p35430029-positive|Hallett 2022]]

**Four subtypes are treated as one disorder.** Functional seizures, functional movement disorders,
persistent postural-perceptual dizziness and functional cognitive disorder are variants of a single disorder
with shared causes and mechanisms, each with its own distinctive features.
[[P35430029 Hallett 2022 Functional neurological disorder subtypes and mechanisms#^p35430029-subtypes|Hallett 2022]]

**Mechanism research offers models, not a confirmatory test.** Current proposals include overactivity of
limbic circuits, an internal predictive model of the symptom, and disturbance of the networks that normally
give movement a sense of being voluntary - group-level models from imaging and experimental work that explain
how symptoms can arise without structural damage, but do not yet confirm the diagnosis in one person.
[[P35430029 Hallett 2022 Functional neurological disorder subtypes and mechanisms#^p35430029-caution-mechanism|Appraisal: Hallett 2022]]

**Dissociation is common, especially with functional seizures.** Across a meta-analysis of 75 studies,
dissociative symptoms were elevated compared with both healthy and neurological controls, most strongly in
people with functional seizures, though the comparisons are cross-sectional and some measures overlap
conceptually with the functional symptoms themselves.
[[P36451595 Campbell 2022 Dissociation in functional neurological disorder#^p36451595-scores|Campbell 2022]]

**Treatment evidence favours specific, matched approaches.** An umbrella review of 17 systematic reviews
found consistent benefit from cognitive behavioural therapy for functional seizures, especially within
multidisciplinary programmes, and moderate improvement in motor symptoms with physiotherapy, though overall
certainty was moderate at best.
[[P41111084 Mavroudis 2025 Treatment of functional neurological disorder#^p41111084-cbt|Mavroudis 2025]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Someone has had several episodes of leg shaking, and every scan and
blood test so far has come back normal. Read this the way the positive-diagnosis model requires, not as
"nothing showed up, so it must be functional by elimination." The correct next step is to look for the
specific positive examination features associated with functional seizures, such as characteristic patterns
of onset or variability within and between episodes, that a trained clinician would assess directly. A
diagnosis reached only by exclusion, with no positive features described, is a weaker claim than one built
on documented positive signs, even though both might end with the same label.

## Common confusions

- "Functional neurological disorder means the symptoms are not real." The symptoms are described as
  genuinely experienced; the disorder concerns how they arise, not whether they are felt.
  [[P35430029 Hallett 2022 Functional neurological disorder subtypes and mechanisms#^p35430029-positive|Hallett 2022]]
- "It is diagnosed only when every test comes back normal." It is a positive diagnosis based on
  characteristic examination findings, not a diagnosis reached purely by exclusion.
  [[P35430029 Hallett 2022 Functional neurological disorder subtypes and mechanisms#^p35430029-positive|Hallett 2022]]
- "A stressful event must be found for the diagnosis to be valid." Stressful experiences often precede the
  disorder but are absent in many patients, so the diagnosis does not depend on identifying one.
  [[P35430029 Hallett 2022 Functional neurological disorder subtypes and mechanisms#^p35430029-positive|Hallett 2022]]

## What the sources do not establish

The mechanism models come from group-level imaging and experimental work and are not a diagnostic test for
one person; the dissociation findings come from a meta-analysis with high heterogeneity where some measures
overlap with the functional symptoms studied; and the treatment evidence, while most consistent for
cognitive behavioural therapy and physiotherapy, is rated moderate certainty at best.
[[P41111084 Mavroudis 2025 Treatment of functional neurological disorder#^p41111084-certainty|Mavroudis 2025]]
