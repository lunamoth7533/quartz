# Tic disorders and Tourette syndrome

**Module.** M06 Neurology · **Topic.** [[Tic disorders and Tourette syndrome]]

## Why this matters

Tourette syndrome is defined by tics, but for most people who have it, tics are not the only, or even the
main, source of difficulty. Learning how often other conditions co-occur, and why, protects against
crediting every outcome to the tics themselves.

## The core model

**Tourette syndrome is a hyperkinetic movement disorder with a childhood onset.** It is a common
neurodevelopmental condition affecting up to 1% of people, defined by several motor and vocal tics beginning
in childhood, and classed among the hyperkinetic movement disorders alongside chorea and dystonia.
[[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-definition|Shekhawat 2026]]

**The course varies rather than following one fixed path.** Tics may remit by adulthood while associated
symptoms persist, but many people continue to have tics as adults, and some experience worsening, so there is
no single expected trajectory to measure a given case against.
[[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-course|Shekhawat 2026]]

**Co-occurring conditions are the rule, not the exception.** In a large structured-interview study of 1,374
people with Tourette syndrome, 85.7% had at least one co-occurring psychiatric disorder, and 72.1% met
criteria for OCD or ADHD specifically. Most of these co-occurring conditions began between ages four and ten,
close to the age tics themselves typically start.
[[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-prevalence|Hirschtritt 2015]]
[[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-ocdadhd|Hirschtritt 2015]]

**Mood comorbidity may run through OCD rather than through tics directly.** The high rate of mood disorders
in that study was statistically accounted for by co-occurring OCD, suggesting some of the shared genetic and
clinical links travel through OCD and ADHD rather than through Tourette syndrome itself. This matters for
attribution: a mood symptom in someone with tics is not automatically a tic-related symptom.
[[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-mediation|Hirschtritt 2015]]

**Treatment is offered in graded classes, not as a single path.** A practice guideline sets out watchful
waiting, a specific behavioural therapy, medication, and, for adults with severe tics that have not responded
to other treatment, deep brain stimulation, as classes of option to be weighed together with the person's
circumstances and co-occurring conditions.
[[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-options|Pringsheim 2019]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A child with diagnosed Tourette syndrome is also described as
anxious and has trouble finishing schoolwork. Before treating anxiety and inattention as consequences of the
tics, apply the co-occurrence logic. Because roughly seven in ten people with Tourette syndrome meet criteria
for OCD or ADHD, and mood-related comorbidity has been statistically linked to OCD rather than to tics
directly, the reasonable first step is separate assessment for OCD, ADHD and anxiety in their own right. If
the anxiety centres on urges to perform specific rituals, that points toward OCD; if the schoolwork
difficulty is about sustained attention rather than tic-related distraction, that points toward ADHD. Either
way, the tics and the co-occurring condition need their own evidence, not just co-occurrence.

## Common confusions

- "Tics explain every difficulty in someone with Tourette syndrome." Co-occurring OCD, ADHD and anxiety are
  common enough to be the rule, and mood comorbidity appears to run through OCD specifically.
  [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-mediation|Hirschtritt 2015]]
- "Tics always persist unchanged into adulthood." The course varies: some people remit, many continue to
  have tics, and some worsen. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-course|Shekhawat 2026]]
- "Treatment guidance ranks specific medicines for an individual." The guideline names classes of option to
  be weighed with the person's circumstances, not a ranked prescription list.
  [[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-options|Pringsheim 2019]]

## What the sources do not establish

The co-occurrence figures come from a research sample assembled partly through family and genetic studies,
probably more affected than people with tics in the general community, so the headline rate describes that
sample rather than everyone with tics. The guideline names classes of option without comparative effect
sizes here.
