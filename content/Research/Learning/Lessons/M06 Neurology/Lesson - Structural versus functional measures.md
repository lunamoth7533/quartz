# Structural versus functional measures

**Module.** M06 Neurology · **Topic.** [[Structural versus functional measures]]

## Why this matters

Measures differ in what they sample: anatomy, activity-related signal, electrical activity, chemistry. Most
misreadings of neuroscience happen when a measure is asked a question it was not designed for. This lesson is a
short taxonomy with a long reach into the condition modules.

## The core model

The NINDS overview covers history and examination, CT and MRI imaging, EEG and laboratory tests, and what each
measures.
[[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

MRI is a family of methods: conventional structural MRI images anatomy, while functional MRI measures
activity-related signal change rather than electrical activity itself.
[[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-caution-modality|AI synthesis: Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

So the working taxonomy is: structure (what is there), function (activity-related signals at a coarse temporal
resolution), electrophysiology (electrical activity with millisecond resolution), and chemistry or laboratory
measures (molecules in blood or tissue).

Two consequences matter. First, a measure can be reliable and objective and still answer only the question it
was designed for; a thickness measurement cannot report circuit dynamics. Second, structural findings in this
vault such as group cortical thickness differences describe anatomy, not activity.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

One terminology trap deserves naming. "Functional" describes activity-related signals in imaging, and it
describes daily activity and participation in outcome research. A paper reporting "functional outcomes" is
usually reporting the second kind, while a paper reporting "functional connectivity" reports the first; confusing
them sends readers looking for the wrong kind of data.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A question asks whether a treatment changes moment-to-moment brain
activity during a task.

Choose the measure that can answer it. Structural MRI gives anatomy over a slow timescale, so it cannot.
Functional MRI gives activity-related blood-oxygen-level-dependent signal with seconds of resolution, which
can answer a coarse temporal question. EEG gives summed electrical activity with millisecond resolution, which
answers a timing question. Each choice commits you to a resolution and to a family of analysis assumptions.

The complete answer names the measure, its resolution, what it samples, and what it cannot say — including that
none of these measures establishes why the activity changed.

## Common confusions

- "All brain measures measure brain activity." Some measure anatomy, some electricity, some blood flow
  correlates, some chemistry.
- "Higher resolution is better." Resolution in one dimension trades against another, and the question decides.
- "Objective means unambiguous." Objective measures still require interpretation against norms, models and
  context.

## What the sources do not establish

The NINDS overview describes modality categories without sensitivity, specificity or clinical thresholds, and
this vault holds no source that validates any imaging measure as a clinical test for the conditions it
discusses.
