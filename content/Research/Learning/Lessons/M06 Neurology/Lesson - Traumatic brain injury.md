# Traumatic brain injury

**Module.** M06 Neurology · **Topic.** [[Traumatic brain injury]]

## Why this matters

A head injury, PTSD and ADHD can all produce poor concentration, irritability and disturbed sleep, so a
symptom appearing after an injury does not by itself say which of these labels, if any, fits. This lesson
teaches how severity grading, timing and comparison groups are used to avoid crediting an outcome to the
wrong cause.

## The core model

**Severity is graded at the time of injury, not by the outcome.** Traumatic brain injury is classed as mild,
moderate or severe at the time it happens: mild injury, or concussion, means loss of consciousness under 30
minutes, post-traumatic amnesia under 24 hours, or any altered consciousness, and mild injuries make up more
than 90% of cases. Because the grade describes the acute injury, a mild grade does not rule out lasting
difficulties. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-severity|Ginsburg 2025]]

**Persisting symptoms need a before-and-after comparison.** Long-term risks include persisting headache,
dizziness, poor concentration and depression, but later difficulties may reflect the injury, pre-injury
problems, or both, which is why attribution needs information from before the injury.
[[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-persisting|Ginsburg 2025]]

**PTSD after traumatic brain injury is common but not graded by severity.** Across civilian studies at low
risk of bias, PTSD after traumatic brain injury pooled at 15.6%, similar after mild and after
moderate-to-severe injury - which argues against a simple dose-response reading where worse injury produces
more PTSD.
[[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-severity|Van Praag 2019]]

**The ADHD association survives scrutiny only after severe injury.** Children with traumatic brain injury
already had higher rates of pre-injury ADHD than the general population, and after mild or moderate injury,
later ADHD diagnoses were not clearly more common than in controls; only after severe injury did the rate
rise clearly. Because ADHD itself raises the chance of being injured, only the severe-injury association
survives once pre-injury status is accounted for.
[[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-preinjury|Asarnow 2021]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A teenager has a mild concussion during sport, and six months later
a teacher reports new difficulty concentrating in class. Before crediting the concussion, ask what this
lesson's reasoning implies: the severity grade and how it was assigned, how long since the injury, whether
there is a comparison group of uninjured peers, and whether attention was checked before the injury. Because
mild injury accounts for most cases and the evidence does not show a clear ADHD rise after mild or moderate
injury once pre-injury status is considered, the concussion alone is a weak explanation here unless
pre-injury attention was known to be typical and no other explanation fits better.

## Common confusions

- "Mild means minor and short-lived." The severity grade describes the injury at the time, not the outcome;
  persisting symptoms can follow a mild injury.
  [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-severity|Ginsburg 2025]]
- "A symptom after injury proves the injury caused it." Later difficulties may reflect pre-injury problems,
  the injury, or both, so attribution needs a before-and-after comparison.
  [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-persisting|Ginsburg 2025]]
- "Worse brain injury always means more PTSD." Pooled PTSD prevalence was similar after mild and after
  moderate-to-severe injury in the civilian studies reviewed.
  [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-severity|Van Praag 2019]]

## What the sources do not establish

The severity and symptom picture comes from a clinical reference chapter rather than a systematic review,
the PTSD estimate varies widely across the studies pooled into it, and the ADHD findings come from paediatric
cohorts, so they do not describe adults or military samples.

## Check yourself

1. Why does a mild severity grade not rule out lasting difficulties?
2. Why does the pooled PTSD rate being similar across injury severities complicate a simple "worse injury,
   worse outcome" story?
3. Why does the ADHD-traumatic brain injury association survive mainly for severe injury?

## Answer notes

1. Because severity is graded at the time of the injury, describing the acute event rather than the outcome.
   [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-severity|Ginsburg 2025]]
2. Because if PTSD tracked injury severity in a simple way, the pooled rate after severe injury should be
   clearly higher than after mild injury, which the pooled figures do not show.
   [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-severity|Van Praag 2019]]
3. Because pre-injury ADHD rates are already elevated in children who go on to have a traumatic brain injury,
   and once accounted for, only the severe-injury comparison still shows a raised rate.
   [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-severe|Asarnow 2021]]
