# Allostasis and chronic stress

**Module.** M13 Neuroendocrinology and Neuroimmunology · **Topic.** [[Allostasis and chronic stress]]

## Why this matters

"Chronic stress" is used in everyday speech as if it were one thing that either is or is not present. This lesson takes the HPA axis from the previous lesson and asks what happens when its machinery runs repeatedly - and shows that "chronic stress" is actually at least four different, separately measured things that a single claim needs to keep apart.

## The core model

Allostasis is the idea that physiological stability is maintained through change rather than through a fixed set point: mediators such as cortisol, autonomic activity and inflammatory signals adjust to demand. That is why the same hormone can be protective when it responds briefly to a real demand and damaging when the same response becomes frequent, prolonged or poorly shut off. Allostatic load is the name for the cumulative cost of that repeated or poorly regulated adjustment, and the framework treats the brain as both the controller of these responses and a target of their wear. [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-allostasis|Stress mediators]]

Because the mediators of allostasis interact with each other, measuring only one of them - say, cortisol alone - captures only part of the system. The same idea shows up at the receptor level in the HPA-axis lesson: glucocorticoid actions are classified as permissive, suppressive, stimulatory or preparative depending on context, which is another way of saying no single reading tells the whole story. [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-mediators|Stress mediators]]

Social conditions belong inside the exposure side of this picture, not off to the side as background variation. Low social status, low control and chronic strain are treated as conditions that shape stress exposure and health outcomes in their own right, and the strongest population-level pattern here is distributional: sustained physiological activation tracks social disadvantage. That pattern says where to look for causes across a population; it does not, on its own, measure what is happening inside any one individual. [[F65 OpenStax Stress and illness#^f65-social|Stress and Illness]]

There is no single "chronic stress" variable to measure. Exposure (what happened), appraisal (how it was experienced), physiological mediators (what changed in the body) and cumulative outcome (allostatic load) are four different constructs, measured in four different ways, and a study that measures one and reports conclusions about another has overreached. Allostatic load itself is usually estimated from a composite index built from hormonal, cardiovascular, metabolic and inflammatory markers - because no single measure represents it - but different studies choose different components and weightings, so two "load" indices can rank the same group of people differently. Any claim about load should name which index it used. [[F65 OpenStax Stress and illness#^f65-immune|Stress and Illness]]

A 2023 narrative review restated allostatic load as the cumulative burden of life events and chronic stress, with overload occurring once challenges exceed a person's coping capacity, and argued for combining biomarkers with clinical rating scales rather than relying on biomarkers alone - a wider composite that helps clinical description while making cross-study indices harder to compare directly. [[P37253338 Sonino 2023 Allostatic load and endocrine disorders#^p37253338-definition|Sonino 2023]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A workplace wellness programme claims it "reduced employees' allostatic load by 20%," based on a single follow-up blood pressure reading taken before and after the programme.

Apply the four-construct framework. Blood pressure is one physiological mediator among several that make up any allostatic-load composite; it is not exposure, not appraisal, and not, by itself, a validated load index, since load is typically built from several markers together and different studies weight those markers differently. A change in one mediator is a real but narrow finding.

The accurate statement: "employees' blood pressure fell after the programme; this is one physiological mediator, not a validated composite allostatic-load index, and it does not by itself establish that overall cumulative stress burden changed."

## Common confusions

- "Chronic stress is one measurable thing." It is at least four different constructs - exposure, appraisal, mediators, cumulative load - measured differently.
- "One biomarker equals allostatic load." Load is usually a composite of several markers, and different studies build that composite differently.
- "Allostasis is bad and should be prevented." Allostasis is the normal, adaptive process of adjusting to demand; the concern is repeated or poorly regulated activation, not the response itself.

## What the sources do not establish

The framework is widely used and supported by animal and human correlational evidence, but composite load indices vary between studies and causal direction is difficult to establish in observational work; a load score from one study's index is not directly comparable to a load score built from a different index.
