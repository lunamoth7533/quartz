# Serotonin-noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Serotonin-noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors]]

## Why this matters

SNRIs add a second transporter to the SSRI story, and that addition changes both what the class is used for and
what its risks are. This lesson uses SNRIs to show why "the same class, in a different indication" is a
question you always have to ask separately, rather than something you can infer from the mechanism alone.

## The core model

SNRIs block reuptake of both serotonin and noradrenaline. Venlafaxine, the reference member, is approved in the
United States for major depression, social anxiety disorder and panic disorder; duloxetine, another member, is
the antidepressant with the clearest evidence in chronic pain.
[[P30570984 Singh 2024 Venlafaxine pharmacology and uses#^p30570984-class|Singh 2024]] Venlafaxine and duloxetine
are the SNRIs named in the largest network meta-analysis of acute depression treatment; drugs that block only
the noradrenaline transporter, such as reboxetine and atomoxetine, form a separate group rather than belonging
to this class.
[[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-drugs|Cipriani 2018]];
[[P31211282 Fasipe 2019 Novel antidepressants and agomelatine#^p31211282-classes|Fasipe 2019]] As with the
SSRIs, transporter blockade is immediate while clinical change in depression takes weeks.

In adult depression, SNRIs were among the drugs that all beat placebo in the largest network, with small and
uncertain differences between individual drugs - the same pattern seen across most antidepressant classes.
[[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-placebo|Cipriani 2018]] Chronic
pain is where this class stands apart: duloxetine ranked first on every efficacy outcome in a synthesis of pain
trials, with moderate-to-high-certainty evidence of a small to moderate benefit, milnacipran ranked next, and
evidence for other antidepressants in pain was low certainty.
[[P37160297 Birkinshaw 2023 Antidepressants for chronic pain#^p37160297-duloxetine|Birkinshaw 2023]] Venlafaxine
also appears on off-label lists for fibromyalgia, neuropathy, migraine prevention and other conditions, but an
off-label list records what is prescribed in practice, not what has been tested in trials.
[[P30570984 Singh 2024 Venlafaxine pharmacology and uses#^p30570984-offlabel|Singh 2024]]

On adverse effects, duloxetine and venlafaxine were among the antidepressants with the highest dropout rates in
head-to-head depression trials, and discontinuation symptoms apply to the class as they do to other
antidepressants, at rates that differ between drugs.
[[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-acceptability|Cipriani 2018]];
[[P38851198 Henssler 2024 Antidepressant discontinuation symptom incidence#^p38851198-incidence|Henssler 2024]]
In the pain trials, safety evidence was very low certainty for every antidepressant tested, with no drug having
reliable long-term data.
[[P37160297 Birkinshaw 2023 Antidepressants for chronic pain#^p37160297-gaps|Birkinshaw 2023]] The noradrenergic
half of the mechanism has a measurable signature in a neighbouring drug group: the noradrenaline-selective drugs
atomoxetine and viloxazine raised blood pressure and pulse against placebo in ADHD trials - though carrying that
finding across to SNRIs by shared mechanism, rather than by a finding about SNRIs themselves, is this library's
own inference.
[[P40203844 Farhat 2025 Cardiovascular safety of ADHD medications#^p40203844-haemodynamic|Farhat 2025]];
[[P40203844 Farhat 2025 Cardiovascular safety of ADHD medications#^p40203844-caution-route|Appraisal: Farhat 2025]]

In bipolar depression, an international task force judged the evidence for antidepressants weak overall and
named SNRIs, alongside tricyclic and tetracyclic drugs, as possibly carrying a higher switch risk - shifting
mood into hypomania, mania or mixed states - than SSRIs or bupropion. That class comparison rests on expert
consensus rather than randomised evidence, and bipolar-I use was confined to alongside a mood stabiliser.
[[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-switch|Pacchiarotti 2013]];
[[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-caution-consensus|Appraisal: Pacchiarotti 2013]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A wellness article claims: "Duloxetine treats chronic pain, so
antidepressants as a class must relieve pain." Apply this lesson's reading rule: name the indication before
generalising. Duloxetine's pain evidence is specific to duloxetine, with milnacipran a weaker second and every
other antidepressant tested at only low certainty - it is not a class-wide finding. The pain trials themselves
were conducted in people without clinically low mood, so the result says nothing about antidepressant action in
depression, and a depression trial result says nothing about pain relief in return. The mechanism these drugs
share - blocking two transporters - does not by itself predict which specific outcome, in which specific
population, will show a benefit; each indication needs its own trial evidence.

## Common confusions

- "SNRIs are just stronger SSRIs." Adding noradrenaline transporter block changes the adverse-effect profile and
  the evidence base, including a distinct chronic-pain indication, not simply the strength of the same effect.
- "A drug's off-label use list is evidence it works for those conditions." An off-label list records prescribing
  practice, not trial support.
- "One antidepressant's pain benefit means the whole class helps pain." Duloxetine's pain evidence does not
  transfer automatically to other members of its own class, let alone to other classes.

## What the sources do not establish

These sources do not measure whether SNRIs raise blood pressure and pulse the way the noradrenaline-selective
ADHD drugs do - that connection is this library's inference, not a tested finding - and no SNRI evidence
specific to autistic people or to anxiety disorders beyond venlafaxine's approvals is sourced here.

## Answer notes

1. Blockade of the noradrenaline transporter in addition to the serotonin transporter that SSRIs already block.
2. Duloxetine; the pain trials were conducted in people without clinically low mood, so the result says nothing
   about antidepressant action, and long-term safety data are lacking.
3. Because it comes from an expert task force's consensus judgement rather than from randomised comparisons
   between the drug classes.
