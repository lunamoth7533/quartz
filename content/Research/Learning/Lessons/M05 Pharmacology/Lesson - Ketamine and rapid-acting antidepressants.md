# Ketamine and rapid-acting antidepressants

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Ketamine and rapid-acting antidepressants]]

## Why this matters

Every class earlier in this module takes weeks to show a clinical effect. This group is defined by not doing
that, and the speed is exactly what makes the evidence easy to overstate. This lesson closes the module by
applying its most important habit - name the drug, the measured time point and the certainty grade - to the
group of treatments where the temptation to skip that step is strongest.

## The core model

Rapid-acting antidepressants are grouped by speed rather than by target: ketamine infusion, intranasal
esketamine and a dextromethorphan-bupropion combination all acted sooner than monoamine antidepressants in
trials, though with broadly similar effect sizes rather than larger ones.
[[P40282849 Gao 2025 Pharmacological monotherapy for depressive disorders#^p40282849-rapid|Gao 2025]] Ketamine
itself is an NMDA receptor blocker - a mechanism class one review proposes admitting among the antidepressants
in its own right, distinct from the reuptake and enzyme-inhibition routes covered earlier in this module.
[[P31211282 Fasipe 2019 Novel antidepressants and agomelatine#^p31211282-classes|Fasipe 2019]]

The initiating mechanisms proposed for these drugs differ - NMDA receptor subtypes, a different glutamate
receptor route, AMPA receptor potentiation and, for the unrelated psychedelic drugs, serotonin receptor
activation. Despite those different starting points, one synthesis argues that they converge on stronger
excitatory synapses and an altered capacity for later plasticity, a process called metaplasticity, and proposes
that convergence as a treatment target in its own right. That account rests largely on preclinical work and does
not show that conventional monoamine antidepressants work the same way.
[[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-convergence|Brown 2024]];
[[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-caution-scope|Appraisal: Brown 2024]]

What has actually been measured, and how confidently? In unipolar depression, ketamine may raise response and
remission and lower depression scores at 24 hours, but only on very low-certainty evidence. Esketamine probably
increases remission and lowers scores at 24 hours, on moderate-certainty evidence from roughly 800 to 900
participants - a meaningfully stronger evidence base than ketamine's, from repeated intranasal dosing rather
than a single infusion.
[[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-ketamine|Dean 2021]];
[[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-esketamine|Dean 2021]] Esketamine's trial
dropout was not clearly different from placebo, but ketamine's psychotomimetic effects were noticeable enough
that they may have broken blinding in some trials - a methodological problem, because participants and raters
who can guess which arm they are in can bias a subjective symptom-rating outcome.
[[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-esketamine|Dean 2021]];
[[P34623633 Dean 2021 Ketamine for bipolar depression#^p34623633-blinding|Dean 2021]] Long-term safety and
benefit with repeated treatment are not established, and the reviewing Cochrane authors called for long-term
comparative trials and real-world monitoring rather than treating the 24-hour result as the whole story.
[[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-gaps|Dean 2021]]

Bipolar depression has thinner and shorter evidence again. Of the glutamate-system drugs tested there, only
ketamine beat placebo, on response 24 hours after a single infusion added to a mood stabiliser, in two trials
totalling 33 participants on low-certainty evidence; it showed no clear remission advantage, and its effect was
described as rapid but transient.
[[P34623633 Dean 2021 Ketamine for bipolar depression#^p34623633-response|Dean 2021]] Two small trials say
nothing about maintenance, switching over time, or what repeated treatment would show.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A clinic's marketing page states: "Our ketamine treatment produces
lasting remission from depression." Apply the module's closing reading rule: name the drug, the route, and the
time point, because each combination is a separate claim. "24-hour response after a single infusion" is one
claim, measured with low to very low certainty in the sources checked here. "Remission after repeated
intranasal dosing" is a different claim - esketamine's, not ketamine's, and measured with moderate certainty. "A
durable, lasting benefit" is a third claim, and it is the one the evidence checked here does not establish;
reviewers explicitly called for long-term trials because that question is still open. The marketing sentence
quietly swaps the third claim in for the first two, which is the single most common way rapid-acting-drug
evidence gets overstated.

## Common confusions

- "Fast-acting means more effective." Faster onset has so far come without a larger effect size than the
  monoamine classes.
- "A 24-hour result tells you about durability." A 24-hour outcome measures speed; whether benefit persists with
  repeated treatment is a separate, still-open question.
- "Ketamine and esketamine have the same evidence strength." Esketamine's evidence is moderate certainty from a
  larger trial base; ketamine's is very low certainty.

## What the sources do not establish

Whether benefit persists with repeated ketamine or esketamine, and at what long-term cost, is unknown; misuse
potential and effects outside trial settings are not covered here; and no evidence in PTSD, ADHD or autism is
sourced for this group.

## Answer notes

1. Speed of onset relative to monoamine antidepressants, not a shared target - the group spans NMDA receptor
   blockade, other glutamate-receptor mechanisms and, for related psychedelic drugs, serotonin receptor
   activation.
2. Because a noticeable drug effect can let participants or raters guess which treatment arm they are in, which
   can bias a subjective outcome measure and may have broken blinding in some trials.
3. A response measured 24 hours after a single infusion; remission measured after repeated intranasal dosing;
   and a durable, lasting benefit - only the first two have been measured with meaningful certainty.
