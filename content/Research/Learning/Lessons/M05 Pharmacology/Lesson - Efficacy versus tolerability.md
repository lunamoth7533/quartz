# Efficacy versus tolerability

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Efficacy versus tolerability]]

## Why this matters

Two treatments with identical efficacy can differ enormously in what they cost the person taking them. Trial
reporting usually captures that difference as dropout, which is a crude instrument. This lesson teaches you to
read benefit and burden as separate axes.

## The core model

Efficacy is how well a treatment reduces the target problem under study; tolerability is the burden and side
effects it creates.
[[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-tolerability|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]

Dose-effect analyses treat efficacy and discontinuation for adverse events as separate outcomes, and they found
that dropout rose with dose for amphetamines and methylphenidate above study-specific ranges.
[[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-tolerability|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]

In adults, atomoxetine and guanfacine had lower acceptability than placebo in a component network meta-analysis
of 113 trials.
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-acceptability|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]

And in an earlier synthesis, amphetamines were less well tolerated than placebo in both children and adults,
with the pattern differing by drug and age group.
[[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-tolerability|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]

The critical reading skill is to notice what the tolerability measure is. Most trials operationalise it as
all-cause or adverse-event discontinuation, which undercounts bothersome effects that people endure rather than
report, and it confounds tolerability with perceived efficacy and with the logistics of trial participation.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A trial reports that drug A and drug B had similar efficacy on the
primary symptom scale, and similar rates of discontinuation for adverse events, and concludes that they are
"equally well tolerated".

Interrogate the conclusion. What was recorded between the two ends of the dropout measure — appetite change,
sleep disruption, irritability, fatigue, emotional blunting? Were side effects solicited with a systematic
checklist, rated for severity, and reported per person rather than as group means? Did the trial measure what
participants themselves cared about?

The defensible conclusion: "the two drugs had similar efficacy on this scale and similar dropout for adverse
events; burden below the dropout threshold was not comparably characterised, so the tolerability comparison is
incomplete."

## Common confusions

- "Tolerability is adherence." Adherence includes many reasons; tolerability is one contributor.
- "A low dropout rate means few side effects." It means few people left the trial for that reason.
- "Higher dose means more benefit." The dose-effect analyses show plateaus and rising discontinuation, not a
  monotonic relationship.

## What the sources do not establish

These syntheses describe aggregate dose-effect and acceptability patterns in trial populations; none of them
provides guidance for an individual, and the dose ranges are study-specific. This vault deliberately omits
numerical dose and titration detail.

## Check yourself

1. Define efficacy and tolerability in one sentence each.
2. What does the dropout measure miss?
3. Why can two drugs be "equally effective" and not "equally tolerable"?

## Answer notes

1. Efficacy is how much the target outcome improves under study conditions; tolerability is the burden the
   treatment imposes.
2. Burdensome effects that people continue to endure, and the reasons people leave that are not adverse events.
3. Because dropout and side-effect measurement capture only part of the burden, and the profiles can differ
   even when symptom reduction is similar.
