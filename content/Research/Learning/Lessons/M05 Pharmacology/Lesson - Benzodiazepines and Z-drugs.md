# Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs]]

## Why this matters

Benzodiazepines and the Z-drugs are effective within weeks and risky over months, and both halves of that
sentence are true at once. Reading the evidence for this class means holding short-term relief and
long-accumulating harm as two separate questions answered by two different kinds of study.

## The core model

Benzodiazepines are positive modulators of the GABA-A receptor: rather than switching the receptor on by
themselves, they strengthen the inhibitory signal that the brain's own GABA is already producing.
[[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-mechanism|Griffin 2013]] The Z-drugs — zolpidem,
zopiclone, eszopiclone and zaleplon — were introduced as insomnia alternatives to benzodiazepines and act on the
same GABA receptors.
[[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-motive|Treves 2018]] Because one mechanism
amplifies whatever inhibition is already present, the same drug class produces several different clinical
effects — sedative, anti-anxiety, anticonvulsant and muscle-relaxant — depending on dose and which receptor
subtype is involved.
[[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-effects|Griffin 2013]]

Short-term trial evidence is solid. In insomnia disorder, benzodiazepines, zolpidem, zopiclone and eszopiclone
all improved self-rated sleep quality more than placebo in acute trials, with moderate-to-high certainty.
[[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-acute|De Crescenzo 2022]] But almost none
of that benefit was shown to last: among the group, only eszopiclone beat placebo over the longer term, and even
that finding carried very low certainty.
[[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-longterm|De Crescenzo 2022]]

The harms accumulate on a different timescale and are documented through different kinds of study. Z-drug use in
older adults was linked to a substantially higher chance of fracture, contradicting the common assumption that
they are the safer alternative to benzodiazepines.
[[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-fractures|Treves 2018]] A regulatory review of
complex sleep behaviours — actions like driving or eating while not fully awake — on Z-drugs found 20 deaths and
46 serious injuries,
[[P32323442 Harbourt 2020 Z-drugs and complex sleep behaviours#^p32323442-cases|Harbourt 2020]] a third of them
in people who had already had a milder episode of the same behaviour.
[[P32323442 Harbourt 2020 Z-drugs and complex sleep behaviours#^p32323442-recurrence|Harbourt 2020]] In PTSD
specifically, benzodiazepines neither treated nor prevented the disorder across 18 studies,
[[P26164054 Guina 2015 Benzodiazepines for PTSD#^p26164054-ineffective|Guina 2015]] and reviewers regard them as
relatively contraindicated soon after a traumatic event, given reported links to worse severity and poorer
outcomes from trauma-focused therapy.
[[P26164054 Guina 2015 Benzodiazepines for PTSD#^p26164054-conclusion|Guina 2015]]
[[P26164054 Guina 2015 Benzodiazepines for PTSD#^p26164054-harms|Guina 2015]] Combined with opioids, the risk
compounds further: among more than 300,000 insured adults, overlapping benzodiazepine and opioid prescriptions
were linked to roughly double the odds of an overdose-related emergency visit or hospital admission.
[[P28292769 Sun 2017 Opioid and benzodiazepine co-use and overdose#^p28292769-overdose|Sun 2017]]

**Reading rule.** Keep short-term efficacy and accumulating harm in separate mental columns: efficacy is
measured over weeks on sleep or anxiety scales in randomised trials, while dependence, falls, complex behaviours
and overdose come from longer observational data with their own confounding, so neither kind of evidence answers
the other's question.
[[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-caution-outcomes|Appraisal: De Crescenzo 2022]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Imagine someone argues: "This trial proved the sleeping pill works, so
long-term safety worries must be overblown." Separate the two claims the argument runs together. The acute trial
evidence for improved self-rated sleep quality is genuinely strong for several of these drugs. But that trial,
run over a few weeks, was not designed to detect fracture risk, complex sleep behaviours, or dependence, which
show up in registries and case series tracking much larger groups over much longer periods.

The accurate statement keeps both results standing side by side: "the short-term trial evidence for sleep
improvement is solid, and it says nothing about the separately measured long-term risks."

## Common confusions

- "Z-drugs are the safe alternative to benzodiazepines." Z-drug use was linked to a higher fracture risk in
  older adults, contradicting that reputation.
- "If a trial shows a drug works, its safety profile must be settled too." Efficacy and harm are typically
  measured in different kinds of study over different timeframes.
- "Benzodiazepines are a reasonable first response right after a traumatic event." Reviewers describe them as
  relatively contraindicated in that situation given the reported harms.

## What the sources do not establish

The Z-drug fracture and complex-behaviour findings come from observational and case-series data rather than
randomised comparisons, so how these drugs compare head-to-head with benzodiazepines on the same harms is not
established, and the PTSD review mixes different study designs and populations.
[[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Treves 2018]]
[[P26164054 Guina 2015 Benzodiazepines for PTSD#^p26164054-limit|Guina 2015]]
