# Muscarinic antipsychotics

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Muscarinic antipsychotics]]

## Why this matters

Until September 2024, every approved antipsychotic acted on dopamine receptors. The approval of
xanomeline-trospium broke that pattern, giving psychiatry its first approved antipsychotic that reaches its
effect through a completely different receptor family. This lesson uses that single drug to practice reading a
genuinely new mechanism claim against genuinely short evidence.

## The core model

Xanomeline-trospium treats psychosis by stimulating muscarinic acetylcholine receptors rather than by blocking
dopamine D2 receptors, and the combination has no direct D2-blocking activity.
[[F127 FDA prescribing information COBENFY#^f127-mechanism|COBENFY prescribing information]]
[[P38691387 Kaul 2024 Xanomeline-trospium EMERGENT-3 trial#^p38691387-efficacy|Kaul 2024]] Its own approved
prescribing information is unusually candid: it states that xanomeline's mechanism of action in schizophrenia is
unclear, while noting that efficacy is thought to follow from stimulating M1 and M4 receptors in the brain.
[[F127 FDA prescribing information COBENFY#^f127-mechanism|COBENFY prescribing information]] Xanomeline binds
all five muscarinic receptor subtypes with similar affinity but activates M1 and M4 most, which is why it is
paired with trospium, a second drug that is largely restricted to blocking muscarinic receptors outside the
brain.
[[F127 FDA prescribing information COBENFY#^f127-pharmacodynamics|COBENFY prescribing information]]

Pooling three five-week placebo-controlled trials, xanomeline-trospium reduced overall psychotic symptom scores,
positive symptoms, negative symptoms and global severity, and roughly doubled the response rate compared with
placebo, with moderate-certainty evidence.
[[P39724748 Fabiano 2025 Xanomeline-trospium meta-analysis#^p39724748-efficacy|Fabiano 2025]] Because the drug
does not touch dopamine receptors, it also largely avoids the dopamine-linked side effects: extrapyramidal
symptoms, weight gain and sleepiness were similar to placebo, while stomach-related effects such as nausea and
constipation were more common than on placebo, mostly mild to moderate.
[[P39724748 Fabiano 2025 Xanomeline-trospium meta-analysis#^p39724748-safety|Fabiano 2025]]

The longest data available come from an open-label extension running to 52 weeks, in which gastrointestinal
effects eased over time and no new motor, weight or metabolic problems appeared among people who stayed in the
study.
[[P41634905 Kaul 2026 Xanomeline-trospium 52-week extension#^p41634905-safety|Kaul 2026]] But only 34 of the 156
people who enrolled actually completed the full year.
[[P41634905 Kaul 2026 Xanomeline-trospium 52-week extension#^p41634905-uptake|Kaul 2026]] A different route to
the same receptor family, a compound called emraclidine, works by amplifying acetylcholine's own signal at the
M4 receptor rather than activating the receptor directly — the same amplifying logic that benzodiazepines use at
a different receptor.
[[P36528376 Krystal 2022 Emraclidine phase 1b trial#^p36528376-compound|Krystal 2022]]

**Reading rule.** Treat "muscarinic antipsychotic" as a genuinely new but thinly tested claim: the drug's own
label calls its mechanism unclear, its efficacy evidence comes from five-week placebo trials, and its one-year
data come from an extension that most participants did not finish.
[[P41634905 Kaul 2026 Xanomeline-trospium 52-week extension#^p41634905-caution-attrition|Appraisal: Kaul 2026]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Suppose a news article claims: "A landmark new antipsychotic causes none
of the side effects doctors used to worry about." Rather than accepting the sweeping claim, ask which side
effects the drug avoids and why — here, the absence of dopamine-linked movement and prolactin effects follows
directly from the drug not blocking dopamine receptors at all, which is a mechanistic explanation, not a claim
that the drug has no side effects. Then check what new side effects appear in its place: nausea, constipation
and a faster resting heart rate showed up more often than on placebo.

The accurate summary trades "no side effects" for "a different side-effect profile that follows its different
target."

## Common confusions

- "No dopamine side effects means no side effects." The drug trades one side-effect profile for another,
  centred on the gut and heart rate.
- "The mechanism is fully understood because the drug is approved." The prescribing information itself calls the
  mechanism in schizophrenia unclear.
- "One year of open-label data proves long-term safety." Most enrolled participants did not complete the
  extension, which limits what the completers' data can show.

## What the sources do not establish

There is no head-to-head trial comparing xanomeline-trospium against dopamine-blocking antipsychotics, so its
place relative to the established drugs is not settled, and whether selective M4 modulation like emraclidine
treats psychosis at all cannot be answered by a safety-only study.
[[P38691387 Kaul 2024 Xanomeline-trospium EMERGENT-3 trial#^p38691387-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Kaul 2024]]
[[P36528376 Krystal 2022 Emraclidine phase 1b trial#^p36528376-caution-efficacy|Appraisal: Krystal 2022]]

## Answer notes

1. Xanomeline binds muscarinic receptors throughout the body, so trospium, which blocks muscarinic receptors
   mainly outside the brain, is added to limit peripheral side effects.
2. Extrapyramidal symptoms and weight gain were similar to placebo; gastrointestinal effects such as nausea were
   more common than placebo.
3. Most of the people who enrolled did not complete the full year, so the completers may not represent everyone
   who started treatment.
