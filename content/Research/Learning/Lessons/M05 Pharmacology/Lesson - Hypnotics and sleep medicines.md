# Hypnotics and sleep medicines

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Hypnotics and sleep medicines]]

## Why this matters

"Sleep medicine" is not one mechanism but at least three different routes to the same outcome: strengthening
inhibition, blocking an arousal signal, or shifting the body clock. Confusing the routes is easy because they
are marketed and discussed together; separating them is the point of this lesson.

## The core model

The drugs trialled for insomnia disorder include benzodiazepines and Z-drugs, orexin receptor antagonists,
melatonin and the melatonin-receptor agonist ramelteon, an antihistamine, and sedating antidepressants — a
genuinely varied list rather than one drug family.
[[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-acute|De Crescenzo 2022]] Benzodiazepines
and Z-drugs strengthen GABA-A receptor signalling, as covered in the previous lesson. Orexin receptor
antagonists take the opposite approach: insomnia is framed as a state of hyperarousal, orexin as one of the
brain's key promoters of that arousal and vigilance, and these drugs block orexin's signal for a time rather
than touching GABA receptors at all.
[[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-arousal|Muehlan 2023]]
[[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-mechanism|Muehlan 2023]]

Melatonin takes a third, entirely different route. It is a hormone released by the pineal gland that helps
regulate the timing of the sleep-wake cycle, acting through MT1 and MT2 receptors, and in the United States it
has no approved medical indication and is sold as a dietary supplement rather than a regulated medicine.
[[P30521244 Savage 2024 Melatonin pharmacology and uses#^p30521244-receptors|Savage 2024]]
[[P30521244 Savage 2024 Melatonin pharmacology and uses#^p30521244-status|Savage 2024]] That difference in route
shows up directly in the trial evidence: melatonin and ramelteon showed no material overall benefit for
insomnia disorder in adults, unlike several of the GABA- and orexin-acting drugs.
[[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-limited|De Crescenzo 2022]]

Melatonin's evidence looks different again outside adult insomnia disorder. In children with neurodevelopmental
disabilities, it lengthened diary-reported sleep by roughly half an hour compared with placebo
[[P30710339 Parker 2019 Melatonin for sleep in children with neurodisabilities#^p30710339-sleep|Parker 2019]]
and was generally well tolerated,
[[P30710339 Parker 2019 Melatonin for sleep in children with neurodisabilities#^p30710339-safety|Parker 2019]]
though nearly every trial in that body of evidence carried a high or unclear risk of bias.
[[P30710339 Parker 2019 Melatonin for sleep in children with neurodisabilities#^p30710339-bias|Parker 2019]]
That is a genuinely different population and outcome measure from the adult insomnia-disorder trials discussed
above, and the two results should not be merged into one blanket claim about "melatonin."

**Reading rule.** Before accepting any claim about a "sleep medicine," name which route it uses — adding
inhibition, blocking an arousal signal, or shifting the body clock — and which population and outcome the
supporting trial actually measured, since a result for self-rated sleep quality in adults with insomnia disorder
does not automatically speak to circadian problems, to children, or to next-day function.
[[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-caution-outcomes|Appraisal: De Crescenzo 2022]]
[[P30521244 Savage 2024 Melatonin pharmacology and uses#^p30521244-caution-roles|Appraisal: Savage 2024]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Suppose an advertisement claims: "Melatonin and prescription sleep aids
work the same way, just naturally." Check the mechanism claim against what each drug actually does: melatonin
acts on MT1 and MT2 receptors to influence circadian timing, while benzodiazepines and Z-drugs strengthen GABA-A
inhibition and orexin antagonists block a separate arousal signal. These are three distinct routes to promoting
sleep, not variations on one mechanism, and the trial evidence reflects that difference: melatonin showed no
material overall benefit in adult insomnia disorder trials, unlike some of the GABA- and orexin-acting drugs.

The accurate statement replaces "works the same way" with "reaches sleep through a different route, with
different supporting evidence."

## Common confusions

- "All sleep medicines work through the same mechanism." At least three distinct routes are represented across
  this group.
- "Melatonin has no effect because it's 'natural'." Its evidence differs by population — limited overall benefit
  in adult insomnia disorder trials, a modest effect in children with neurodevelopmental disabilities.
- "A finding in one sleep-medicine population applies to all of them." Adult insomnia-disorder trials and
  paediatric neurodevelopmental-disability trials are different evidence bases.

## What the sources do not establish

The paediatric melatonin trials carry very high heterogeneity and rely on parent-kept sleep diaries rather than
objective measures, so which children benefit most is not established, and the advantages claimed for orexin
antagonists come from comparing separate studies of different drug classes rather than a single head-to-head
trial.
[[P30710339 Parker 2019 Melatonin for sleep in children with neurodisabilities#^p30710339-caution-diary|Appraisal: Parker 2019]]
[[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Muehlan 2023]]

## Check yourself

1. Name three distinct mechanistic routes to promoting sleep represented among these drugs.
2. Why did melatonin and ramelteon fare differently from benzodiazepines and Z-drugs in the adult
   insomnia-disorder trials?
3. Why shouldn't the children's melatonin findings be treated as the same evidence base as the adult
   insomnia-disorder trials?

## Answer notes

1. Strengthening GABA-A inhibition, blocking orexin signalling, and shifting circadian timing through melatonin
   receptors.
2. Melatonin and ramelteon showed no material overall benefit in those trials, while several GABA- and
   orexin-acting drugs did.
3. They come from a different population (children with neurodevelopmental disabilities rather than adults with
   insomnia disorder), a different outcome measure (parent-kept diaries), and carry a high or unclear risk of
   bias.
