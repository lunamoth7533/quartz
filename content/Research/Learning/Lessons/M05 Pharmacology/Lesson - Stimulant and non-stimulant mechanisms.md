# Stimulant and non-stimulant mechanisms

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Stimulant and non-stimulant mechanisms]]

## Why this matters

Stimulant and non-stimulant medications for ADHD are usually presented as two different categories, almost
opposites. Underneath the labels, both raise the same two transmitters in the same brain circuit, by different
molecular routes. Seeing that shared target changes how you read comparisons between them, and it closes out
this module's tour of psychiatric drug classes by returning to the receptor-level thinking the module started
with.

## The core model

Both stimulant and non-stimulant ADHD medications increase noradrenaline and dopamine signalling in prefrontal
circuits, just by different molecular routes and on different time courses. Reuptake blockade raises the
concentration of transmitter available outside the cell, and drugs differ in which transporter they act on and
in additional actions such as promoting transmitter release: the classic contrast pairs amphetamine-type release
with methylphenidate-type reuptake blockade, while atomoxetine selectively blocks noradrenaline reuptake.
[[F12 NIGMS What happens to medicine in your body#^f12-adme|What happens to medicine in your body]]

The reason the prefrontal cortex is the target of interest is a specific relationship between arousal and
performance. Prefrontal function depends on noradrenaline and dopamine following an inverted-U shape: too little
signalling and too much signalling both impair performance, with the beneficial range attributed to postsynaptic
alpha-2A receptors and a moderate degree of D1 receptor stimulation.
[[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-catecholamines|Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD]]
That inverted-U shape is the mechanistic reason clonidine and guanfacine, the alpha-2 agonists from the previous
lesson, belong in the same family as the stimulants and atomoxetine: all of them are trying to move signalling
toward the same beneficial middle range, from different starting points and by different routes.
[[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-mechanisms|Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD]]

The trial evidence sits one level up from this mechanism, and rater matters as much as drug. In a 2018 network
comparison, effects on clinician-rated symptoms appeared for every drug studied in children and adolescents,
while teacher-rated effects appeared for only two — showing that who is rating the outcome shapes what a trial
finds.
[[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]
In adults, stimulants and atomoxetine both had higher efficacy than placebo at 12 weeks on self-reported and
clinician-reported scales, while most treatment components were similar to placebo on how often people stopped
treatment altogether, and atomoxetine and guanfacine specifically had lower acceptability.
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-stimulants|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-acceptability|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

A 2026 synthesis adds a dose-response layer without adding any titration guidance: median efficacy plateaued
above study-specific dose ranges for several of these drugs in children,
[[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-doseeffect|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]
while discontinuation for adverse effects rose above those same ranges for amphetamines and methylphenidate —
[[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-tolerability|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]
a pattern the authors read as arguing against both under-treating and mechanically raising dose.
[[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-interpretation|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]
Group-level curves like these describe averages across many people; they do not set anything for one individual,
and this vault reproduces none of the numbers involved.
[[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-caution-individual|Appraisal: ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]

**Reading rule.** Keep three claims in separate compartments: the mechanism claim about why a class of drugs
could plausibly work, the group-level claim about average short-term effects on a named rater's scale, and the
individual claim about what a particular person will experience. Mechanism and group averages do not answer the
third question.
[[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-caution-choice|Appraisal: ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Suppose a comparison chart states: "Stimulants and non-stimulants work
in opposite ways, so if one type doesn't help, the other definitely will." Check the mechanism claim first: both
classes are described as raising noradrenaline and dopamine signalling in the same prefrontal circuit, just by
different molecular routes, which is a difference in mechanism detail, not opposite directions of effect. Then
check what the trial evidence can and cannot say: efficacy differs by rater and by drug, and response varies
substantially between individuals in ways mechanism alone does not predict.

The defensible statement becomes: "these classes reach the same target circuit through different molecular
routes, and trial evidence supports both classes on average, but neither the mechanism nor the group averages
guarantee that a specific non-stimulant will help a specific person who did not respond to a stimulant."

## Common confusions

- "Stimulant and non-stimulant means opposite mechanisms." Both raise noradrenaline and dopamine signalling in
  the same circuit, by different routes.
- "A drug's mechanism predicts an individual's response." Mechanism explains why a class could work in general;
  it does not predict one person's outcome.
- "Teacher and clinician ratings should agree if the drug works." Rater and setting can shape which effects a
  trial detects.

## What the sources do not establish

The inverted-U account combines imaging, genetic and animal pharmacology evidence rather than clinical trial
data, individual response varies substantially and is not predicted by mechanism, and longer-term functional and
cardiovascular questions remain under-investigated relative to the short-term symptom evidence.
