# Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors]]

## Why this matters

SSRIs are the class most people picture when they hear "antidepressant," and they are the class this module uses
to show how one shared mechanism can read very differently depending on the condition it is read against - firm
in one, weak in another, and a specific caution in a third.

## The core model

SSRIs share one action: they block reuptake of serotonin at the transporter, keeping it active in the synaptic
cleft for longer, even though individual members differ in chemical structure and kinetics.
[[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-mechanism|Bruggeman 2023]]
Citalopram, escitalopram, fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, paroxetine and sertraline are the members named in the
largest network meta-analysis of acute depression treatment, and guidelines rank the class ahead of the
tricyclics for major depression while it is also used for anxiety, obsessive-compulsive, panic and eating
disorders. [[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-drugs|Cipriani 2018]]
As with other monoamine antidepressants, transporter blockade is immediate while clinical change in depression
unfolds over weeks - the delay this module's previous lesson treats as the field's central open question.

In that network meta-analysis, every drug, SSRIs included, produced more responders than placebo, but
differences between individual drugs shrank once placebo-controlled trials were counted, and most head-to-head
comparisons carried wide uncertainty; fluoxetine was one of only two drugs with fewer dropouts than placebo.
[[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-placebo|Cipriani 2018]];
[[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-acceptability|Cipriani 2018]]
That ranking describes acute treatment in trials that excluded bipolar, psychotic and treatment-resistant
depression, and it is not a guide to choosing between drugs.
[[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-caution-ranking|Appraisal: Cipriani 2018]]

SSRIs are described as less toxic than the MAOIs and tricyclics that came before them, though reported adverse
effects include low sodium, sexual dysfunction, sedation or activation, stomach upset, uncommon seizures and
impaired platelet function, and citalopram and escitalopram can lengthen a cardiac conduction interval at
higher exposure.
[[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-uses|Bruggeman 2023]];
[[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-adverse|Bruggeman 2023]]
Stopping matters too: about 31% of people had at least one discontinuation symptom against 17% on placebo,
severe symptoms in about 3%, with rates differing between drugs - and a list of possible harms is not a
frequency estimate for ordinary use.
[[P38851198 Henssler 2024 Antidepressant discontinuation symptom incidence#^p38851198-incidence|Henssler 2024]];
[[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-caution-frequency|Appraisal: Bruggeman 2023]]

The same transporter mechanism reads differently once you leave adult depression. In PTSD it is the firmest
evidence outside depression itself: SSRIs beat placebo on response, about 58% against 35%, on moderate-certainty
evidence, and nearly every guideline names them first-line - though the trials enrolled PTSD rather than ICD-11
complex PTSD, so that transfer is untested.
[[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-ssri|Williams 2026]];
[[P34575284 Martin 2021 Treatment guidelines for PTSD#^p34575284-firstline|Martin 2021]] In pooled placebo-
controlled autism trials, antidepressants had only a small effect on restricted and repetitive behaviours, a
result drawn from few small trials.
[[P35948343 Liang 2022 Antidepressants for autism symptoms#^p35948343-effects|Liang 2022]] In bipolar disorder,
an international task force found antidepressants used widely on weak evidence and flagged a switch concern -
shifting mood into hypomania, mania or mixed states - confining bipolar-I use to alongside a mood stabiliser.
[[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-evidence|Pacchiarotti 2013]];
[[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-bipolar-i|Pacchiarotti 2013]]
One mechanism, three separate evidence pictures.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A forum post claims: "SSRIs are proven safe and effective, full stop."
Test that against this lesson. "Effective" for what - and rated by whom? The evidence is moderate-to-very-low
certainty in adult depression, moderate certainty in PTSD, and only a small, uncertain effect in autism trials.
"Safe" compared with what - the class is described as less toxic than the drugs it replaced, but it still
carries a defined discontinuation-symptom rate and, in bipolar disorder specifically, a switch concern that has
nothing to do with toxicity in the usual sense. A single "safe and effective, full stop" claim erases exactly
the condition-by-condition differences that make the evidence readable; the honest version of the claim always
names the condition.

## Common confusions

- "One mechanism means one evidence picture." The same transporter action carries very different certainty and
  strength of evidence across depression, PTSD, autism and bipolar disorder.
- "Less toxic than older classes means side-effect free." SSRIs still carry defined adverse effects and a
  measurable discontinuation-symptom rate.
- "A trial ranking of drugs is prescribing advice." Rankings describe average results in a specific trial
  population, not a recommendation for any one person.

## What the sources do not establish

These sources do not explain why one person responds to one SSRI and not another, do not cover SSRIs for core
ADHD symptoms, and do not establish whether the PTSD findings transfer to ICD-11 complex PTSD.

## Answer notes

1. Blocking reuptake of serotonin at the transporter, which keeps it active in the synaptic cleft for longer.
2. Because the trials excluded bipolar, psychotic and treatment-resistant depression, and most drug-to-drug
   comparisons carried wide uncertainty, so the ranking describes an average trial result, not individual
   guidance.
3. Firmest evidence and first-line status in PTSD; a small and uncertain effect in autism trials; and a switch
   concern, rather than a toxicity concern, in bipolar disorder.
