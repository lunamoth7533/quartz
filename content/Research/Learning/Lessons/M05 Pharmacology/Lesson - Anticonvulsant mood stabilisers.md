# Anticonvulsant mood stabilisers

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Anticonvulsant mood stabilisers]]

## Why this matters

Valproate, carbamazepine and lamotrigine are grouped together as "mood stabilisers" in bipolar disorder, but
that label describes what they are used for, not what they do at the molecular level, and it hides the fact
that the three drugs point in different directions across mania, depression and long-term maintenance. Learning
to unpack the group label is the skill this lesson builds.

## The core model

These three drugs are, first and foremost, antiseizure medications repurposed for mood episodes in bipolar
disorder; the group is defined by clinical use, not by a shared mechanism.
[[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-uses|Rogawski 2004]]
Valproate's own mechanism is broad and only partly worked out: alongside strengthening GABA-mediated inhibition,
it acts on several intracellular signalling pathways and changes how genes are expressed.
[[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-actions|Rosenberg 2007]] Which, if any,
of those actions actually prevents a mood episode has not been demonstrated for valproate or for the other two
drugs.
[[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-caution-transfer|Appraisal: Rogawski 2004]]
[[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-limit|Rosenberg 2007]]

Their evidence in bipolar disorder is sharply polarity-specific — meaning each drug protects against one pole of
illness more than the other. In a maintenance network comparing long-term outcomes, lamotrigine reduced
depressive but not manic recurrence, valproate reduced manic but not depressive recurrence, and carbamazepine
did not beat placebo for preventing either kind of episode.
[[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-polarity|Kishi 2021]] Lithium, which
is not itself an anticonvulsant, is the usual comparator, and in the same network it was the drug that reduced
recurrence of both poles at once — something none of the three anticonvulsants achieved alone.
[[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-any|Kishi 2021]]

The three drugs also diverge on a serious pregnancy risk. About one in ten children exposed to valproate in the
womb had a major congenital malformation, compared with roughly two to three in a hundred children of women
without epilepsy — a clearly raised risk.
[[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-valproate|Bromley 2023]] Carbamazepine
carried a smaller increase, and lamotrigine showed no clear rise in the same comparison.
[[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-others|Bromley 2023]]
[[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-lamotrigine|Bromley 2023]] A second
serious risk, severe skin reactions, is tied to a specific genetic marker: the HLA-B*15:02 gene variant is
strongly linked to carbamazepine-triggered Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis, and more
weakly linked to reactions on lamotrigine.
[[P38527170 Tham 2024 HLA-B and antiseizure drug skin reactions#^p38527170-association|Tham 2024]]

**Reading rule.** Whenever you see "mood stabiliser" used as a single category, replace it mentally with the
specific drug, the phase of illness (mania, depression or maintenance) and the polarity of recurrence (manic or
depressive) that its evidence actually covers.
[[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-polarity|Kishi 2021]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Imagine a support forum post says: "Mood stabilisers prevent both mania
and depression, so it doesn't matter which one you're on." Check that claim against the maintenance network
data: lamotrigine's evidence points toward depressive recurrence, valproate's toward manic recurrence, and
carbamazepine did not separate from placebo for either.

The corrected version is not "any mood stabiliser prevents both poles" but "the three anticonvulsant mood
stabilisers have different, polarity-specific evidence, and only lithium showed protection against both poles in
this comparison" — a distinction that matters for reading any single-drug claim about this group.

## Common confusions

- "Mood stabiliser is a mechanism, like antidepressant or antipsychotic." It is a use-based label covering drugs
  with different, partly unknown mechanisms.
- "All three anticonvulsants protect against both mania and depression." Their maintenance evidence is
  polarity-specific, and only lithium covered both poles in the same comparison.
- "A negative test for HLA-B*15:02 means no risk of a serious skin reaction." A negative result does not remove
  the risk; it lowers the strength of one specific risk factor.

## What the sources do not establish

No source identifies which specific action, among valproate's many candidate mechanisms, is the one that
actually stabilises mood.
[[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-unknown|Rosenberg 2007]] The pregnancy
risk figures come from epilepsy populations, so their transfer to bipolar use rests on pharmacological reasoning
rather than direct measurement in bipolar samples.
[[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-caution-indication|Appraisal: Bromley 2023]]

## Check yourself

1. Are valproate, carbamazepine and lamotrigine grouped together by mechanism or by use?
2. Which of the three showed the clearest depressive-recurrence protection in the maintenance network, and which
   showed manic-recurrence protection?
3. Which drug in this group carries the strongest pregnancy malformation risk?

## Answer notes

1. By clinical use in bipolar disorder; their underlying mechanisms differ and are only partly understood.
2. Lamotrigine for depressive recurrence, valproate for manic recurrence; carbamazepine did not clearly beat
   placebo for either.
3. Valproate, with about one in ten exposed children showing a major malformation.
