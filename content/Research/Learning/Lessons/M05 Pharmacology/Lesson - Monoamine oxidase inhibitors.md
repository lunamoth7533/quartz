# Monoamine oxidase inhibitors

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Monoamine oxidase inhibitors]]

## Why this matters

MAOIs raise monoamine levels through a completely different chemical step than the reuptake-blocking classes
covered so far, and that different step is exactly why the class carries its own interaction rules. Reading
MAOI evidence well means keeping "which drug" attached to every safety claim, because the class's defining risk
belongs to some members far more than others.

## The core model

Monoamine oxidase inhibitors raise synaptic monoamine concentrations by inhibiting the monoamine oxidase
enzymes that normally break monoamines down, rather than by blocking the reuptake transporters that SSRIs,
SNRIs and tricyclics act on.
[[P29083800 Easton-Carr 2026 Monoamine oxidase inhibitor toxicity#^p29083800-mechanism|Easton-Carr 2026]]
Isocarboxazid, phenelzine, tranylcypromine and moclobemide are the members with trial evidence against placebo
in depression, and selegiline is the class's example of a selective inhibitor, also used as an MAO-B inhibitor.
[[P31211282 Fasipe 2019 Novel antidepressants and agomelatine#^p31211282-classes|Fasipe 2019]];
[[P30969670 Patel 2025 Monoamine oxidase inhibitors overview#^p30969670-selectivity|Patel 2025]] Prescribing fell
once SSRIs arrived, but MAOIs remain in use for treatment-resistant depression and some anxiety disorders.
[[P29083800 Easton-Carr 2026 Monoamine oxidase inhibitor toxicity#^p29083800-use|Easton-Carr 2026]]

Two properties of an individual drug change what "MAOI" means in practice: whether it is selective for one form
of the enzyme, and whether it binds reversibly or irreversibly. Because those properties belong to specific
drugs rather than to the class as a whole, risk statements have to attach to named drugs, not to "MAOIs" in
general.
[[P30969670 Patel 2025 Monoamine oxidase inhibitors overview#^p30969670-selectivity|Patel 2025]];
[[P30969670 Patel 2025 Monoamine oxidase inhibitors overview#^p30969670-caution-selectivity|Appraisal: Patel 2025]]

On efficacy, a 2024 network meta-analysis of 83 double-blind trials found that all four tested MAOIs
outperformed placebo and that, as a group, they matched other antidepressant classes in efficacy, with
tolerability and acceptability comparable under trial conditions.
[[P39001570 Gimenez-Palomo 2024 MAOIs for depressive episodes#^p39001570-efficacy|Giménez-Palomo 2024]];
[[P39001570 Gimenez-Palomo 2024 MAOIs for depressive episodes#^p39001570-tolerability|Giménez-Palomo 2024]] The
authors describe MAOIs as third-line options for treatment-resistant depression that remain underused in
practice - but trial acceptability, measured with diet and other medicines controlled, does not describe the
interaction burden of everyday use outside a trial.
[[P39001570 Gimenez-Palomo 2024 MAOIs for depressive episodes#^p39001570-context|Giménez-Palomo 2024]];
[[P39001570 Gimenez-Palomo 2024 MAOIs for depressive episodes#^p39001570-caution-trials|Appraisal: Giménez-Palomo 2024]]

Interactions are the class-defining issue. MAOIs interact in clinically important ways with many medicines and
with foods containing tyramine, and the toxicity literature describes SSRIs as less toxic than the MAOIs that
preceded them.
[[P29083800 Easton-Carr 2026 Monoamine oxidase inhibitor toxicity#^p29083800-interactions|Easton-Carr 2026]];
[[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-uses|Bruggeman 2023]] But
that risk is not evenly spread across the class: the non-selective, irreversible drugs phenelzine and
tranylcypromine can provoke a hypertensive crisis with tyramine-rich foods, while selective MAO-B inhibitors
such as selegiline carry a markedly lower hypertensive risk.
[[P30969670 Patel 2025 Monoamine oxidase inhibitors overview#^p30969670-selectivity|Patel 2025]] Toxicity itself
can present as agitation, flushing, a fast heart rate, altered blood pressure, muscle twitching, exaggerated
reflexes, seizures and high fever, and needs prompt treatment; poisoning is rarer now that prescribing has
fallen, but it can still be life-threatening.
[[P30969670 Patel 2025 Monoamine oxidase inhibitors overview#^p30969670-toxicity|Patel 2025]] Bipolar depression,
like treatment-resistant and atypical depression, still lacks dedicated MAOI trials, so the class's general
efficacy result cannot be carried across to that population without evidence of its own.
[[P39001570 Gimenez-Palomo 2024 MAOIs for depressive episodes#^p39001570-limit|Giménez-Palomo 2024]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A patient-advocacy blog posts a blanket warning: "Never combine
antidepressants with tyramine-containing foods." Apply this lesson's reading rule and name the drug before
accepting the warning as written. The tyramine interaction is documented for the non-selective, irreversible
MAOIs - phenelzine and tranylcypromine are the named examples - not for antidepressants generally, and even
within the MAOI class a selective drug such as selegiline carries a markedly lower version of the same risk. A
blanket claim about "antidepressants" folds a serious, well-documented, drug-specific warning into a much larger
and mostly unrelated group of medicines, which makes the warning both too broad and, for the drugs it actually
concerns, too vague to be useful without naming them.

## Common confusions

- "MAOIs work by blocking reuptake, like SSRIs." They inhibit the enzyme that breaks monoamines down, a
  different step from transporter blockade.
- "All MAOIs carry the same food-interaction risk." Selectivity and reversibility are drug-specific properties;
  selective MAO-B inhibitors carry a lower hypertensive risk than non-selective, irreversible drugs.
- "Third-line means poorly supported." The class matched other antidepressants in efficacy in a large network;
  its third-line position reflects interaction burden, not weak efficacy.

## What the sources do not establish

No MAOI evidence specific to ADHD, autism or PTSD is sourced in this library, how often serious interactions
occur in current practice is not measured by the sources checked here, and whether MAOIs help specifically in
bipolar depression awaits dedicated trials.

## Answer notes

1. Inhibiting the monoamine oxidase enzymes that break monoamines down, rather than blocking reuptake
   transporters.
2. Whether the drug is selective for one form of the enzyme, and whether it binds reversibly or irreversibly.
3. Because the third-line ranking reflects the class's interaction burden and the need for dietary and
   medication caution, not a smaller treatment effect in the trials that have been run.
