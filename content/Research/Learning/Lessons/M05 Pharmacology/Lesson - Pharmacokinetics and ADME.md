# Pharmacokinetics and ADME

**Module.** M05 Pharmacology · **Topic.** [[Pharmacokinetics and ADME]]

## Why this matters

Pharmacokinetics is the half of pharmacology that explains why the same label dose is not the same exposure.
It is also the vocabulary that makes half-life, steady state and monitoring intelligible, so it comes first in
the module. Nothing here is dosing advice.

## The core model

Pharmacokinetics describes what the body does to a drug: absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion.
[[F12 NIGMS What happens to medicine in your body#^f12-adme|What Happens to Medicine in Your Body?]]

Bioavailability is the fraction of a dose that reaches the bloodstream in active form, and it varies with route
and formulation.
[[F12 NIGMS What happens to medicine in your body#^f12-bioavailability|What Happens to Medicine in Your Body?]]

Read the sequence as a filter chain. Absorption depends on route, formulation, food and gut function.
Distribution depends on blood flow, tissue affinity and body composition. Metabolism depends on enzymes whose
activity varies between people and changes with other drugs, diet and illness. Excretion depends on kidney and
liver function. Each step is variable, and the variability compounds.

That is why two people taking the same dose can have different concentrations, different effects and different
side effects — and why "the dose" is a population average written on a label rather than a personal guarantee.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Two people take the same labelled dose of the same medicine. One reports
strong effects and side effects at a low dose; the other reports nothing at a higher one.

Walk the chain. Absorption: same formulation, but one took it with food. Distribution: different body
composition. Metabolism: one takes another medicine that inhibits the enzyme that clears this drug, so exposure
rises. Excretion: kidney function differs with age. Adherence: one may have taken the dose with a different
schedule.

The correct conclusion is not that one person is "sensitive" and the other "resistant" in a fixed way. It is
that exposure differs for identifiable reasons, and that assessing them belongs to clinical care with the
appropriate measurements. This library stops at understanding the principles.

## Common confusions

- "Bioavailability is the same as absorption." It is the fraction that reaches the bloodstream in active form,
  which includes surviving first-pass metabolism.
- "Metabolism means breakdown into inactive pieces." Sometimes it produces active metabolites.
- "The label dose is the exposure." It is an input; exposure is what the body does with it.

## What the sources do not establish

The NIGMS pages are general-audience explanations; they give no quantitative parameters for any medicine and no
clinical guidance. This vault contains no source that would let you calculate a personal concentration or
adjust a dose.

## Answer notes

1. Absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion — the steps a drug takes through the body.
2. Because absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion vary with formulation, food, body composition,
   enzymes, organ function and other drugs.
3. The fraction of a dose that reaches the bloodstream in active form; route changes how much survives the
   first pass through the gut and liver.
