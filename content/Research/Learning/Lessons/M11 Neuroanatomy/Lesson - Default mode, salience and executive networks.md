# Default mode, salience and executive networks

**Module.** M11 Neuroanatomy and Systems Neuroscience · **Topic.** [[Default mode, salience and executive networks]]

## Why this matters

These three named networks are what every condition domain in this vault reaches for when discussing
group-level brain-imaging findings. This closing lesson applies the discipline built across this module - name
the definition, the sample, the state and the effect size - to all four conditions the vault covers at once,
so the temptation to average them into "one broken circuit" can be checked directly.

## The core model

The default mode network is tied to self-reference, memory and mind wandering; the salience network,
anchored in anterior insula and anterior cingulate cortex, picks out the most relevant events; and an
executive network spanning frontal and parietal cortex supports top-down control. The triple network model
proposes that aberrant salience mapping, and difficulty engaging and disengaging these networks, underlie
cognitive dysfunction across conditions generally. [[P21908230 Menon 2011 Triple network model of psychopathology#^p21908230-model|Menon 2011]] [[P21908230 Menon 2011 Triple network model of psychopathology#^p21908230-disorders|Menon 2011]] In this model, the anterior insula shifts
activity toward attention and working memory after a salient event, but that switching role is inferred
mainly from correlational imaging, and a model built to explain what many conditions share necessarily
explains their similarities rather than what tells them apart. [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-hub|Menon 2010]] [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-caution-model|Appraisal: Menon 2010]]

Applying that caution to each condition shows why. In ADHD, a mega-analysis found weaker default-mode
anticorrelation with task networks in diagnosed children and along a dimensional measure of ADHD traits in
undiagnosed people, but the effects were small (d = 0.14-0.17), and resting-state data of this kind cannot
show that weaker segregation causes attentional lapses. [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-anticorrelation|Norman 2023]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-caution-effect|Appraisal: Norman 2023]] In bipolar disorder, a meta-analysis of 39
datasets found lower default-mode connectivity during acute mood episodes, but in remission that reduction
was absent and connectivity was instead higher than in controls - reversing direction with mood state, and
compared across separate studies rather than within the same people over time. [[P32259712 Wang 2020 Network dysfunction across bipolar mood states#^p32259712-acute|Wang 2020]] [[P32259712 Wang 2020 Network dysfunction across bipolar mood states#^p32259712-caution-state|Appraisal: Wang 2020]] Autism is
one of the conditions the triple network model addresses; a normative-modelling study across six diagnoses
found any single region's grey-matter deviation was shared by fewer than 7% of people with the same
diagnosis, yet the deviations still converged on shared systems, with the salience network implicated across
diagnoses rather than specific to one. [[P37580620 Segal 2023 Heterogeneity of brain abnormalities#^p37580620-regional|Segal 2023]] [[P37580620 Segal 2023 Heterogeneity of brain abnormalities#^p37580620-circuits|Segal 2023]] In adult PTSD, weaker default-mode connectivity tracked trauma exposure
generally rather than PTSD specifically, while stronger somatomotor-to-default-mode coupling was the pattern
specific to PTSD, so a comparison against non-exposed people mixes the effect of trauma with the effect of
the disorder. [[P34688728 Bao 2021 Large-scale networks in adult PTSD#^p34688728-trauma|Bao 2021]] [[P34688728 Bao 2021 Large-scale networks in adult PTSD#^p34688728-caution-controls|Appraisal: Bao 2021]]

The reading rule tying all four together: ask which network definition was used, how much data each person
contributed, which comparison group was chosen, what mood or clinical state the sample was in, and how large
the effect was - because even a difference that survives those checks describes a group, not an individual.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

Suppose a hypothetical summary claims: "New research proves the default mode network is broken in ADHD,
bipolar disorder, autism and PTSD alike, confirming one shared brain circuit explains all four conditions."
Walking the four findings through the reading rule dismantles that sentence. The ADHD finding is a small,
dimensional effect present even without a diagnosis. The bipolar finding reverses direction between acute and
remitted states, so "broken" cannot specify one direction. The autism-relevant finding is shared across six
unrelated diagnoses, so it cannot distinguish autism from the other five. The PTSD finding is mostly a
trauma-exposure effect, with a different coupling pattern doing the PTSD-specific work. Four different
directions, populations and specificities do not add up to one shared broken circuit.

## Common confusions

- "Weaker network connectivity means the network is broken." The findings above are small, reverse with mood
  state, or shared across many diagnoses rather than showing damage.
- "A resting-state finding proves the connectivity difference causes the symptom." These are correlational
  designs; they cannot establish that a network pattern causes attentional, mood or other symptoms.
- "A group-level network difference can diagnose or subtype an individual." None of these findings are ready
  to do that for any one person.

## What the sources do not establish

These sources do not establish whether the anterior insula causes network switches or only changes alongside
them, whether any of these patterns is specific to one diagnosis given their convergence across several, or
whether adult PTSD network findings apply to complex PTSD; a resting-state meta-analysis focused specifically
on autism has not yet been sourced in this library.

## Check yourself

1. Why does a model built to explain what many conditions share have trouble explaining what makes any one of
   them distinct?
2. Why can the bipolar default-mode finding not be summarised as simply "lower connectivity in bipolar
   disorder"?
3. Name three things a reader should ask about before accepting a network finding in any one of these four
   conditions.

## Answer notes

1. Because a model designed around common ground across conditions describes their similarities by
   construction, so it is not built to capture what distinguishes one condition from another.
2. Because the direction reverses with mood state - lower during acute episodes, higher than controls in
   remission - so a single "lower connectivity" summary hides that reversal.
3. Any three of: the network's definition, the amount of scan data per person, the comparison group, the
   clinical or mood state of the sample, and the size of the effect.
