# Prefrontal cortex

**Module.** M11 Neuroanatomy and Systems Neuroscience · **Topic.** [[Prefrontal cortex]]

## Why this matters

The prefrontal cortex is the region most often named in accounts of ADHD, bipolar disorder and complex PTSD,
which makes it the clearest place in this module to practise a discipline: before crediting "the prefrontal
cortex" with a function or a disorder, name the sector, the measure, the population and the effect size.
Skipping that step is how a modest finding turns into an overstated headline.

## The core model

Clinical reviews divide the prefrontal cortex into sectors with different emphases: dorsolateral cortex tied
to working memory and planning; ventrolateral cortex to inhibition and monitoring; medial cortex to
self-knowledge and emotion regulation; orbitofrontal cortex to inhibition and social reasoning. These are
tendencies from lesion and imaging work, not one function per sector, and prefrontal networks regulate
attention and inhibit impulses in ways that depend on arousal-linked chemical signalling, with both too
little and too much impairing performance. [[P34881727 Jones 2021 Executive dysfunction and the prefrontal cortex#^p34881727-subdivisions|Jones 2021]] [[P34881727 Jones 2021 Executive dysfunction and the prefrontal cortex#^p34881727-caution-mapping|Appraisal: Jones 2021]]

This region also matures unusually slowly: development runs for decades, moving from sensorimotor cortex
toward transmodal association cortex such as the prefrontal region. The trajectories are gradual and
estimated at the group level, so a round number like "mature at 25" compresses a slow process into a false
precision the data do not support. [[P34270921 Sydnor 2021 Neurodevelopment of the association cortices#^p34270921-decades|Sydnor 2021]] [[P34270921 Sydnor 2021 Neurodevelopment of the association cortices#^p34270921-caution-age|Appraisal: Sydnor 2021]]

The three condition accounts in this vault illustrate the reading rule rather than escaping it. In ADHD, a
mega-analysis of 1,696 youths with ADHD and 6,737 controls found stronger resting coupling between striatum
and prefrontal-adjacent cortex, but the largest effect was only around d = 0.15 - small enough that the two
groups overlap almost entirely. [[P38476041 Norman 2024 Subcortico-cortical connectivity in ADHD#^p38476041-striatal|Norman 2024]] [[P38476041 Norman 2024 Subcortico-cortical connectivity in ADHD#^p38476041-caution-effect|Appraisal: Norman 2024]] In bipolar disorder, a large consortium study found
thinner frontal cortex on average, but medication use and length of illness were also associated with
cortical measures in the same sample, so the thickness difference mixes diagnosis with treatment and illness
course. [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|Hibar 2018]] [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-caution-groups|Appraisal: Hibar 2018]] In PTSD, a meta-analysis found reduced activity in ventromedial
prefrontal and anterior cingulate cortex specifically in PTSD, while amygdala and insula over-activity was
shared with other anxiety-related conditions - but that work predates the complex PTSD category, so it
cannot simply be relabelled as evidence about complex PTSD. [[P17898336 Etkin 2007 Functional neuroimaging of anxiety meta-analysis#^p17898336-ptsd|Etkin 2007]] [[P17898336 Etkin 2007 Functional neuroimaging of anxiety meta-analysis#^p17898336-caution-cptsd|Appraisal: Etkin 2007]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

Imagine a hypothetical article claims: "Brain scans confirm that ADHD, bipolar disorder and PTSD are all
caused by a weak prefrontal cortex." Applying the reading rule to each condition shows why that sentence
erases three different findings. The ADHD finding is a small connectivity difference, not a measure of
weakness. The bipolar finding is a structural difference not yet separated from medication and illness
duration. The PTSD finding is a task-based activity difference in specific regions, found in adult samples
not tested for the complex PTSD presentation this vault covers. None of the three is "a weak prefrontal
cortex" in any comparable sense - they are three measures, in three populations, at three effect sizes, and
the flattening phrase throws that structure away. The accurate version names each condition's measure and
limitation separately rather than reaching for one label that fits all three.

## Common confusions

- "These findings prove prefrontal dysfunction causes the disorder." Each is a group-level association with
  its own confound or effect-size limitation, not a demonstrated cause.
- "Sectors of the prefrontal cortex each do exactly one job." Sector-function pairings are tendencies, and
  executive function depends on distributed networks.
- "PTSD prefrontal findings apply directly to complex PTSD." The sourced PTSD imaging predates the complex
  PTSD category.

## What the sources do not establish

These sources do not establish how sharply prefrontal sectors specialise given their dependence on
distributed networks, exactly when prefrontal development should be called complete, whether bipolar-related
thinning reflects the illness rather than its treatment, or which prefrontal differences in PTSD precede
trauma rather than follow it.

## Check yourself

1. Why is "the prefrontal cortex does X" usually an incomplete claim on its own?
2. Why can bipolar-related cortical thinning not be attributed to the illness alone?
3. Why can adult PTSD prefrontal findings not be treated as evidence about complex PTSD?

## Answer notes

1. Because prefrontal sectors show tendencies rather than one function each, and a full claim needs the
   sector, the measure, the population and the effect size stated.
2. Because medication use and length of illness were also associated with cortical measures in the same
   sample, mixing diagnosis, treatment and illness course together.
3. Because the sourced PTSD imaging work predates the complex PTSD category and has not itself tested that
   presentation.
