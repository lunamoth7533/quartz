# Insula

**Module.** M11 Neuroanatomy and Systems Neuroscience · **Topic.** [[Insula]]

## Why this matters

The insula turns up in findings across every condition this library covers - ADHD, bipolar disorder, autism
and complex PTSD - which makes it tempting to treat "insula hyperactivity" as a shared explanation. This
lesson gives two reasons to slow down: the insula is not one territory, and a finding shared across many
diagnoses cannot, by itself, explain what is specific to any one of them.

## The core model

The insula is cortex hidden in the lateral sulcus, divided into anywhere from 2 to 13 subdivisions depending
on the method used to draw the boundaries. Because the count changes with the parcellation scheme, "anterior
insula" in one study and another may not refer to the same territory - the first thing to check before
comparing two insula findings. [[P28644199 Uddin 2017 Structure and function of the human insula#^p28644199-parcels|Uddin 2017]] [[P28644199 Uddin 2017 Structure and function of the human insula#^p28644199-caution-parcellation|Appraisal: Uddin 2017]]

Interoception - the sensing of the body's internal state - runs from back to front through this structure. A
dedicated system carries homeostatic signals such as pain, temperature, hunger and air hunger to the dorsal
posterior insula, its primary cortical representation; a proposed re-representation in the right anterior
insula is then read as the basis for the felt sense of the bodily self and of emotion. [[P12965300 Craig 2003 Interoception#^p12965300-self|Craig 2003]]

The anterior insula is also cast as a hub in a different model: it is proposed to detect salient events and
shift brain activity between networks for outward-directed attention and for self-related thought, so that
attentional resources redirect toward whatever triggered the shift. That switching role is inferred mainly
from correlational imaging, so whether the anterior insula causes the switch, rather than simply changing
alongside it, is not established. [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-hub|Menon 2010]] [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-caution-model|Appraisal: Menon 2010]]

Psychiatric findings involving the insula are shared across conditions rather than specific to one. In a
transdiagnostic study, people using mental health services performed worse than comparison participants on a
heartbeat-detection task, while anxiety and depression were associated with higher self-rated interoceptive
sensibility - accuracy, confidence and self-report pulled apart rather than moving together, so
"interoception" names several different measurable things. [[P36709619 Critchley 2023 Cardiac interoception in mental health services#^p36709619-mismatch|Critchley 2023]] Separately, a normative-modelling study across six
diagnoses - several of which this library covers - found that any one region's grey-matter deviation was
rarely shared by people with the same diagnosis, yet those scattered deviations still converged on common
large-scale systems, with the salience network involved across diagnoses rather than tied to one. [[P37580620 Segal 2023 Heterogeneity of brain abnormalities#^p37580620-circuits|Segal 2023]] The
reading rule this points to: name the insular zone and atlas, name the signal (performance, confidence,
questionnaire, or brain measurement), and remember that a finding shared across diagnoses cannot explain what
is specific to any single one.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

Suppose a hypothetical summary claims: "Insula hyperactivity has now been found in ADHD, autism, bipolar
disorder and PTSD, proving a single shared salience circuit causes all four conditions." Two checks apply.
First, the parcellation check: were the four studies using the same definition of "insula," or could
"anterior insula" mean different territories across four separate research groups? Second, the
shared-versus-specific check: if salience-network differences turn up across six or more unrelated diagnoses,
not just these four, the finding describes something many conditions have in common - plausibly a general
marker of distress or heterogeneous variation - rather than a mechanism explaining why one particular
condition develops. The corrected claim specifies each study's parcellation, names the measure used in each
condition, and states plainly that convergence on a shared network does not amount to a diagnosis-specific
cause.

## Common confusions

- "Insula activity during a task is direct evidence of interoceptive awareness." Not unless the design
  isolates awareness from performance and self-report, which the evidence shows can all disagree.
- "The anterior insula causes the network switch." The switching claim comes from correlational imaging and
  has not been shown to be causal.
- "A finding shared across many diagnoses explains any one of them." Shared findings describe what diagnoses
  have in common, not what distinguishes them.

## What the sources do not establish

These sources do not establish which parcellation of the insula is correct, whether alexithymia involves
altered insular processing itself rather than only altered self-reported interoception, or whether the
insular over-activity found in adult PTSD task studies applies to complex PTSD specifically.

## Check yourself

1. Why does the number of insular subdivisions reported in a study matter for comparing two studies?
2. What did the transdiagnostic heartbeat-detection study show about "interoception" as a single concept?
3. Why can't a salience-network finding shared across six diagnoses explain what is specific to any one of
   them?

## Answer notes

1. Because parcellation schemes range from 2 to 13 subdivisions, so a label like "anterior insula" can refer
   to different territories in different studies unless the atlas is stated.
2. That task accuracy, task confidence and questionnaire self-report can move independently, so
   "interoception" is several different measurable things rather than one.
3. Because a feature common to many unrelated diagnoses describes what they share, not what makes any single
   diagnosis distinct from the others.
