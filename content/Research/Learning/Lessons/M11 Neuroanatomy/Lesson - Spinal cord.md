# Spinal cord

**Module.** M11 Neuroanatomy and Systems Neuroscience · **Topic.** [[Spinal cord]]

## Why this matters

The spinal cord is the clearest case in this module of structure predicting function with real precision.
Because sensory and motor territories are laid out in an orderly, learnable pattern, the level and pattern of
a deficit can be reasoned out rather than memorised - a habit this module returns to for every region that
follows.

## The core model

The cord is organised into segments, each corresponding to the level at which its roots enter or leave and
each innervating one dermatome. Nerve roots exit at different levels relative to the vertebrae in the neck
than lower down, which is why a clinical level and a radiological level can differ by a segment. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-segments|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]
Cross-sections show sensory input entering dorsally and motor output leaving ventrally: dorsal structures
receive sensory input, ventral structures contain motor neurons, and intermediate zones carry interneurons
and autonomic cells - segment and horn together locate a function with unusual precision.

Two long systems run the cord's length in characteristic positions: ascending tracts carry sensory modalities
segregated by type and by crossing point, and descending tracts carry motor commands in lateral and medial
groups. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] The crossing points differ between systems: the dorsal-column system, carrying fine
touch and proprioception, crosses near the brainstem, while the spinothalamic system, carrying pain and
temperature, crosses within a segment or two of entry. A hemisection of the cord therefore produces a
dissociated pattern - loss of fine touch and proprioception below the lesion on the same side as the damage,
and loss of pain and temperature on the opposite side. Deriving that pattern from the crossing rules, rather
than memorising it, is what makes the logic usable at unusual levels and incomplete lesions. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]

The cord is not only a cable of tracts. Reflex arcs - sensory input, integration and a motor response - can
occur with minimal cortical involvement, and spinal circuits generate reflexes and adjust their own
excitability. [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-reflex|Motor Responses]] Because a reflex arc closes within the cord at a known segment, an absent or exaggerated
reflex localises to that segment's circuitry or to the descending control acting on it - the reflex exam maps
local, segmental function on top of the tract exam's map of the long systems. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

Suppose a hypothetical research summary reports that an experimentally produced hemisection of the spinal
cord at a thoracic segment in an animal model caused "loss of function below the lesion," without further
detail. Applying segmental and crossing logic specifies what the summary should have said.

Because the dorsal-column system crosses near the brainstem, cutting one half of the cord leaves its fibres
from below the lesion unable to reach the brain - so fine touch and proprioception should be lost on the same
side as the cut, below the injury. Because the spinothalamic system crosses within a segment or two of
entering the cord, most of its fibres from the intact side have already crossed to the damaged half before
ascending - so pain and temperature should be lost on the opposite side, again below the lesion. The precise
claim is "ipsilateral dorsal-column loss and contralateral spinothalamic loss below a thoracic hemisection,"
not a blanket "loss of function below the lesion."

## Common confusions

- "A spinal injury produces the same loss of everything below the level." The pattern depends on which tracts
  and horns are damaged, not just how far down the damage sits.
- "The dorsal horn is where motor output leaves." Motor neurons are ventral; the dorsal horn receives sensory
  input.
- "Reflex testing only checks whether the reflex works." An abnormal reflex also localises to a segment or to
  the descending control over it.

## What the sources do not establish

These sources describe typical segmental and tract organisation; they do not quantify how often individual
variation produces an atypical presentation, and they offer no guidance on treatment or prognosis for any
specific injury.

## Check yourself

1. Why can a clinical level and a radiological level for the same spinal problem differ by a segment?
2. In a cord hemisection, why is fine-touch loss on the same side as the damage while pain-and-temperature
   loss is on the opposite side?
3. What does an absent reflex tell an examiner, beyond that the reflex "does not work"?

## Answer notes

1. Because nerve roots exit at different levels relative to the vertebrae in different parts of the spine, so
   the vertebral level does not always match the exact segment involved.
2. The dorsal-column system crosses near the brainstem, so its fibres from below the lesion are cut before
   crossing; the spinothalamic system crosses within a segment or two of entry, so its fibres are already on
   the damaged side when the cut occurs.
3. It localises the problem to that reflex's segment of spinal circuitry or to the descending pathways that
   control it.
