# Interoception and autonomic pathways

**Module.** M11 Neuroanatomy and Systems Neuroscience · **Topic.** [[Interoception and autonomic pathways]]

## Why this matters

Interoception is the sensing half and the autonomic pathways are the control half of one loop that makes the
nervous system an organ that both monitors and regulates the body. This lesson gives that loop's anatomy
directly, and gives the reading rule needed before treating any single "interoception" measure as a window
onto emotional self-awareness.

## The core model

Interoception is the sensing of the body's physiological condition - visceral state, temperature, pain,
hunger, muscular and autonomic signals - carried by afferents that ascend separately from the classical
touch-and-proprioception pathways described earlier in this module; these signals report the body's internal
state rather than the external world. [[P12965300 Craig 2003 Interoception#^p12965300-system|Interoception]] Small-diameter afferents from body tissues ascend through a
dedicated lamina-I spinothalamic route and its cranial equivalents, projecting first to autonomic brainstem
nuclei and then to the dorsal posterior insula, the primary cortical representation. A further
re-representation in the right anterior insula is proposed as a basis for the subjective feeling of bodily
state. [[P12965300 Craig 2003 Interoception#^p12965300-insula|Interoception]]

The output side is centrally organised rather than simply reflexive at each organ. Brain sites that receive
visceral input interconnect with the populations that control sympathetic and parasympathetic outflow and
with endocrine and limbic circuitry, together forming a central autonomic network; thermoregulation shows how
autonomic, endocrine and behavioural responses are coordinated for a single physiological demand rather than
handled by separate systems. [[F63 Neuroscience Online central autonomic control#^f63-network|Central Control of the Autonomic Nervous System]] Sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions differ in outflow and
signalling, but they are cooperative as well as opposing, and most organs receive input from both. [[F32 OpenStax Divisions of the autonomic nervous system#^f32-outflow|Divisions of the Autonomic Nervous System]] [[F32 OpenStax Divisions of the autonomic nervous system#^f32-cooperation|Divisions of the Autonomic Nervous System]] A recorded signal such
as heart rate variability reflects this whole integrated system, so a change in it rarely identifies a single
specific cause.

The reading rule follows directly from the anatomy: name the signal being measured (cardiac, respiratory,
gastric), the measure used (an awareness task, an evoked potential, a self-report questionnaire), and the
level being described (the afferent signal, the insular representation, or overt behaviour). There are two
honest boundaries on how far this model can be pushed. First, the insular claim is anatomical and functional
at the group level, and the meta-representation proposal - that right anterior insula activity constitutes a
felt sense of self - is the literature's interpretation of converging imaging at the group level, not a
measured outcome any individual reading can produce. Second, a clinical or individual claim built on top of
it, such as treating a given emotion as identical to a given bodily signal, needs its own separate
measurement, because the anatomical proposal does not itself deliver a readout.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

Imagine a hypothetical wellness device claims to "measure your interoception" from heart-rate data alone and
reports a single score, stating that a higher score means greater emotional self-awareness. This makes two
separate leaps. The first is from a narrow cardiac signal to "interoception" as a whole - interoception
covers respiratory, gastric and other channels too, so a heart-rate-only score is at best one channel of a
broader system. The second is from that score to "emotional self-awareness," which conflates whatever the
device measures with subjective, felt awareness, when the underlying evidence shows these can move
independently: task performance, confidence in that performance, and self-reported sensibility have been
found to dissociate, sometimes in opposite directions, in the same people. [[P36709619 Critchley 2023 Cardiac interoception in mental health services#^p36709619-mismatch|Critchley 2023]] A single number cannot represent a
system with that much internal disagreement, and treating it as a direct readout of emotional self-awareness
extends the construct well past what a one-channel measure can support.

## Common confusions

- "Sympathetic and parasympathetic systems simply oppose each other." They are cooperative as well as
  opposing, and most organs receive input from both divisions.
- "Heart rate variability pinpoints one specific cause." It reflects the whole integrated autonomic system,
  so a change in it rarely isolates a single cause.
- "An interoceptive task score is a direct measure of emotional awareness." Task performance, confidence and
  self-report can move independently, and using one such measure to stand in for emotional awareness extends
  the construct beyond what it captures.

## What the sources do not establish

The right-anterior-insula meta-representation proposal is the literature's interpretation of converging
group-level imaging, not a measured outcome or an individual readout, and these sources do not establish that
any specific bodily signal is equivalent to any specific named emotion for a given person.

## Check yourself

1. Why are interoceptive afferents described as a separate system from touch and proprioception?
2. Why is heart rate variability a poor tool for identifying one specific cause of a change in autonomic
   state?
3. What three things should an interoception claim specify before it can be evaluated?

## Answer notes

1. Because they carry information about the body's internal physiological state through a dedicated
   lamina-I route to the insula, distinct from pathways carrying touch and proprioceptive information about
   the external world and body position.
2. Because it reflects the combined output of an integrated central autonomic network rather than any single
   input, so the same change could arise from several different underlying causes.
3. The specific signal (cardiac, respiratory, gastric), the specific measure used (task, evoked potential,
   questionnaire), and the level being described (afferent signal, insular representation, or behaviour).
