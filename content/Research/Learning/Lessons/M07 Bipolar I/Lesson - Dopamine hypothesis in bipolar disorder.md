# Dopamine hypothesis in bipolar disorder

**Module.** M07 Bipolar I · **Topic.** [[Dopamine hypothesis in bipolar disorder]]

## Why this matters

This lesson is about the difference between a hypothesis that organises research and a test that decides
something. The dopamine hypothesis is a useful framework and a frequently over-stated claim, and learning to
tell those apart is a transferable skill.

## The core model

Converging pharmacological and imaging findings support a model in which elevated striatal D2/3 receptor
availability accompanies mania, while increased dopamine transporter levels accompany depression, with
inconsistent findings elsewhere.
[[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-state|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]

The pharmacology is genuinely two-sided: both dopamine agonists and antidopaminergic drugs have improved
bipolar depressive symptoms in pharmacological studies, which cannot be reconciled with a simple "more dopamine
means mania" story.
[[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-pharmacology|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]

The authors present the receptor-and-transporter homeostasis model as speculation, with treatment implications
if it were confirmed.
[[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-speculative|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]

Two cautions follow from the review's own design. The evidence relies substantially on pharmacological studies,
which can affect other monoamine systems as well.
[[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-limit|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]
And a framework that organises research questions is not a diagnostic test.
[[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-caution-framework|AI synthesis: Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Design a study that could disconfirm the hypothesis rather than merely
fail to support it.

Start from a prediction the hypothesis makes. If altered dopamine signalling contributes to episode states, then
a manipulation that changes dopamine signalling in a defined way should change the relevant state in a
consistent direction, and the size of the change should track the degree of signalling change, in a sample
defined by phase and free of confounds such as medication.

A disconfirming result would be: manipulation produces the predicted signalling change, measured directly, and
the state does not change or changes in the opposite direction, replicated in an independent sample. Merely
finding no difference in a small imaging study with medication confounds does not disconfirm; it under-tests.

That asymmetry — what would count as evidence against — is what separates a hypothesis from a slogan.

## Common confusions

- "A hypothesis that explains a lot is probably true." Explanatory range and evidential support are different
  properties.
- "If the hypothesis is speculative, dopamine is irrelevant." The findings are real; the causal model that
  unifies them is the speculative part.
- "Imaging findings establish mechanism." They describe a difference, usually in small samples with treatment
  confounds.

## What the sources do not establish

The review is a mechanistic synthesis with a scarce imaging base; it does not provide a diagnostic test, a
validated model or individual prediction, and the authors label the central model as speculation. Nothing here
supports a chemical explanation of a person's episodes.

## Check yourself

1. What pattern of findings supports the hypothesis?
2. Why is the drug evidence two-sided?
3. What makes the central model speculative rather than established?
4. Describe one result that would count as disconfirming.

## Answer notes

1. Elevated striatal D2/3 availability with mania and increased dopamine transporter levels with depression,
   with inconsistencies elsewhere.
2. Because both dopamine agonists and antidopaminergic drugs have improved depressive symptoms, which a simple
   directional model does not predict.
3. The homeostasis model is an interpretation that the authors present as a hypothesis awaiting confirmation.
4. A direct, well-controlled manipulation of dopamine signalling that produces the predicted signalling change
   without the predicted state change, replicated independently.
