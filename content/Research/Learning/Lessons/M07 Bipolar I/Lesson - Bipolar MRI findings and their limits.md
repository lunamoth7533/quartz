# Bipolar MRI findings and their limits

**Module.** M07 Bipolar I · **Topic.** [[Bipolar MRI findings and their limits]]

## Why this matters

Imaging is the most over-interpreted evidence in psychiatry, and this study is the vault's best case for reading
it responsibly: very large, carefully analysed, and still unable to answer the questions people most want to
ask. The lesson is about what a well-conducted group study can and cannot deliver.

## The core model

Cortical grey matter was thinner bilaterally in frontal, temporal and parietal regions across 6503 individuals,
with the largest effects in specific frontal regions.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

Commonly prescribed drugs, including lithium, antiepileptics and antipsychotics, were associated with cortical
measures.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-medication|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

Longer illness duration was associated with reduced thickness.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-course|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

The authors describe the analysis as extensive on confounding, which is also a statement about how much
confounding there was to explore in a cross-sectional comparison.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-limit|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

So the findings are associations among people who differ in treatment, illness duration and state; the
medication association is a finding, not a nuisance. Group averages cannot diagnose an individual.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-caution-groups|AI synthesis: ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Sketch a study that could separate medication effects from illness
effects.

Start with a first-episode sample, medication-naive at baseline, scanned before treatment. Follow them with
repeated scans while recording treatment exposure prospectively, dose changes and clinical state. Compare
trajectories between people who remain on different treatments, and include a control group followed over the
same interval. Pre-specify which structural measures and covariates are of interest.

Even that design has limits: treatment is not randomly assigned in practice, medication-naive samples may not
represent all presentations, and repeated scans bring measurement variability. The design reduces the
confounding that the cross-sectional study could only model — which is the point of doing it.

## Common confusions

- "A large study resolves causation." Size improves precision; it does not add design features.
- "Medication confound means the finding is invalid." It means the causal attribution is unclear, and the
  medication association is informative in its own right.
- "Group difference means a biomarker." A biomarker needs calibration and predictive performance, which this
  design does not provide.

## What the sources do not establish

The record is abstract-level in this vault and the study is cross-sectional and observational; it does not
provide individual prediction or clinical thresholds, and the fine-grained analysis is not available here.

## Check yourself

1. What three associations does the study report?
2. Why is the medication association important rather than inconvenient?
3. What design features would be needed to attribute a difference to illness rather than treatment?

## Answer notes

1. Thinner cortex in bipolar participants, medication associations with cortical measures, and longer illness
   duration with reduced thickness.
2. Because treatment is part of the phenomenon and can shape the measure, so it must be interpreted rather
   than removed.
3. Prospective, longitudinal measurement with treatment recorded over time, ideally starting before treatment
   in first-episode samples, with appropriate controls.
