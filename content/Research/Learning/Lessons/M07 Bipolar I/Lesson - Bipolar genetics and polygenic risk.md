# Bipolar genetics and polygenic risk

**Module.** M07 Bipolar I · **Topic.** [[Bipolar genetics and polygenic risk]]

## Why this matters

This is the applied version of the genetic-inference lesson: a very large, well-powered study that still cannot
answer a personal question. It also shows why two studies of "the same" diagnosis can disagree — because the
diagnosis is assembled differently.

## The core model

A multi-ancestry meta-analysis of 158,036 cases and 2.8 million controls identified 298 genome-wide
significant loci and 36 credible genes, a fourfold increase over earlier work.
[[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-loci|Bipolar disorder genomics]]

The authors report differences in genetic architecture depending on how patients were ascertained and on
subtype.
[[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|Bipolar disorder genomics]]

Cell-type analyses implicate GABAergic interneurons and medium spiny neurons, with common and rare variant
signals converging.
[[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-cells|Bipolar disorder genomics]]

The study notes high heritability, in the range of 60 to 80 percent, while stating that most of the underlying
variation remains unexplained.
[[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-limit|Bipolar disorder genomics]]

And the architecture is statistical: it describes populations and does not predict an individual's course.
[[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-caution-prediction|AI synthesis: Bipolar disorder genomics]]

The phrase "credible genes" deserves unpacking. In fine-mapping, a credible set is usually a shortlist of
variants in a region that are statistically compatible with the association signal; a credible-gene list is a
further, gene-level prioritisation built on top of that variant evidence and functional annotation. The
abstract reports 36 prioritised genes without describing those intermediate steps, so this lesson treats them
as leads for follow-up rather than as established causal genes.
[[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-translation|10 Years of GWAS Discovery]]
The chain runs locus to variant evidence, then to candidate or effector gene inference, then to experimental
follow-up, and each link adds assumptions; that is why the cell-type findings are hypotheses rather than
conclusions.
[[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-limit|10 Years of GWAS Discovery]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Two studies report different top loci for bipolar disorder. One
recruited from inpatient units; the other used community volunteers and self-report.

Explain the difference without assuming error. Inpatient samples are enriched for severe presentations with
psychosis or mania requiring hospital care; community and self-report samples include milder and differently
classified presentations. Genetic associations are estimates of the relationship between variants and the trait
as defined in that sample, so different case definitions produce different estimates. Genotype array coverage
and ancestry composition add further differences.

The correct reading: the studies are about overlapping but non-identical traits, and the difference in results
is information about how case definition shapes genetic architecture rather than a contradiction to resolve.

## Common confusions

- "Heritability of 60-80 percent means the diagnosis is genetic." It describes the share of variation in
  studied populations; it says nothing about one person's outcome.
- "More loci means better prediction." Loci describe architecture; prediction needs calibration and validation.
- "A credible gene list is a mechanism." Genes are leads about biological processes, and the study itself
  reports most variation remains unexplained.

## What the sources do not establish

This vault's record of the study is abstract-level, so pathway and cell-type detail is reported as the paper's
summary rather than verified full text; the study provides no clinical score and no individual prediction.
