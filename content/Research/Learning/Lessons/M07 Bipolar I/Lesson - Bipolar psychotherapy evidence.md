# Bipolar psychotherapy evidence

**Module.** M07 Bipolar I · **Topic.** [[Bipolar psychotherapy evidence]]

## Why this matters

Psychotherapy for bipolar disorder is an adjunct to medication, tested as manualised protocols with named
components. This lesson is about reading an evidence base in which the active ingredient is precisely what you
cannot see — and about doing that carefully rather than dismissively.

## The core model

Across 20 two-group trials, adding manualised psychotherapy to medication was associated with lower recurrence
than control, with an odds ratio of 0.56.
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

Component analysis of 13 therapy elements found that psychoeducation with supervised skills practice in family
or group formats was linked to fewer recurrences than individual delivery.
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-components|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

CBT was linked to stabilising depressive symptoms with a standardized mean difference of -0.32, while family
and interpersonal therapy pointed in the same direction less certainly.
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-symptoms|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

Retention was higher for family or conjoint therapy and for brief psychoeducation, which is itself a meaningful
outcome.
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-retention|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

The limit is methodological: component analyses estimate average contributions across heterogeneous protocols
and cannot identify what produced one person's improvement.
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-caution-ingredients|AI synthesis: Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]
The authors also note heterogeneity in populations, duration and follow-up.
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-limit|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. You want to test whether regularising daily routines is the active
ingredient in a family psychoeducation programme.

Turn the hypothesis into a measurable contrast. Measure rhythm stability directly (sleep timing variability,
activity patterns) rather than assuming it, randomise participants to a version that emphasises rhythm work
versus a version with equal contact time and no rhythm emphasis, and pre-specify recurrence and symptom
outcomes. Then test mediation: does the rhythm measure change more in the rhythm arm, and does that change
predict the outcome?

Read the mediation result as evidence, not proof. A randomised intervention does not randomise the mediator:
both arms may move the rhythm measure, the measure is imperfect and confounded, and its change may not track the
outcome. Equal rhythm improvement in both arms therefore fails to provide specific evidence for the rhythm
story rather than showing that the story is wrong, and outcome improvement without rhythm change would point at
a different active ingredient. The component analysis could not separate these possibilities, which is exactly
why the trial is worth doing.

## Common confusions

- "Adjunct means optional." It means added to medication in the trials; the evidence is about that combination.
- "Components are known active ingredients." They are statistical contributions averaged across protocols.
- "Retention is a soft outcome." Leaving treatment early is a real consequence with real costs.

## What the sources do not establish

The analysis is a component network meta-analysis of outpatient trials; it does not identify individual
mechanisms and its conclusions are tempered by heterogeneity. This vault holds no source that tests a single
component in a randomised design.

## Check yourself

1. What was the recurrence finding, and in which comparison?
2. What did the component analysis suggest, and what is the limit of that suggestion?
3. Which outcome besides symptoms differed by therapy format?
