# Gene-environment interplay and epigenetics

**Module.** M12 Genetics and Neurodevelopment · **Topic.** [[Gene-environment interplay and epigenetics]]

## Why this matters

"Genes and environment interact" is a true, overused sentence covering three distinct phenomena with different designs and evidence; telling them apart turns a slogan into something evaluable.

## The core model

The first phenomenon is interaction: an exposure's effect differs by genotype. This is a claim about an effect estimate, so reading it means reading the estimate, its confidence interval and its design - a genotype-by-exposure cell is always smaller than either group alone, so a study powered for a main effect can still be too small to detect a difference in it. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-expression|Human genetics]]

The second is correlation between genotype and environment (rGE), working through three routes: passive (parents supply both genes and rearing environment), evocative (a child's heritable traits draw different reactions from others), and active (people select and shape environments partly on their own heritable tendencies). An environment-outcome association can therefore reflect genetic influence on the environment itself rather than a causal effect of it, so a design has to say which route it can rule out. [[P39232197 Zhou 2024 Gene-environment correlation family environment academic development#^p39232197-finding|Gene-environment correlation: family environment in academic development]]

A twin study of academic development tested this directly, asking whether the family environment mediated a polygenic score's effect between families or within them. Mediation appeared almost entirely between families - consistent with parents shaping environments based on their own genetic makeup ("genetic nurture") rather than each child's own genotype driving their environment. [[P39232197 Zhou 2024 Gene-environment correlation family environment academic development#^p39232197-passive|Gene-environment correlation: family environment in academic development]] [[P39232197 Zhou 2024 Gene-environment correlation family environment academic development#^p39232197-twin|Gene-environment correlation: family environment in academic development]]

The third, epigenetics, is different again: marks such as DNA methylation change how a cell uses its DNA without altering the sequence. These marks are part of ordinary development - how one genome builds many cell types - and respond to experience, which is why they are studied as a route from environment to lasting biological change. [[F69 NHGRI Epigenomics fact sheet#^f69-definition|Epigenomics fact sheet]] [[F69 NHGRI Epigenomics fact sheet#^f69-methods|Epigenomics fact sheet]]

Most human epigenetic studies measure marks in blood, since it is accessible, not brain tissue. A blood measurement is not a stand-in for brain unless correspondence is shown for that mark and site. One study searching for blood-brain correlated sites found several thousand candidates and confirmed a handful in an actual disease comparison - real evidence for those sites, not a licence to treat blood generally as a brain proxy. [[P39060467 Mendonca 2024 Cross-tissue DNA methylation blood-brain CpGs#^p39060467-limit|Cross-tissue DNA methylation patterns]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A fictional study finds children raised in calmer households show lower stress reactivity later, and a summary claims: "Calm parenting causes lower stress reactivity." Name the routes that could produce this correlation otherwise. Passive rGE: parents predisposed to lower reactivity may both create calmer households and pass that tendency on directly. Evocative rGE: a temperamentally calmer child may evoke calmer responses from parents, reversing the headline's arrow. Active rGE, more relevant later: a person predisposed to lower reactivity may seek calmer environments as they grow. None of this proves the parenting effect is fake - it means the correlation needs a design that can separate the routes (twin, adoption or similar) before the causal claim is supported.

## Common confusions

- "Interaction and correlation are the same thing." Interaction is about effects differing by genotype; correlation is about genotype and environment being statistically linked in the first place.
- "An epigenetic mark found in blood shows what is happening in the brain." Cross-tissue correspondence must be shown for the specific mark and site.
- "A parent-child environmental correlation proves a causal effect." It may instead reflect passive, evocative or active gene-environment correlation.

## What the sources do not establish

Interaction and correlation are well established as concepts, with empirical demonstrations in the cited studies; a validated set of blood-based biomarkers standing in for brain state does not exist, and human transgenerational transmission of trauma-related marks remains contested.

## Answer notes

1. Interaction means an exposure's effect differs by genotype; correlation means genotype and environment are statistically associated in the first place.
2. Passive (parents supply both genes and environment), evocative (heritable traits draw particular responses from others), and active (a person selects or shapes environment based on heritable tendencies).
3. Marks are tissue-specific; correspondence between a blood mark and brain tissue must be demonstrated for that specific mark and site first.
