# Sensitive periods

**Module.** M12 Genetics and Neurodevelopment · **Topic.** [[Sensitive periods]]

## Why this matters

"Critical period" and "sensitive period" get used loosely to argue a developmental window has closed for good, often supporting intervention-timing claims the mechanism research does not actually make. This lesson closes the module by showing what a sensitive period is, mechanistically, and where the evidence stops.

## The core model

A sensitive (or critical) period is a developmental window during which experience has an unusually large, lasting effect on a circuit's organisation; outside it, the effect is smaller. Different systems mature on different, overlapping schedules, so windows differ by function, and an intervention timed well for one system may be timed poorly for another. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]]

The best-studied case is primary visual cortex, with an actual mechanism rather than just a timeline: the window opens when a specific, late-developing type of inhibitory connection matures enough to sharpen the activity patterns that drive circuit refinement - inhibition, not excitation, is the trigger. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]]

The window also closes through an identifiable mechanism: structural consolidation, particularly reorganisation of the extracellular matrix (the scaffold between cells) around the circuit's refined connections, locks in the outcome. Because both opening and closing have specific molecular handles, "critical period" names an active, manipulable biological process with a beginning and an end, not just an age on a chart. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-consolidation|Critical period plasticity]]

That manipulability is why some researchers propose reactivating the closed process later in life, for example to support recovery in amblyopia (reduced vision from an eye that did not develop typically) - explicitly framed, in the source proposing it, as a research strategy, not a treatment. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-reactivation|Critical period plasticity]]

A sensitive period is not a script. Experience during a window is an input that interacts with an already genetically guided developmental programme; variation in outcome is the expected case, and missing a window does not lock in a single fixed trajectory the way the popular metaphor implies. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. An article claims: "The sensitive period for social skill development closes at age seven, so intervention after that age cannot help." Apply the reading rule this domain uses throughout: state the function, species, measured marker, and whether the claim is mechanism or intervention outcome. The mechanistic account above concerns visual cortex, studied mainly in animals, with specific molecular markers. A claim about human "social skill development," with an age but no marker or cited mechanism study, has not shown the same trigger-and-consolidation machinery applies to that function, in humans, closing at that age - it may be an inference from behaviour, not a measurement. The research also does not, by itself, support "cannot help after this age" - that is a separate claim about intervention efficacy, needing its own trial evidence.

## Common confusions

- "There is one sensitive period for brain development." Different systems have different windows on different schedules.
- "A stated closing age for a human sensitive period is a direct measurement." Most such claims are inferences from behavioural data, not measurements of the trigger-and-consolidation machinery seen in animals.
- "Because a window can close, intervention afterward is guaranteed not to help." That is a separate empirical question the developmental-mechanism research alone does not settle.

## What the sources do not establish

The trigger-and-closure mechanism is firmly established for visual cortex in animal models and supported by clinical observation of amblyopia treatment windows; evidence for the same mechanism in humans, for functions such as language or social development, is far more limited and indirect, and reactivation of a closed window in humans remains a research proposal.
