# Cognitive models of ADHD

**Module.** M08 ADHD · **Topic.** [[Cognitive models of ADHD]]

## Why this matters

Headlines often present a single study as "the" explanation for ADHD. This lesson teaches the opposite habit:
several competing, overlapping cognitive accounts each have group-level support, and none is necessary or
sufficient for the diagnosis.

## The core model

**Executive-function accounts.** The most prominent account holds that ADHD stems from a primary deficit in
executive functions, the processes that keep a goal active over time. Across 83 studies, ADHD groups did worse
on every executive task, with medium effects most consistent for inhibition, vigilance, working memory and
planning.
[[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-theory|Willcutt 2005]]
[[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-effects|Willcutt 2005]]
Because deficits were absent in many individuals, the same meta-analysis judged executive weakness an important
component but not a necessary or sufficient cause - a medium group difference leaves the two distributions
largely overlapping.
[[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-necessary|Willcutt 2005]]

**Dual pathway accounts and delay aversion.** This model replaced a single deficit with two separable routes,
one cognitive and one motivational, each affecting only some people. The motivational route centres on delay
aversion, a preference for a smaller immediate reward, which the heterogeneity lesson's nine-task study showed
dissociating from inhibitory and timing measures. A proposed mechanism, the dopamine transfer deficit
hypothesis, rests mainly on one rat strain and still needs direct human tests.
[[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-model|Sonuga-Barke 2010]]
[[P38384667 Tripp 2024 Dopamine transfer deficit in ADHD#^p38384667-dtd|Tripp 2024]]
[[P38384667 Tripp 2024 Dopamine transfer deficit in ADHD#^p38384667-caution-translation|Appraisal: Tripp 2024]]

**State regulation.** This account holds that performance depends on how fast events arrive: fast rates
over-activate and add impulsive errors, slow rates under-activate and produce slow, variable responses. A
meta-analysis of Go/No-Go studies found exactly that pattern for speed and errors - but event rate did not
change response variability, and ADHD-control differences persisted regardless, so state explains part of the
gap without becoming the cause.
[[P23062355 Metin 2012 Event rate effects in ADHD#^p23062355-rate|Metin 2012]]
[[P23062355 Metin 2012 Event rate effects in ADHD#^p23062355-variability|Metin 2012]]

**Default-mode interference.** This account proposes that internally directed brain activity is not kept
separate enough from task networks. In a mega-analysis of over 1,300 children with ADHD, this separation was
weaker on average - but the effects were small, and a resting-state design cannot show that weaker separation
causes a lapse during a task.
[[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-size|Norman 2023]]
[[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-caution-effect|Appraisal: Norman 2023]]

**Reading rule.** For any cognitive claim about ADHD, name the process, the task, and the comparison group, then
ask whether the result is an average difference or a statement about everyone with the diagnosis.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A hypothetical press release states: "Brain scans reveal the true cause
of ADHD: a weaker default mode network explains every core symptom."

Check the effect size first: real default-mode differences were small, not the kind a phrase like "true cause"
implies. Check the design: resting-state connectivity is measured outside any task, so it cannot show that
weaker separation causes a lapse at a specific moment, only that it correlates with a diagnosis on average.
Check what the claim leaves out: executive-function, delay-related and state-regulation accounts each explain
part of the same group difference, so no single account has shown it explains "every symptom" for everyone.

The accurate rewrite: in a large sample, children with ADHD showed a small, group-level difference in resting
network separation - one candidate piece of a multi-part picture, not a demonstrated single cause.

## Common confusions

- "Executive dysfunction is what ADHD is." An important but non-necessary, non-sufficient component; deficits
  are absent in many people with the diagnosis.
- "A brain-based finding is automatically stronger evidence." Small effect sizes and correlational designs limit
  a finding's weight regardless of which level it comes from.
- "These accounts compete, so one will replace the others." They describe overlapping facets, possibly different
  subgroups, rather than rivals for one truth.

## What the sources do not establish

None of these accounts has produced an individual diagnostic test, and whether they describe genuinely distinct
subgroups or facets present in everyone with ADHD is unresolved. The delay-aversion mechanism draws mainly on
one rodent strain.
