# CBT for adult ADHD

**Module.** M08 ADHD · **Topic.** [[CBT for adult ADHD]]

## Why this matters

CBT is the best-evidenced non-pharmacological intervention for adult ADHD in this vault, and the review that
supports it rates the evidence low quality. Reading it well means holding both of those facts: the pooled
result points in a favourable direction, and the certainty rating limits how much weight that result carries
while durability and functional benefit remain open.

## The core model

In a Cochrane review of 14 randomised trials with 700 adults, adding CBT to medication beat medication alone on
clinician-rated and self-rated core symptoms, and CBT also beat a waiting list on self-reported symptoms. The
14 trials are the review's total; the adjunctive-to-medication comparison is a subset of them.
[[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

The authors rated the evidence low quality for short-term benefit and noted limited long-term follow-up,
heterogeneous outcomes and a geographically narrow trial base.
[[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

Some secondary outcomes improved, including self-reported depression and anxiety, while quality-of-life results
varied.
[[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-secondary|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

The 2025 adult synthesis adds context by reporting what each rater saw. Its medications had higher efficacy
than placebo on both self-reported and clinician-reported scales, while cognitive behavioural therapy,
cognitive remediation, mindfulness, psychoeducation and transcranial direct current stimulation were better
than placebo only on clinician-reported measures. Most therapeutic components were similar to placebo on
all-cause discontinuation, while atomoxetine and guanfacine had lower acceptability.
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-stimulants|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]];
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-nonpharm|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]];
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-acceptability|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]

The reading skill here is to separate three claims: symptoms improved with CBT added to medication over the
short term in that review; the certainty of that estimate is low; and the effect on daily functioning and
durability is unresolved.

It is worth being precise about what "low quality" changes. It does not mean the trials found nothing; the pooled
comparison favoured adding CBT to medication. It means the estimate is uncertain enough that a decision should
not rest on it alone, and that better-designed or longer trials could move it. Weak evidence is not no evidence,
which is why the treatment stays available for discussion while the follow-up studies are done.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Design the follow-up that would answer the durability question.

Specify the question first: do the symptom gains and any functional changes persist 12 months after treatment
ends? Then the design: a randomised trial with treatment and control arms, pre-specified outcomes at end of
treatment and at 6 and 12 months, blinded outcome assessment where possible, and a functional measure in
addition to symptom scales. Retention matters, because the people who benefit most may be the ones who stay.

Anticipate the confound: participants may seek other treatment after the trial. The analysis plan has to record
and account for that. None of this is exotic; it is what "limited long-term follow-up" leaves undone, and it is
the honest way to move the evidence base forward.

## Common confusions

- "Low-quality evidence means the treatment does not work." It means the estimate is uncertain.
- "Adding CBT helped symptoms, so it helps daily life." Quality of life and functioning were not consistently
  improved.
- "CBT replaces medication." The trials tested CBT added to medication.

## What the sources do not establish

Neither source establishes durability, functional improvement or individual response, and the Cochrane review's
low certainty rating is the explicit limit on how strongly the short-term finding should be used.

## Check yourself

2. Low-quality rating for short-term benefit, limited long-term follow-up, heterogeneous outcomes and a narrow
