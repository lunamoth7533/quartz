# Rater and measurement effects in ADHD

**Module.** M08 ADHD · **Topic.** [[Rater and measurement effects in ADHD]]

## Why this matters

Every treatment claim in this module rests on someone's rating of someone else's behaviour. Before believing a
treatment "worked," ask who rated it - who is watching changes what gets reported, and that is a feature of the
measurement itself, not an error waiting to be fixed.

## The core model

**Rater divergence at trial scale.** In a network meta-analysis of ADHD medication trials, every drug beat
placebo on clinicians' ratings in children at 12 weeks, but on teachers' ratings only methylphenidate and
modafinil did; adults showed a different set of drugs again. The same trials produced a broad effect on one
rater's scale and a narrow one on another's.
[[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

**The pattern repeats for non-drug treatments.** In adults, stimulants and atomoxetine beat placebo on both
self-reported and clinician-reported scales, while cognitive behavioural therapy, cognitive remediation,
mindfulness, psychoeducation and brain stimulation beat placebo only on clinician-reported measures.
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-stimulants|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-nonpharm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]
A Cochrane review of cognitive behavioural interventions makes the point directly: a benefit claim should name
who rated it.
[[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|Appraisal: Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]]

**Disagreement is not automatically error.** Parents and teachers watch different settings and demands, so
diverging reports can reflect real situational variation rather than either rater being wrong.

**Instruments carry assumptions, and reliability is not validity.** Comparing scores across groups or time
assumes an instrument measures the same construct the same way - measurement invariance - which comes in
configural, metric and scalar levels with progressively stronger requirements.
[[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-definition|Measurement invariance]]
[[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-levels|Measurement invariance]]
Building a scale means checking its content before use, then reporting reliability (alpha, omega, test-retest)
and, separately, validity against other measures - a scale can be highly consistent while still missing the
construct it claims to measure.
[[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-phases|Developing and validating scales]]
[[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-reliability|Developing and validating scales]]

**Pooling does not make the problem disappear.** Averaging across raters would hide the pattern, which is why
careful reviews report raters separately and grade certainty down for inconsistency instead.
[[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-domains|GRADE]]

**Reading rule.** Before treating a treatment claim as settled, name the rater, the setting they observed, and
whether they knew which treatment was given.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A hypothetical school pilot reports: "An attention-training programme
significantly improved ADHD symptoms on teacher ratings; parent ratings showed no significant change." A parent
concludes the programme does not work.

Name the raters first: teachers observed the structured classroom the programme targeted, parents observed a
different setting - a real difference between settings is plausible before assuming either rater is wrong. Ask
about blinding: if teachers knew who was enrolled, expectation could shape their ratings, and the summary does
not say. Ask what "significant" means quantitatively, since a significant result on one scale and a null one on
another can reflect a real effect difference, a difference in scale sensitivity, or sample size alone.

The accurate summary is neither "it works at school but not at home" nor "it does not work." It is: teacher
ratings improved and parent ratings did not, and without blinding and sensitivity information, that is a
rater-and-setting result, not a settled verdict.

## Common confusions

- "Disagreement between informants means one is wrong." It often reflects genuine situational variation.
- "Pooling clinician and self-ratings gives the 'true' effect." Pooling can hide a real rater-dependent pattern.
- "A reliable questionnaire is a valid one." Reliability is consistency; validity is whether it measures the
  intended construct, checked separately.

## What the sources do not establish

No established individual biomarker exists here for ADHD, so thresholds rest on reported symptoms and history as
judged by specifiable informants. How best to combine multiple informants for classification remains debated.

## Answer notes

1. Clinicians and teachers observed different settings; every drug beat placebo on clinician ratings, while only
   two did on teacher ratings.
2. Reliability describes consistency of scores; validity describes whether the instrument measures the intended
   construct, checked separately.
3. Pooling would hide a real rater-dependent pattern; grading certainty down for inconsistency is the accepted
   alternative.
