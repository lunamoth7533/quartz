# ADHD across the lifespan

**Module.** M08 ADHD · **Topic.** [[ADHD across the lifespan]]

## Why this matters

ADHD was long treated as a childhood condition, and much of the evidence base still reflects that origin. The
lifespan view changes which samples, outcomes and questions matter, and it is the first thing to check when
reading any ADHD claim.

## The core model

ADHD can persist from childhood into adulthood with changing presentation and continuing impairment for many
people; estimated adult prevalence is around 2.5 percent, and up to 70 percent of childhood-onset cases continue
with impairing symptoms.
[[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-base|ADHD in adults review]]

The World Federation consensus statement curates 208 empirically supported assertions about ADHD, endorsed by
80 authors across 27 countries, using stated inclusion rules for the evidence it counts.
[[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-scope|World Federation ADHD consensus statement]]

Open questions in the adult literature include possible late-onset presentations, emotional dysregulation as a
core symptom, and how impairment is defined.
[[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults review]]

The methodological warning is about samples. Course and outcome differ between clinical and community samples,
so prevalence and prognosis depend on who was studied: a clinic-based sample is enriched for severity and
comorbidity, and a community sample includes people who never present for care.

Make that concrete. A community cohort that follows children without requiring clinic referral includes mild
presentations, and some participants drift below symptom thresholds while still reporting everyday difficulty. A
clinic cohort has already passed through a referral filter that enriches it for severity, comorbidity and family
stress. Both are legitimate samples answering different questions, and neither is the "true" picture of ADHD.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A study of 8-year-olds reports that symptoms remit in most participants
by age 14, and a summary concludes that ADHD "usually resolves by adolescence".

Check the sample and the measurement. If recruitment was from a community sample and remission was defined by
crossing a symptom threshold rather than by absence of impairment, the study may be describing reduced symptom
scores rather than resolved difficulties. Clinical samples, which include more severe presentations, often show
higher persistence. Adult outcomes also involve impairment in work, study and relationships, which symptom
scales do not capture.

The accurate sentence: "in that community sample, a majority no longer met the symptom threshold at follow-up;
persistence estimates depend on the sample and on whether impairment is measured."

## Common confusions

- "It is a childhood condition." It is a lifespan condition that can be diagnosed in adulthood and often
  persists with changing presentation.
- "Symptoms equal impairment." Symptom counts and functional impairment are different measurements that can
  diverge.
- "Prevalence is one number." Estimates depend on definitions, instruments and samples.

## What the sources do not establish

The adult review is a narrative review, and the consensus statement is a synthesis of existing literature with
inclusion thresholds; neither supports individual prediction, and this vault holds no source on prognosis for a
specific person.

## Check yourself

1. Why do prevalence estimates for ADHD vary?
2. What does the adult review list as open questions?
3. Why can two studies of persistence disagree without either being wrong?

## Answer notes

1. Because definitions, instruments, informants and samples differ, and clinic-based samples differ from
   community samples.
2. Possible late-onset presentations, emotional dysregulation as a core symptom, and the definition of
   impairment, among others.
3. Because persistence depends on how it is measured — symptom threshold versus impairment — and on which
   population was followed.
