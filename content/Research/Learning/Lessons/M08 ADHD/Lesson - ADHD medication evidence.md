# ADHD medication evidence

**Module.** M08 ADHD · **Topic.** [[ADHD medication evidence]]

## Why this matters

This is the vault's densest cluster of trial evidence: three syntheses, dozens of drugs, hundreds of trials. It
is also where readers most often take a group ranking and treat it as a personal recommendation. The lesson is
about the structure of the evidence, not about what any person should take.

## The core model

In adults, stimulants and atomoxetine improved core symptoms on both self- and clinician-rated scales at
12 weeks in a synthesis of 113 randomised trials with 14,887 participants.
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-stimulants|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]

In children and adolescents, all included drugs beat placebo on clinicians' ratings, but only methylphenidate
and modafinil did on teachers' ratings, across 133 double-blind trials.
[[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]

The authors of that earlier synthesis found insufficient data at 26 and 52 weeks and called for urgent research
on long-term effects.
[[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]

A dose-effect synthesis across 113 trials found that median efficacy plateaued above study-specific dose ranges
for some drugs, while discontinuation for adverse events rose with dose for amphetamines and methylphenidate
above those ranges.
[[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-doseeffect|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]];
[[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-tolerability|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]

The structural caveats matter more than the rankings. Network comparisons mix direct and indirect evidence, and
in the 2018 synthesis most indirect comparisons carried low or very low confidence.
[[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-limit|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]
Group rankings and head-to-head averages are not prescribing guidance for one person.
[[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-caution-choice|AI synthesis: ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A ranking table places drug A above drug B for efficacy. What does the
ranking actually tell you?

Ask three questions. Was there a direct head-to-head trial, or is the comparison indirect through the network?
How precise is the comparison — the confidence interval may include no difference? And for which population and
outcome was the ranking estimated: children, adults, clinician-rated symptoms at 12 weeks?

A careful reading says: "in this network, drug A had a higher estimated efficacy than drug B for this outcome
in this population, with [wide/narrow] uncertainty; the comparison may rest on indirect evidence." What it does
not say: which drug will suit a person, what the long-term difference is, or how the two compare on the
outcomes that matter most to them.

## Common confusions

- "Network meta-analysis compares every pair directly." Much of it is indirect, with uncertainty that the
  confidence ratings reflect.
- "Efficacy ranking is the treatment decision." Tolerability, comorbidity, preference and monitoring all enter.
- "Twelve weeks is long-term." It is short-term; the syntheses say so.

## What the sources do not establish

None of the syntheses provides individual prescribing guidance, and dose ranges in the dose-effect analysis are
study-specific. This vault deliberately omits numerical dose and titration content, and nothing here supports
changing any medication.

## Check yourself

1. What did the adult synthesis find for medications at 12 weeks?
2. Why did the child and adolescent synthesis differ between clinician and teacher ratings?
3. What did the dose-effect analysis find about efficacy and discontinuation?
4. Why is a network ranking not a prescribing recommendation?

## Answer notes

1. Stimulants and atomoxetine improved core symptoms on self- and clinician-rated scales.
2. The review reports that results differed by rater: on teacher ratings only methylphenidate and modafinil
   showed benefit among the included drugs, while clinician ratings favoured all drugs. Why they differed is
   not established by the abstract; candidate explanations include the settings, samples, instruments and
   time points each rater sampled, and they remain hypotheses rather than findings of the review.
3. Efficacy plateaued above study-specific dose ranges for some drugs while adverse-event discontinuation rose
   with dose for amphetamines and methylphenidate.
4. Because it estimates average comparisons in trial populations, often indirectly, and does not account for
   the person, their comorbidity, preferences or monitoring needs.
