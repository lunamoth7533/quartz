# ADHD diagnosis and measurement

**Module.** M08 ADHD · **Topic.** [[ADHD diagnosis and measurement]]

## Why this matters

ADHD has no confirmatory laboratory test, so assessment rests on history, rating scales and impairment across
settings. That makes measurement quality the central scientific issue, and it makes the rater a variable in
every treatment result.

## The core model

The use of objective diagnostic measures is listed among the open questions in adult ADHD.
[[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults review]]

Rating scales measure reports. An informant's report and a self-report are different measurements, and
clinician-rated and self-rated outcomes can disagree.
[[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI synthesis: CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

Treatment trials make the consequence concrete. A 2025 synthesis of 113 adult trials found that stimulants and
atomoxetine reduced core symptoms on both self- and clinician-rated scales at 12 weeks, while cognitive
behavioural therapy, cognitive remediation, mindfulness, psychoeducation and transcranial direct current
stimulation showed smaller symptom benefits.
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-stimulants|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]];
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-nonpharm|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]

The consensus statement behind much of the narrative literature used explicit inclusion rules, drawing on
studies with more than 2000 participants or meta-analyses of five or more studies.
[[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-methods|World Federation ADHD consensus statement]]

Put together: assessment is multi-source by design, and any single number — a scale score, a report, a task
result — is one measurement among several. Informant disagreement does not settle a diagnosis, and it does not
invalidate the measurement either.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A trial reports that a treatment improved ADHD symptoms, with a large
effect on self-report and a small effect on clinician rating. The abstract says the treatment "improved ADHD".

Rewrite the finding. The two ratings disagree in magnitude, and both are measurements with different vantage
points: self-report captures the person's experience and is sensitive to expectation; clinician rating captures
observed presentation in a short interaction and may miss daily context. The honest summary names the rater and
the size: "self-reported symptoms improved substantially and clinician-rated symptoms improved modestly on
average in this trial population."

The next question is which change matters for the person's daily life — which is the functional-outcome
question, and in this literature it is often not measured.

## Common confusions

- "No lab test means it is not real." It means assessment relies on history and reported impairment, which is
  how many clinical constructs are assessed.
- "Disagreement between raters means one is wrong." Different raters sample different contexts.
- "Symptom scales measure impairment." They measure reported symptoms; impairment is a separate construct.

## What the sources do not establish

None of the sources provides a validated objective diagnostic test, and this vault does not support diagnosis.
The reviews describe group-level instruments and outcomes, not individual assessment decisions.
