# Motivation and reward

**Module.** M04 Psychology · **Topic.** [[Motivation and reward]]

## Why this matters

"Motivation" and "reward" get used as if they were single things - a tank either full or empty. The research keeps them apart: a belief about capability, a belief about an outcome's value, and a measurable dopamine signal are different objects, and conflating them is where clinical misreading starts, especially around apathy and anhedonia.

## The core model

Motivation covers the initiation and persistence of goal-directed behaviour, described through several traditions - drive, arousal, expectancy accounts - that differ in what they treat as the explanatory unit. [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-theories|Motivation]]

The bridge construct across these traditions is expectancy-value: beliefs about capability (can I do this?) and beliefs about outcome value (is it worth doing?) jointly predict effort and persistence. An intervention that changes only one arm often underperforms, because the other arm still holds the behaviour back - and this gives apathy, anhedonia and amotivation a more precise home, each describable as a change in the expectancy arm, the value arm, or both, sharper than a global "motivation deficit". [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-efficacy|Motivation]]

At the neural level, the most cited candidate mechanism is that midbrain dopamine neurons respond to reward-prediction error - the difference between an outcome and what was expected - mapping onto reinforcement learning's teaching signal for updating value estimates. But dopamine carries additional and heterogeneous signals beyond reward, including salience and movement-related information, and different neuron populations carry different signals, so "reward" is an approximation rather than a complete description, and which population was recorded changes what a result means. [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-rpe|Reward prediction error]] [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-scope|Reward prediction error]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-heterogeneity|What does dopamine mean?]]

That heterogeneity is why the literature separates wanting, liking and learning: the pull toward a cue, the pleasure of getting the reward, and the prediction-error signal that updates value normally co-occur, which hides how separable they are - but pharmacology and addiction research split them cleanly. Treating a reward-prediction error as a "happiness meter" is a category error the primary literature warns against: it is a learning signal, not a report of pleasure. [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A news summary of a dopamine study claims "scientists found the brain's happiness chemical spikes when we win."

Unpack the claim in three steps. What did the study measure - a self-reported feeling, or a firing-rate change time-locked to an outcome? If firing rate, is it aligned with reward size, or with how much better or worse than expected (a prediction-error pattern) - different signals, different implications. Which neurons were recorded, since not all dopamine populations track reward information? A defensible summary reads "neural activity linked to reward-prediction error increased", not "the happiness chemical spiked" - the second phrase swaps a learning signal for a pleasure report.

## Common confusions

- "Dopamine is the brain's pleasure or happiness chemical." Dopamine populations carry heterogeneous signals, including learning and salience information, not a single pleasure signal.
- "Wanting, liking and learning are the same thing because they happen together." They co-occur in ordinary life but dissociate cleanly under pharmacological and experimental manipulation.
- "Low motivation is one problem with one fix." It can reflect low capability beliefs, low outcome-value beliefs, or both, which call for different responses.

## What the sources do not establish

The sources describe a well-replicated neural signal (reward-prediction error) and a well-supported psychological bridge construct (expectancy-value); neither source translates cleanly into a measurement of anhedonia or apathy in a clinical population, and self-reported motivation is known to diverge from behavioural measures of effort, so the two should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Answer notes

1. Beliefs about capability and beliefs about outcome value; changing only one leaves the other arm still limiting effort and persistence.
2. Because dopamine neuron populations carry heterogeneous signals, including salience and movement information, not only reward-related information, and different populations carry different signals.
3. Wanting is the appetitive pull toward a cue, liking is the pleasure of consumption, and learning is the prediction-error signal that updates value; they co-occur in healthy life, which hides that pharmacology and addiction research can separate them.
