# EMDR

**Module.** M04 Psychology · **Topic.** [[EMDR]]

## Why this matters

EMDR is unusual among trauma therapies because its defining component - eye movements paired with recalling a distressing memory - has been dismantled and tested on its own. This closing lesson uses EMDR to practise two skills the module builds toward: testing whether a component earns its place in a package, and reading a trial's primary outcome before its secondary ones.

## The core model

EMDR is a trauma-focused psychotherapy in which a person brings a distressing memory to mind while making eye movements; PTSD symptom severity is the outcome its trials are built around. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-comparators|Simpson 2025]]

Dismantling trials test the eye-movement question directly: full procedure versus a version with eye movements removed. The full procedure came out moderately ahead (about 0.41), moderated by treatment fidelity. Outside therapy, recalling a distressing memory while moving the eyes made it feel markedly less vivid, the largest laboratory effect reviewed. Vividness ratings are proxies, though, and dismantling trials are small, so the component's contribution reads as plausible rather than settled. [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-therapy|Lee 2013]] [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-lab|Lee 2013]] [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-caution-dismantling|Appraisal: Lee 2013]]

In adult PTSD, EMDR's main evidence base: across 16 recent trials combined with those behind the 2018 NICE guideline, EMDR beat waitlist or usual care and was statistically indistinguishable from trauma-focused CBT, usually over a shorter course - one reason a modelling study ranked it most cost-effective. But most trials were small, nearly all at moderate or high risk of bias, and a null contrast is not a designed non-inferiority result. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-comparators|Simpson 2025]] [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-duration|Simpson 2025]] [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-bias|Simpson 2025]] [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-caution-null|Appraisal: Simpson 2025]]

The bipolar trial is the clearest example here of reading a primary outcome first. In 77 trauma-exposed people with bipolar disorder, relapse over 24 months - the primary outcome - did not differ between EMDR and supportive therapy. At 12 months, EMDR led on mood symptoms and functioning as secondary outcomes, and trauma-focused work was not followed by more relapse or dropout - reassuring, but secondary results after a negative primary outcome carry less weight. [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-relapse|Hogg 2024]] [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-symptoms|Hogg 2024]] [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-safety|Hogg 2024]] [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-caution-primary|Appraisal: Hogg 2024]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A summary of the bipolar EMDR trial reads "EMDR improved mood symptoms and functioning in bipolar disorder," and stops there.

Read the primary outcome first, always. This trial's pre-specified primary outcome was relapse over 24 months, and found no difference - the trial did not hit its main target. The mood and safety findings highlighted were secondary outcomes at 12 months, worth reporting, especially that trauma-focused work did not increase relapse or dropout. But leading with positive secondary findings while omitting the negative primary one inverts the trial's hierarchy of evidence; the accurate summary states the primary result first.

## Common confusions

- "Dismantling trials settle whether eye movements are essential." The trials are small and fidelity-moderated, so the component's contribution is plausible, not settled.
- "Matching trauma-focused CBT proves equal effectiveness." A non-significant difference in a superiority trial is not a designed non-inferiority result.
- "Positive secondary outcomes rescue a trial that missed its primary outcome." They are hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory - the primary outcome is what the trial was powered to test.

## What the sources do not establish

The adult-PTSD trials are mostly small and at moderate-to-high risk of bias; the eye-movement case rests on a synthesis predating recent trials; and no EMDR trial sourced here is defined by ICD-11 complex PTSD, autism or ADHD, so claims here do not extend automatically to those populations.

2. In adult PTSD, what did EMDR outperform and what did it not clearly outperform?
3. Why does the bipolar-disorder trial's negative primary outcome matter for interpreting its positive secondary findings?

## Answer notes

1. The full procedure moderately outperformed a version without eye movements, but trials were small and fidelity affected the size, so the finding is plausible, not definitive.
2. EMDR outperformed waitlist and usual care; it did not clearly outperform trauma-focused CBT, differing non-significantly in a trial base mostly at moderate-to-high risk of bias.
3. Because the pre-specified primary outcome (24-month relapse) showed no difference, so the positive secondary findings at 12 months are hypothesis-generating, not a confirmed benefit.
