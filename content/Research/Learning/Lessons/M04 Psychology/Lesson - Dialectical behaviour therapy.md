# Dialectical behaviour therapy

**Module.** M04 Psychology · **Topic.** [[Dialectical behaviour therapy]]

## Why this matters

Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) is the most-studied psychotherapy for borderline personality disorder here, and has been adapted for complex PTSD presentations and bipolar disorder. Reading its evidence well means tracking precisely what outcome moved in each trial - "DBT works" hides three quite different findings.

## The core model

DBT is a cognitive-behavioural treatment combining behavioural and cognitive techniques to teach skills for managing emotions, tolerating distress and keeping relationships working, and it is aimed above all at emotional escalation and the dangerous behaviour that can follow it - self-harm and suicide attempts are the outcomes its trials count first. [[P32368793 Storebo 2020 Psychological therapies for borderline personality disorder#^p32368793-dbt|Storebø 2020]]

In borderline personality disorder, its home condition, the picture is broad but thin: across 75 trials of tailored psychotherapies, DBT accounted for about one-third, and against usual care it reduced borderline severity and self-harm and improved functioning. But each DBT estimate came from only three to seven small, low-quality trials, and tailored therapies as a group did not differ clearly from one another - so DBT's evidence base is the largest without being the strongest, and "why DBT specifically" stays open. [[P32368793 Storebo 2020 Psychological therapies for borderline personality disorder#^p32368793-dbt|Storebø 2020]] [[P32368793 Storebo 2020 Psychological therapies for borderline personality disorder#^p32368793-models|Storebø 2020]] [[P32368793 Storebo 2020 Psychological therapies for borderline personality disorder#^p32368793-caution-certainty|Appraisal: Storebø 2020]]

Outside its home condition, the evidence narrows to one trial per condition. In 193 women with abuse-related PTSD and borderline features, an adapted, phase-based version called DBT-PTSD and an established trauma therapy both reduced clinician-rated PTSD severity, with a small advantage and lower dropout for DBT-PTSD - but the sample was defined by DSM-5 PTSD plus borderline criteria, not ICD-11 complex PTSD, so the finding does not automatically transfer. [[P32697288 Bohus 2020 DBT-PTSD versus cognitive processing therapy#^p32697288-ptsd|Bohus 2020]] [[P32697288 Bohus 2020 DBT-PTSD versus cognitive processing therapy#^p32697288-remission|Bohus 2020]] [[P32697288 Bohus 2020 DBT-PTSD versus cognitive processing therapy#^p32697288-caution-sample|Appraisal: Bohus 2020]]

In 100 adolescents with bipolar spectrum disorder, a year of DBT reduced suicide attempts compared with standard psychotherapy, while mood symptoms and episodes improved equally in both arms - DBT changed suicide-related behaviour specifically, not the course of the mood episodes, since mood improved the same amount either way. [[P37703037 Goldstein 2024 DBT for adolescents with bipolar disorder#^p37703037-suicide|Goldstein 2024]] [[P37703037 Goldstein 2024 DBT for adolescents with bipolar disorder#^p37703037-mood|Goldstein 2024]] [[P37703037 Goldstein 2024 DBT for adolescents with bipolar disorder#^p37703037-caution-scope|Appraisal: Goldstein 2024]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A summary states "DBT is proven to treat bipolar disorder," citing the adolescent trial that found fewer suicide attempts with DBT.

Ask which outcome actually moved before accepting the claim. In that trial, suicide attempts fell with DBT relative to standard psychotherapy, while mood symptoms and episode frequency improved equally in both arms. "DBT treats bipolar disorder" implies the illness course changed; the actual finding is narrower - DBT changed one serious outcome without outperforming the comparison on mood. The defensible statement is "DBT reduced suicide attempts in bipolar adolescents beyond standard psychotherapy, without an additional mood benefit" - real and important, but far more specific than "DBT treats bipolar disorder."

## Common confusions

- "DBT's borderline personality disorder evidence is high-certainty because it is the most-studied therapy there." It is the largest evidence base, but the individual trials behind it are small and rated low quality, and tailored therapies did not clearly outperform one another.
- "DBT-PTSD's results apply to anyone with complex PTSD." The trial sample was defined by DSM-5 PTSD plus borderline features, not an ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis.
- "DBT reducing suicide attempts in bipolar adolescents means it treats the underlying mood disorder." Mood symptoms and episodes improved equally in both trial arms; only suicide-related behaviour showed a DBT-specific advantage.

## What the sources do not establish

Outside borderline personality disorder, the evidence is one randomised trial per condition, so neither the complex-PTSD-presentation finding nor the bipolar finding has been replicated; whether DBT's benefit comes from its specific skills or from structural features it shares with other tailored therapies is unresolved, since subgroup comparisons in borderline personality disorder found no clear difference between models.
