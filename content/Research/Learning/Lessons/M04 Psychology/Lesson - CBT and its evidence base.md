# CBT and its evidence base

**Module.** M04 Psychology · **Topic.** [[CBT and its evidence base]]

## Why this matters

CBT is the most-studied psychological treatment in this vault, and "CBT" is also the vaguest term in it. This
lesson is about reading treatment evidence when the treatment is a family of protocols rather than a molecule.

## The core model

Cognitive behavioural therapy is a structured, skills-based approach that targets the relationship between
thoughts, behaviour and mood.
[[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

The model can be tested with specific additions. Adjunctive CBT added to medication beat medication alone on
clinician-rated and self-rated core ADHD symptoms in adults, with the authors rating the evidence low quality
for short-term benefit.
[[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]];
[[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

In bipolar disorder, adjunctive manualised psychotherapy was associated with lower recurrence than control, and
a component analysis found that psychoeducation with supervised skills practice in family or group formats was
linked to fewer recurrences than individual delivery.
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]];
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-components|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

The limit is built into the method: component analyses estimate average contributions across heterogeneous
protocols and cannot say what produced one person's improvement.
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-caution-ingredients|AI synthesis: Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]
And retention differed by format, which is a practical outcome in its own right.
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-retention|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Two studies both claim "CBT for insomnia improves depression". Study A
uses six sessions of CBT-I in adults with insomnia and depression. Study B uses 20 sessions of CBT for
depression with a sleep-hygiene module in the same population.

Compare fairly. Hold the population constant and look at the treatment: different protocols, different dose,
different mechanisms. Look at the outcome: self-reported depression, clinician-rated depression, or actigraphy
and diary-based sleep? Look at the comparison: waitlist, treatment as usual, or an active control? Look at
follow-up: end of treatment or six months later?

A fair conclusion: both studies tested a protocol in a population and found effects on named outcomes; the
protocols are not interchangeable and the comparison across them cannot attribute the effect to "CBT".

## Common confusions

- "CBT is one treatment." It is a family of manuals, doses, formats and delivery modes.
- "Adding therapy always helps." The evidence supports specific additions in specific populations, with
  low-quality ratings in places.
- "Improvement proves the model." Outcome improvement is compatible with several mechanisms, including
  non-specific ones.

## What the sources do not establish

Neither source identifies active ingredients in a way that transfers to an individual, and both are reviews of
trials in defined populations. This vault holds no source that compares different CBT protocols head to head
within one population.
