# Attention and executive function

**Module.** M04 Psychology · **Topic.** [[Attention and executive function]]

## Why this matters

Attention and executive function are the psychological vocabulary that the ADHD and autism modules rely on, and
they are also the constructs most often measured by narrow laboratory tasks. The gap between the task and the
daily life is where a great deal of misreading happens.

## The core model

Attention and executive function describe selection of information and the control processes that hold goals,
inhibit habits and switch tasks.
[[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-networks|Prefrontal catecholamine mechanisms]]

The prefrontal cortex is described as critical for screening distractions, inhibiting impulses and planning, and
its regulation depends on dopamine and noradrenaline released according to arousal state.
[[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-catecholamines|Prefrontal catecholamine mechanisms]]

That mechanistic account sits at the level of networks and neuromodulation. The clinical question is different:
whether a person's difficulties in daily life are captured by the tests used to measure these constructs, and
how far executive dysfunction belongs in the definition of adult ADHD is explicitly listed as an open question
in the recent review.
[[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults review]]

Laboratory tasks isolate a process under controlled conditions. Daily life involves motivation, context,
strategy, fatigue and the interaction of several demands at once, so a task score is a proxy with known
limits, and a group difference says little about one person.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A study finds that adults with ADHD perform worse than controls on a
sustained-attention task, with a modest effect size, and a commentary says the finding "confirms that ADHD is a
deficit in sustained attention".

Interrogate each step. The task measures performance under specific conditions with a specific reward structure;
the group difference is an average with overlapping distributions; and the study design cannot establish that
attention failure causes the daily difficulties. Meanwhile, the same people may perform typically on some days
or in high-interest contexts, which the task does not capture.

The defensible statement: "in this task, the ADHD group performed worse on average; sustained attention is one
component of the presentation, and task performance does not determine daily functioning or diagnosis."

## Common confusions

- "Attention is one ability." It is a family of processes (alerting, orienting, sustained, selective) that
  different tasks measure differently.
- "Executive function is a single score." It is an umbrella for several control processes that dissociate.
- "A label explains performance." Describing someone as inattentive restates the observation rather than
  explaining it.

## What the sources do not establish

The mechanistic review combines animal pharmacology with human imaging and does not validate any task as a
diagnostic measure; the adult review lists objective diagnostic measures among its open questions. This vault
therefore treats attention measures as proxies and does not use them for diagnosis.
