# Shame and guilt

**Module.** M04 Psychology · **Topic.** [[Shame and guilt]]

## Why this matters

Shame and guilt get used interchangeably in everyday speech, but research treats them as separable emotions with different correlates and clinical stakes. Getting the distinction right - and noticing when an outcome scale already contains guilt or shame items - keeps an association from being mistaken for a mechanism.

## The core model

Shame is a negative view of the self, experienced either through one's own eyes (internal shame) or through the imagined eyes of others (external shame). Guilt concerns responsibility for events, and becomes maladaptive specifically when that responsibility is exaggerated beyond what the situation warrants or detached from any real situation at all. [[P21219057 Kim 2011 Shame guilt and depressive symptoms#^p21219057-external|Kim 2011]] [[P21219057 Kim 2011 Shame guilt and depressive symptoms#^p21219057-guilt-variants|Kim 2011]]

The distinction earns its keep in the data. Across 108 studies, shame was more closely tied to depressive symptoms than ordinary guilt was - but that gap closed for maladaptive guilt specifically: exaggerated responsibility for uncontrollable events and free-floating guilt were tied to depression just as closely as shame. External shame showed the largest associations of all. Some of that pattern is an artefact worth naming: depression scales that mention guilt as a symptom item inflate guilt's apparent link to depression, since part of the association is shared wording rather than an independent relationship. [[P21219057 Kim 2011 Shame guilt and depressive symptoms#^p21219057-shame|Kim 2011]] [[P21219057 Kim 2011 Shame guilt and depressive symptoms#^p21219057-external|Kim 2011]] [[P21219057 Kim 2011 Shame guilt and depressive symptoms#^p21219057-caution-content|Appraisal: Kim 2011]]

Guilt has a specific and sizeable literature in trauma. Across 163 studies of adult trauma survivors, trauma-related guilt correlated with PTSD symptoms measured at the same time, and more weakly with symptoms measured later - every symptom cluster was involved, strongest after war-related trauma and weakest after motor-vehicle accidents. The predictive estimate rests on only 19 longitudinal data points, making guilt a reasonable candidate for a factor that helps maintain PTSD rather than an established cause. [[P35781354 Kip 2022 Trauma-related guilt and PTSD#^p35781354-association|Kip 2022]] [[P35781354 Kip 2022 Trauma-related guilt and PTSD#^p35781354-moderators|Kip 2022]]

Autism adds a specific piece: in a survey of trauma-exposed adults, autistic participants reported more severe PTSD symptoms and more negative post-trauma appraisals - especially alienation, shame and fear - and those appraisals statistically accounted for a share of the link between being autistic and having more severe symptoms. Because this is cross-sectional, that mediation is a decomposition of data collected once, not evidence that shame appraisals caused the symptoms. [[P41454656 Prosser 2026 Trauma appraisals in autistic adults#^p41454656-severity|Prosser 2026]] [[P41454656 Prosser 2026 Trauma appraisals in autistic adults#^p41454656-caution-mediation|Appraisal: Prosser 2026]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A summary reports "a study found guilt strongly predicts PTSD," citing the 163-study synthesis on trauma-related guilt.

Ask which comparison the study made. The large, consistent number is a same-time correlation - guilt and PTSD symptoms measured together - supporting "associated," not "predicts later PTSD." The genuinely predictive estimate, guilt measured first and symptoms later, exists but rests on far fewer longitudinal points, and the association there was smaller. The defensible summary keeps the two apart: guilt is robustly associated with PTSD symptoms at the same time point, and a much more thinly evidenced candidate for predicting them later - "strongly predicts" overstates the smaller, longitudinal slice.

## Common confusions

- "Shame and guilt are basically the same emotion." They are separable: shame is a negative self-view; guilt concerns responsibility, tying to depression only when maladaptive.
- "A depression scale that includes guilt items proves guilt causes depression." Part of the association is the scale counting guilt as a symptom, inflating the apparent link.
- "Guilt associated with PTSD symptoms means guilt causes PTSD." The strong evidence is a same-time association; the longitudinal, more cause-like evidence is thinner and smaller.

## What the sources do not establish

Nearly all this evidence is correlational, self-reported and heterogeneous, and the only prospective estimate - guilt predicting later PTSD - is small and limited; the autism finding comes from a single cross-sectional survey and does not show what drives the pattern.

## Answer notes

1. Shame is a negative view of the self, held internally or imagined through others' eyes; guilt concerns responsibility for events, and becomes similarly harmful when that responsibility is exaggerated or detached from the situation.
2. Because the scale itself counts guilt-related content as a symptom of depression, so some of the correlation is shared item content rather than an independent relationship.
3. Because the large, robust finding is a same-time correlation between guilt and PTSD symptoms; the genuinely predictive, later-symptom evidence is much smaller in volume and in effect size.
