# Glial cell types

**Module.** M02 Neurobiology · **Topic.** [[Glial cell types]]

## Why this matters

Popular accounts of almost every condition in this library mention "glia," "neuroinflammation" or "microglial
activation" as if glia were one vague supporting tissue. Knowing which of the four distinct cell families a claim
is actually about turns a hand-wave into a precise statement.

## The core model

The nervous system contains neurons plus astrocytes, oligodendrocytes and their peripheral counterpart Schwann
cells, microglia, and the ependymal cells lining the ventricles. As a group they provide scaffolding, insulation,
nutrient transport, waste handling and immune mediation - a division by cell type, not by function.
[[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]]

Astrocytes contact both synapses and blood vessels, which is the anatomical basis for two roles: buffering the
extracellular ions and transmitters around a synapse, and helping build and maintain the blood-brain barrier.
[[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-support|Blood Brain Barrier]]

Oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells produce myelin, the insulating wrap that speeds conduction. One
oligodendrocyte wraps segments of many central axons, while each Schwann cell wraps a single peripheral segment -
a difference that matters for disease and repair, since losing one oligodendrocyte affects more fibres.
[[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-limit|Nervous Tissue]]

Microglia are the brain's resident immune cells, and direct imaging changed what that means. Watching mouse
cortex under a microscope showed microglial processes constantly extending and retracting in uninjured tissue,
then converging to shield an injury site within minutes.
[[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-surveillance|Microglial surveillance]]
[[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-response|Microglial surveillance]]
That is constant, active surveillance, not a cell that sits idle until summoned.

Ependymal cells line the ventricles and are part of the system that produces, circulates and reabsorbs
cerebrospinal fluid; because the fluid is reabsorbed rather than drained, an obstruction anywhere changes
pressure throughout the system.
[[F30 OpenStax Circulation and the central nervous system#^f30-ventricles|Circulation and the Central Nervous System]]

"Support cell" undersells all four families: astrocytes gate signalling at the barrier and the synapse, microglia
survey and remodel contacts, and oligodendrocytes are themselves responsive to activity. Glia create and maintain
the conditions neurons need to signal, and in several cases take part in signalling directly.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A hypothetical lab notebook describes an hour of live imaging of mouse
cortex, before and after a fine needle briefly touches the tissue. Predict each family's behaviour first:
astrocyte processes near the prick should buffer any local ion and transmitter surge; microglial processes,
already moving continuously at rest, should reorient toward the injury rather than starting to move only once
damage occurs; oligodendrocytes should change little, since myelin maintenance runs on a slower timescale.

The claim to avoid: "the microglia activated when the needle touched the tissue." The accurate one: "the
microglia, already continuously sampling the tissue, redirected their processes toward the injury" - a shift in
an ongoing activity, not a switch from off to on.

## Common confusions

- "Glia are passive support cells." Astrocytes gate the blood-brain barrier; microglia survey and remodel
  contacts continuously.
- "Microglia sit idle until an injury occurs." Live imaging shows constant movement in undamaged tissue.
- "One glial cell of any type serves one axon." True for Schwann cells, false for oligodendrocytes.

## What the sources do not establish

The microglial surveillance evidence comes from mouse cortex, not human brain. How much human synaptic signalling
depends on astrocyte activity remains contested, and microglial states are increasingly described as graded
rather than a simple resting-versus-activated switch.

2. What did live imaging show about microglia in undamaged tissue, and why does that matter?
3. Why does the oligodendrocyte-versus-Schwann-cell difference matter for injury?

## Answer notes

1. Astrocytes (barrier and buffering), oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells (myelin), microglia (surveillance),
   ependymal cells (cerebrospinal fluid).
2. Their processes moved and sampled continuously even without injury, reframing microglia as active
   surveillants rather than cells that switch on only when damage occurs.
3. One oligodendrocyte myelinates segments of several axons, so its loss affects more fibres than the loss of a
   single Schwann cell, which serves only one segment.
