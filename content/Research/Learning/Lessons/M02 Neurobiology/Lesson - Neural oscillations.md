# Neural oscillations

**Module.** M02 Neurobiology · **Topic.** [[Neural oscillations]]

## Why this matters

A "raised theta/beta ratio" or "abnormal beta power" is exactly the kind of finding quoted as proof of a
diagnosis. Both examples below are far weaker as diagnostic evidence than the headline version suggests, which is
why oscillation claims need a reading rule before they are accepted.

## The core model

Neural oscillations are rhythmic fluctuations in the electrical activity of neuron populations, recorded as
extracellular fields in which synaptic currents are the largest contributor.
[[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-synaptic|Buzsáki 2012]]
Scalp EEG measures these rhythms with millisecond timing but little spatial detail, reported in bands from delta
through gamma.
[[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-eeg|Brain imaging]]
A rhythm reaching the scalp reflects summed, time-aligned synaptic activity across a large population, not a
direct readout of one region's spiking, and different circuits can produce the same rhythm; EEG trades spatial
precision for timing, so a disagreement with imaging is not automatically an error in either.
[[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-sources|Buzsáki 2012]]
[[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-caution-scalp|Appraisal: Buzsáki 2012]]

In ADHD, nine studies of 6-to-18-year-olds produced pooled effect sizes for a raised theta/beta ratio of 0.75 and
0.62 across two age bands, but heterogeneity was significant and the authors treat both as overestimates. The
difference also shrank across a decade of studies as the ratio rose in comparison groups, so the authors conclude
it cannot reliably diagnose ADHD, though a deviant subgroup may carry prognostic value.
[[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-effect|Arns 2013]]
[[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-drift|Arns 2013]]
[[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-diagnosis|Arns 2013]]
Training EEG rhythms fares no better: with probably blinded ratings, neurofeedback did not significantly reduce
ADHD symptoms across 20 trials.
[[P39661381 Westwood 2025 Neurofeedback with blinded outcomes#^p39661381-blinded|Westwood 2025]]

In bipolar disorder, across 20 quantitative EEG studies with 475 patients, raised beta and gamma activity was the
most consistent finding, alpha and theta changed direction with region and mood state, and delta changes were
more marked in bipolar I. Power fell with treatment, and quantitative EEG reportedly separated bipolar disorder
from depression and schizophrenia - but these were small, varied studies that cannot establish a diagnostic
signature alone.
[[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-bands|Bahadori 2026]]
[[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-treatment|Bahadori 2026]]
[[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-caution-diagnostic|Appraisal: Bahadori 2026]]

The reading rule: name the band and its definition, the electrodes, eyes open or closed, state and medication,
and whether the result is a power, a ratio, or a sleep-stage measure - none of that is a diagnostic marker on its
own.
[[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-caution-marker|Appraisal: Arns 2013]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A fictional article states: "A new EEG study found higher beta-band power
in adults with bipolar disorder, proving the condition is caused by overactive brain rhythms." Which band, and
eyes open or closed? Was mood state recorded, given alpha and theta findings change direction with mood and power
falls with treatment? Is "higher" a group average with overlapping distributions?

The defensible rewrite: "higher average beta-band power was found in a group of adults with bipolar disorder;
this is a group-level association, not proof of cause, and does not identify which circuit produced it."

## Common confusions

- "A raised theta/beta ratio diagnoses ADHD." The difference has shrunk across studies and cannot reliably
  diagnose.
- "Neurofeedback is well-established." Blinded-outcome trials found no meaningful benefit.
- "A band-power difference identifies the responsible circuit." It is a summed, indirect measure.

## What the sources do not establish

That EEG mainly reflects synaptic currents is the best-established layer here. Condition findings are weaker: the
ADHD ratio is heterogeneous and does not diagnose; neurofeedback shows no benefit on blinded outcomes; the
bipolar findings come from small, varied studies without a pooled estimate.

1. Mainly summed synaptic currents of a large population, several steps removed from single-neuron firing.
2. The group difference is heterogeneous, has shrunk across studies, and cannot reliably diagnose.
3. Any three of: band and definition, electrodes, eyes open/closed, state, medication, power/ratio/sleep-stage.
