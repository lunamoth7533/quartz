# Adult neurogenesis

**Module.** M02 Neurobiology · **Topic.** [[Adult neurogenesis]]

## Why this matters

Few claims in popular neuroscience are repeated as confidently as "you can grow new brain cells." Whether adult
humans do this in any meaningful amount is a live, published scientific disagreement, and the honest way to teach
it is to show both sides rather than pick a winner the evidence has not settled.

## The core model

Adult neurogenesis is the production of new neurons after development. Its best-studied site is the hippocampal
dentate gyrus, where rodents generate new neurons throughout life; whether adult humans do is disputed.
[[P33762406 Moreno-Jiménez 2021 Evidence for adult hippocampal neurogenesis#^p33762406-background|Moreno-Jiménez 2021]]
[[P33762407 Sorrells 2021 Few if any new adult hippocampal neurons#^p33762407-decline|Sorrells 2021]]
The dentate gyrus is the hippocampal circuit's entry point from entorhinal cortex - why both sides of the debate
focus on it.
[[F61 Neuroscience Online hippocampus#^f61-circuit|Hippocampus]]

Because direct evidence is hard to obtain in living people, the debate turns on post-mortem markers. One group
reads its data as showing neurogenesis continuing into old age, falling in Alzheimer's disease; another finds
progenitors and immature-neuron markers easily in infants, rarely in adults.
[[P33762406 Moreno-Jiménez 2021 Evidence for adult hippocampal neurogenesis#^p33762406-persistence|Moreno-Jiménez 2021]]
[[P33762407 Sorrells 2021 Few if any new adult hippocampal neurons#^p33762407-decline|Sorrells 2021]]

Neither argument settles it. Proponents argue tissue processing can suppress the markers until undetectable.
[[P33762406 Moreno-Jiménez 2021 Evidence for adult hippocampal neurogenesis#^p33762406-methods|Moreno-Jiménez 2021]]
Sceptics reply that the marker mostly looks mature in adults, also labels mature neurons elsewhere, needs no
unusual processing to detect, and that adults show no zone of dividing precursors - and call for direct imaging
or birth-dating methods to resolve it.
[[P33762407 Sorrells 2021 Few if any new adult hippocampal neurons#^p33762407-markers|Sorrells 2021]]
[[P33762407 Sorrells 2021 Few if any new adult hippocampal neurons#^p33762407-rnaseq|Sorrells 2021]]

A related idea is the neurogenic hypothesis of antidepressant action: prolonged antidepressant treatment increases
new-neuron birth in the adult rodent hippocampus, and animal studies where neurogenesis is blocked suggest it may
be needed for some antidepressant effects - answering a real puzzle, since the classic monoamine account fits the
weeks-long clinical delay poorly.
[[P33771348 Malberg 2021 Adult neurogenesis and antidepressant treatment#^p33771348-increase|Malberg 2021]]
[[P33771348 Malberg 2021 Adult neurogenesis and antidepressant treatment#^p33771348-ablation|Malberg 2021]]
[[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|How do antidepressants work?]]
But necessity evidence comes from animal ablation, while the human side rests on post-mortem counts of disputed
sensitivity.
[[P33771348 Malberg 2021 Adult neurogenesis and antidepressant treatment#^p33771348-caution-translation|Appraisal: Malberg 2021]]

Keep four claims separate: whether adult humans make new hippocampal neurons, how many, whether any matter for
behaviour, and whether antidepressants work through them - disputed, unknown, and animal-based, respectively.
[[P33762407 Sorrells 2021 Few if any new adult hippocampal neurons#^p33762407-caution-absence|Appraisal: Sorrells 2021]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A fictional advertisement claims: "Our product boosts adult neurogenesis,
so it will lift your mood." Whether adult humans generate meaningful new hippocampal neurons at all is itself
unresolved. Even the strongest evidence linking neurogenesis to mood is animal ablation work, not evidence that
increasing it above baseline improves mood in people.

The defensible rewrite: "whether adult humans meaningfully replace hippocampal neurons is disputed, and animal
studies link neurogenesis to some antidepressant effects rather than showing it causes a mood benefit."

## Common confusions

- "Scientists have settled that adult humans don't make new neurons." An ongoing disagreement, not a resolved
  negative.
- "Antidepressants are established to work by growing new brain cells." A hypothesis, mainly from animal
  ablation studies.
- "A marker of 'young neurons' is unambiguous." Sceptics argue it also labels mature neurons elsewhere.

## What the sources do not establish

Lifelong rodent hippocampal neurogenesis, and its rise with antidepressant treatment, are experimental findings.
In humans the question is unresolved, argued in reviews each presenting one side of a published debate. The link
to mood is a hypothesis from preclinical ablation work, not a demonstrated human mechanism.

2. What is the neurogenic hypothesis of antidepressant action, and what is its strongest supporting evidence?
3. Name the four claims to keep separate when reading a statement about adult neurogenesis and mood.

## Answer notes

1. One side argues neurogenesis persists and tissue processing explains negative findings; the other argues
   markers of new neurons are rare in adults. Both rest on post-mortem tissue read differently.
2. That antidepressants work partly by increasing new-neuron birth; its strongest evidence is animal studies
   where blocking neurogenesis reduced some antidepressant effects.
3. Whether new neurons form; how many; whether any affect behaviour; whether antidepressants work through them.
