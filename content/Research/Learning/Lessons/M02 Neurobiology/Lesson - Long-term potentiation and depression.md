# Long-term potentiation and depression

**Module.** M02 Neurobiology · **Topic.** [[Long-term potentiation and depression]]

## Why this matters

LTP and LTD are the leading cellular candidates for how experience changes circuits. They are also routinely
over-claimed: a plasticity-like signal in a human brain becomes "the mechanism of memory" in a single step.
This lesson gives you the evidence and the guardrail.

## The core model

Long-term potentiation is an activity-dependent, persistent increase in synaptic strength; long-term depression
is its complementary decrease. Both are treated as cellular models of learning-related change.
[[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-definition|Bliss and Cooke 2011]]

What makes them credible candidates is a set of properties: they last, they are input-specific, so one set of
synapses on a cell can change without its neighbours, and they are associative, so weak input can be
potentiated when paired with strong input.
[[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-properties|Bliss and Cooke 2011]]

Polarity depends on calcium entry and on the balance of kinase and phosphatase activity; persistent activation
recruits gene expression and new protein synthesis for the durable forms.
[[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-mechanism|Bliss and Cooke 2011]]

Timing is one of the cleanest findings: a presynaptic spike followed by a postsynaptic spike within roughly
50 ms favours LTP, while the reverse order favours LTD.
[[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-timing|Bliss and Cooke 2011]]

Previous activity also matters: metaplasticity describes how a synapse's history changes its response to the
next induction protocol.
[[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-metaplasticity|Bliss and Cooke 2011]]

The limit is where this lesson earns its place. Most mechanistic evidence comes from reduced preparations such
as hippocampal slices. Non-invasive stimulation in humans produces LTP-like or LTD-like changes, and the review
says it remains important to show that these effects have the same defining features.
[[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Bliss and Cooke 2011]]
So a change in a slice constrains what a memory mechanism could look like; it does not show what a person
learned.
[[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-caution-inference|AI synthesis: Bliss and Cooke 2011]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A study reports that a non-invasive stimulation protocol increases a
measured plasticity-like response in motor cortex, and a press release calls it "proof that the brain learns
through LTP".

Test the claim against the properties. Did the measurement show persistence (minutes, hours) rather than a
transient change? Did it show input specificity, or a global change in excitability? Did it show associativity?
Was there a behavioural memory whose retention timescale matched the physiological change, and was the
physiological measure taken from the relevant circuit?

If the answers are "partly, unknown, no, no", the accurate sentence is: "the protocol changed a plasticity-like
measure in motor cortex; whether the same mechanism mediates a specific memory has not been shown." That is not
scepticism for its own sake; it is the difference between a mechanism under study and a finished explanation.

## Common confusions

- "LTP is memory." LTP is a family of synaptic phenomena that satisfy criteria proposed for a memory mechanism.
- "More LTP is better learning." Metaplasticity and saturation mean the relationship is not monotonic in either
  direction.
- "Human studies cannot say anything." They can constrain timing and location; they simply have to demonstrate
  the defining features before claiming identity with the animal mechanism.

## What the sources do not establish

The review is a 2011 narrative overview, so later work is not included, and it reports no human behavioural
outcome linked to a specific synaptic change. This vault therefore teaches LTP and LTD as mechanisms with
strong animal evidence and open human translation, and the coverage matrix records the matching question.

## Check yourself

1. State the three properties that make LTP a candidate memory mechanism.
2. What determines whether a synapse potentiates or depresses after a pairing?
3. Why is a plasticity-like human measurement not sufficient evidence for a learning mechanism?
4. What would you need to see to connect an LTP-like change to a specific memory?

## Answer notes

1. Longevity, input specificity and associativity.
2. Calcium entry and the balance of kinase and phosphatase activity, shaped by spike timing and prior activity.
3. Because the measurement may not have the defining properties — persistence, input specificity, associativity
   — and may not be taken from the circuit that encodes the memory.
4. A physiological change with the defining properties in the relevant circuit, plus a behavioural memory whose
   time course and specificity match it.
