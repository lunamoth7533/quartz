# Circuit development and homeostasis

**Module.** M02 Neurobiology · **Topic.** [[Circuit development and homeostasis]]

## Why this matters

"Critical period" and "use it or lose it" are two of the most borrowed phrases in popular developmental science,
and both get flattened. This lesson gives the mechanism behind each, so a timing claim can be checked against
what actually opens and closes a window.

## The core model

Brain circuit development runs through proliferation, migration, differentiation, synapse formation and
experience-dependent refinement. These overlap across regions rather than completing one after another, which is
why the same intervention can work differently depending on when it happens.
[[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-sequence|Normal development of brain circuits]]
[[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]]

Growth and loss happen together: synapses are overproduced and then eliminated, leaving - at the neuromuscular
junction - fewer inputs per muscle fibre but greater strength in survivors, the clearest illustration that
pruning builds a better-matched circuit rather than signalling failure.
[[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-pruning|Normal development of brain circuits]]
[[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]

Which inputs survive is decided by activity-dependent competition, favouring active inputs, and direct
competition, favouring axons that capture more trophic support - both at once, which is why "use it or lose it"
is directionally right but mechanistically incomplete.
[[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-competition|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]
[[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-limit|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]

Sensitive windows have identifiable triggers, not just convenient ages. In visual cortex, activity-dependent
development is triggered by maturation of local inhibitory connections, and structural consolidation involves
reorganisation of the extracellular matrix during the critical period.
[[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]]
[[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-consolidation|Critical period plasticity]]

Different tracts mature on different timetables, so there is no single "finished" age - myelination, for example,
continues past the point when synapse numbers have already peaked.
[[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]]
[[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]]

"Homeostasis" refers to negative-feedback adjustments keeping activity within range while refinement continues,
counterbalancing the runaway strengthening that rules like long-term potentiation would otherwise produce alone.
Early experience also interacts with genetically guided development rather than simply triggering it, so
variation in outcome is expected, not an anomaly.
[[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-lasting|Synaptic Plasticity]]
[[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Long-term potentiation and depression]]
[[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A hypothetical animal study reports that a drug temporarily reducing
local inhibition in adult visual cortex reopened a sensitive-period-like state, partly restoring function
impaired by early deprivation. This suggests reactivating one component of the machinery can shift an adult
circuit toward plasticity, in that species and pathway - it does not show the effect is safe, generalises, lasts,
or works the same way in humans.

The defensible statement: "this is evidence for a mechanism worth studying, not evidence of a treatment ready for
people."

## Common confusions

- "Use it or lose it fully explains which synapses survive." Direct trophic competition runs alongside activity.
- "A critical period is just an age range." It is a window tied to a specific trigger, not a calendar date.
- "The brain finishes developing at one age." Different tracts mature on different schedules.

## What the sources do not establish

Sensitive periods are established mainly in animal models allowing direct manipulation; human evidence comes from
imaging trajectories and clinical observation. Whether sensitive-period mechanisms can be safely reopened in
humans is a research question, not an established treatment.
[[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-reactivation|Critical period plasticity]]

## Check yourself

1. Why is "use it or lose it" an incomplete description of how synapses are selected during development?
2. What makes a sensitive period a window with "identifiable components" rather than just an age range?
3. Why does circuit development need homeostatic mechanisms alongside activity-dependent strengthening?

## Answer notes

1. Direct competition for trophic support also decides which inputs survive, alongside activity.
2. It opens and closes with specific biological events, such as inhibitory-circuit maturation, not a fixed age.
3. Activity-dependent strengthening alone would produce runaway activity increases; homeostasis keeps the
   circuit in range.
