# Genes, environment and polygenic risk

**Module.** M02 Neurobiology · **Topic.** [[Genes, environment and polygenic risk]]

## Why this matters

Genetics is the most common place where population statistics get mistaken for personal destiny, and also a
place where real biology gets dismissed as "just statistics". This lesson keeps both: genes carry functional
variation, and the findings are distributional.

## The core model

Genes are expressed in interaction with environment and experience; a genotype is a set of instructions read
differently in different conditions rather than a fixed destiny.
[[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-expression|Human Genetics]]

Heritability estimates describe how much of the variation in a population is associated with genetic
differences, not how strongly genes determine one person.
[[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-heritability|Human Genetics]]

For common conditions, the genetic contribution is polygenic: many variants of small individual effect. The
bipolar genomic analysis identified 298 loci and 36 credible genes, most with small effects, and the authors
describe the architecture as statistical.
[[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-loci|Bipolar disorder genomics]]

Variation can also carry larger individual effects when it is rare. Autism exome sequencing identified 102 risk
genes, enriched in gene regulation and neuronal development, with different subgroups of genes enriched in
developmental delay and in autism.
[[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-genes|Autism exome sequencing study]];
[[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-subgroups|Autism exome sequencing study]]

Notice what that buys and what it does not. It buys mechanistic leads about development and signalling. It does
not say that a person with one of those variants will have a particular profile, because the same variant
appears across different traits and the same trait appears across different variants.

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Someone with a family history of a condition asks: "What does
heritability of 60 to 80 percent mean for me?"

Translate carefully. The number describes the share of trait variation in the studied population that is
associated with genetic differences under those environmental conditions. It is not a personal probability,
and it does not mean that 60 to 80 percent of the person's outcome is fixed. Family history does change risk,
but the translation from a population parameter to a personal expectation requires a validated risk model,
which is a different piece of evidence.

The accurate answer: "In the populations studied, genetic differences account for a large share of the
variation in this condition. That tells you genetic influences matter at population scale; it does not tell
you what will happen for one person."

## Common confusions

- "Heritability is about individuals." It is a variance decomposition in a population.
- "A risk gene causes the condition." Rare variants can carry larger effects, but they still act probabilistically
  and interact with other variation and environment.
- "Polygenic risk means it is not biology." Population-level statistics are the signature of many small
  biological effects; statistics and mechanism describe the same biology at different resolutions.

## What the sources do not establish

The genetics teaching source is introductory, and the two research sources describe association architecture
rather than causal pathways or clinical prediction. Nothing in this vault supports interpreting a personal
genetic result, and no source here reports a validated decision tool.

## Check yourself

1. Why does heritability not answer a personal question?
2. What is the difference between a common-variant polygenic finding and a rare-variant finding?
3. Rewrite "this gene causes autism" as an accurate statement.

## Answer notes

1. Because it partitions variance in a population under particular conditions, and a personal outcome depends
   on that person's whole genotype, environment and chance.
2. Common-variant findings are many small effects detectable across the genome; rare variants can carry larger
   individual effects but are individually scarce and still probabilistic.
3. Something like: "variants in this gene are associated with increased population-level risk for autism in the
   studied samples; they do not determine any individual's traits or support needs."
