# Meta-analysis and review limits

**Module.** M01 Research Methods and Evidence Literacy · **Topic.** [[Meta-analysis and review limits]]

## Why this matters

A pooled estimate is the most persuasive number in the room. It is also a number assembled by people making
dozens of decisions, from studies that may themselves be biased. This lesson is about reading a review as a
piece of research rather than as a verdict.

## The core model

Review production has grown faster than the underlying evidence. The number of systematic reviews and
meta-analyses has escalated sharply, many are redundant, and overlapping meta-analyses of the same questions
are documented.
[[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-volume|Mass production of systematic reviews]];
[[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-redundancy|Mass production of systematic reviews]]

Conflicts of interest are part of the picture: reviews are often produced by authors with industry ties, and
the essay reports that results tend to align with sponsor interests where studied.
[[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-conflict|Mass production of systematic reviews]]

Misleading conclusions can be inherited from flawed included studies or created by review-level choices that
ignore bias.
[[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-misleading|Mass production of systematic reviews]]

Cochrane methods respond to this by requiring an explicit certainty rating per outcome, weighing risk of bias,
imprecision, inconsistency, indirectness and publication bias, and by telling authors not to reduce results to
significance thresholds.
[[F22 Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions#^f22-certainty|Cochrane Handbook]]

A local example shows what the caution looks like in practice: a meta-analysis of psychological interventions
for complex trauma reports some indications of publication bias in its own evidence base and finds lower
effects in participants with childhood trauma.
[[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]

The summary rule: a review is a method with a paper trail. Ask which studies were eligible, how they were
appraised, what was pre-specified, who funded and wrote it, and what the certainty rating is.
[[P27620683 Ioannidis 2016 Mass production of systematic reviews#^p27620683-caution-use|AI synthesis: Mass production of systematic reviews]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. Two meta-analyses of the same therapy question appear a year apart. The
first includes 22 trials and reports a moderate pooled effect with low certainty; the second includes 30 trials
and reports a smaller pooled effect with moderate certainty.

Do not simply prefer the newer or bigger review. Check overlap: are the 30 trials the 22 plus eight more, or a
different selection? Check the protocol: were outcomes, subgroups and exclusion rules pre-specified, and do
they differ? Check what changed: the added trials may be larger, better blinded or from different populations.
Check funding and authorship. Check whether certainty improved because of the new evidence or because the
second team was more generous in its ratings.

A fair conclusion: the second review adds evidence and its estimate is smaller; the difference is itself data
about the question, and the certainty ratings tell you how much to lean on either number.

## Common confusions

- "Meta-analysis corrects the literature." It summarises the literature and inherits its problems.
- "More studies means better." More of the same biased studies can narrow an interval around the wrong answer.
- "A null pooled result means no effect." It may mean heterogeneous studies, insensitive outcomes or too
  little evidence; the certainty rating conveys which.

## What the sources do not establish

The critique essay is a synthesis of meta-research, not a measurement of the frequency of misleading reviews in
any specific field, and its literature base ends in 2016. Nothing here tells you that any particular review in
this vault is or is not sound; that judgement requires opening it.

## Check yourself

1. Name three review-level decisions that change a pooled estimate.
2. Why does an industry-funded review require more scrutiny rather than automatic rejection?
3. What does a certainty rating add to a pooled effect?
4. If two reviews disagree, what is your first question?

## Answer notes

1. Inclusion criteria, handling of risk of bias, choice of outcome and time point, and the analysis model.
2. Funding is a risk factor for alignment with sponsor interests, not proof; you check the methods, but you do
   not discard the data.
3. A structured statement of how much confidence the estimate supports, given identified threats.
4. Whether they were answering the same question about the same studies and outcomes.
