# Psychophysiology methods

**Module.** M01 Research Methods and Evidence Literacy · **Topic.** [[Psychophysiology methods]]

## Why this matters

A heart-rate monitor, a skin-conductance sensor and a cortisol sample all promise a number that
seems to come straight from the body. That appeal is also the trap: every signal sits downstream
of several overlapping control systems, and turning it into a claim about "stress" takes a chain
of assumptions that is easy to skip.

## The core model

Psychophysiology records body signals continuously without needing self-report, which is what
makes these measures attractive - and exactly why they are shaped by more than the one thing a
researcher wants to study.
[[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-metrics|Heart rate variability metrics]]
Different signals index different layers: skin conductance tracks sweat-gland activity; heart rate
and its variability reflect respiratory, reflex and autonomic influences; pupil size responds to
light as much as arousal; cortisol reflects a slower hormonal system. Because these layers are
related but not identical, two signals disagreeing is not automatically a mistake - the layers may
simply be doing different things at that moment.
[[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-metrics|Heart rate variability metrics]]

Heart rate variability shows how much context one measure needs: its metrics split into families
with different recording needs, typical values shift with age and posture, and one commonly
reported ratio is disputed because breathing rate confounds it.
[[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-lfhf|Heart rate variability metrics]]
Recording conditions are not incidental - time of day, caffeine, movement and medication can shift
a signal by an amount comparable to the effect under study, so a claim omitting them cannot be
evaluated.
[[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-confounds|Heart rate variability metrics]]

Internal signals are also not only something researchers measure from outside - they are
information the nervous system itself uses. Signals from internal organs travel to the brain,
arriving first in one part of the insular cortex, proposed as part of how the brain builds a sense
of the body's own state - a model supported by converging evidence, not something a sensor reads
off directly.
[[P12965300 Craig 2003 Interoception#^p12965300-system|Interoception]]
[[P12965300 Craig 2003 Interoception#^p12965300-insula|Interoception]] Moving from any signal to a
psychological construct takes a chain of assumptions, and each link deserves to be stated rather
than assumed.
[[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-limit|Heart rate variability metrics]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A study measures heart rate variability before and after
a ten-minute breathing exercise, finds it increased, and concludes the exercise "reduced stress."
Check recording conditions first: posture, time of day and caffeine intake could each shift the
numbers by as much as the reported effect. Check the layer measured: variability reflects
respiratory as well as autonomic influences, and the exercise directly changes breathing pattern, a
known confound for this exact measure. Check the inferential leap: even a genuine change in one
signal is not a direct readout of "stress."

The defensible conclusion is narrower: variability changed under controlled conditions; whether
that reflects reduced stress specifically, rather than the direct respiratory effect of controlled
breathing, is a separate question this measure alone cannot answer.

## Common confusions

- "Heart rate variability is a direct readout of stress or calm." It is a signal shaped by
  breathing, posture and medication, several steps from any one psychological state.
- "One signal represents the whole stress response." Skin conductance, heart measures, cortisol
  and pupil size index different, only partly overlapping systems.
- "A physiological measure needs no context to interpret." Recording conditions can shift these
  signals by as much as the effect under study.

## What the sources do not establish

Composite indices are often treated as single, clean constructs without validation in the specific
population studied, and a single short recording cannot separate the influences layered into it.
The interoception model is a synthesis of converging evidence, not something one measurement
confirms directly.

## Check yourself

1. Why can two physiological signals disagree without either one being wrong?
2. Name three factors that can shift heart rate variability independent of any psychological
   state.
3. Why is a breathing exercise a particularly tricky intervention to study using heart rate
   variability?
4. What chain of reasoning is required to move from a physiological signal to a claim about a
   psychological construct?

## Answer notes

1. Each signal indexes a different, only partly overlapping physiological layer, so they can
   genuinely diverge without error.
2. Recording length, posture and time of day, plus caffeine, medication or recent movement.
3. Heart rate variability is itself shaped by breathing pattern, which the exercise directly
   changes.
4. Naming the signal, the recording conditions, the physiological layer, and the assumption
   linking that layer to the construct claimed.
