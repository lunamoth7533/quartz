# Measurement validity and reliability

**Module.** M01 Research Methods and Evidence Literacy · **Topic.** [[Measurement validity and reliability]]

## Why this matters

Every claim in this vault ultimately rests on someone having measured something. Measurement is not a
formality that happens before the science; it is the part of the science that decides what the numbers can
mean. This lesson gives you a checklist that works on any scale you meet.

## The core model

Scale development runs through three phases and nine steps: generating items and checking their content
validity; constructing the scale through pre-testing, sampling, item reduction and dimensionality checks; and
evaluating the result with reliability and validity testing.
[[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-phases|Developing and Validating Scales]]

Content validity comes first, before administration, when the items are judged against the domain they claim
to cover.
[[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-content|Developing and Validating Scales]]

Reliability statistics such as Cronbach's alpha, ordinal alpha, McDonald's omega and test-retest coefficients
answer whether scores are consistent; each carries assumptions about the structure of the items.
[[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-reliability|Developing and Validating Scales]]

Validity comes later and comes in families: criterion validity and construct validity, each evaluated through
relationships with other measures and groups.
[[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-validity|Developing and Validating Scales]]

The distinction earns its keep in clinical research. In the ADHD literature, clinician-rated and self-rated
outcomes can disagree, which makes the rater part of the measurement rather than a neutral observer.
[[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI synthesis: CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

So the working rule: reliable is not valid, valid is not universal, and a score always carries its population,
purpose and rater.
[[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-caution-score|AI synthesis: Developing and Validating Scales]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A study uses a new 12-item "emotional regulation" scale in adults with
ADHD, reports a Cronbach's alpha of 0.89, and concludes that emotional regulation improved with treatment.

Audit it in order. Content: were the items generated from a defined domain and reviewed, and did they cover
regulation without mixing in adjacent constructs? Structure: did the authors test dimensionality rather than
assuming one factor? Reliability: alpha 0.89 tells you the items co-vary, not what they measure. Validity: what
criterion or construct evidence links the score to regulation, and in which population? Transfer: was it
validated in adults with ADHD, or borrowed from another group? Rater: self-report or informant?

The revised conclusion: "In this sample, a 12-item self-report scale showed internal consistency and changed
over treatment; whether the change represents emotional regulation, and whether it transfers beyond this
sample, depends on validity evidence the study reports (or does not)."

## Common confusions

- "High alpha means a good scale." It means the items hang together; a scale can be internally consistent and
  measure the wrong thing.
- "Validated once, valid everywhere." Validity is population- and purpose-specific.
- "Objective measures have no rater." Every measure has a protocol and a person applying it; the question is
  how much their judgement enters the number.

## What the sources do not establish

The primer gives conventions, not a universal threshold, and it does not evaluate any specific instrument used
in this vault's clinical sources. This library therefore treats rating scales as reports with known limits
rather than as direct readouts of mechanisms.

## Check yourself

1. Distinguish reliability from validity in one sentence each.
2. Why is content validity assessed before the scale is administered?
3. Name three validity families and what each compares the score against.
4. A trial reports improvement on a self-report scale but not on a clinician-rated scale. What do you record?

## Answer notes

1. Reliability: consistency of scores. Validity: whether the scores support the interpretation claimed for
   them in a defined population and purpose.
2. Because it concerns whether the items cover the intended domain at all; no amount of later statistics
   repairs items that miss the construct.
3. Content validity (whether the items cover the intended domain at all), criterion validity (comparison with a
   criterion or prediction), and construct validity (relationships with related and unrelated constructs,
   including known-groups comparisons).
4. Both results with their raters, and the conclusion that the measured change depends on who reported it.
