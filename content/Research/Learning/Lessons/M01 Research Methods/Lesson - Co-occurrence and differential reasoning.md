# Co-occurrence and differential reasoning

**Module.** M01 Research Methods and Evidence Literacy · **Topic.** [[Co-occurrence and differential reasoning]]

## Why this matters

Clinical categories are useful simplifications, and real presentations do not respect their boundaries. Two
errors follow: forcing one label when more than one applies, and treating overlap as proof that the categories
are meaningless. This lesson teaches the middle path, using autism and ADHD as the worked case.

## The core model

ADHD and autism frequently co-occur. They share some phenotypic features while remaining distinct diagnostic
categories with separate criteria.
[[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-definition|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

The history matters for reading older research. The earlier diagnostic system treated autism as an exclusion
for an ADHD diagnosis, which limited research on the overlap; the revision allows a comorbid diagnosis.
[[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-history|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

Family and twin evidence reviewed there suggests partly shared familial and genetic contributions, which is one
reason the overlap is not simply a measurement artefact.
[[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-shared|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

Co-occurrence also carries functional weight: registry data cited in the review associate it with lower quality
of life and poorer adaptive functioning than either condition alone.
[[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-frequency|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

Two cautions keep this reasoning honest. First, co-occurrence is a group-level, definition-dependent
observation: it does not establish that one condition causes the other.
[[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-caution-label|AI synthesis: Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]
Second, definitions themselves are constructs: current ICD-11 and DSM-5 categories are symptom-based, and
trauma type or duration alone does not determine a diagnosis.
[[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

For ADHD specifically, how to define and measure functional impairment remains an open question, which means
the boundary of "impairing" is itself under discussion.
[[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults review]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

This scenario is invented for practice. A 9-year-old is referred for attention difficulties. The assessment
finds social-communication differences that began early, repetitive routines, and clear hyperactivity and
inattention in two settings. Teachers say the inattention is the main problem; parents describe both.

Reason in three steps. First, hold both hypotheses: the pattern could meet criteria for both conditions, and
the earlier exclusion rule no longer forbids that. Second, test the pattern against each set of criteria and
ask which features are shared. Third, ask what each hypothesis changes about support: attention difficulties
may need environmental and instructional adjustment, while social-communication differences may need different
support, and the combination is associated in the evidence with greater functional difficulty.

The unreasonable moves are the two extremes: forcing a single label because the referral mentions attention,
or treating the overlap as evidence that the categories should be collapsed. The evidence supports reasoning
about both, with explicit uncertainty about which features drive which difficulties.

## Common confusions

- "Overlap means the diagnoses are wrong." Overlap is expected when conditions share risk factors and when
  categories are defined by symptom patterns rather than causes.
- "Co-occurrence proves a shared cause." Shared familial and genetic contributions are evidence for partly
  shared risk, not proof of a single mechanism.
- "Differential reasoning means picking one." It means testing alternatives and often reporting more than one.

## What the sources do not establish

The co-occurrence review is narrative, emphasises preschool data and cites registry samples that vary in
definition and informant. Nothing in this vault provides prevalence estimates that can be applied to a
particular clinic or country, and no source here supports lay diagnosis of either condition.

## Answer notes

1. Because children with both presentations were excluded from ADHD samples, so the co-occurring group was
   under-studied and its treatment needs were invisible to that literature.
2. Family and twin findings showing partly shared familial and genetic contributions, as reviewed by Leitner.
3. The percentage changes with the sample, the informant and the instrument, so it describes a defined
   measurement rather than a fixed property of nature.
