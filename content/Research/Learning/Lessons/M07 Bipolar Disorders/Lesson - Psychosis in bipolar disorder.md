# Psychosis in bipolar disorder

**Module.** M07 Bipolar Disorders · **Topic.** [[Psychosis in bipolar disorder]]

## Why this matters

Psychosis inside a bipolar episode sits at a contested boundary with schizophrenia. Learning how that boundary is drawn, and what "mood-incongruent" content does and does not imply, stops a delusion's content alone from being read as proof of a different illness.

## The core model

Psychotic features, most often delusions, occur within manic or depressive episodes and are most frequent in bipolar I and during mania; across a meta-analysis of 285 studies, delusions outnumbered hallucinations and thought disorder. [[P41847966 Shao 2026 Psychotic symptoms in bipolar disorder#^p41847966-type|Shao 2026]] Content is mood-congruent when it fits the prevailing mood (grandiosity during mania, guilt during depression) and mood-incongruent when it does not. Broadly defined mood-incongruent psychosis was common in that pooled literature, at about 47% of bipolar samples - but a co-occurring schizophrenia diagnosis was uncommon, at about 8%. [[P41847966 Shao 2026 Psychotic symptoms in bipolar disorder#^p41847966-incongruent|Shao 2026]] [[P41847966 Shao 2026 Psychotic symptoms in bipolar disorder#^p41847966-schizophrenia|Shao 2026]] Because the pooled studies mixed classification systems and definitions of incongruence, 47% signals "common," not a precise rate. [[P41847966 Shao 2026 Psychotic symptoms in bipolar disorder#^p41847966-caution-definitions|Appraisal: Shao 2026]]

Genetics traces a gradient rather than a wall. In 4436 UK bipolar cases, polygenic liability for schizophrenia was highest in schizophrenia itself, then schizoaffective bipolar disorder and bipolar I, with no clear association for bipolar II; within bipolar disorder, liability rose from no psychosis to mood-congruent to mood-incongruent features. [[P29167880 Allardyce 2018 Schizophrenia polygenic liability and mood-incongruent psychosis#^p29167880-gradient|Allardyce 2018]] [[P29167880 Allardyce 2018 Schizophrenia polygenic liability and mood-incongruent psychosis#^p29167880-incongruent|Allardyce 2018]] A shift this size describes populations; it cannot assign one person a diagnosis. [[P29167880 Allardyce 2018 Schizophrenia polygenic liability and mood-incongruent psychosis#^p29167880-caution-scores|Appraisal: Allardyce 2018]]

DSM-5 draws its boundary over the whole illness course, not from content alone. Psychosis confined to mood episodes keeps the diagnosis a mood disorder with psychotic features. Two weeks or more of psychosis without prominent mood symptoms points toward schizoaffective disorder or schizophrenia, which also requires full mood episodes for most of the illness since psychosis began. [[P23707642 Malaspina 2013 Schizoaffective disorder in DSM-5#^p23707642-rule|Malaspina 2013]] [[P23707642 Malaspina 2013 Schizoaffective disorder in DSM-5#^p23707642-lifecourse|Malaspina 2013]] This replaced a DSM-IV category that was unreliable and over-used. [[P23707642 Malaspina 2013 Schizoaffective disorder in DSM-5#^p23707642-problem|Malaspina 2013]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

A hypothetical case write-up describes a patient with an elaborate grandiose delusion - claiming ownership of a country - during a week of clearly manic mood, with no psychosis before or after. Does the delusion's extremity suggest schizophrenia? Applying the DSM-5 rule, the question is not how dramatic the content is but whether psychosis occurred only within the mood episode. Since it did, with no two-week stretch of psychosis without mood symptoms, the diagnosis stays a mood disorder with psychotic features - and even incongruent content alone would not point to schizophrenia, since incongruent content is common in bipolar samples without a co-occurring schizophrenia diagnosis.

## Common confusions

- "An extreme delusion signals schizophrenia." The boundary rule is about timing relative to mood episodes, not content severity. [[P23707642 Malaspina 2013 Schizoaffective disorder in DSM-5#^p23707642-rule|Malaspina 2013]]
- "Mood-incongruent content usually means schizophrenia is also present." A co-occurring schizophrenia diagnosis was uncommon even though incongruent content was common. [[P41847966 Shao 2026 Psychotic symptoms in bipolar disorder#^p41847966-schizophrenia|Shao 2026]]
- "A polygenic score can tell you which diagnosis a person has." The gradient is a population average, not an individual test. [[P29167880 Allardyce 2018 Schizophrenia polygenic liability and mood-incongruent psychosis#^p29167880-caution-scores|Appraisal: Allardyce 2018]]
- "Schizoaffective disorder just means psychosis plus some mood symptoms." It requires full mood episodes for most of the illness since psychosis began. [[P23707642 Malaspina 2013 Schizoaffective disorder in DSM-5#^p23707642-lifecourse|Malaspina 2013]]

## What the sources do not establish

Whether mood-incongruent psychosis marks a distinct subgroup or the severe end of liability shared with schizophrenia is unresolved. The genetic gradient rests on one case-control sample using scores from an earlier schizophrenia study. The DSM-5 boundary rule's promised gains in reliability were expectations at publication, not yet demonstrated here.
