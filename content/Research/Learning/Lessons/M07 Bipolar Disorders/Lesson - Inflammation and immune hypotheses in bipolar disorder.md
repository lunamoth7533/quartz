# Inflammation and immune hypotheses in bipolar disorder

**Module.** M07 Bipolar Disorders · **Topic.** [[Inflammation and immune hypotheses in bipolar disorder]]

## Why this matters

"Inflammation causes bipolar disorder" sounds modern and mechanistic, but tracing exactly what has been shown - group-average marker differences, in a model borrowed from depression research - against what is being inferred from it, shows how far this evidence actually reaches.

## The core model

The mechanism these hypotheses lean on comes from depression research: peripheral immune signals reach the brain and produce sickness behaviour, a coordinated set of changes in activity, appetite, sleep and mood. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-sickness|Dantzer 2008]] The review proposing this framework treats it as a model in which prolonged inflammatory signalling can contribute to depressive symptoms in vulnerable people, through cytokine signalling, altered tryptophan metabolism and vagal transmission as interacting routes. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-depression|Dantzer 2008]] [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-mechanism|Dantzer 2008]] It is a proposed model, not an established mechanism, even for depression - and bipolar hypotheses generalise it a second time, to a different disorder. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-limit|Dantzer 2008]]

A 2013 systematic review pooled 13 studies of more than 500 patients measuring cytokine concentrations by affective state and found TNF-alpha, soluble TNF receptor type 1 and soluble interleukin-2 receptor elevated across affective states. [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-design|Munkholm 2013]] [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-finding|Munkholm 2013]] Meta-analytic differences like these describe group averages; they do not identify a cause of bipolar disorder or provide a usable test for any one person. [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-caution|Appraisal: Munkholm 2013]]

A 2025 meta-analysis of 97 studies sharpened that picture considerably: TNF-alpha and its soluble receptors were higher in bipolar disorder than in controls during mania and depression, but not during euthymia (a stable, symptom-free period). [[P41082830 McIntyre 2025 TNF-alpha in bipolar disorder meta-analysis#^p41082830-elevated|McIntyre 2025]] [[P41082830 McIntyre 2025 TNF-alpha in bipolar disorder meta-analysis#^p41082830-state|McIntyre 2025]] A marker that rises specifically with episodes and settles between them fits a marker responding to the episode just as well as one that helps cause it. [[P41082830 McIntyre 2025 TNF-alpha in bipolar disorder meta-analysis#^p41082830-caution-state|Appraisal: McIntyre 2025]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

A hypothetical supplement advertisement claims: "Science proves inflammation causes bipolar mood episodes, so our anti-inflammatory supplement can prevent them." Test that against the meta-analytic evidence. The 2025 update found elevated markers specifically during mood episodes, not during stable, symptom-free periods. That state-linked pattern is exactly what you would also expect if the episode itself - through sleep loss, altered appetite, stress hormones or treatment - raised the marker, rather than the marker causing the episode. Neither the relationship's direction nor a treatment benefit follows from a cross-sectional group difference; the ad's causal leap and its treatment claim both go beyond what these studies can show.

## Common confusions

- "The sickness-behaviour mechanism is an established fact, now confirmed in bipolar disorder." It is a proposed model even for depression generally, and bipolar hypotheses extend it a second time to a different disorder. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-limit|Dantzer 2008]]
- "Markers stay elevated all the time, so inflammation is a stable trait." The 2025 meta-analysis found elevation during mania and depression but not during euthymia - a state-linked pattern. [[P41082830 McIntyre 2025 TNF-alpha in bipolar disorder meta-analysis#^p41082830-state|McIntyre 2025]]
- "Higher markers in patients than controls proves inflammation is a cause." Such cross-sectional designs cannot separate cause from consequence. [[P41082830 McIntyre 2025 TNF-alpha in bipolar disorder meta-analysis#^p41082830-caution-state|Appraisal: McIntyre 2025]]
- "Since markers correlate with bipolar disorder, anti-inflammatory treatment should work." Group-average marker differences do not identify a cause or supply an individual treatment target. [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-caution|Appraisal: Munkholm 2013]]

## What the sources do not establish

Whether elevated markers reflect the condition itself, its treatment, or associated health factors such as sleep and diet cannot be resolved by cross-sectional studies. The route from cytokines to mood symptoms remains a proposed model rather than a demonstrated pathway, even in the depression literature it is borrowed from.

## Check yourself

1. Where does the mechanistic story behind bipolar inflammation hypotheses come from, and what does that borrowing mean for how solid it is?
2. What did the 2025 meta-analysis of 97 studies add to the earlier 2013 finding, and why does that change matter?
3. Why can't a cross-sectional study showing higher cytokine levels in bipolar patients than controls establish that inflammation causes the disorder?

## Answer notes

1. It comes from depression research on how peripheral immune signals produce sickness behaviour; that mechanism is itself a proposed model rather than an established fact even for depression, and it is generalised a second time here to a different disorder.
2. The 2013 analysis reported markers elevated "across affective states"; the 2025 meta-analysis found TNF-alpha and its receptors elevated during mania and depression but not euthymia - a state-specific pattern consistent with a marker that responds to the episode rather than necessarily driving it.
3. Circulating markers are affected by infection, sleep, medication, body composition and illness state itself, so a group difference could be a consequence of the episode or its treatment rather than a contributor, and a cross-sectional design cannot establish which direction the relationship runs.
