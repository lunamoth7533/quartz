# Bipolar depression and mixed features

**Module.** M07 Bipolar Disorders · **Topic.** [[Bipolar depression and mixed features]]

## Why this matters

Depression, not mania, fills most of the time a person with bipolar disorder is unwell, and "mixed features" - activation and depression appearing together - is where the two poles blur into each other. Reading a claim about either requires knowing whether it describes one episode or the whole disorder, and how the mood states involved were measured.

## The core model

Classification manuals separate two levels of description. ICD-11 gives distinct clinical entries for depressive, manic, mixed and hypomanic episodes before it gets to the disorder categories, each with its own essential features and course qualifiers - so an episode-level claim is specific to the system that produced it, and ICD-11's rules differ from DSM-5-TR in places. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-use|ICD-11 CDDR]]

Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania - one episode is enough - yet depressive episodes are common and occupy far more of the time a person is actually unwell across the lifetime course. That asymmetry is the practical reason bipolar disorder is so often first recognised as depression: the presenting state is usually depressive, which is why history-taking asks about past activation rather than relying on the current picture. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|NIMH]] [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|NIMH]]

Mixed features - episodes where depressive and manic or hypomanic symptoms co-occur - are where this picture gets more complicated still. A 2025 secondary analysis of 903 participants in a multisite cohort found that depressive severity dominated suicidal ideation overall, and that hypomania with mixed features only stood out as a distinctive risk pattern when mood was coded using clinical thresholds - not when the same data were modelled on a continuous scale. [[P40517219 Macellaro 2025 Mixed states and suicidal ideation#^p40517219-depression|Macellaro 2025]] [[P40517219 Macellaro 2025 Mixed states and suicidal ideation#^p40517219-mixed|Macellaro 2025]] [[P40517219 Macellaro 2025 Mixed states and suicidal ideation#^p40517219-modelling|Macellaro 2025]] Whether mixed features look like a distinct, important pattern therefore depends partly on how the researcher chose to classify mood in the first place. [[P40517219 Macellaro 2025 Mixed states and suicidal ideation#^p40517219-caution-coding|Appraisal: Macellaro 2025]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

Two hypothetical studies use the same underlying data. Study A reports: "Mixed hypomania is a distinct, important risk marker for suicidal thoughts." Study B reports: "Once depression severity is modelled continuously, mixed hypomania adds nothing extra." These look contradictory. Before concluding one team erred, check how each defined mood: clinical categories (mixed episode present or absent) or a continuous severity score? The 2025 analysis above shows both patterns can appear in the same dataset - a categorical cut can make a group look distinctive that a continuous model absorbs into the general severity trend. The disagreement is about measurement choice, not a factual dispute about the participants.

## Common confusions

- "An episode label means the same thing in every classification system." ICD-11's rules differ from DSM-5-TR in places, so an episode-level claim is specific to its system. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-use|ICD-11 CDDR]]
- "Since mania defines bipolar I, mania is what fills most of a patient's time." Depressive episodes and symptoms occupy far more of the long-term course. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|NIMH]]
- "Whether mixed features carry extra suicide risk is a settled fact." It depended, in one 2025 analysis, on whether mood was coded categorically or continuously. [[P40517219 Macellaro 2025 Mixed states and suicidal ideation#^p40517219-caution-coding|Appraisal: Macellaro 2025]]
- "A single presenting episode tells you the whole diagnosis." Bipolar disorder is often first recognised as depression because the depressive episode is usually what brings someone to care. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|NIMH]]

## What the sources do not establish

Where mixed features end and rapid cycling begins is still debated and not resolved by any source here. The 2025 analysis is a secondary, cross-sectional look at one cohort, so it cannot show whether coding choice would produce the same pattern in a different sample. Neither source establishes psychotic-feature evidence specific to bipolar depression as distinct from mania.

## Check yourself

1. Why is bipolar I diagnosed on the basis of mania, even though depression fills more of the time a person is unwell?
2. Why does an episode-level description in a classification manual matter separately from the disorder-level diagnosis?
3. What did the 2025 secondary analysis find about mixed features and suicidal ideation, and what did that finding depend on?

## Answer notes

1. Diagnosis requires only one manic episode, but depressive episodes are more frequent and occupy more of the long-term course - which is also why the disorder is so often first recognised as depression.
2. Because manuals like ICD-11 describe each episode type separately from the disorder categories, with rules that differ from other systems such as DSM-5-TR, so a claim about "an episode" needs its source system named.
3. Depressive severity dominated suicidal ideation overall, and mixed hypomania stood out as distinctive only when mood was coded with clinical thresholds rather than modelled continuously - the pattern depended on the coding choice, not a single fixed fact about mixed states.
