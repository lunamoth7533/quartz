# Hypomania

**Module.** M07 Bipolar Disorders · **Topic.** [[Hypomania]]

## Why this matters

Hypomania is quieter than mania, and that quietness is the single biggest reason bipolar II goes undiagnosed for years. Understanding where the threshold sits, and what a screening questionnaire can and cannot do, is what lets a reader judge any claim that "X% of depressed patients actually have bipolar disorder."

## The core model

Hypomania needs an elevated or irritable mood with increased activity lasting at least four days, without the marked impairment or hospital care that would make it mania. [[P29630220 Dailey 2023 Mania reference chapter#^p29630220-criteria|Dailey 2023]] That four-day, no-major-impairment line is a convention set by the diagnostic manual, not a natural boundary discovered in nature - moving it moves people across the line without changing them. [[P29630220 Dailey 2023 Mania reference chapter#^p29630220-caution-dsm|Appraisal: Dailey 2023]]

Because hypomania rarely brings someone to hospital and can look like simply feeling good, it is missed. Across ten primary-care studies, about 17% of people being treated for depression turned out to have unrecognised bipolar disorder. [[P30933689 Daveney 2019 Unrecognised bipolar disorder in primary care#^p30933689-prevalence|Daveney 2019]] Questionnaire-based and interview-based methods of counting these cases gave estimates that did not differ significantly, which suggests the gap is not just a measurement artefact of one method. [[P30933689 Daveney 2019 Unrecognised bipolar disorder in primary care#^p30933689-method|Daveney 2019]]

Screening tools exist precisely to catch this, and a meta-analysis of 53 accuracy studies against structured interviews shows why no single number describes them. The Hypomania Checklist (HCL-32) had summary sensitivity of 81% and specificity of 67% in psychiatric services; the Mood Disorder Questionnaire (MDQ) had 66% and 79% in the same kind of setting - one tool catches more true cases, the other produces fewer false alarms. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-accuracy|Carvalho 2015]] In primary care, the MDQ at a common cut-off missed most true cases but rarely flagged someone who did not have the disorder. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-primary|Carvalho 2015]] With specificity this variable across settings, a positive screen is a reason to ask structured questions about past hypomania, not a diagnosis in itself. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-caution-screen|Appraisal: Carvalho 2015]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

A wellness article claims: "This 5-question quiz correctly identifies bipolar II 95% of the time." Before trusting that number, ask three things this lesson has just covered. First, 95% of what - sensitivity (catching true cases) or specificity (correctly clearing people who do not have it)? A tool can be excellent on one and weak on the other, as the HCL-32 and MDQ comparison shows. Second, in what population was it validated - a psychiatric clinic, where more people already have the disorder, or a general community sample, where accuracy figures typically look different? Third, what was it checked against - a structured diagnostic interview, or just people's later self-report? Without those three answers, "95% accurate" is not yet a claim that can be evaluated, let alone acted on.

## Common confusions

- "Hypomania is basically harmless mild mania." It is under-recognised, not trivial, and its concealment is exactly why bipolar II is often missed for years. [[P30933689 Daveney 2019 Unrecognised bipolar disorder in primary care#^p30933689-prevalence|Daveney 2019]]
- "A positive screen means you have bipolar II." A positive screen should open a structured interview about past episodes, not settle the diagnosis. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-caution-screen|Appraisal: Carvalho 2015]]
- "Four days is a scientifically fixed cutoff." It is a diagnostic-manual convention that could, in principle, be drawn elsewhere. [[P29630220 Dailey 2023 Mania reference chapter#^p29630220-caution-dsm|Appraisal: Dailey 2023]]
- "All screening questionnaires perform about the same." The HCL-32 and MDQ trade off sensitivity and specificity differently. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-accuracy|Carvalho 2015]]

## What the sources do not establish

Screening accuracy comes mostly from psychiatric-service samples; few studies test these tools in primary care, where most missed cases are actually found. The unrecognised-bipolar-disorder estimate rests on ten cross-sectional studies, which cannot show how a case unfolds over time. Neither source identifies which specific person, screened once, truly has bipolar II.

## Answer notes

1. Hypomania needs at least four days of elevated or irritable mood with increased activity, without the marked impairment or hospital admission that defines mania.
2. Because sensitivity and specificity depend on the setting (psychiatric service versus primary care) and the reference standard used, and different tools trade the two off differently.
3. Treat it as a reason to ask structured questions about past hypomanic episodes, not as a stand-alone diagnosis, since specificity is too variable for a positive screen to settle the question alone.
