# Mood monitoring and early warning signs in bipolar disorder

**Module.** M07 Bipolar Disorders · **Topic.** [[Mood monitoring and early warning signs in bipolar disorder]]

## Why this matters

"Watch for early warning signs" advice assumes a person can reliably notice their own approaching episode. The strongest evidence shows this works differently for depression than for mania, and that a monitoring model built on one group often fails when applied to someone new.

## The core model

Asked plainly during an episode about the period beforehand, 59% of patients in one sample volunteered sleep disturbance; asked specifically about sleep problems, 83% reported it, with disturbance recalled to start earlier before depression (about three months out) than before mania (about five weeks out). [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-prevalence|Basquin 2024]] [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-timing|Basquin 2024]] That jump under prompting shows how much a prodrome estimate depends on how the question is asked; the whole method is a reconstruction shaped by what people remember once already unwell. [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-caution-recall|Appraisal: Basquin 2024]]

Passive sensing tries to sidestep memory by reading behaviour directly. Across 57 studies of wearables and phone sensors, mania and hypomania were linked to more and more variable activity and to shorter or advanced sleep - a group-level pattern consistent enough to keep pursuing. [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-patterns|Kutcher 2026]] But the base behind it is thin: about 60% of studies enrolled 50 or fewer participants and 81% monitored a year or less, with external validation - testing a model on people it was not built from - usually absent. [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-limit|Kutcher 2026]] [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-caution-validation|Appraisal: Kutcher 2026]]

That gap matters for what happens when a model is tested on new people. In one small wearable study, models built on movement, electrodermal and heart-rate signals tracked episode severity within their own trained individuals with moderate accuracy, but generalised poorly to patients they had never seen. [[P36939345 Anmella 2023 Wearable digital biomarkers of mood episodes#^p36939345-accuracy|Anmella 2023]] [[P36939345 Anmella 2023 Wearable digital biomarkers of mood episodes#^p36939345-generalization|Anmella 2023]] A pattern that works inside one person's own data is a within-person regularity; turning it into a rule for a stranger needs its own evidence. [[P36939345 Anmella 2023 Wearable digital biomarkers of mood episodes#^p36939345-caution-sample|Appraisal: Anmella 2023]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

A wellness app claims: "Our algorithm detects 85% of manic episodes before they start." Before trusting that, ask what "detects" was tested against: the same people whose data trained it, or new users it never saw? The wearable study above shows these numbers can differ sharply - a model can track severity well within a familiar person's data while doing far worse on someone new. An accuracy figure without that distinction is not yet a claim a new user can rely on.

## Common confusions

- "Not noticing warning signs beforehand means there weren't any." Prompted, specific questions raised reported sleep disturbance from 59% to 83% in the same sample. [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-prevalence|Basquin 2024]]
- "Self-monitoring catches approaching mania as well as it catches depression." Sleep-disturbance onset was recalled earlier and more consistently before depression than before mania. [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-timing|Basquin 2024]]
- "Passive sensing has already proven it can predict episodes for a new person." The patterns are group-level, most studies are small and short, and external validation is usually absent. [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-caution-validation|Appraisal: Kutcher 2026]]
- "An algorithm's reported accuracy applies to any new user." Models generalised poorly to patients outside the group they were trained on. [[P36939345 Anmella 2023 Wearable digital biomarkers of mood episodes#^p36939345-caution-sample|Appraisal: Anmella 2023]]

## What the sources do not establish

Whether prodromal sleep and activity change triggers an episode, or is simply its first symptom, remains open. No source here shows a model that forecasts a future episode, rather than classifying the current state, validated on new people and new periods. Monitoring's burden, adherence and privacy costs are not weighed against its benefit here.

## Answer notes

1. Plain, open recall undercounts prodromal symptoms; asking specifically about sleep prompted patients to report disturbance they had not volunteered, showing the estimate depends on how the question is asked.
2. Mania and hypomania were linked to more and more variable activity and to shorter or advanced sleep; the limitation is that most studies were small and short, with external validation on new people usually absent.
3. Models tracked episode severity within their trained individuals with moderate accuracy but generalised poorly to patients they had never seen - a reported accuracy figure does not automatically transfer to a new user, which is exactly what such app claims imply.
