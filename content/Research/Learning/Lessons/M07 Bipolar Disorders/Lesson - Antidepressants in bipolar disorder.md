# Antidepressants in bipolar disorder

**Module.** M07 Bipolar Disorders · **Topic.** [[Antidepressants in bipolar disorder]]

## Why this matters

Antidepressants are widely prescribed in bipolar disorder even though the evidence for their efficacy and safety there is genuinely weak. The central safety worry - switching from depression into mania, hypomania or a mixed state - is exactly where headline claims most often outrun what a specific trial actually measured.

## The core model

Across masked trials of two to sixteen weeks in acute bipolar depression, antidepressants as a class appeared efficacious but carried more manic switch than antipsychotics did. [[P37595997 Yildiz 2023 Drug treatment for bipolar depression#^p37595997-antidepressants|Yildiz 2023]] "As a class" is doing real work in that sentence: it averages over different drugs that may not behave alike, and it says nothing about which one carries the risk. [[P37595997 Yildiz 2023 Drug treatment for bipolar depression#^p37595997-limit|Yildiz 2023]]

A 2025 network meta-analysis of antidepressants specifically found no single drug with a switch rate significantly above placebo, rated low confidence overall, with most data from add-on designs. [[P40823496 Oliva 2025 Switch to mania after antidepressants#^p40823496-placebo|Oliva 2025]] [[P40823496 Oliva 2025 Switch to mania after antidepressants#^p40823496-regimen|Oliva 2025]] Only venlafaxine showed a consistent switch signal across its own studies. [[P40823496 Oliva 2025 Switch to mania after antidepressants#^p40823496-venlafaxine|Oliva 2025]] Because switches are rare over a few weeks, a real risk can still exist without reaching significance, and a class-wide average never describes any one person's risk. [[P40823496 Oliva 2025 Switch to mania after antidepressants#^p40823496-caution-acute|Appraisal: Oliva 2025]]

The one trial that looked beyond the acute episode complicates the picture further. In bipolar I, continuing adjunctive escitalopram or bupropion for 52 weeks after remission, rather than switching to placebo at 8 weeks, did not significantly lower the risk of a new mood episode overall. [[P37530824 Yatham 2023 Duration of adjunctive antidepressant maintenance#^p37530824-primary|Yatham 2023]] Underneath that null result, depressive recurrence was less frequent with continuation (17% against 40%), while mania or hypomania was numerically more frequent (12% against 6%, not significant). [[P37530824 Yatham 2023 Duration of adjunctive antidepressant maintenance#^p37530824-polarity|Yatham 2023]] The trial stopped early, leaving it underpowered, with its two secondary signals pulling opposite ways rather than pointing to one answer. [[P37530824 Yatham 2023 Duration of adjunctive antidepressant maintenance#^p37530824-limit|Yatham 2023]] [[P37530824 Yatham 2023 Duration of adjunctive antidepressant maintenance#^p37530824-caution-power|Appraisal: Yatham 2023]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

A news piece claims: "Landmark trial proves staying on antidepressants longer prevents bipolar relapse." Check that against what the trial reported. The primary outcome - any new mood episode - was not significantly reduced by continuing the antidepressant. Underneath it, depression fell but mania or hypomania rose slightly, a non-significant move the other way. The trial also stopped early, which is why neither secondary signal reached significance alone. The accurate summary: "A year of continued treatment did not significantly reduce relapse overall in this underpowered trial, and its secondary results moved in opposite directions for the two poles" - inconclusive with a hint, not proof.

## Common confusions

- "A switch signal for one drug applies to the whole class." Only venlafaxine showed a consistent signal; most others did not reach significance. [[P40823496 Oliva 2025 Switch to mania after antidepressants#^p40823496-venlafaxine|Oliva 2025]]
- "No significant switch difference means antidepressants are proven safe." Few switches over a short trial leave a real risk possible without reaching significance. [[P40823496 Oliva 2025 Switch to mania after antidepressants#^p40823496-caution-acute|Appraisal: Oliva 2025]]
- "The 52-week trial proved continuation prevents relapse." Its primary outcome was not significant, and it stopped early. [[P37530824 Yatham 2023 Duration of adjunctive antidepressant maintenance#^p37530824-primary|Yatham 2023]]
- "Fewer depressive recurrences on continuation means no downside." Mania or hypomania recurrence was numerically higher, an opposite-direction signal. [[P37530824 Yatham 2023 Duration of adjunctive antidepressant maintenance#^p37530824-polarity|Yatham 2023]]

## What the sources do not establish

Whether months of use genuinely raises switch or cycling risk is unresolved: acute trials cannot show it, and the one maintenance trial was too small. Whether drug classes truly differ in switch risk, as some consensus proposes, is not confirmed when trials show a consistent signal for only one drug.

## Answer notes

1. Antidepressants as a class appeared efficacious for bipolar depression but carried more manic switch than antipsychotics; "as a class" averages over individual drugs that may behave differently, so it hides which drug, if any, drives the risk.
2. No single antidepressant showed a switch rate significantly above placebo in a low-confidence analysis; venlafaxine was the one drug with a consistent switch signal across its own studies.
3. The primary outcome (any new mood episode) was not significantly reduced by continuation; depressive recurrence was numerically lower and mania or hypomania numerically higher (not significant) - two signals pointing opposite ways, from an underpowered, early-stopped trial.
