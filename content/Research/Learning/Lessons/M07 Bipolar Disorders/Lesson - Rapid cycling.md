# Rapid cycling

**Module.** M07 Bipolar Disorders · **Topic.** [[Rapid cycling]]

## Why this matters

Rapid cycling is not a diagnosis of its own - it is a count of episodes within a time window, applied on top of bipolar I or II. Treating it as a description rather than a separate disease is what keeps trigger and outcome claims about it honest.

## The core model

Rapid cycling means four or more full mood episodes within 12 months, separated either by two months of remission or by a switch to the opposite pole. [[P29630220 Dailey 2023 Mania reference chapter#^p29630220-rapid|Dailey 2023]] It is common: pooled across reviews, about 22% of people with bipolar disorder meet it in a given year and about 36% meet it at some point in their life. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-prevalence|Miola 2023]]

Of its correlates, two have the strongest evidence: rapid cycling is linked to suicide attempts and to a poor response to mood stabilisers. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-strong|Miola 2023]] Antidepressant exposure, by contrast, has only weak (level-3) support as a correlate across the same reviews, alongside genetic predisposition and metabolic or thyroid disturbance. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-weak|Miola 2023]] The one randomised comparison available is small but useful: among 68 patients on mood stabilisers whose depression had responded to an antidepressant, those with rapid cycling who kept taking it had about three times as many episodes a year as similar patients without rapid cycling - a difference that was absent among patients randomised to stop the antidepressant instead. [[P26142612 El-Mallakh 2015 Antidepressants and rapid cycling in STEP-BD#^p26142612-episodes|El-Mallakh 2015]] [[P26142612 El-Mallakh 2015 Antidepressants and rapid cycling in STEP-BD#^p26142612-discontinue|El-Mallakh 2015]] That subgroup was small and preselected for people whose depression had already responded to the drug, so it cannot be read as a general antidepressant-safety verdict. [[P26142612 El-Mallakh 2015 Antidepressants and rapid cycling in STEP-BD#^p26142612-caution-subgroup|Appraisal: El-Mallakh 2015]]

Because rapid cycling is counted over a chosen window rather than measured as a stable trait, its associations with suicide attempts and poor treatment response may partly reflect shared severity or treatment history rather than the label itself doing the work. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-caution-state|Appraisal: Miola 2023]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

A hypothetical case note reads: "Patient X had five mood episodes this year, so their rapid cycling caused their poor response to lithium." That sentence treats "rapid cycling" as a cause, but it is a descriptive label for a pattern of episodes, not a separate mechanism that does something to the person. The count and the poor response are two observations that happen to correlate strongly across studies - the label does not explain why. To actually test what changes the course, a comparison is needed, like the one El-Mallakh ran: does continuing versus stopping a specific treatment change the episode count in a controlled comparison, holding the starting point fixed? Only that kind of design, not the correlation alone, can support a causal claim about what to do next.

## Common confusions

- "Rapid cycling is a distinct type of bipolar disorder." It is a course specifier applied within bipolar I or II, not a diagnosis of its own. [[P29630220 Dailey 2023 Mania reference chapter#^p29630220-rapid|Dailey 2023]]
- "Antidepressants are as strongly linked to rapid cycling as poor mood-stabiliser response is." Antidepressant exposure has only weak, level-3 support; suicide attempts and stabiliser response have the strongest evidence. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-weak|Miola 2023]]
- "The STEP-BD subgroup analysis proves antidepressants generally cause rapid cycling." It was a small, preselected subgroup of antidepressant responders, not a general trial. [[P26142612 El-Mallakh 2015 Antidepressants and rapid cycling in STEP-BD#^p26142612-caution-subgroup|Appraisal: El-Mallakh 2015]]
- "A calm year means rapid cycling is permanently resolved." It is counted over a chosen window and can apply in one year and not the next.

## What the sources do not establish

Whether antidepressants provoke rapid cycling, or are simply prescribed more often to people already prone to it, is unresolved. Whether the reported thyroid, metabolic and immune correlates are causes, consequences or markers of treatment is unclear. Treatment trials designed specifically for rapid cycling are not yet summarised here.
