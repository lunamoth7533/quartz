# Bipolar II disorder

**Module.** M07 Bipolar Disorders · **Topic.** [[Bipolar II disorder]]

## Why this matters

Bipolar II is easy to hear as "bipolar I, but milder," since it is defined by hypomania rather than full mania. The evidence contradicts that framing on the point that matters most: risk to life. Getting this right changes how seriously a bipolar II diagnosis is taken, and how it is told apart from borderline personality disorder.

## The core model

Bipolar II is defined by hypomanic and major depressive episodes, with hypomania less severe than the mania that defines bipolar I. Head-to-head comparisons from about 90,000 patients show a genuinely different profile, not a fainter copy: more depressive episodes, comorbidity and rapid cycling, fewer hospitalisations and psychotic features - and, critically, a similar risk of suicide attempts. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-more|Hernandorena 2023]] [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-less|Hernandorena 2023]] [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-same|Hernandorena 2023]] The diagnosis is also stable, holding up over many years of follow-up rather than drifting into something else. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-stable|Hernandorena 2023]]

That similar suicide risk sits inside a wider mortality picture: bipolar disorders as a group shorten life by roughly 10 to 20 years, mainly through cardiovascular disease and suicide. [[P33278937 McIntyre 2020 Bipolar disorders seminar#^p33278937-mortality|McIntyre 2020]] Fewer hospitalisations for mania does not mean a safer illness overall.

Bipolar II is also frequently weighed against borderline personality disorder, since both involve marked mood variability. The two are built from different material: borderline personality disorder is long-standing instability of mood, self-image and relationships with strong sensitivity to rejection, while bipolar II is defined by discrete episodes with a beginning and an end. [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|NIMH borderline]] They are not mutually exclusive - about 22% of people with bipolar disorder overall also meet criteria for borderline personality disorder, rising to about 38% in bipolar II samples - and the authors of that comparison read the evidence as highlighting differences between the conditions more than similarities. [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-prevalence|Fornaro 2016]] [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-type2|Fornaro 2016]] [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-differences|Fornaro 2016]]

These comparisons come from people whose bipolar II had already been diagnosed; anyone not yet identified may look different on some measures. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-caution-selection|Appraisal: Hernandorena 2023]]

## Worked example (hypothetical)

A hypothetical blog post argues: "Bipolar II is basically bipolar I lite, so it's the less dangerous version." Test that claim piece by piece rather than accepting the summary. Less dangerous by hospitalisation rate? Yes - fewer people with bipolar II are hospitalised for mania, since they do not have full manic episodes. Less dangerous by suicide-attempt risk? No - pooled comparisons found no significant difference between the two types. "Less dangerous" is true for one outcome and false for the outcome that matters most for safety; the accurate summary keeps the two findings separate rather than collapsing them into one adjective.

## Common confusions

- "Bipolar II is bipolar I but milder, full stop." It differs in several directions at once - more depression and comorbidity, fewer hospitalisations - not uniformly milder. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-more|Hernandorena 2023]]
- "Fewer hospitalisations means a lower risk of dying." Suicide-attempt risk did not differ from bipolar I. [[P33278937 McIntyre 2020 Bipolar disorders seminar#^p33278937-mortality|McIntyre 2020]]
- "Borderline personality disorder and bipolar II are the same thing." Their comparison reads as showing more differences than similarities, even though the two can co-occur. [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-differences|Fornaro 2016]]
- "These findings describe everyone with bipolar II." They describe people already recognised; undiagnosed cases may differ. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-caution-selection|Appraisal: Hernandorena 2023]]

## What the sources do not establish

Whether bipolar II is a genuinely separate condition or a point on a shared spectrum with bipolar I is unresolved. No biological measure yet separates bipolar II from borderline personality disorder in an individual case, and how much of their comorbidity reflects true co-occurrence rather than overlapping criteria is unclear.

## Answer notes

1. Bipolar II has more depressive episodes, comorbidity and rapid cycling than bipolar I, but fewer hospitalisations and psychotic features.
2. Pooled comparisons found suicide-attempt risk did not differ significantly between the two types, so a less severe elevated pole does not mean a safer illness overall.
3. Borderline personality disorder is described as long-standing instability of mood, self-image and relationships with rejection sensitivity, while bipolar II is defined by discrete episodes; they can co-occur, more often in bipolar II samples, without being identical constructs.
