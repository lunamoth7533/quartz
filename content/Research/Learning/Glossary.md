# Glossary

Working definitions for the vocabulary this vault uses. Each term links to the lesson that teaches it; the lesson in turn cites the source blocks. These are teaching definitions, not new claims, and where a term is contested the entry says so.

**87 terms.**

| Term | Working definition | Taught in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effect size | The magnitude of a difference or association, reported separately from statistical significance. | [[Lesson - Effect sizes and uncertainty]] |
| Absolute risk reduction (ARR) | The risk difference between groups; it must be read against the baseline risk. | [[Lesson - Effect sizes and uncertainty]] |
| Relative risk reduction (RRR) | The absolute reduction divided by baseline risk; it cannot be interpreted without the control event rate. | [[Lesson - Effect sizes and uncertainty]] |
| Number needed to treat (NNT) | One divided by the absolute risk reduction; it changes with baseline risk even when the relative effect does not. | [[Lesson - Effect sizes and uncertainty]] |
| Confidence interval | A compatibility range for a parameter under the data and model, not a probability distribution for the parameter. | [[Lesson - Effect sizes and uncertainty]] |
| P value | A measure of compatibility between data and a test hypothesis; it is not the probability that the hypothesis is true. | [[Lesson - Effect sizes and uncertainty]] |
| Statistical significance | A threshold convention applied to a test result, not a finding about importance. | [[Lesson - Effect sizes and uncertainty]] |
| Reliability | Consistency of scores across items, occasions or raters; it does not establish that the scores mean the right thing. | [[Lesson - Measurement validity and reliability]] |
| Validity | Whether a measure supports the interpretation claimed for it in a defined population and purpose (content, criterion and construct validity families). | [[Lesson - Measurement validity and reliability]] |
| Rater effect | The way who reports an outcome changes the result, as when clinician and self ratings disagree. | [[Lesson - Measurement validity and reliability]] |
| Systematic review | A review that pools studies under explicit rules; it inherits the biases of the included studies and adds its own choices. | [[Lesson - Meta-analysis and review limits]] |
| Meta-analysis | Statistical pooling of study estimates, whose meaning depends on the studies available and the decisions made about them. | [[Lesson - Meta-analysis and review limits]] |
| Publication bias | Distortion from missing or selectively reported studies, flagged but not solved by a review's own caution. | [[Lesson - Meta-analysis and review limits]] |
| Certainty of evidence | A structured rating of confidence in an estimate across domains such as risk of bias, imprecision and inconsistency. | [[Lesson - Meta-analysis and review limits]] |
| Heritability | The share of variation in a trait associated with genetic differences in a studied population and environment. | [[Lesson - Genetic inference and polygenic scores]] |
| Genome-wide association study (GWAS) | A study that tests many variants across the genome for statistical association with a trait. | [[Lesson - Genetic inference and polygenic scores]] |
| Polygenic score | A weighted sum of variant effects estimated in a discovery sample and applied in another sample; a population-level statistic. | [[Lesson - Genetic inference and polygenic scores]] |
| Confounder | A factor associated with both the exposure and the outcome that can distort their apparent relationship. | [[Lesson - Bias and confounding]] |
| Selection effect | Distortion created by how participants entered or stayed in a study. | [[Lesson - Bias and confounding]] |
| Observational versus experimental design | Observation describes what co-occurs; manipulation with random assignment supports stronger causal inference. | [[Lesson - Evidence types and causal inference]] |
| Target population | The population a result is meant to describe, which the study sample may only partly represent. | [[Lesson - Reading a study and matching populations]] |
| Functioning and participation | Reported activity and involvement in life situations, as distinguished from symptoms or capacity. | [[Lesson - Functional outcomes and measurement]] |
| Co-occurrence | Two conditions appearing together more often than chance in a sample; it is definition- and sample-dependent. | [[Lesson - Co-occurrence and differential reasoning]] |
| Differential reasoning | Holding more than one explanation open while testing which pattern fits the evidence. | [[Lesson - Co-occurrence and differential reasoning]] |
| Neuron | A cell that receives input at dendrites, integrates it at the cell body and transmits output along an axon. | [[Lesson - Neurons and glia]] |
| Glia | Non-neuronal cells that scaffold, insulate, nourish and modulate neurons, and respond on their own timescales. | [[Lesson - Neurons and glia]] |
| Myelin | Insulating wraps formed by glia that speed conduction between the nodes of Ranvier. | [[Lesson - Nervous tissue and myelin]] |
| Grey and white matter | Tissue descriptions: cell bodies and local processing versus long-range myelinated axons. | [[Lesson - Nervous tissue and myelin]] |
| Resting membrane potential | The negative-inside voltage produced by ion gradients across a neuron's membrane. | [[Lesson - Ion gradients and membrane potential]] |
| Action potential | An all-or-none spike triggered when depolarisation crosses threshold, regenerated along the axon. | [[Lesson - Action potentials]] |
| Graded potential | A local voltage change that decays with distance and must summate at the integration point to matter. | [[Lesson - Excitation and inhibition balance]] |
| Synapse | A connection where a signal passes between cells, chemically through transmitter release or electrically through gap junctions. | [[Lesson - Synaptic signalling model]] |
| Ionotropic and metabotropic receptors | Receptors that open an ion channel directly versus those that act through second messengers. | [[Lesson - Pharmacodynamics and receptors]] |
| Long-term potentiation (LTP) | A persistent, input-specific increase in synaptic strength following patterned activity. | [[Lesson - Long-term potentiation and depression]] |
| Long-term depression (LTD) | The complementary persistent decrease in synaptic strength. | [[Lesson - Long-term potentiation and depression]] |
| Metaplasticity | The way previous activity changes how susceptible a synapse is to later plasticity. | [[Lesson - Long-term potentiation and depression]] |
| Hub and network | The idea that a brain region is a node in distributed loops rather than a single-purpose centre. | [[Lesson - Brain regions and networks]] |
| Excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP) | A postsynaptic voltage change that moves the cell toward threshold. | [[Lesson - Excitation and inhibition balance]] |
| Inhibitory postsynaptic potential (IPSP) | A postsynaptic voltage change that moves the cell away from threshold. | [[Lesson - Excitation and inhibition balance]] |
| Dopamine | A catecholamine transmitter with different roles in movement, motivation, reward and control depending on the pathway. | [[Lesson - Dopamine signalling]] |
| Noradrenaline | A catecholamine transmitter associated with arousal and alertness, with inverted-U effects in prefrontal circuits. | [[Lesson - Noradrenaline signalling]] |
| Serotonin | A monoamine transmitter with roles across mood, sleep, appetite and other systems. | [[Lesson - Serotonin signalling]] |
| Acetylcholine | A transmitter at neuromuscular junctions, autonomic ganglia and brain circuits for arousal and memory. | [[Lesson - Acetylcholine signalling]] |
| Glutamate and GABA | The main excitatory and inhibitory amino-acid transmitters of the adult central nervous system. | [[Lesson - Glutamate and GABA]] |
| HPA axis | The slower hormonal stress pathway ending in cortisol release, alongside the fast sympathetic response. | [[Lesson - Stress response and the HPA axis]] |
| Cortisol | A hormone that mobilises energy and follows a daily rhythm; it is not a single stress measure. | [[Lesson - Stress response and the HPA axis]] |
| Sympathetic and parasympathetic branches | Autonomic divisions that often oppose each other while cooperating on the same targets. | [[Lesson - Autonomic regulation]] |
| Heart rate variability (HRV) | Beat-to-beat variation in heart timing, summarised by time-domain, frequency-domain or non-linear metrics. | [[Lesson - Autonomic regulation]] |
| LF/HF ratio | A contested frequency-domain ratio sometimes labelled sympathovagal balance; it is sensitive to respiration and other influences. | [[Lesson - Autonomic regulation]] |
| Circadian rhythm | An approximately daily internal timing system that interacts with sleep pressure and light exposure. | [[Lesson - Circadian rhythms and sleep]] |
| Attention | Selection and prioritisation of information, supported by control processes that hold goals and suppress distraction. | [[Lesson - Attention and executive function]] |
| Executive function | Control processes including inhibition, working memory and switching, measured through narrow laboratory proxies. | [[Lesson - Attention and executive function]] |
| Classical conditioning | Learning in which a neutral cue comes to evoke a response after pairing with a meaningful stimulus. | [[Lesson - Learning and conditioning]] |
| Operant conditioning | Learning through consequences, where reinforcement increases and punishment decreases a behaviour. | [[Lesson - Learning and conditioning]] |
| Extinction | New learning that inhibits an old association rather than erasing it, which is why responses can return. | [[Lesson - Learning and conditioning]] |
| Encoding, storage and retrieval | The three memory processes, each with distinct failure modes. | [[Lesson - Memory processes]] |
| Reconstructive memory | Recall rebuilt from fragments and expectations, so confidence and accuracy can diverge. | [[Lesson - Memory processes]] |
| Emotion regulation | The processes that shape which emotions arise, when, and how they are expressed and recovered from. | [[Lesson - Emotion regulation]] |
| Appraisal | The interpretation of a demand that shapes whether a stress response is activated and how it feels. | [[Lesson - Emotion regulation]] |
| Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) | A family of structured, skills-based protocols targeting the relationship between thoughts, behaviour and mood. | [[Lesson - CBT and its evidence base]] |
| Pharmacokinetics (ADME) | What the body does to a drug: absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion. | [[Lesson - Pharmacokinetics and ADME]] |
| Bioavailability | The fraction of a dose that reaches the bloodstream in active form; it varies with route and formulation. | [[Lesson - Pharmacokinetics and ADME]] |
| Half-life and steady state | The time for the drug amount to halve, and the plateau reached when intake and elimination balance. | [[Lesson - Half-life and steady state]] |
| Pharmacodynamics | What a drug does to the body, usually through receptors, enzymes, transporters or channels. | [[Lesson - Pharmacodynamics and receptors]] |
| Agonist and antagonist | Molecules that mimic or enhance a signal versus those that block it. | [[Lesson - Pharmacodynamics and receptors]] |
| Efficacy | How much a treatment changes the target outcome under the conditions studied. | [[Lesson - Efficacy versus tolerability]] |
| Tolerability | The burden a treatment imposes, often operationalised as discontinuation for adverse events. | [[Lesson - Efficacy versus tolerability]] |
| Therapeutic index or window | The exposure range between too little and too much drug; narrow windows need tighter control and often monitoring. | [[Lesson - Therapeutic index, monitoring and interactions]] |
| Central and peripheral nervous system | The brain and spinal cord versus the nerves carrying signals to and from the rest of the body. | [[Lesson - Nervous system divisions]] |
| Neurological examination | History and examination that generate the question technology is then chosen to answer. | [[Lesson - Neurological examination]] |
| Structural MRI | Imaging of anatomy, such as cortical thickness or volume. | [[Lesson - Structural versus functional measures]] |
| Functional MRI (fMRI) | Imaging of activity-related signal change, which is not a direct measure of electrical activity. | [[Lesson - Structural versus functional measures]] |
| EEG | Recording of summed electrical activity with high temporal and limited spatial resolution. | [[Lesson - MRI versus EEG]] |
| Mania | The defining syndrome of bipolar I disorder, with depressive episodes common but not required. | [[Lesson - Bipolar I episodes and course]] |
| Illness phase | The current clinical state (for example acute mania, acute depression or maintenance) that organises treatment evidence. | [[Lesson - Phase-specific bipolar treatment evidence]] |
| Mood stabiliser | A drug class term for medicines used to reduce recurrence risk in bipolar disorder; mechanism varies by drug. | [[Lesson - Lithium mechanisms and uncertainty]] |
| ICD-11 complex PTSD | A diagnosis combining core PTSD symptoms with disturbances in self-organization, absent from DSM-5. | [[Lesson - CPTSD and disturbances in self-organization]] |
| Disturbances in self-organization | Affect dysregulation, a persistently negative self-concept and relational difficulties. | [[Lesson - CPTSD and disturbances in self-organization]] |
| Autism spectrum disorder | A developmental construct covering social-communication differences plus restricted, repetitive or sensory features. | [[Lesson - Autism heterogeneity and support needs]] |
| Rare variant | A genetic variant with low population frequency that may carry larger effect, as in autism exome findings. | [[Lesson - Autism genetics and rare variants]] |
| Screening versus diagnosis | Screening sorts who should be assessed further and can produce false positives and false negatives; diagnosis is a comprehensive, multi-source evaluation, and a score cannot make or exclude it. | [[Lesson - Adult autism assessment and differential considerations]] |
| Developmental history | Evidence about early development gathered from informants or documents where available; seeking it does not imply that adult self-report is inherently unreliable. | [[Lesson - Adult autism assessment and differential considerations]] |
| Competing explanations in assessment | Differential assessment keeps co-occurring conditions, mental and physical disorders and other neurodevelopmental conditions open rather than stopping at the first fit. | [[Lesson - Adult autism assessment and differential considerations]] |
| Accommodation | A change to the environment, communication or demands that improves access; it changes the fit between person and setting rather than the person. | [[Lesson - Autistic sensory and communication access]] |
| Masking (camouflaging) | Suppressing autistic characteristics to pass in non-autistic settings; the review links it to reported harms rather than benefit. | [[Lesson - Autistic sensory and communication access]] |
| ADHD | A lifespan condition defined by persistent, impairing inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity. | [[Lesson - ADHD across the lifespan]] |
| Impairment | The functional consequence of symptoms in daily life; how to define and measure it remains an open question in adult ADHD. | [[Lesson - ADHD diagnosis and measurement]] |

## Using the glossary

Read the definition, then open the lesson and find the claim the term belongs to. If a term appears in a source you are reading but not here, add it with the lesson that should own it rather than inventing a definition in a source note (see [[Learning Maintenance]]).
