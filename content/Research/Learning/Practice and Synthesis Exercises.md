# Practice and Synthesis Exercises

These exercises build the skills the curriculum emphasises: tracing a claim to its evidence, reasoning about
confounding, comparing endpoints, and keeping mechanism separate from clinical evidence. Attempt each one before
reading the worked answer, and write your answer down; the writing is where the reasoning shows itself.

## 1. Claim tracing

Choose any supported claim in `Topics/` that cites a source you have not opened. Open the source block, read it,
and decide: does the block say what the claim says it says, or something narrower? Then rewrite the claim so that
it matches the block exactly.

**Worked answer.** In [[Bipolar psychotherapy evidence]], the claim states that adjunctive manualised
psychotherapy was linked to lower recurrence than control, with an odds ratio attached. The block behind it
[[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Miklowitz 2021]]
reports the same association across 20 two-group trials, but it is an average across outpatient samples with
heterogeneous protocols. A trace that keeps the number must also keep "across these trials" and "outpatients";
dropping either turns the group association into a personal prediction, which the source does not support.

## 2. Confounding

The imaging lesson reports thinner cortex in frontal, temporal and parietal regions in bipolar disorder, with
medication exposure associated with the same measures.
[[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|ENIGMA cortical findings]]
List two alternative explanations for the thickness difference and describe the study that could separate them.

**Worked answer.** First, medication exposure: lithium and antipsychotics were associated with cortical measures,
so the difference may partly reflect treatment history rather than illness alone. Second, illness duration and
state at scanning, since longer illness was associated with reduced thickness. A design to separate them would
follow people from first episode with repeated scanning, record treatment exposure prospectively, and compare
patients who differ in medication trajectory while holding illness duration and symptoms as close as possible.
That design is demanding, which is exactly why the observational finding stays a group association.

## 3. Comparing endpoints

An adult ADHD synthesis found that stimulants and atomoxetine beat placebo on core symptoms at 12 weeks, and that
medications were not efficacious on additional outcomes such as quality of life.
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-stimulants|Ostinelli 2025, symptoms]];
[[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Ostinelli 2025, additional outcomes]]
Write a two-sentence summary that would be accurate in both directions.

**Worked answer.** Across 113 randomised trials in adults, stimulants and atomoxetine reduced core ADHD symptoms
at 12 weeks compared with placebo. The same synthesis did not find benefit on secondary outcomes such as quality
of life, so a symptom improvement cannot be reported as a life improvement without evidence about that endpoint.

## 4. Mechanism versus clinical evidence

Lithium affects multiple cellular signalling pathways, and the mechanism that prevents recurrence remains only
partially understood. [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-signalling|Alda 2015, signalling]];
[[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-unknown|Alda 2015, mechanism uncertainty]]
Explain why that uncertainty does not mean the treatment lacks evidence.

**Worked answer.** Mechanism questions and effect questions are answered by different evidence. Randomised trials
estimate whether recurrence changes under treatment, which does not depend on knowing the molecular pathway;
the mechanism answer would explain how the effect arises, and its absence leaves a different gap. Confusing the
two produces the false conclusion that weak mechanism knowledge means weak clinical evidence.

## 5. Absolute and relative effects

An intervention reduces an outcome from 4% to 3%. Another reduces it from 40% to 30%. Both are described as a
"25% reduction". Which description is misleading and why?
[[P26952180 Ranganathan 2016 Absolute and relative risk#^p26952180-rrr|Ranganathan 2016]]

**Worked answer.** The relative reduction is identical, but the absolute changes are one percentage point and
ten percentage points, with numbers needed to treat of 100 and 10 respectively. The relative figure without the
baseline is the misleading one, because it hides how many people actually benefit.

## 6. Classification boundaries

Two trials both claim to study "complex PTSD": one recruits people with ICD-11 complex PTSD, the other recruits
adults exposed to complex trauma who meet a PTSD diagnosis. Both report large effects. Explain why the two
numbers are not directly comparable and what you would record about each.
[[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-caution-category|AI synthesis: Hu 2025, category boundary]];
[[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|VA, symptom-based definitions]]

**Worked answer.** The samples are defined by different constructs: one by a formal ICD-11 diagnosis that adds
self-organization symptoms, the other by trauma exposure plus PTSD criteria. Reported effects describe those
samples, so the populations, instruments and severity distributions differ. Record the diagnostic instrument,
the recruitment definition and the baseline severity for each, and treat the numbers as evidence about two
different groups.

## 7. Build your own

Take a claim you believe after reading a lesson, and write the sentence that would falsify it. Then name the study
design and the sample that sentence implies. If you cannot write a falsifiable version, the belief is probably a
framework rather than a finding, and it belongs in `Arguments/` as an open question.
