# Neuroanatomy and Systems Neuroscience Map

This domain covers the physical organisation of the nervous system and the systems built on it: directions and planes, methods, regional anatomy, and the sensory, motor and association systems that cut across regions.

## Reference overview

Neuroanatomy is the physical description of the nervous system, and systems neuroscience is what
happens when those descriptions are asked to explain function. The domain's discipline is that
structure is the most secure layer of the two: where a structure sits and what it connects to are
observable, whereas what it "does" is an inference that depends on method, species and task.

**Vocabulary before claims.** Directions and planes give a consistent frame for describing
position and section, which is why clinical and radiological descriptions always specify plane and
level; the same vocabulary is what makes a lesion's effect interpretable. [[Anatomical axes and planes]] [[History and localisation]]

**Methods determine what can be claimed.** Staining distinguishes grey from white matter, tracers
establish connection direction in animals, lesion studies provide functional evidence with the
caveat of fibres of passage, and human imaging estimates structure and connection indirectly. A
connectional claim in humans is therefore a statistical inference from a method with known biases,
not a wiring diagram. [[Neuroanatomical methods]] [[White matter and tracts]]

**Regions are hubs, not functions.** Cortex is laminated and columnar; the thalamus relays and
participates in cortical loops; the hypothalamus coordinates endocrine and autonomic output;
hippocampal and amygdala systems support memory and salience; the basal ganglia and cerebellum
shape movement; brainstem and spinal cord carry cranial, segmental and long-tract organisation.
Each of those sentences hides a circuit that has its own article, and the discipline is to name
the circuit rather than the region when making a functional claim. [[Cerebral cortex and lobes]]
[[Cortical layers and columns]] [[Thalamus and hypothalamus]] [[Hippocampal system]] [[Amygdala]]
[[Basal ganglia]] [[Cerebellum and brainstem]] [[Spinal cord]]

**Systems cut across regions.** Sensory pathways run as multi-neuron chains that cross the midline;
motor commands descend through lateral and medial systems; association cortex integrates across
modalities within networks whose statistical structure is a separate level of description from the
anatomy that carries them. [[Sensory and motor systems]] [[Association cortex and networks]]
[[Brain regions and networks]]

**Interoception closes the loop.** Visceral afferents and autonomic efferents make the nervous
system an organ that both controls and senses the body, which is why autonomic anatomy belongs in
this domain rather than only in physiology. [[Interoception and autonomic pathways]] [[Autonomic regulation]]

**Evidence boundaries.** Anatomical description is the most secure material here; connectional
claims in humans are indirect and method-dependent; functional attribution from lesions is limited
by fibres of passage, plasticity and compensation; and network language ('the default mode
network') names a statistical regularity rather than an anatomical object. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]]
[[P22722855 Churchland 2012 Neural population dynamics#^p22722855-claim|Neural population dynamics]]

**Where to start.** [[Anatomical axes and planes]] and [[Neuroanatomical methods]] for the vocabulary, [[Basal ganglia]] and [[Cerebral cortex and lobes]] for regional logic, and [[Sensory and motor systems]] for how the parts cooperate.
