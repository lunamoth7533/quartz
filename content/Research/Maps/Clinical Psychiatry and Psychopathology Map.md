# Clinical Psychiatry and Psychopathology Map

This domain covers the description and classification of psychopathology, the main construct families, and the reasoning that connects symptoms to formulation. It is general reference material, not a self-diagnostic checklist.

## Reference overview

Clinical psychiatry is the study of how distress and dysfunction are described, classified and
explained. Its reference value here is conceptual rather than prescriptive: the domain explains
what a diagnosis is, how classification systems differ, why co-occurrence is the rule rather than
the exception, and where the boundary lies between a construct and a measurement.

**Symptoms, syndromes and diagnoses are three different objects.** A symptom is an experience or
sign; a syndrome is a recurrent pattern of them; a diagnosis is a decision rule that links a
pattern to a category with implications for prognosis and treatment. Every diagnostic system
encodes choices about thresholds and durations, which is why the same presentation can attract
different labels across systems. [[Symptoms syndromes and diagnoses]] [[ICD-11 versus DSM-5 classification]]

**Categorical and dimensional descriptions answer different questions.** Categories simplify
communication and access to care; dimensions preserve information about severity and overlap.
Evidence from taxometric and hierarchical work supports dimensional structure for many
psychopathology constructs, while service systems continue to require categories - so both persist,
and the interesting questions are about calibration between them. [[Categorical versus dimensional classification]] [[Personality constructs and traits]]

**Construct families share features across boundaries.** Mood, anxiety and fear, psychosis,
obsessive-compulsive, dissociative, substance-related and neurodevelopmental constructs are
distinguished by their characteristic features, yet they overlap substantially in symptoms,
genetics and course; network and developmental formulations describe that overlap as a property of
the phenomena rather than a failure of the classification. [[Mood disorders]] [[Anxiety and fear constructs]] [[Psychosis constructs]] [[OCD and related constructs]] [[Dissociative constructs]]
[[Substance-related constructs]] [[Network and developmental formulations]]

**Co-occurrence drives clinical reasoning.** Because conditions co-occur far above chance,
differential reasoning cannot proceed by elimination; it has to weigh base rates, chronology and
which construct explains the most with the fewest assumptions. [[Co-occurrence and differential reasoning]]

**Function, recovery and context are part of the construct.** Impairment is written into most
definitions, functional outcomes are measured separately from symptoms, and social determinants
including stigma act on outcomes through identifiable mechanisms rather than as background noise.
[[Functional outcomes and measurement]] [[Recovery function and social context]] [[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-argument|Stigma as a fundamental cause]]

**Evidence boundaries.** Classification is descriptive, not aetiological; measurement instruments
have documented reliability and validity limits; group-level findings do not diagnose individuals;
and this library is reference material rather than a self-assessment or treatment guide.
[[Measurement validity and reliability]] [[Reviews, guidelines and preprints]]

**Where to start.** [[Symptoms syndromes and diagnoses]] and [[Categorical versus dimensional classification]] for the framework, then the condition hubs [[Bipolar I Map]], [[ADHD Map]], [[Autism Map]] and [[CPTSD Map]]. ## Reference spine

Description has levels. A symptom is a feature, a syndrome is a cluster that co-occurs, and a diagnosis is a named category with specified criteria used for communication, statistics and planning. Defining disorder requires harm or dysfunction and is not settled by unusualness alone. [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-definition|What Are Psychological Disorders?]] [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-harm|What Are Psychological Disorders?]]

Classification is contested ground. Categorical systems are criticised for arbitrary thresholds, unclear boundaries, frequent co-occurrence and heterogeneity within categories; dimensional models such as HiTOP and research frameworks such as RDoC respond to those problems, and network accounts treat symptoms as interacting components instead of indicators of a latent disorder. [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|The Hierarchical Taxonomy of Psychopathology]] [[F121 NIMH RDoC#^f121-purpose|Research Domain Criteria]] [[P28127906 Borsboom 2017 Network theory#^p28127906-claim|A network theory of mental disorders]]

International and national systems differ. ICD-11 gives essential features and boundaries for each category and includes complex PTSD, while DSM-5-TR uses its own criteria and handles that territory differently, so the system must always be stated. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-purpose|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|Complex PTSD history and definitions]]

Construct families are described in their own articles: mood, anxiety and fear, psychosis, obsessive-compulsive, dissociative, neurodevelopmental, substance-related and personality. Each has its own evidence base and its own measurement problems. [[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-patterns|Depression]] [[F81 NIMH Anxiety disorders#^f81-family|Anxiety Disorders]] [[F83 NIMH Schizophrenia#^f83-features|Schizophrenia]] [[F82 NIMH Obsessive-compulsive disorder#^f82-pattern|Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder]] [[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-group|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]] [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-reward|Drugs and the Brain]] [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|Borderline Personality Disorder]]

Context is part of the clinical picture. Stigma operates as a structural cause of inequality through constrained access to resources, and functioning and participation are outcomes in their own right rather than proxies for symptoms. [[P23488505 Hatzenbuehler 2013 Stigma as fundamental cause#^p23488505-argument|Stigma as a fundamental cause of health inequalities]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-framework|International Classification of Functioning]]

## Evidence boundaries

Diagnostic reliability varies by category, and many findings cited in this domain are group-level differences with substantial overlap. Nothing here is a diagnosis for any person or a treatment plan.
