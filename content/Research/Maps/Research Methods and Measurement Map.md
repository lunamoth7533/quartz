# Research Methods and Measurement Map

This domain is the lens for everything else in the library: how studies are designed, how constructs are measured, what causal claims require, and how evidence is synthesised and evaluated.

## Reference overview

Methods is the domain that makes the rest of the reference readable. It covers how studies are
designed, how constructs are operationalised, how causal claims are licensed, and how a body of
evidence is summarised - and its central lesson is that every one of those steps adds an
assumption that can fail independently.

**Designs trade internal against external validity.** Experiments manipulate and randomise, which
licenses causal inference within the studied conditions; observational designs capture what
actually happens at the cost of confounding; qualitative designs access meaning, process and
context that structured instruments miss, with their own evaluative criteria. The three answer
different questions rather than competing on one scale. [[Experimental designs]] [[Observational designs]] [[Qualitative designs]]

**Measurement comes before analysis.** Reliability bounds validity; validity is specific to the
inference being made; invariance testing asks whether the same construct is being measured across
groups; and scale development establishes structure and norms before a score means anything.
[[Measurement validity and reliability]] [[Measurement invariance]]

**Causal language has entry requirements.** Confounding, selection effects, missingness and
reverse causation each generate plausible alternatives to a causal reading; designs that hold
confounders constant, or that exploit instruments such as genetic variants, carry more weight than
adjustment in observational data alone. [[Causality and counterfactuals]] [[Bias and confounding]] [[Evidence types and causal inference]]

**Uncertainty is quantifiable and routinely ignored.** Effect sizes with intervals, absolute as
well as relative measures, and explicit statements about what a null result can and cannot mean
are the working tools; a non-significant contrast is not evidence of equivalence. [[Effect sizes and uncertainty]] [[P27913917 Network meta-analysis introduction#^p27913917-assumption|Network meta-analysis: an introduction for clinicians]]

**Synthesis changes the question.** Meta-analysis pools estimates under assumptions about
comparability and bias; network meta-analysis adds a transitivity assumption and yields rankings
whose uncertainty is easily understated; component analyses estimate average contributions of
packaged interventions rather than the effect of an ingredient. [[Meta-analysis and review limits]]
[[Trial endpoints, benefit and harms]]

**Integrity is part of method.** Publication bias, analytic flexibility, low power and
non-reproducible pipelines distort the evidential base before any single study is read; open
practice, preregistration and reporting standards exist to make those distortions visible.
[[Replication and publication bias]] [[Reviews, guidelines and preprints]] [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-practice|Neuroimaging reproducibility]]
[[P26315443 Open Science Collaboration 2015 Reproducibility#^p26315443-result|Reproducibility]]

**Evidence boundaries.** Methods advice is not a truth machine: a well-analysed study can still be
wrong, and translation from one population, species or measurement system to another is an
assumption that must be argued. [[Translational validity]] [[Association versus individual prediction]] [[Longitudinal and within-person inference]]

**Where to start.** [[Experimental designs]], [[Observational designs]] and [[Qualitative designs]] for design; [[Measurement validity and reliability]] for instruments; [[Effect sizes and uncertainty]] and [[Replication and publication bias]] for inference. ## Reference spine

Designs determine claims. Randomised experiments support causal inference about an intervention because assignment breaks the link with confounders; observational designs describe associations that must be argued into causal ones; qualitative designs answer questions about meaning and process. [[F09 Noba Research designs#^f09-designs|Research designs]] [[F09 Noba Research designs#^f09-confounds|Research designs]] [[P18626033 Qualitative research criteria#^p18626033-criteria|Evaluative criteria for qualitative research in health care]]

Measurement comes before comparison. Reliability and validity are separate properties, and comparing scores across groups or time requires measurement invariance; without it a group difference may be a difference in the instrument. [[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-validity|Developing and validating scales]] [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-levels|Measurement invariance conventions and reporting]]

Synthesis has standards. PRISMA specifies what a systematic review must report, GRADE separates certainty from recommendation strength, and network meta-analysis adds indirect comparisons under the transitivity assumption. [[P33782057 PRISMA 2020#^p33782057-purpose|PRISMA 2020]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]] [[P27913917 Network meta-analysis introduction#^p27913917-assumption|Network meta-analysis]]

Effect interpretation is a discipline of its own. Absolute, relative and standardised effects answer different questions, significance is not magnitude, and a group association is not an individual prediction. [[P23997866 Sullivan 2012 Using effect size#^p23997866-magnitude|Using effect size]] [[P23997866 Sullivan 2012 Using effect size#^p23997866-significance|Using effect size]] [[P26952180 Ranganathan 2016 Absolute and relative risk#^p26952180-arr|Absolute and relative risk]]

Integrity practices address documented problems. Published effects are typically larger than replication effects, and the mechanisms - low power, analytic flexibility, selective reporting, publication incentives - are systemic rather than individual. Preregistration, registered reports and open data are the responses. [[P26315443 Open Science Collaboration 2015 Reproducibility#^p26315443-result|Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science]] [[P33954258 Munafo 2017 Reproducible science#^p33954258-metadata|A manifesto for reproducible science]] [[P29531091 Nosek 2018 Preregistration revolution#^p29531091-distinction|The preregistration revolution]]

## Evidence boundaries

Methodological guidance defines standards rather than producing findings: a checklist is not evidence about a treatment. Tools such as GRADE rate certainty; they do not create it. [[F22 Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions#^f22-caution-authority|AI appraisal: Cochrane Handbook]]

## Where the method argument is won

Three habits make the difference between a finding and a claim. First, name the design before the result: a randomised comparison supports a causal claim about the contrast actually tested, while an observational estimate describes a sample and leaves confounding paths open. Second, separate the levels - description, association, prediction, mechanism - because each needs different evidence, and most overstatement happens when a descriptive result is reported as a mechanism. Third, report uncertainty with the outcome in its own units: effect sizes can be statistically conclusive and trivial, and certainty grades belong beside the estimate rather than in a footnote. The articles below develop each of these; the measurement articles add the fourth habit, which is checking that the instrument means the same thing in the populations being compared.
