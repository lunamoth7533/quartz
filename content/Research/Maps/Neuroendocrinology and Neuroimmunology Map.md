# Neuroendocrinology and Neuroimmunology Map

This domain covers the signalling systems that connect the brain to the rest of the body: endocrine axes, autonomic pathways, circadian timing and immune signalling.

## Reference overview

This domain covers the three systems that connect the brain to the rest of the body: the endocrine
axes that release hormones, the autonomic nervous system that controls organs within seconds, and
the immune signalling that crosses the barrier. The organising idea is that these are not
peripheral support systems; they are inputs to brain function whose state changes what neural
circuits do.

**The HPA axis is a feedback loop with two timescales.** Hypothalamic releasing hormone drives
pituitary adrenocorticotropic hormone, which drives adrenal cortisol, and cortisol restrains
further release at several sites. Secretion is pulsatile and circadian, effects run from rapid
membrane-associated actions to slower genomic ones, and the same hormone acts differently at
different concentrations because two receptor populations differ in affinity. [[HPA axis]]
[[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|Glucocorticoids and stress]]

**Chronic stress is a construct, not a measurement.** Allostatic load names the cumulative cost of
repeated adjustment through mediators such as cortisol and catecholamines. It is built from
multiple measurements over time; no single sample reports it, and single values are weak indices
because time of day, posture, waking time and medication all move them. [[Allostasis and chronic stress]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-load|Stress mediators]]

**The autonomic arm is fast and measurable only indirectly.** Sympathetic and parasympathetic
outflows cooperate as well as oppose, and their central control comes from a distributed network.
Peripheral indices such as heart-rate variability reflect the net effect of several inputs on one
organ, and their values depend on recording length, posture, breathing, medication and age - which
is why they are estimates of a state rather than readouts of a nerve. [[Autonomic regulation]]
[[Autonomic measurement and heart rate variability]] [[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-caution-marker|AI synthesis: Heart rate variability metrics]]

**Timing systems couple the two.** Circadian clocks in the brain and periphery set daily
structure, and sleep timing is regulated by the clock interacting with homeostatic sleep drive;
that is the mechanism behind rhythm-based clinical strategies. [[Circadian clock biology]]
[[Circadian rhythms and sleep]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]]

**The immune interface is real but not a one-way explanation.** Microglia survey the parenchyma
continuously, inflammatory signalling produces sickness behaviour, and the barrier is selective
rather than absolute. Associations between immune markers and psychiatric conditions are robust in
aggregate and heterogeneous in detail, which is exactly the pattern expected when a common
signalling system is measured in heterogeneous conditions. [[Neuroimmune interactions and microglia]] [[Cytokines and inflammation in psychiatric conditions]] [[Blood-brain barrier]]
[[Microbiome-gut-brain axis]]

**Evidence boundaries.** Most mechanistic work is animal or cell-based; human studies are
correlational and often cross-sectional; and a marker associated with a condition at group level
does not measure that condition in an individual. [[HPA axis]] [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-limit|Inflammation to sickness and depression]]

**Where to start.** [[HPA axis]] and [[Allostasis and chronic stress]] for the stress system, [[Autonomic measurement and heart rate variability]] for measurement, and [[Cytokines and inflammation in psychiatric conditions]] for the immune interface. ## Reference spine

The stress system has two arms. The sympathetic response is fast and neural; the HPA axis is slower and hormonal, ending in cortisol release with feedback on hypothalamus and pituitary. What glucocorticoids do during stress is contested, and the classification into permissive, suppressive, stimulatory and preparative actions is a proposal rather than a settled taxonomy. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-axis|What is Stress]] [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-drive|The Adrenal Glands]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|How do glucocorticoids influence stress responses?]]

Autonomic control is distributed. A central autonomic network links visceral input to sympathetic and parasympathetic output and to endocrine and limbic circuitry, and heart rate variability reflects several influences rather than one. [[F63 Neuroscience Online central autonomic control#^f63-network|Central Control of the Autonomic Nervous System]] [[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-metrics|Heart rate variability metrics]]

Timing is molecular and distributed. The core clock is a transcriptional feedback loop, most tissues have their own clocks, and outputs extend to metabolism and immune function, which is why circadian disruption has effects beyond sleep. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Transcriptional architecture of the mammalian circadian clock]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-outputs|Transcriptional architecture of the mammalian circadian clock]]

Immune signalling reaches the brain through defined interfaces. Microglia survey continuously and respond within minutes to barrier disruption; peripheral immune signals produce sickness behaviour, and prolonged signalling is proposed to contribute to depressive symptoms in a subset. [[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-surveillance|Resting microglial cells are highly dynamic surveillants]] [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-sickness|From inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-subset|The role of inflammation in depression]]

Chronicity has a framework. Allostasis describes stability through change, and allostatic load describes the cost of repeated activation, with the brain as both regulator and target. [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-allostasis|Protective and damaging effects of stress mediators]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-load|Protective and damaging effects of stress mediators]]

## Evidence boundaries

Hormone and autonomic physiology are well characterised; immune contributions to psychiatric conditions are largely correlational in humans, and single-measure indices of chronic activity are weak.
