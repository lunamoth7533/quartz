# Neurochemistry Map

Neurochemistry here means signalling systems and the stress and sleep regulation they support, with one-transmitter explanations treated as oversimplified.

**Contents.** [[#Reference overview|Overview]], [[#Reference spine|Reference spine]], [[#Concept register|Concept register]], [[#Where this domain connects|Cross-domain links]], [[#Evidence boundaries|Evidence boundaries]], [[#Learning route|Learning route]], [[#Core sequence|Core sequence]]

## Reference overview

Neurochemistry is the study of the molecules that carry and shape neural signalling, and of the
systems that produce them. Its central lesson is that a transmitter is not a message: the message
is the combination of the transmitter, the receptors present, the circuit state and the timescale.
Every practical rule in this domain follows from that decomposition.

**A life cycle with several intervention points.** Small-molecule transmitters are synthesised
locally from precursors, packaged into vesicles, released on calcium entry, and cleared by reuptake
or enzymatic breakdown. Each step is a control point: precursor supply, synthetic enzymes,
packaging, release probability, receptors, transporters and degradative enzymes. Knowing which step
a drug touches predicts its onset and duration better than its class name does. [[Transmitter synthesis, release and clearance]] [[Monoamine reuptake and degradation]]

**Receptor families convert chemistry into physiology.** Ionotropic receptors act within
milliseconds and locally; metabotropic receptors act through second messengers over seconds to
hours and can change what a cell will do next. Because the same transmitter binds receptors of
opposite sign, direction is a property of the synapse rather than of the molecule, and because
selectivity is graded rather than absolute, off-target effects are the rule in pharmacology.
[[Receptor families and second messengers]] [[Pharmacodynamics and receptors]]

**The named systems differ in geometry.** Dopamine, noradrenaline, serotonin and acetylcholine
arise from small, widely projecting populations and act as modulators as much as transmitters;
glutamate and GABA carry most fast point-to-point transmission; histamine and adenosine report
motor and metabolic state; peptides and endocannabinoids signal outside the classical
point-to-point frame, one by volume transmission and the other by retrograde feedback.
[[Dopamine signalling]] [[Noradrenaline signalling]] [[Serotonin signalling]] [[Acetylcholine signalling]] [[Glutamate and GABA]] [[Histamine signalling]] [[Adenosine signalling]]
[[Neuropeptides and opioids]] [[Endocannabinoid signalling]]

**State is part of the mechanism.** Neuromodulators change the properties of cells and synapses, so
the same anatomical circuit can produce different outputs, and circuits with different parameters
can produce similar outputs. Transmitter effects are therefore state-dependent by construction,
and a result obtained in one arousal or behavioural state does not transfer automatically to
another. [[Neuromodulation and circuit state]] [[Dynamical systems models]]

**Evidence boundaries.** The most quantitative claims in this domain come from reduced
preparations; receptor-level effects in tissue do not translate directly into clinical effect
sizes, and the monoamine-deficit framing of psychiatric conditions is not supported by the
pharmacology that made it popular. Chemical-imbalance statements should be rewritten as statements
about treatment targets and receptor-level actions. [[Chemical imbalance framing]] [[Synaptic signalling model]] [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|How do antidepressants work]]

**Where to start.** [[Transmitter synthesis, release and clearance]] for the life cycle, [[Receptor families and second messengers]] for the decision point, and [[Neuromodulation and circuit state]] for why transmitter effects depend on context. ## Reference spine

Neurochemistry studies the molecules that carry and shape neural signalling, and the machinery that makes, releases, receives and clears them. Its central lesson is that transmitter identity is the beginning of an explanation rather than the end.

The life cycle is shared across small-molecule transmitters: synthesis by specific enzymes, packaging into vesicles, calcium-triggered release, receptor binding, and clearance by transporters or degradation. [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-classes|Biogenic Amine Neurotransmitters]] [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-calcium|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] [[F108 Neuroscience Online acetylcholine#^f108-inactivation|Acetylcholine Neurotransmission]] Peptides differ: they are processed from precursors in successive steps and act beyond a single contact. [[F107 Neuroscience Online neuropeptides and nitric oxide#^f107-processing|Neuropeptides and Nitric Oxide]] [[F107 Neuroscience Online neuropeptides and nitric oxide#^f107-transmission|Neuropeptides and Nitric Oxide]]

Receptor identity decides effect. Ionotropic receptors produce fast depolarisation or hyperpolarisation; metabotropic receptors act through second messengers, changing excitability or transcription over longer windows. The same transmitter can excite in one place and inhibit in another, and dopamine's D1- and D2-type receptors are the standard illustration. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]

Each major system has a characteristic organisation. Histamine neurons sit in the tuberomamillary nucleus and are active during waking, with H3 autoreceptors limiting their own release. [[P18626069 Haas 2008 Histamine in the nervous system#^p18626069-source|Histamine in the nervous system]] [[P18626069 Haas 2008 Histamine in the nervous system#^p18626069-receptors|Histamine in the nervous system]] Dopamine neurons project through several systems and carry signals that include reward prediction error without being reducible to it. [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-dopamine|Biogenic Amine Neurotransmitters]] [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-rpe|Reward prediction error]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]] Endocannabinoids are synthesised on demand and act retrogradely, showing how far signalling departs from a point-to-point model. [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-synthesis|An Introduction to the Endogenous Cannabinoid System]] [[P26698193 Lu 2016 Endogenous cannabinoid system#^p26698193-retrograde|An Introduction to the Endogenous Cannabinoid System]]

Modulation then changes what a circuit does rather than what it says. Neuromodulators alter neuronal and synaptic properties across populations, so the same anatomy produces different outputs in different states. [[P23040802 Marder 2012 Neuromodulation#^p23040802-reconfigure|Neuromodulation of neuronal circuits]] [[P23040802 Marder 2012 Neuromodulation#^p23040802-state|Neuromodulation of neuronal circuits]]

## Evidence boundaries

Transmitter pharmacology is precisely established, and claims that a psychiatric condition is a transmitter imbalance are generally not supported by that precision. ## Evidence gaps

- Transmitter-level findings rarely identify a single cause for a psychiatric condition.
- Cortisol and other biomarkers vary with time of day and context, complicating single-sample claims.
