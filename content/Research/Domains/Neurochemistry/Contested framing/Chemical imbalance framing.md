# Chemical imbalance framing

**Definition.** One-transmitter explanations treat mood or behaviour as the result of a chemical being high or low. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-caution-framing|Appraisal: Cells of the Nervous System]]

## Supported claims

- The psychology chapter presents the imbalance perspective and also documents receptor-dependent mixed effects; it does not resolve whether transmitter levels explain disorders. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-caution-framing|Appraisal: Cells of the Nervous System]]
- The dopamine model for bipolar disorder is presented by its authors as speculation. [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-speculative|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]
- Lithium acts at multiple cellular signalling nodes rather than at one receptor. [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-signalling|Lithium pharmacology review]]

## How it works

**Origin and appeal.** The chemical-imbalance framing is a popular simplification of the monoamine hypotheses of mood disorders: that psychiatric conditions reflect too much or too little of a transmitter - depression resulting from deficient serotonergic, noradrenergic or dopaminergic transmission, with serotonin in depression the most familiar version - and that drugs work by correcting the deficit. It is a cultural shorthand that spread from pharmacological explanation into public understanding because it is simple, because it makes drug action look like replacement therapy, and because it gives patients a physiological story that removes blame. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-caution-framing|Appraisal: Cells of the Nervous System]]

**Why it does not survive contact with the pharmacology.** Monoamine-based accounts explain acute pharmacological action poorly relative to the weeks-long clinical time course: increasing transmitter availability happens within hours, while clinical improvement typically takes weeks. That mismatch is one of the main arguments against a simple deficit-and-replacement model - monoamine elevation alone is not an adequate mechanistic account - and it motivated emotional-processing and plasticity accounts as complements rather than replacements. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|How do antidepressants work]] [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-processing|How do antidepressants work]] [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-plasticity|How do antidepressants work]] Reviews of dopamine's role in bipolar disorder likewise find state-dependent and inconsistent patterns rather than a single direction. [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-state|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]

**Receptor identity breaks the direction claim.** The same transmitter can depolarise or hyperpolarise depending on the receptor, and D1-type and D2-type dopamine receptors can have opposing actions. A statement of the form 'this condition involves too little X' therefore has no coherent physiological meaning without naming the receptor, the circuit and the timescale. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-transmitters|Cells of the Nervous System]]

**What can be said instead.** Transmitters, receptors and circuits are genuinely involved in psychiatric conditions, and drugs that act on them genuinely help some people: they change the system's signalling, and those changes are measurable. The mapping from transmitter-level change to symptom change is mediated by downstream adaptation and is condition-, drug- and outcome-specific. The evidence-consistent summary is therefore: transmitter systems are targets of effective treatments, and transmitter-level deficits are not established causes of the conditions themselves - the evidence does not support a general claim that a specific chemical level explains the condition. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-limit|How do antidepressants work]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-limit|Communication Between Neurons]] The educational source in this library presents the imbalance view as one perspective among others and documents the criticisms alongside it. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-caution-framing|Appraisal: Cells of the Nervous System]]

**Why the distinction matters practically.** A framing that equates a condition with a measurable deficit invites two mistakes: assuming a blood test or scan can diagnose it, and assuming that 'correcting' a level predicts response. Neither follows: diagnostic practice rests on clinical criteria, and response is established by trials in populations. [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]]

**Why precision here is not pedantry.** Patients who were told a monoamine story often feel misled when they encounter the updates, and clinicians inherit the job of unwinding it. Writing the accurate version - treatment target, not demonstrated cause - avoids both the false mechanism and the opposite overcorrection that suggests drugs work by no mechanism at all. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-limit|Cells of the Nervous System]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** This framing is the pharmacological version of a claim that also appears in genetics and neuroimaging in this library - each domain has its own version of "reduce a condition to one measurable quantity" - and the same critical rule applies: name the pathway, the time course and what the measure actually indexes before treating the simplified story as an explanation. ## Limitation or common misconception

Rejecting the slogan does not make neurochemistry irrelevant: medicines act on signalling systems, while the path from receptor to experience is indirect.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Molecular Psychiatry).** Shows what newer serotonin evidence can and cannot carry: cortical 5-HT2A binding was lower in antidepressant-free depression and tracked severity. [[P41053435 Chapman 2025 Cortical 5-HT2A receptors in depression#^p41053435-invivo|Chapman 2025]] [[P41053435 Chapman 2025 Cortical 5-HT2A receptors in depression#^p41053435-severity|Chapman 2025]] Post-mortem studies were mostly negative, and treatment appeared to lower binding further. [[P41053435 Chapman 2025 Cortical 5-HT2A receptors in depression#^p41053435-postmortem|Chapman 2025]] This implicates the serotonin system without measuring a serotonin level or showing a deficit that causes depression. [[P41053435 Chapman 2025 Cortical 5-HT2A receptors in depression#^p41053435-caution-direction|Appraisal: Chapman 2025]]
- **2023 · Commentary (Molecular Psychiatry).** A reply to the serotonin umbrella review first published in 2022 argues that its finding of no consistent evidence was overstated. [[P37322065 Jauhar 2023 Critique of the serotonin umbrella review#^p37322065-limit|Jauhar 2023]] [[P37322065 Jauhar 2023 Critique of the serotonin umbrella review#^p37322065-critique|Jauhar 2023]] The authors cite the review's methods, selective reporting, over-simplification and misread tryptophan-depletion and molecular-imaging work. [[P37322065 Jauhar 2023 Critique of the serotonin umbrella review#^p37322065-reasons|Jauhar 2023]] Both sides can be right about different claims, because 'implicated' is not 'deficient'. [[P37322065 Jauhar 2023 Critique of the serotonin umbrella review#^p37322065-caution-claims|Appraisal: Jauhar 2023]]
