# Amino Acid Neurotransmitters

**Publisher.** Neuroscience Online, McGovern Medical School (UTHealth), Section 1 Chapter 13

**Source.** <https://nba.uth.tmc.edu/neuroscience/m/s1/chapter13.html>

**Verification.** text opened and checked on 2026-09-25; retrieval saved as `receipts/source-checks/F106.txt`. Educational or official reference material, not primary research.

## Key points (as published)

Excitatory transmission, producing EPSPs, is mediated largely by the acidic amino acid glutamate.
^f106-excitatory

Inhibitory transmission is mediated primarily by glycine in the spinal cord and by GABA, a glutamate metabolite, in the brain.
^f106-inhibitory

The chapter distinguishes excitatory and inhibitory synapses by morphology and spatial arrangement on the target cell, which affects how inputs summate.
^f106-spatial

## Scope and limitations

A textbook chapter on amino acid transmission. It presents the canonical excitatory/inhibitory division, which does not cover every signalling role of these molecules.
^f106-limit
