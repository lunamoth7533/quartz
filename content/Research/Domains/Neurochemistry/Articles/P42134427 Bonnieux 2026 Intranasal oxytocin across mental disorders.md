# Does intranasal oxytocin reduce symptoms of mental disorders? A meta-analysis of clinical trials.

**Authors.** Bonnieux J, Gumuchian ST, et al. (14 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42134427/)

**Published.** 2026 in Neuroscience and biobehavioral reviews (first online 2026-05-13).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2026.106749](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2026.106749) · PMID [42134427](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42134427/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Pooled across 42 trials, oxytocin's advantage over placebo was small and not statistically significant (g = 0.17, 95% CI -0.05 to 0.38), with large between-trial heterogeneity (I² = 77.4%).
^p42134427-overall

Dropping two substance-use trials with very large effects cut the estimate to g = 0.05 (95% CI -0.03 to 0.12) and removed the heterogeneity.
^p42134427-outliers

Analysed by diagnosis, only schizophrenia-spectrum trials showed a small significant effect (g = 0.12); dose, number of administrations, added psychosocial treatment and age did not moderate results, while trials with more women showed larger effects.
^p42134427-moderators

The authors note that trial methods rarely matched optimal protocols.
^p42134427-methods

## Scope as reported

A random-effects meta-analysis of double-blind randomised trials comparing intranasal oxytocin with placebo on symptom outcomes across several diagnoses (pooled N = 1,922); diagnoses, protocols and outcome measures varied between trials.
^p42134427-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.
