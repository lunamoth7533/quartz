# Cortical 5-HT2A receptors in depression and suicide: a systematic review and meta-analysis of in vivo and post-mortem imaging studies.

**Authors.** Chapman GE, Turner G, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41053435/)

**Published.** 2025 in Molecular psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41380-025-03233-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03233-4) · PMID [41053435](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41053435/) · PMCID [PMC12602341](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12602341/)

**Verification.** Full text read via PubMed Central (PMC12602341) on 2026-09-27, with metadata matched by PMID; figures, tables and supplementary material were not examined. Read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked full text)

In living patients free of antidepressants for at least a week, cortical 5-HT2A binding was lower than in controls in frontal, prefrontal, cingulate and anterior cingulate cortex, and in temporal cortex on sensitivity analysis, with moderate effect sizes; most studies used antagonist or inverse-agonist tracers.
^p41053435-invivo

In frontal and cingulate regions the study-level effect tracked baseline depression severity, and exploratory pooling of never-medicated patients found lower binding in anterior cingulate cortex.
^p41053435-severity

Post-mortem binding studies were almost entirely negative for depression and for suicide, and within-patient studies suggested that antidepressant treatment lowers binding further.
^p41053435-postmortem

## Scope as reported

Ten in vivo case-control studies (188 patients, 170 controls) and 19 post-mortem studies; the authors note that several analyses were underpowered, that washout periods and tracer pharmacology varied, and that post-mortem methods were heterogeneous.
^p41053435-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Lower receptor binding is a receptor-level group difference whose meaning depends on tracer pharmacology; it is not a measure of serotonin level and not evidence that a serotonin deficit causes depression.
^p41053435-caution-direction
