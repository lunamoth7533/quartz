# Intranasal Oxytocin in Children and Adolescents with Autism Spectrum Disorder.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Over 24 weeks, change on the primary outcome (Aberrant Behavior Checklist modified Social Withdrawal subscale) was essentially the same with oxytocin and placebo: least-squares mean difference -0.2 (95% CI -1.5 to 1.0; P = 0.61).
^p34644471-primary

Secondary social and cognitive measures generally did not separate the groups, and adverse events were alike in frequency and severity.
^p34644471-secondary

The trial followed laboratory studies and small trials that had suggested benefit, at a time when many autistic children were already receiving oxytocin in practice.
^p34644471-rationale

## Scope as reported

A multicentre phase 2 trial (SOARS-B) randomising participants aged 3-17 years, stratified by age and verbal fluency; 277 participants with a baseline and at least one follow-up rating formed the modified intention-to-treat sample.
^p34644471-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A large null result on the prespecified outcome weighs against a general social benefit; it cannot exclude effects in particular subgroups, on other outcome measures or with other delivery methods, which remain hypotheses needing their own trials.
