# Excitatory/inhibitory imbalance in autism: the role of glutamate and GABA gene-sets in symptoms and cortical brain structure.

**Authors.** Hollestein V, Poelmans G, et al. (24 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36681677/)

**Published.** 2023 in Translational psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41398-023-02317-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-023-02317-5) · PMID [36681677](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36681677/) · PMCID [PMC9867712](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9867712/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Aggregated common variation in a glutamate gene-set was associated with every autism symptom severity score on the ADOS-2 and ADI-R within the autistic group.
^p36681677-symptoms

In adolescents and adults, cortical regions with higher expression of glutamate and of GABA genes showed larger autistic-neurotypical differences in cortical thickness, in opposite directions for the two gene-sets, and expression profiles also tracked thickness in sensory subgroups.
^p36681677-cortex

## Scope as reported

Observational multi-site cohort; cortical gene expression comes from reference profiles rather than participants' own tissue.
^p36681677-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Gene-set associations show that glutamate- and GABA-related variation relate differently to symptoms and structure; they do not measure synaptic excitation or inhibition, so they cannot confirm a single excitation-inhibition imbalance.
^p36681677-caution-distinct
