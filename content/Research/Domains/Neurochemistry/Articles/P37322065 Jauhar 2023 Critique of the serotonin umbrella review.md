# A leaky umbrella has little value: evidence clearly indicates the serotonin system is implicated in depression.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The authors respond to a recent umbrella review that had found no consistent evidence linking serotonin to the pathophysiology of depression, and argue that this conclusion is overstated.
^p37322065-critique

They attribute the overstatement to weaknesses in the review's methods, selective reporting of data, over-simplification and misreading of neuropsychopharmacological findings, and use tryptophan depletion and serotonergic molecular imaging as their worked examples.
^p37322065-reasons

## Scope as reported

A commentary on the umbrella review of serotonin and depression first published online in 2022; it argues from selected literatures rather than presenting a new systematic synthesis.
^p37322065-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Part of the dispute is which claim is under test: 'the serotonin system is implicated in depression' and 'low serotonin causes depression' are different propositions, and evidence for the first does not rescue the second.
^p37322065-caution-claims
