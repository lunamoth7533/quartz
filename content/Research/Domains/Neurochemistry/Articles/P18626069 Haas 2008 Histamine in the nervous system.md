## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Brain histaminergic neurons sit exclusively in the tuberomamillary nucleus of the posterior hypothalamus and project throughout the central nervous system.
^p18626069-source

They are active during waking and contribute to wakefulness and attention, linking a single transmitter to sleep-wake regulation.
^p18626069-waking

H1 and H2 receptor actions are mostly excitatory while H3 receptors act as inhibitory auto- and heteroreceptors, so the same transmitter can oppose itself.
^p18626069-receptors
