## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The authors describe the locus coeruleus as a small brainstem group of noradrenaline-containing neurons, conserved across vertebrates, that projects throughout the nervous system and bears on arousal and learning.
^p38842506-conserved

Because recordings are spreading beyond rats and monkeys to other species, they propose shared anatomical and electrophysiological criteria for deciding that a recorded or imaged brainstem neuron belongs to the nucleus.
^p38842506-criteria

## Scope as reported

Review with consensus recommendations for animal recording and imaging work; it does not address human imaging.
^p38842506-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

If identifying the nucleus in an animal recording needs explicit criteria, human proxies such as pupil size or self-rated arousal need at least as much validation before they are read as locus coeruleus activity.
^p38842506-caution-identity
