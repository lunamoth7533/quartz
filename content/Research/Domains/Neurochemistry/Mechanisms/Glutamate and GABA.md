# Glutamate and GABA

**Definition.** Glutamate and GABA are the main excitatory and inhibitory amino-acid transmitters of the adult central nervous system. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]

## Supported claims

- Glutamate receptors depolarise adult postsynaptic cells while glycine and GABA receptors hyperpolarise them. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]
- Autism risk-gene expression was enriched in excitatory and inhibitory lineages, consistent with multiple imbalance paths. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing study]]

## How it works

**Who does what.** Excitatory transmission producing EPSPs is mediated largely by glutamate, which carries most fast excitatory transmission; inhibitory transmission is mediated primarily by GABA in the brain, which carries most fast inhibition there, and by glycine in the spinal cord. Together they set the moment-to-moment excitatory/inhibitory state of a circuit, and cortical excitability is framed as a balance between the two. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-excitatory|Amino Acid Transmitters]] [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-inhibitory|Amino Acid Transmitters]]

**Metabolic coupling.** GABA is itself a glutamate metabolite - glutamate is its precursor - and the two are linked through the tricarboxylic acid cycle, so the two channels are metabolically linked rather than independent systems, which is why they are treated as one system. Manipulating one pathway can change the other, and the enzymes that interconvert them are a pharmacological target. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-inhibitory|Amino Acid Transmitters]] [[P12467378 Petroff 2002 GABA and glutamate#^p12467378-metabolism|GABA and glutamate]] GABA synthesis depends on two isoforms of its rate-controlling enzyme, and human GABA metabolism is distinctive: human brain has unusually high concentrations of two GABA metabolites with anticonvulsant properties, homocarnosine among them - one of several ways in which human GABA metabolism differs from the standard animal model. [[P12467378 Petroff 2002 GABA and glutamate#^p12467378-metabolism|GABA and glutamate]] [[P12467378 Petroff 2002 GABA and glutamate#^p12467378-human|GABA and glutamate]]

**Recycling between cell types.** Glutamate released at synapses is cleared by transporters and re-supplied to neurons largely through the glutamine cycle, in which a glial enzyme converts it to glutamine that neurons take up and convert back. This is the clearest case in the transmitter world where the recycling step is a shared tissue function rather than a presynaptic property, and it explains why astrocytic function shows up in excitatory transmission claims. [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-support|Blood Brain Barrier]] [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-requirements|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]]

**Receptor families with opposite kinetics.** Both transmitters act on ionotropic and metabotropic receptors. Glutamate acts through ionotropic receptors that depolarise and through metabotropic receptors that modulate; GABA acts through ionotropic receptors that hyperpolarise and metabotropic receptors that act more slowly. The result is that each transmitter can produce fast and slow effects in the same tissue, and drugs that target one family leave the other intact; the metabotropic receptors are current drug targets, which is why the pair matters far beyond electrophysiology. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]

**Spatial logic.** Inhibitory synapses are placed to control specific inputs and the site of generation of the spike, not simply to subtract excitation globally. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-spatial|Amino acid transmitters]]

**From molecules to a balance claim.** Because the same transmitter can act at receptors of opposite sign and because inhibitory contacts differ in where they land, an E/I statement requires a level of description: single cell, local circuit or network. The chapter's own caveat is that the canonical excitatory/inhibitory division is a teaching frame that does not cover every signalling role of these amino acids. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-spatial|Amino Acid Transmitters]] [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-limit|Amino Acid Transmitters]]

**Where pharmacology sits.** Benzodiazepines act as positive modulators at GABA-A receptors, enhancing GABA-A receptor function and inhibitory signalling rather than activating the receptor directly, whereas most psychoactive drugs are receptor or transporter ligands. The distinction between an agonist and an allosteric modulator is not cosmetic: the modulator's effect depends on how much endogenous transmitter is present, which is why its effects vary with state. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-mechanism|Benzodiazepine pharmacology]] [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How do medicines work]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Excitatory/inhibitory balance is the physiological substrate that the neuroanatomy and computational-brain-theories domains use when they describe network arithmetic; a change in glutamate/GABA ratio at the cellular level can only be translated into a network-level claim once the four E/I-balance meanings described elsewhere are kept distinct. [[Excitation and inhibition balance]] [[Network and developmental formulations]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Excitation and inhibition are often described in broad terms; local measurement is difficult and findings do not transfer across conditions.

## Recent research

- **2023 · Systematic review (Molecular Psychiatry).** Supports the 'level of description' point above with post-mortem data from depression: GABAergic and glutamatergic alterations appear across many regions. [[P37495889 Hu 2023 Excitation-inhibition topography in depression#^p37495889-brainwide|Hu 2023]] Their direction differs by region, with weaker GABAergic and stronger glutamatergic signalling in limbic areas but higher GABAergic signalling in lateral prefrontal cortex. [[P37495889 Hu 2023 Excitation-inhibition topography in depression#^p37495889-topography|Hu 2023]] Expression in post-mortem tissue is only a proxy for signalling in living circuits. [[P37495889 Hu 2023 Excitation-inhibition topography in depression#^p37495889-caution-proxy|Appraisal: Hu 2023]]
- **2023 · Cohort study (Translational Psychiatry).** In a multi-site autism cohort, variation in a glutamate gene-set tracked symptom severity. [[P36681677 Hollestein 2023 Glutamate and GABA gene-sets in autism#^p36681677-limit|Hollestein 2023]] [[P36681677 Hollestein 2023 Glutamate and GABA gene-sets in autism#^p36681677-symptoms|Hollestein 2023]] Glutamate and GABA gene expression related to cortical thickness differences in opposite directions. [[P36681677 Hollestein 2023 Glutamate and GABA gene-sets in autism#^p36681677-cortex|Hollestein 2023]] Gene-set associations do not measure excitation or inhibition, so they cannot confirm a single imbalance. [[P36681677 Hollestein 2023 Glutamate and GABA gene-sets in autism#^p36681677-caution-distinct|Appraisal: Hollestein 2023]]

## Connections

- [[Excitation and inhibition balance]] - the network-level concept built from these two transmitters; its several meanings must be kept apart before a cellular finding is read as imbalance.
- [[Action potentials]] - the spike these transmitters push toward or away from threshold, and whose initiation site inhibitory synapses are placed to control.
- [[Autism genetics and rare variants]] - where the excitatory and inhibitory lineages enter condition genetics, with autism risk-gene expression enriched in both.
- [[Neuromodulation and circuit state]] - the slower layer acting on these fast channels; modulators change neuronal and synaptic properties, so one wiring diagram can yield different outputs.
- [[Receptor families and second messengers]] - the ionotropic versus metabotropic split that gives both transmitters fast and slow effects in the same tissue.
- [[Ketamine and rapid-acting antidepressants]] - ketamine blocks NMDA receptors, and rapid-acting drugs are proposed to converge on stronger excitatory synapses.
