# Dopamine signalling

**Definition.** Dopamine is a catecholamine transmitter with roles in movement, motivation, reward processing and cognitive control depending on the pathway. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-transmitters|Cells of the Nervous System]]

## Supported claims

- Dopamine receptor subtypes can have opposite effects: D1 receptors are excitatory while D2-type receptors are inhibitory. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]
- In bipolar research, elevated D2/3 availability accompanies mania while increased dopamine transporter levels accompany depression, with inconsistent findings elsewhere. [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-state|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]
- Both dopamine agonists and antidopaminergics have improved bipolar depressive symptoms in pharmacological studies. [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-pharmacology|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]]

## How it works

**Three projection systems, three jobs.** Dopamine neurons sit in the midbrain and project through several systems: mesostriatal (to the striatum, involved in movement, action selection and reinforcement learning), mesolimbocortical (to limbic and cortical targets, involved in motivation and learning) and periventricular (to the hypothalamus and pituitary, involved in endocrine control). [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-dopamine|Biogenic Amines]] Naming the molecule does not name the function: the same transmitter carries different messages because the target circuits differ, which is the first reason a single functional label cannot survive.

**Receptor logic.** D1-type and D2-type receptors couple to different intracellular cascades and can have opposing effects on the same cell, and receptor subtypes differ in affinity, so the effect of a dopamine signal depends on which receptors are present and on the local concentration - which is why drugs that 'act on dopamine' can have opposite net consequences. Motor side effects of dopamine-blocking drugs and the therapeutic effects attributed to the same blockade illustrate the point: one target, different circuits, different consequences. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]

**The prediction-error finding, and its limits.** The best-supported formal account of dopamine in behaving animals is reward prediction error: midbrain dopamine activity encodes the difference between outcome and prediction, with activation when outcomes exceed prediction and reduced activity when they fall short, which makes the signal a teaching signal for learning rather than a hedonic reading and links electrophysiology to reinforcement learning. That is a claim about firing patterns under specific task conditions, generalised to a computational role. [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-rpe|Dopamine reward prediction error coding]] [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-learning|Dopamine reward prediction error coding]]

**What 'reward' gets wrong.** The prediction-error mapping is also the finding most often over-simplified, and the formulation itself rules out the simple version: a signal that codes the difference between expected and received value cannot be read as 'pleasure' or 'motivation' alone, because its meaning depends on the comparison. Dopamine neurons are heterogeneous, carry additional signals, and are not uniformly a reward channel; the literature review below the domain makes the same argument in more detail - dopamine's role is best described by the computation being performed, not by a common-sense word. [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-heterogeneity|What does dopamine mean?]]

**Drugs and the reward account.** Addictive drugs can drive reward circuitry much more strongly than natural rewards, and repeated exposure changes circuits involved in reward, stress and self-control. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-reward|Drugs and the Brain]] [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-adaptation|Drugs and the Brain]] That account explains part of substance-related harm; it does not explain every case, and the page itself notes that response varies with drug, route, genetics and environment. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-variation|Drugs and the Brain]]

**Where clinical claims attach.** The bipolar findings - elevated D2/3 availability with mania, increased dopamine transporter levels with depression, inconsistent findings elsewhere, and improvement of bipolar depressive symptoms with both dopamine agonists and antidopaminergics - are state-dependent and partly contradictory patterns; they support 'dopamine is involved in the pharmacology of mood episodes' and not 'dopamine excess causes mania'. [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-state|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]] [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-pharmacology|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]] A statement such as "mania involves increased dopamine" is a hypothesis about group-level pharmacology and imaging signals, and the effects of both agonists and dopamine-blocking drugs in bipolar depression are one reason the picture resists a single direction. [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-caution|What does dopamine mean?]]

**Reading rule.** Any sentence of the form 'dopamine does X' should be rewritten with a pathway, a receptor family and an experimental paradigm; without all three the claim is unfalsifiable. [[F62 Neuroscience Online biogenic amines#^f62-limit|Biogenic Amines]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Dopamine receptor logic is the mechanistic foundation the pharmacology domain builds on when it discusses antipsychotic, stimulant and antidepressant mechanisms; a claim about D1 vs D2 receptor sign on this page determines what a downstream pharmacology claim can say. ## Limitation or common misconception

Dopamine is not one signal: pathways, receptor subtypes and brain regions do different jobs with the same molecule.
