# Trauma and stress responses

**Definition.** Trauma responses are the physiological and psychological consequences of overwhelming events, shaped by appraisal, context and prior experience. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

## Supported claims

- Acute stress mobilisation is adaptive; chronic activation is associated with wear on regulation, mood and health. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-chronic|What Is Stress?]]
- ICD-11 and DSM-5 classify trauma-related disorders differently, and current definitions are symptom-based. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]; [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

## How it works

**Appraisal comes first.** Stress responses depend on how an event is appraised and on prior experience: the same demand can be experienced as a threat or a challenge, which changes the physiological response, so the same exposure produces different responses in different people. That is why a stressor cannot be defined independently of the person's interpretation, and why exposure measures in trauma research record the event rather than the response. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-appraisal|What is stress?]]

**Physiology runs on two timescales.** The sympathetic response is fast and the HPA axis is slower, ending in cortisol release that mobilises energy and modulates many systems; cortisol secretion follows ACTH from the pituitary, which follows hypothalamic CRH, with corticosteroid feedback limiting further release. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-axis|What is stress?]] [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-drive|The Adrenal Glands]] The contrast between adaptive acute mobilisation and the wear of sustained activation on regulation, mood and physical health is the distinction between a response and a burden, and allostatic load describes the cumulative cost of repeated or poorly managed responses, with the brain as both regulator and target. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-chronic|What is stress?]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-load|Stress mediators]]

**The mediators are not simply 'stress hormones'.** A long-standing dispute concerns whether glucocorticoids enable, stimulate or suppress the stress response, and one influential classification proposes permissive, suppressive, stimulatory and preparative actions depending on context and timing, so 'stress hormone' is not a single-direction description. [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-contested|Glucocorticoids and stress]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|Glucocorticoids and stress]]

**Immune signalling is part of the response.** Peripheral immune signals reach the brain and produce sickness behaviour - coordinated changes in activity, appetite, sleep and mood - which is one route by which stress physiology connects to mood and behaviour. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-sickness|Inflammation to sickness and depression]] Prolonged inflammatory signalling is proposed to contribute to depressive symptoms in vulnerable individuals; the causal claim is a model rather than an established mechanism. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-depression|Inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-limit|Inflammation to sickness and depression]]

**The classification step is separate.** Only some responses become disorders: current definitions are symptom-based rather than exposure-based, and exposure to a traumatic event is necessary but not sufficient for a diagnosis, so a stress response and a diagnosis are different objects. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-exposure-required|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Trauma and stress responses draw on the neuroendocrine-neuroimmune axis (HPA, autonomic, inflammatory) as the physiological substrate the clinical-psychiatry domain's diagnostic categories sit on top of, so reading them together makes both the biology and the classification explicit. [[Stress response and the HPA axis]] [[ICD-11 versus DSM-5 classification]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Exposure to traumatic events is common and does not imply a disorder.
