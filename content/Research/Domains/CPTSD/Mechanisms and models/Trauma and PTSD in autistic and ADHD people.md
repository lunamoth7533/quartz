# Trauma and PTSD in autistic and ADHD people

## Definition

This framework covers trauma exposure, PTSD and complex PTSD in autistic people and in people with ADHD: how often they are recorded, why they are harder to recognise, and what is known about adapting trauma treatment. Autism and ADHD frequently co-occur and share some features while remaining distinct categories, so trauma-related disorders add a third overlapping condition to a pair that already overlaps. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-definition|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]] [[P41868251 Quinton 2026 PTSD assessment and treatment in autism#^p41868251-gaps|Quinton 2026]]

## How it works

**Recorded rates and screening rates diverge in autism.** Diagnosed PTSD in diagnosed autistic samples is recorded at rates close to general-population estimates, yet studies using screening tools report heavier symptom loads, and a systematic review of the recent literature describes more severe PTSD symptoms in autistic than in non-autistic samples, with rates at least comparable. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-discrepancy|Mansour 2025]] [[P41868251 Quinton 2026 PTSD assessment and treatment in autism#^p41868251-severity|Quinton 2026]] The gap is itself the finding: either current criteria and services miss trauma reactions in autistic people, or screening over-counts them, and the available designs cannot yet say which. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-caution-underdiagnosis|Appraisal: Mansour 2025]]

**Vulnerability can be traced forward in time.** In a representative twin cohort, the association between childhood autistic traits and PTSD at 18 held among children who had been exposed to trauma, so the link is not explained by exposure alone. [[P40133042 Quinton 2025 Autistic traits and later PTSD#^p40133042-exposed|Quinton 2025]] A cross-sectional survey points to one candidate route: negative post-trauma appraisals of alienation, shame and fear were more common in autistic adults and statistically mediated their higher symptom severity. [[P41454656 Prosser 2026 Trauma appraisals in autistic adults#^p41454656-severity|Prosser 2026]] That mediation is a decomposition of cross-sectional associations, not a demonstrated cause. [[P41454656 Prosser 2026 Trauma appraisals in autistic adults#^p41454656-caution-mediation|Appraisal: Prosser 2026]]

**ADHD and PTSD travel together, unevenly.** Diagnosed co-occurrence of ADHD and PTSD is more common in females, a difference that appears in adult samples and is larger where ADHD is the primary diagnosis or structured interviews are used. [[P40252259 Wilson 2025 Sex differences in ADHD and PTSD#^p40252259-age|Wilson 2025]] [[P40252259 Wilson 2025 Sex differences in ADHD and PTSD#^p40252259-design|Wilson 2025]] In a cohort of combat-deployed veterans, ADHD was about twice as common among those with current PTSD. [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-ptsd|Knight 2025]] How comorbidity persists over time is itself listed among the open questions for adult ADHD. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults: evidence base]]

**Why assessment is harder: overlapping features.** In the same veterans, ADHD self-report measures agreed poorly with each other and with a prior diagnosis, the practical form of overlap: poor concentration can be read as ADHD, as PTSD, or as both. [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-measures|Knight 2025]] [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Knight 2025]] Adult autism assessment likewise lists neurodevelopmental, mental, physical, communication and sensory explanations in its differential, the same ground a trauma assessment has to cover. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-differential|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]]

**Why assessment is harder: what counts, and how it is reported.** Autistic and non-autistic adults both described events such as bullying or bereavement as traumatic although these fall outside the formal exposure criterion, and autistic adults reported more events that had happened to them directly, so the definition of a traumatic event changes who is counted. [[P41454656 Prosser 2026 Trauma appraisals in autistic adults#^p41454656-criterion|Prosser 2026]] [[P41454656 Prosser 2026 Trauma appraisals in autistic adults#^p41454656-exposure|Prosser 2026]] Recorded rates in autistic samples also move with the informant and the diagnostic system, and reviewers call for assessment tools validated in autistic people and for study of autism-specific expressions of trauma-related symptoms. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-moderators|Mansour 2025]] [[P41868251 Quinton 2026 PTSD assessment and treatment in autism#^p41868251-gaps|Quinton 2026]] Guidance for autistic adults already asks services to explain language, check understanding and offer communication aids or interpreters, which is where an adapted trauma assessment starts. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f25-communication|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]]

**Adapting treatment.** In general PTSD trials, psychological treatment worked as well after multiple traumatic events as after a single event, which removes one reason to assume that heavily exposed groups benefit less. [[P38219762 Hoppen 2024 Single versus multiple trauma treatment#^p38219762-conclusion|Hoppen 2024]] Those trials were not selected for autism or ADHD, the autism review treats treatment strategies as still needing validation, and environmental and communicative adjustments are design principles rather than tested effects. [[P41868251 Quinton 2026 PTSD assessment and treatment in autism#^p41868251-caution-treatment|Appraisal: Quinton 2026]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f25-caution|Appraisal: Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]]

**Reading rule.** Before comparing rates or treatment effects, name whether the sample is diagnosed or trait-defined, which events counted as trauma, which instrument and informant were used, and which classification system applied; each of these moves the numbers in this literature. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-moderators|Mansour 2025]] [[P40133042 Quinton 2025 Autistic traits and later PTSD#^p40133042-caution-traits|Appraisal: Quinton 2025]]

## Evidence and status

Prevalence evidence for autism is meta-analytic but rests on recorded diagnoses, with a very wide confidence interval for adults; the prospective evidence is one cohort that measured traits rather than diagnoses; ADHD-PTSD evidence combines a meta-analysis of diagnosed comorbidity with cross-sectional cohorts; the evidence on mechanism is cross-sectional; and for treatment the recent review describes a need for validation rather than a body of adapted trials. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-limit|Mansour 2025]] [[P40133042 Quinton 2025 Autistic traits and later PTSD#^p40133042-limit|Quinton 2025]] [[P40252259 Wilson 2025 Sex differences in ADHD and PTSD#^p40252259-limit|Wilson 2025]] [[P41454656 Prosser 2026 Trauma appraisals in autistic adults#^p41454656-limit|Prosser 2026]] [[P41868251 Quinton 2026 PTSD assessment and treatment in autism#^p41868251-limit|Quinton 2026]] Sex differences in diagnosed comorbidity can reflect exposure, recognition or referral as well as vulnerability. [[P40252259 Wilson 2025 Sex differences in ADHD and PTSD#^p40252259-caution-diagnosis|Appraisal: Wilson 2025]] None of the sources held here tests a trauma treatment adapted for ADHD.

## Uncertainties

- Whether low recorded PTSD prevalence in autistic samples reflects under-recognition, over-inclusive screening, or both.
- Whether events outside the formal exposure criterion should count, and how counting them would change comparisons with general-population rates.
- How to separate ADHD, PTSD and their co-occurrence in a trauma-exposed adult with attention problems when childhood history rests on recall.
- Which adaptations of trauma-focused therapy, such as communication supports, sensory adjustments or pacing, change outcomes.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Clinical psychology review).** Supplies the first pooled prevalence of diagnosed PTSD in diagnosed autistic samples, about 1% point prevalence in children and young people and 2% in adults, which is the baseline against which screening-study figures look high. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-prevalence|Mansour 2025]]
- **2025 · Prospective twin cohort (Journal of child psychology and psychiatry).** Moves the autism-PTSD link from cross-sectional association to childhood-to-adulthood prediction: childhood autistic traits predicted PTSD at 18 (odds ratio about 1.9) after adjustment for sex, family socioeconomic status and IQ. [[P40133042 Quinton 2025 Autistic traits and later PTSD#^p40133042-adjusted|Quinton 2025]] Traits in a general population are not diagnoses. [[P40133042 Quinton 2025 Autistic traits and later PTSD#^p40133042-caution-traits|Appraisal: Quinton 2025]]

- [[Emotion dysregulation across conditions]] - dysregulation is described in ADHD, autism and complex PTSD alike, one reason trauma reactions and neurodevelopmental features are hard to tell apart.
- [[Shame and guilt]] - shame is among the post-trauma appraisals that statistically carried higher PTSD severity in autistic adults; that note sets it beside the shame and guilt meta-analyses.
- [[Suicide and self-harm]] - PTSD, ADHD and autism each carry raised group risk of suicidal behaviour, highest in autistic people who also have ADHD.
- [[ADHD in girls and women]] - the wider picture behind the adult female excess in ADHD-PTSD comorbidity: ADHD in females tends to be recognised later, often after internalising diagnoses.
