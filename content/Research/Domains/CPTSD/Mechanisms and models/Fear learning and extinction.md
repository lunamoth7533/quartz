# Fear learning and extinction

## Definition

Fear conditioning shows how a neutral cue acquires threat value; extinction is the learned reduction of that response when the cue no longer predicts threat. Extinction is the laboratory model for exposure-based treatment.

## How it works

**Acquisition.** Classical conditioning pairs a neutral cue with a meaningful stimulus so that the cue comes to evoke the response on its own. In fear conditioning the meaningful stimulus is aversive, the conditioned response is measurable in behaviour and in autonomic signals, and the paradigm is used as a model of how emotional associations form. [[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-pairing|Classical Conditioning]] [[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-application|Classical Conditioning]]

**Extinction is new learning.** Extinction reduces the conditioned response by repeated presentation of the cue without the outcome - and the response can return, which is the observation that shows extinction is new learning rather than erasure. [[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-extinction|Classical Conditioning]] Because extinction is new learning that competes with the original memory, the original association is suppressed rather than erased, and fear can return after time, context change or a single reminder: through spontaneous recovery over time, reinstatement after a new aversive event, or renewal when the context changes. That is why the laboratory model is described as learning that is learned and suppressed rather than removed, and it is the mechanistic reason exposure-based treatment includes context and relapse planning rather than assuming a fear is deleted. [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-newlearning|Fear extinction]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-model|Fear extinction]]

**Neural basis.** Amygdala circuits support threat learning, with cortical and hippocampal contributions to context and regulation. The amygdala is described through connections with cortical, thalamic, hypothalamic and brain-stem structures, which is why it is treated as a hub rather than a single-function centre, and conditioning and extinction are used as experimental models of how emotional associations form and change. [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-connections|Amygdala]] [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-fear|Amygdala]]

**Measuring the response.** Autonomic readouts are part of the paradigm, and heart rate variability is one of the measures used for autonomic state. Its metrics fall into time-domain, frequency-domain and non-linear families with different recording requirements; baseline values depend on recording length, age, sex and posture; published norms are not interchangeable; and the LF/HF ratio is controversial because autonomic interactions are non-linear and respiration confounds the band. Breathing, heart rate, medication and individual factors all influence recorded values. [[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-metrics|Heart rate variability metrics]] [[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-context|Heart rate variability metrics]] [[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-lfhf|Heart rate variability metrics]] [[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-confounds|Heart rate variability metrics]] A single short recording cannot separate these influences, so autonomic claims in this library stay at the level the measure can support. [[P29034226 Shaffer 2017 Heart rate variability metrics#^p29034226-caution-marker|AI appraisal: Heart rate variability metrics]]

**The stress context.** Fear learning sits inside a broader stress response: appraisal shapes whether a demand is experienced as threat or challenge, the sympathetic branch responds quickly while the HPA axis is slower and ends in cortisol release, and acute responses are adaptive while chronic activation is associated with wear on regulation, mood and health. The conditioning literature supplies the associative mechanism; the stress literature supplies the physiology it runs inside. ## Evidence and status

Conditioning and extinction mechanisms are robust. Direct evidence comes largely from animal work, lesion and stimulation studies, and conditioning in healthy humans, so the account constrains what a human mechanism could look like without describing any person's trauma. [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-limit|Amygdala]]

The link from mechanism to clinical improvement is weaker than the literature often implies. Findings on consolidation and reconsolidation windows were expected to improve treatment design, but clinical translation has been slower than hoped, and the review that examines them notes that translation is incomplete. The laboratory model is therefore strong evidence about one mechanism, and weaker evidence about how to treat trauma-related conditions; the clinical questions live in [[Phase-based trauma therapy evidence]].
