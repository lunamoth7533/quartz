# Adverse childhood experiences

## Definition

Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) are adversities in childhood or adolescence, such as maltreatment or exposure to domestic violence, which often occur together. [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-definition|Hughes 2017]] The ACE framework counts how many types a person experienced and uses the score as an index of cumulative risk for later health problems. [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-group|Baldwin 2021]] It is a population risk framework, not a diagnosis and not a measure of what a particular person went through. [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-caution-screening|Appraisal: Baldwin 2021]]

## How it works

**Counting rather than cataloguing.** Because adversities cluster, the framework sums types instead of studying one at a time, and syntheses often compare people with four or more types against people with none. [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-definition|Hughes 2017]] [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-limit|Hughes 2017]] A count of that kind says nothing about which adversities, what severity or what timing matter. [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-caution-association|Appraisal: Hughes 2017]]

**Dose-response associations.** Across 37 studies of 253,719 adults, people reporting four or more ACEs had higher risk than those reporting none on all 23 health outcomes studied. [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-dose|Hughes 2017]] Strength varied widely: odds ratios under 2 for inactivity, overweight and diabetes, strong associations for mental ill health and problematic drinking, and odds ratios above 7 for problematic drug use and for violence against others or oneself, with considerable heterogeneity for almost half the outcomes. [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-gradient|Hughes 2017]] In the E-Risk twin cohort, each additional ACE raised the risk of later mental (relative risk 1.14) and physical (1.09) health problems beyond sex, socioeconomic disadvantage and earlier health problems. [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-group|Baldwin 2021]] A graded association is compatible with a causal effect but does not demonstrate one. [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-caution-association|Appraisal: Hughes 2017]]

**How much is causal.** Quasi-experimental studies address that question directly. For childhood maltreatment, the unadjusted association with later mental health problems (Cohen's d 0.56) fell to d 0.31 once twin and sibling comparisons, adoption, fixed-effects and similar designs addressed confounding, and the smaller estimate held across designs and outcomes. [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-attenuation|Baldwin 2023]] [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-designs|Baldwin 2023]] The authors read this as a small causal contribution, with part of the raw association reflecting wider genetic and environmental risk. [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-interpretation|Baldwin 2023]] Each design removes confounding only under its own assumptions, so agreement across designs strengthens rather than proves the causal reading. [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-caution-designs|Appraisal: Baldwin 2023]]

**Retrospective and prospective counts identify different people.** Most ACE studies rest on adults recalling their childhood. [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-caution-association|Appraisal: Hughes 2017]] In 16 studies with both kinds of measure, prospective and retrospective reports of maltreatment agreed poorly (kappa 0.19), somewhat better when recall was taken by interview than by questionnaire. [[P30892562 Baldwin 2019 Prospective versus retrospective maltreatment measures#^p30892562-agreement|Baldwin 2019]] [[P30892562 Baldwin 2019 Prospective versus retrospective maltreatment measures#^p30892562-moderators|Baldwin 2019]] The authors conclude that the two approaches pick out different people, who may follow different risk pathways. [[P30892562 Baldwin 2019 Prospective versus retrospective maltreatment measures#^p30892562-groups|Baldwin 2019]] Neither is simply correct: prospective records miss maltreatment that was never detected, and recall is shaped by memory and current state. [[P30892562 Baldwin 2019 Prospective versus retrospective maltreatment measures#^p30892562-caution-truth|Appraisal: Baldwin 2019]]

**Group risk is not individual prediction.** In E-Risk and in the Dunedin cohort, ACE scores discriminated poorly between individuals who did and did not develop problems (area under the curve about 0.58 for mental and 0.60 for physical health, where 0.50 is chance), with similar results for prospective and retrospective measures. [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-individual|Baldwin 2021]] [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-replication|Baldwin 2021]] The authors judge ACE-based targeting of interventions likely to be ineffective, and a score cannot stand in for assessment of a particular child or adult. [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-replication|Baldwin 2021]] [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-caution-screening|Appraisal: Baldwin 2021]]

**Proposed pathways.** Routes from adversity to later illness include cumulative physiological cost, which allostatic load describes as the wear of repeated or poorly managed stress responses with the brain as both regulator and target, and the shared genetic and environmental risk that quasi-experimental designs try to separate out. [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-load|McEwen 2006]] [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-interpretation|Baldwin 2023]] Childhood trauma also appears in treatment research: in a meta-analysis of psychotherapy for adults with complex trauma, participants with childhood trauma showed smaller effects than those with other trauma types. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Hu 2025]] Complex trauma exposure in those trials is not the same as an ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-caution-category|Appraisal: Hu 2025]]

**Reading rule.** Ask whether an ACE figure comes from prospective records or adult recall, whether it contrasts extreme counts or estimates a per-ACE gradient, whether confounding was addressed, and whether the claim concerns groups or a person; an ACE score answers only the group question. [[P30892562 Baldwin 2019 Prospective versus retrospective maltreatment measures#^p30892562-groups|Baldwin 2019]] [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-individual|Baldwin 2021]] [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-attenuation|Baldwin 2023]]

## Evidence and status

Graded associations between ACE counts and later health are consistent across a large meta-analysis and prospective cohorts, with considerable heterogeneity between studies. [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-gradient|Hughes 2017]] [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-group|Baldwin 2021]] Quasi-experimental evidence supports a real but smaller causal contribution of maltreatment to mental health problems. [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-interpretation|Baldwin 2023]] The evidence against using ACE scores to predict for individuals is direct: poor discrimination in two birth cohorts and poor agreement between prospective and retrospective measures. [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-individual|Baldwin 2021]] [[P30892562 Baldwin 2019 Prospective versus retrospective maltreatment measures#^p30892562-agreement|Baldwin 2019]]

## Uncertainties

- Which adversities should count, how they should be weighted, and whether severity, timing and duration matter more than the number of types. [[P29253477 Hughes 2017 Multiple adverse childhood experiences and health#^p29253477-caution-association|Appraisal: Hughes 2017]]
- Which pathways - stress physiology, learning, relationships or shared genetic risk - carry the causal share, and for whom. [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-interpretation|Baldwin 2023]]
- Whether prospective and retrospective reports capture different experiences, different memories or different people. [[P30892562 Baldwin 2019 Prospective versus retrospective maltreatment measures#^p30892562-groups|Baldwin 2019]]
- Whether services organised around ACE scores improve outcomes; no trial of this is held here.

## Recent research

- **2023 · Systematic review and meta-analysis of quasi-experimental studies (The American journal of psychiatry).** Narrows the causal claim: a little over half of the raw maltreatment-mental health association survives designs that address confounding. [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-attenuation|Baldwin 2023]]
- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Journal of affective disorders).** Links childhood trauma to smaller psychotherapy effects in complex trauma samples, a treatment-side reason to record adversity history. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Hu 2025]]
- **2021 · Two prospective birth cohorts (JAMA pediatrics).** Separates population forecasting from individual prediction using the same ACE scores, the clearest test of ACE screening held here. [[P33492366 Baldwin 2021 ACE scores and individual prediction#^p33492366-individual|Baldwin 2021]]

## Connections

- [[Post-traumatic stress disorder]] - a diagnosis often studied alongside childhood adversity; an ACE count is a risk index, while PTSD needs a qualifying event and a defined symptom pattern.
- [[Association versus individual prediction]] - the general rule this framework illustrates: ACE scores forecast group differences but classify individuals barely better than chance.
- [[Gene-environment interplay and epigenetics]] - part of the ACE-outcome association reflects shared genetic and environmental risk, the confounding that quasi-experimental designs try to remove.
- [[Allostasis and chronic stress]] - a proposed physiological route by which repeated adversity accumulates cost, a framework rather than a demonstrated pathway from an ACE count.
- [[Developmental perspectives and attachment]] - early relationships and caregiving, the setting in which most counted adversities happen.
- [[Sensitive periods]] - timing, which the ACE count ignores; windows of heightened plasticity are one reason when adversity occurs could matter.
- [[Psychological interventions for complex trauma]] - where childhood trauma appears as a moderator of psychotherapy effects in complex trauma samples.
- [[Bias and confounding]] - confounding that inflates the raw association, and recall that makes retrospective reports diverge from prospective records.
