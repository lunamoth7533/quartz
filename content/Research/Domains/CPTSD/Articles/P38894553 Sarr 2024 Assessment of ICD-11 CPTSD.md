# A Systematic Review of the Assessment of ICD-11 Complex Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (CPTSD) in Young People and Adults.

**Authors.** Sarr R, Quinton A, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38894553/)

**Published.** 2024 in Clinical psychology & psychotherapy (volume 31, issue 3, pages e3012).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/cpp.3012](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.3012) · PMID [38894553](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38894553/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Most included studies concerned adults, and the International Trauma Questionnaire was the measure used most often to assess ICD-11 complex PTSD.
^p38894553-measures

Studies comparing the two diagnoses reported greater symptom severity, impairment and difficulty in people with complex PTSD than in those with PTSD, in young people as well as adults.
^p38894553-differentiation

The review stresses a sensitive assessment approach adapted to culture and age, and offers only tentative practice recommendations because no gold standard could be identified.
^p38894553-adaptation

## Scope as reported

Narrative synthesis of five databases with quality appraisal; the evidence is dominated by adult clinical samples, and the authors state that gold-standard recommendations cannot yet be made.
^p38894553-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Because most included studies define complex PTSD with the same questionnaire, consistency across studies partly reflects a shared instrument; agreement with clinician interviews is the stronger test of where the boundary with PTSD falls.
^p38894553-caution-circularity
