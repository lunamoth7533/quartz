# Agreement Between Prospective and Retrospective Measures of Childhood Maltreatment: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Baldwin JR, Reuben A, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30892562/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Prospective and retrospective measures of childhood maltreatment agreed poorly (kappa 0.19).
^p30892562-agreement

Agreement was better when retrospective reports came from interviews rather than questionnaires, and in smaller studies; it did not vary with the prospective measure, age at report, sex distribution or study quality.
^p30892562-moderators

The authors conclude that the two approaches pick out different people, who may reach mental illness by different risk pathways, and that research and practice should treat them as distinct.
^p30892562-groups

## Scope as reported

Search of four databases for English-language studies to January 2018; of 450 studies with prospective maltreatment measures, 16 also had retrospective data allowing a kappa to be computed, pooled with random-effects models.
^p30892562-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Poor agreement does not show which measure is right: prospective records miss maltreatment that was never detected at the time, and retrospective reports are shaped by memory and current state, so each identifies a partly different group.
^p30892562-caution-truth
