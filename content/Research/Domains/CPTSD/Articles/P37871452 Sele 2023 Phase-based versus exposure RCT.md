# Comparing phase-based treatment, prolonged exposure, and skills-training for Complex Posttraumatic Stress Disorder: A randomized controlled trial.

**Authors.** Sele P, Hoffart A, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37871452/)

**Published.** 2023 in Journal of anxiety disorders (volume 100, pages 102786).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.janxdis.2023.102786](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.janxdis.2023.102786) · PMID [37871452](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37871452/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Participants were randomised to a phase-based treatment in which skills training preceded narrative therapy, to prolonged exposure, or to skills training alone.
^p37871452-design

From pre- to post-treatment, prolonged exposure reduced DSM-5 PTSD symptoms more than the phase-based treatment, while reductions in ICD-11 complex PTSD symptoms did not differ between arms.
^p37871452-post

By one-year follow-up, prolonged exposure showed larger PTSD improvements than both other arms, and prolonged exposure and skills training showed larger complex PTSD improvements than the phase-based treatment; the predicted advantage of the phase-based sequence was not supported.
^p37871452-followup

## Scope as reported

Single-site residential trial of enhanced treatment versions analysed with mixed models; the authors call for further tests of immediate trauma-focused treatment and of skills training.
^p37871452-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A modest residential sample, with arms that differ in format (group skills sessions versus individual sessions) and in dose, limits generalisation, and the trial compares packaged sequences rather than isolating sequencing itself.
^p37871452-caution-setting
