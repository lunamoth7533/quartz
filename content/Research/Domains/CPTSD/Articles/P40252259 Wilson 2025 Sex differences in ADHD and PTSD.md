# Sex differences in the comorbidity between attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder and posttraumatic stress disorder: A systematic literature review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Wilson J, Fida D, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40252259/)

**Published.** 2025 in General hospital psychiatry (volume 95, pages 32-39).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2025.04.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2025.04.003) · PMID [40252259](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40252259/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Co-occurring ADHD and PTSD was diagnosed more often in females than in males (pooled odds ratio about 1.3).
^p40252259-sex

In age-stratified analyses the female excess appeared only in adult samples.
^p40252259-age

The difference was larger in studies where ADHD was the primary diagnosis and in studies using structured clinical interviews.
^p40252259-design

## Scope as reported

Fixed-effects meta-analysis of 13 studies found through a scoping search of two databases; heterogeneity was low to moderate.
^p40252259-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Sex differences in a diagnosed combination can reflect differences in exposure, recognition or referral as well as vulnerability; the analysis describes diagnosed comorbidity, not mechanism.
^p40252259-caution-diagnosis
