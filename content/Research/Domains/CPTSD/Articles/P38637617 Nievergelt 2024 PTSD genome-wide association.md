# Genome-wide association analyses identify 95 risk loci and provide insights into the neurobiology of post-traumatic stress disorder.

**Authors.** Nievergelt CM, Maihofer AX, et al. (235 named authors; the PubMed record also lists 4 unnamed entries) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38637617/)

**Published.** 2024 in Nature genetics (volume 56, issue 5, pages 792-808).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41588-024-01707-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01707-9) · PMID [38637617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38637617/) · PMCID [PMC11396662](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11396662/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The meta-analysis identified 95 genome-wide significant loci for PTSD, 80 of them new.
^p38637617-loci

Multi-omic analyses pointed to 43 potential causal genes, grouped as synaptic and ion-channel modulators, developmental and axon-guidance genes and transcription factors, synaptic structure genes, and endocrine or immune regulators.
^p38637617-genes

The authors note that PTSD genetics has had lower discoverability than most psychiatric disorders, and that several top genes relate to stress, immune, fear and threat processes.
^p38637617-discoverability

## Scope as reported

Genome-wide association meta-analysis of case-control cohorts, predominantly of European ancestry.
^p38637617-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The loci are population-level associations with PTSD as defined in the contributing cohorts; they are not complex-PTSD-specific findings and do not support genetic prediction for an individual.
^p38637617-caution-ptsd
