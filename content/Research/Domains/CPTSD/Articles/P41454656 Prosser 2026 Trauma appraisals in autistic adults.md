# Post-traumatic stress disorder in autistic and non-autistic adults: The impact of appraisals on reactions to traumatic events.

**Authors.** Prosser R, Rumball F, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41454656/)

**Published.** 2026 in Autism : the international journal of research and practice (volume 30, issue 3, pages 605-625) (first online 2025-12-27).
**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613251403405](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613251403405) · PMID [41454656](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41454656/) · PMCID [PMC12923627](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12923627/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Overall exposure to trauma types did not differ between groups, but autistic adults reported more events that happened to them directly.
^p41454656-exposure

PTSD symptom severity and negative post-trauma appraisals, especially of alienation, shame and fear, were higher in the autistic group, and these appraisals mediated the link between autism and symptom severity.
^p41454656-severity

Both groups described events such as bullying or bereavement as traumatic although these fall outside the formal exposure criterion.
^p41454656-criterion

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional online survey; the authors state that longitudinal designs are needed to establish the direction of effects.
^p41454656-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Mediation in cross-sectional data is a statistical decomposition, not evidence that appraisals cause symptoms, and counting events outside the formal criterion changes what PTSD prevalence means when studies are compared.
^p41454656-caution-mediation
