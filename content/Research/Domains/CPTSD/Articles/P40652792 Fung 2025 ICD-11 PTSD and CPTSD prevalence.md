# Prevalence of ICD-11 post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and complex PTSD in the general populations: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Fung HW, Leousi A, et al. (11 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40652792/)

**Published.** 2025 in Asian journal of psychiatry (volume 110, pages 104610).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.ajp.2025.104610](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajp.2025.104610) · PMID [40652792](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40652792/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In five samples from settings without war exposure and with developed economies, pooled prevalence was about 2% for ICD-11 PTSD and 4% for complex PTSD (7,718 participants).
^p40652792-pooled

In five samples from war-exposed or less economically developed settings, the pooled figures were about 16% for PTSD and 15% for complex PTSD (9,870 participants).
^p40652792-context

Every included study used the ITQ, and most were judged at low risk of bias across quality domains.
^p40652792-quality

## Scope as reported

Pre-registered review of three databases in which 10 of 835 unique records met criteria, with samples from Ukraine, Germany, Ireland, Hong Kong, Israel, Kenya, Nigeria and Ghana. The authors ask for cautious reading because the number of studies is small.
^p40652792-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Each estimate is a probable diagnosis from one self-report questionnaire, so the figures describe ITQ-defined caseness rather than clinician-assessed diagnosis, and the two groupings combine war exposure with economic development, so neither factor can be isolated.
^p40652792-caution-instrument
