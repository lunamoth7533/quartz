# Epidemiology of DSM-5 PTSD and ICD-11 PTSD and complex PTSD in the Netherlands.

**Authors.** Hoeboer CM, Nava F, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39808949/)

**Published.** 2025 in Journal of anxiety disorders (volume 110, pages 102963).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.janxdis.2024.102963](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.janxdis.2024.102963) · PMID [39808949](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39808949/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Lifetime exposure to at least one potentially traumatic event was reported by 81.5% of participants.
^p39808949-exposure

Estimated lifetime DSM-5 PTSD was 11.1% and current DSM-5 PTSD 1.3%; current ICD-11 PTSD was 1.0% and current ICD-11 complex PTSD 1.6%.
^p39808949-prevalence

Risk was higher for women, younger adults, people with lower education and people with a non-Dutch cultural background.
^p39808949-risk

About half of those with probable lifetime PTSD had sought professional help and about one-third had received first-line treatment; lack of knowledge, shame and avoidance were common reasons for not seeking help.
^p39808949-care

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional online self-report survey run in 2023 with an interview subsample; ICD-11 estimates rest on the ITQ and DSM-5 estimates on the PCL-5 and CAPS-5.
^p39808949-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The DSM-5 and ICD-11 figures come from different instruments and algorithms, so the gap between them mixes classification rules with measurement, and help-seeking was self-reported, so the treatment-gap figures are approximate.
^p39808949-caution-systems
