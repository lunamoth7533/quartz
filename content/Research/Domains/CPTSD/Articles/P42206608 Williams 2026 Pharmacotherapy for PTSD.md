# Pharmacotherapy for post traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).

**Authors.** Williams T, Phillips NJ, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42206608/)

**Published.** 2026 in The Cochrane database of systematic reviews (first online 2026-05-28).

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

On treatment response, SSRIs outperformed placebo in 8 trials with 1,078 participants: about 58% improved on an SSRI against 35% on placebo, on moderate-certainty evidence.
^p42206608-ssri

Single small trials suggested benefit from mirtazapine and from the tricyclic amitriptyline (low certainty), while antipsychotics showed no clear benefit (very low certainty).
^p42206608-others

More people withdrew on SSRIs than on placebo, although only about 9% of those on an SSRI stopped because of adverse events.
^p42206608-withdrawal

The authors conclude that SSRIs are first-line drugs for PTSD and that more effective drugs are still needed.
^p42206608-conclusion

## Scope as reported

Cochrane review of randomised trials in adults with PTSD, with results stratified by drug class and by individual drug; the latest search ran to 13 November 2020, so later trials are not included.
^p42206608-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Trials enrolled adults with PTSD as defined when they ran, not ICD-11 complex PTSD, so the class result cannot be assumed to hold for complex presentations; the mirtazapine and amitriptyline signals each rest on a single small trial.
^p42206608-caution-cptsd
