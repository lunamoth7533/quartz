## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review defines resilience as keeping mental health despite adversity, or showing only temporary impairment with quick recovery, and notes that biological work has lagged behind psychological and social research.
^p38483288-definition

It reports preliminary evidence that hippocampal pattern separation and prefrontal cognitive control protect against pathological fear after single, event-type stressors by supporting the perception of safety.
^p38483288-safety

Pursuing and savouring rewards appears protective against broader anxious-depressive dysfunction after more severe or prolonged stress, including severe PTSD.
^p38483288-reward

Progress is limited by how resilience is operationalised, by study designs, by replication and by animal modelling.
^p38483288-methods

## Scope as reported

Narrative review of adult human findings with animal work in the background; the authors describe the central mechanistic conclusions as preliminary.
^p38483288-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Resilience here is an outcome judged after exposure, so the mechanisms are candidate protective processes identified largely in cross-sectional or animal work; they do not predict which exposed person will or will not develop a disorder.
^p38483288-caution-outcome
