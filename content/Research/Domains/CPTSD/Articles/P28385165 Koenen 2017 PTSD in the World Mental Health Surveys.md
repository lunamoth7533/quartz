# Posttraumatic stress disorder in the World Mental Health Surveys.

**Authors.** Koenen KC, Ratanatharathorn A, et al. (35 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28385165/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Lifetime PTSD was 3.9% across the pooled surveys and 5.6% among respondents who had been exposed to trauma.
^p28385165-prevalence

About half of the respondents with PTSD described their symptoms as persistent.
^p28385165-persistence

Treatment seeking was about 54% in high-income countries but roughly 23-29% in lower- and upper-middle-income countries.
^p28385165-treatment

Among the exposed, indicators of social disadvantage (younger age, female sex, being unmarried, less education, lower income, unemployment) went with higher lifetime risk.
^p28385165-risk

## Scope as reported

Analysis of 26 population surveys in which the Composite International Diagnostic Interview assessed trauma exposure, 30-day, 12-month and lifetime PTSD, and treatment in the preceding year; age of onset was examined by country income level.
^p28385165-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Lifetime estimates from structured lay interviews depend on recall of events and symptoms, and PTSD rates shift with the diagnostic rules applied, so these figures describe one assessment approach rather than a fixed global rate; risk factors describe groups, not individuals.
^p28385165-caution-recall
