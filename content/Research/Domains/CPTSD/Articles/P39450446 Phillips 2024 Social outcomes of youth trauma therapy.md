# Systematic review and meta-analysis: do best-evidenced trauma-focused interventions for children and young people with PTSD lead to changes in social and interpersonal domains?

**Authors.** Phillips AR, Halligan SL, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39450446/)

**Published.** 2024 in European journal of psychotraumatology (volume 15, issue 1, pages 2415267).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1080/20008066.2024.2415267](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008066.2024.2415267) · PMID [39450446](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39450446/) · PMCID [PMC11514400](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11514400/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across 13 trials reporting social skills or functioning, trauma-focused treatment showed a small pooled effect that was not statistically significant.
^p39450446-pooled

Of six trials reporting other social outcomes, such as perceived social support, four favoured the trauma-focused treatment.
^p39450446-narrative

Few trials reported social or interpersonal outcomes, heterogeneity was large and not explained by moderators, and the authors suggest treatments may need adapting to address social difficulties.
^p39450446-reporting

## Scope as reported

Pre-registered review of seven databases; 13 trials were pooled and six synthesised narratively.
^p39450446-limit

The review took trauma-focused CBT and EMDR as the two best-evidenced PTSD treatments for people aged 5 to 25 and included only randomised trials of those two approaches.
^p39450446-treatments

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The evidence concerns children and young people with PTSD rather than adults with complex PTSD; its relevance here is the general point that symptom-focused trials under-measure social functioning.
^p39450446-caution-age
