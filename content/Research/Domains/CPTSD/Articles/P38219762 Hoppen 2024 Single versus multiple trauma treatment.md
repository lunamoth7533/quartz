# The efficacy of psychological interventions for adult post-traumatic stress disorder following exposure to single versus multiple traumatic events: a meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials.

**Authors.** Hoppen TH, Meiser-Stedman R, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38219762/)

**Published.** 2024 in The lancet. Psychiatry (volume 11, issue 2, pages 112-122).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/S2215-0366(23)00373-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(23)00373-5) · PMID [38219762](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38219762/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Against passive controls, psychological interventions had large effects both in trials of single-event PTSD (g about 1.04) and in trials of multiple-event PTSD (g about 1.13), with no difference between the two.
^p38219762-efficacy

Against active controls effects were moderate and again did not differ by trauma category; results held in sensitivity and follow-up analyses, and evidence quality was rated moderate to high.
^p38219762-active

Contrary to their hypothesis, the authors conclude that a history of multiple traumas does not reduce the efficacy of psychological treatment for PTSD.
^p38219762-conclusion

## Scope as reported

Pre-registered meta-analysis (searches to April 2023) of adult clinical trials in which at least 70% met full PTSD criteria; multiple-trauma trials were those in which at least half of participants reported two or more events, and heterogeneity was substantial.
^p38219762-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Multiple-event exposure is a trial-level exposure category, not an ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis, so the result speaks to trauma history rather than to self-organisation disturbances.
^p38219762-caution-category
