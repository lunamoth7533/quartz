# Testing predictions from the memory and identity theory of ICD-11 complex posttraumatic stress disorder: Measurement development and initial findings.

**Authors.** Hyland P, Shevlin M, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38991292/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of anxiety disorders (volume 105, pages 102898).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.janxdis.2024.102898](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.janxdis.2024.102898) · PMID [38991292](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38991292/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In this wartime sample, 90% reported lifetime trauma and 9.2% screened positive for ICD-11 complex PTSD.
^p38991292-sample

Newly developed measures of memory and identity disturbance showed satisfactory psychometric properties.
^p38991292-measures

All but one of the structural associations the theory predicts between memory and identity problems and complex PTSD symptoms were observed, alongside several effects the theory did not predict.
^p38991292-structure

## Scope as reported

Single cross-sectional self-report survey analysed with exploratory factor analysis and structural equation modelling; the authors describe the result as initial support.
^p38991292-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Structural models fitted to one cross-sectional survey show that the predicted associations hold, not that memory and identity disturbances cause the symptoms, and the new measures still need validation outside a war-exposed population.
^p38991292-caution-crosssectional
