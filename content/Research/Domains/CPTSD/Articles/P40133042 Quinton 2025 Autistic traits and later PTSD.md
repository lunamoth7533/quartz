# Autistic traits in childhood and post-traumatic stress disorder as young adults: a cohort study.

**Authors.** Quinton AMG, Rumball F, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40133042/)

**Published.** 2025 in Journal of child psychology and psychiatry, and allied disciplines (volume 66, issue 10, pages 1514-1525).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/jcpp.14163](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.14163) · PMID [40133042](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40133042/) · PMCID [PMC12353592](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12353592/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Higher childhood autistic traits were associated with more reported lifetime trauma exposure, lifetime PTSD, general psychopathology and not being in education, employment or training at 18.
^p40133042-associations

Only the associations with PTSD (odds ratio about 1.9) and with general psychopathology held after adjustment for sex, family socioeconomic status and IQ.
^p40133042-adjusted

Among trauma-exposed children, autistic traits remained associated with PTSD after adjustment.
^p40133042-exposed

## Scope as reported

Population cohort using a trait measure rather than autism diagnoses; the authors state that extension to diagnosed autistic children needs testing.
^p40133042-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Trait scores in a general population are not diagnoses, and an association that survives adjustment still leaves open whether autistic traits raise vulnerability, shape how symptoms are reported, or both.
^p40133042-caution-traits
