# A systematic review and meta-analysis of PTSD symptoms at mid-treatment during trauma-focused treatment for PTSD.

**Authors.** Purnell L, Graham A, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39270371/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of anxiety disorders (volume 107, pages 102925).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.janxdis.2024.102925](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.janxdis.2024.102925) · PMID [39270371](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39270371/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

At mid-treatment, trauma-focused therapies showed no evidence of PTSD symptom worsening relative to control conditions.
^p39270371-exacerbation

Analyses restricted to higher-quality trials or passive controls showed small effects favouring trauma-focused treatment by mid-treatment, and depression was also slightly lower by then.
^p39270371-sensitivity

At post-treatment the effect on PTSD symptoms was medium in size.
^p39270371-post

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis of randomised trials with sensitivity analyses; the authors note that worsening could occur before mid-treatment and that people who worsen may drop out and be missing from the analyses.
^p39270371-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

No average worsening at one time point does not rule out transient worsening for some individuals, and the included trials concern PTSD in general, so whether the result holds for severe complex presentations is a separate question.
^p39270371-caution-average
