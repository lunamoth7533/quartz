# Prevalence of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) in autistic children or young people (CYP) and adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Mansour H, Kurana S, et al. (13 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40783299/)

**Published.** 2025 in Clinical psychology review (volume 120, pages 102621).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.cpr.2025.102621](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2025.102621) · PMID [40783299](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40783299/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Pooled point prevalence of diagnosed PTSD was about 1.1% in autistic children and young people and 2.1% in autistic adults; lifetime prevalence was about 5.7% and 2.7% respectively.
^p40783299-prevalence

Estimates varied with co-occurring intellectual disability, gender mix, diagnostic criteria (DSM or ICD) and informant.
^p40783299-moderators

The rates resemble general-population estimates but are lower than screening-tool studies suggest, which the authors read as a possible sign that current criteria miss how autistic people experience and express trauma.
^p40783299-discrepancy

## Scope as reported

Random-effects meta-analysis of diagnosed PTSD in diagnosed autistic samples; the confidence interval for adult point prevalence was very wide.
^p40783299-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Low recorded prevalence alongside high screening scores could reflect under-recognition or over-inclusive screening; the meta-analysis cannot decide between them, so neither figure should be treated as the true rate.
^p40783299-caution-underdiagnosis
