# Psychological interventions for complex trauma

**Definition.** This topic covers whether psychological therapies help adults with complex trauma presentations, separately from which structure they use. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]

## Supported claims

- Pooled psychotherapy effects in a 2025 meta-analysis were significant for PTSD, depression, anxiety and dissociation outcomes. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]
- Effects decreased slightly at follow-up but remained significant except for anxiety and dissociation. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-followup|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]
- Participants with childhood trauma showed lower effects, risk of bias moderated results, and the authors report some indications of publication bias. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]
- Complex trauma exposure in these trials is not the same category as a formal ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-caution-category|AI synthesis: Psychological interventions for complex PTSD]]

## How it works

**The pooled result.** Effects of psychotherapy compared with control were significant for PTSD (k = 27), depression (k = 23), anxiety (k = 13) and dissociation (k = 7) outcomes in complex trauma presentations. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] At follow-up the effect sizes decreased slightly but remained significant except for anxiety and dissociation symptoms, so the durability claim is outcome-specific rather than uniform. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-followup|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]]

**Who responded less well.** Participants with childhood trauma showed lower effects than those with other trauma types, risk of bias and sample composition - the proportion of female participants - moderated results, and the authors report some indications of publication bias. Moderators of this kind are the most informative part of a synthesis because they describe where the average came from, and this pattern is what makes pooled effects fragile to interpret. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-moderators|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-limit|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]]

**The category mismatch.** Complex trauma exposure in these trials is not the same category as a formal ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis, so group averages should not be read as individual prognosis. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-caution-category|AI appraisal: Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] The diagnostic construct is instrument-dependent: the ITQ was developed as a self-report measure whose properties must be established in each new population, and the questionnaire has its own documented limits. [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-development|International Trauma Questionnaire]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-limit|International Trauma Questionnaire]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-limits|International Trauma Questionnaire]]

**Sequencing is a separate question.** The phase-versus-non-phase comparison found no significant differences across most planned contrasts, with selected outcomes better under structured designs, and the comparison was between packaged protocols rather than a test of sequencing; [[Phase-based trauma therapy evidence]] covers that debate. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-comparison|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]] [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-caution-sequence|AI appraisal: Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]

**Mechanistic background.** Extinction-based models supply the learning account behind exposure components, and [[Memory and trauma]] covers the memory side.

**Reading rule.** Report the outcome, the follow-up and the population definition; then check whether the trials measured a formal diagnosis or a trauma-exposure category. The distinction changes what the average describes. [[P40154799 Hu 2025 Psychological interventions for CPTSD#^p40154799-improvement|Psychological interventions for CPTSD]] [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-caution-boundary|AI appraisal: ICD-11 PTSD proposals review]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Pooled effects and moderators in this article are meta-analytic products, so the methods domain's synthesis limits (heterogeneity, publication bias, subgroup interpretation) apply directly to how this article's numbers should be read. [[Meta-analysis and review limits]] [[Replication and publication bias]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Heterogeneous samples and moderator findings mean the pooled effect describes an average, not a prognosis for one person.
