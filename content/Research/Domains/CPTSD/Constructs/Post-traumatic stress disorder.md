# Post-traumatic stress disorder

## Definition

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is a diagnosis that can follow exposure to a traumatic event, marked by re-experiencing, avoidance and a persisting sense of threat or heightened arousal. [[P32644555 Torrico 2024 Posttraumatic stress disorder#^p32644555-definition|Torrico 2024]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-core-clusters|VA: Complex PTSD history and definitions]] It is a symptom-based diagnosis, distinct from the stress response to an event, and it is the foundation of ICD-11 complex PTSD, which requires the PTSD criteria plus disturbances in self-organisation. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|VA: Complex PTSD history and definitions]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-exposure-required|VA: Complex PTSD history and definitions]]

## How it works

**Two manuals, overlapping rules.** The diagnosis entered the DSM in its third edition in 1980; in the DSM criteria as a 2024 reference chapter summarises them, a traumatic exposure is followed by symptoms in four areas: intrusions such as memories or nightmares, avoidance, negative shifts in mood and thinking, and heightened arousal. [[P32644555 Torrico 2024 Posttraumatic stress disorder#^p32644555-dsm|Torrico 2024]] ICD-11 builds PTSD more narrowly around three essential fear-related components - reliving the event in the here and now, avoiding reminders, and a heightened sense of current threat - after an extremely threatening or horrific event. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-core-clusters|VA: Complex PTSD history and definitions]] The current DSM edition is DSM-5-TR, and the WHO's ICD-11 diagnostic manual differs from it in places, so any PTSD figure should name its system. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-limit|ICD-11 CDDR]]

**The systems pick out partly different people.** Estimates using DSM-based items suggest lower adult prevalence under ICD-11 than under older systems, while ICD-11 PTSD also detects some impaired people whom DSM-IV or DSM-5 miss. [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-prevalence|Brewin 2017]] [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-impairment|Brewin 2017]] In a German population survey, DSM-5 and ICD-11 algorithms gave the same probable prevalence (4.7%) but agreed only substantially (kappa 0.62), with ICD-11 caseness approximated from a DSM-5 checklist. [[P40859920 Pettrich 2025 DSM-5 versus ICD-11 PTSD prevalence#^p40859920-prevalence|Pettrich 2025]] [[P40859920 Pettrich 2025 DSM-5 versus ICD-11 PTSD prevalence#^p40859920-agreement|Pettrich 2025]] [[P40859920 Pettrich 2025 DSM-5 versus ICD-11 PTSD prevalence#^p40859920-caution-approximation|Appraisal: Pettrich 2025]]

**Exposure is common; the disorder is not.** In a Dutch probability sample, 81.5% reported lifetime exposure to a potentially traumatic event, yet current PTSD was about 1% under either system. [[P39808949 Hoeboer 2025 PTSD and CPTSD epidemiology Netherlands#^p39808949-exposure|Hoeboer 2025]] [[P39808949 Hoeboer 2025 PTSD and CPTSD epidemiology Netherlands#^p39808949-prevalence|Hoeboer 2025]] Across 26 World Mental Health surveys with 71,083 adults, lifetime PTSD was 3.9% overall and 5.6% among people exposed to trauma. [[P28385165 Koenen 2017 PTSD in the World Mental Health Surveys#^p28385165-prevalence|Koenen 2017]] Among the exposed, markers of social disadvantage - younger age, female sex, being unmarried, less education, lower income and unemployment - went with higher lifetime risk, a group-level pattern rather than an individual forecast. [[P28385165 Koenen 2017 PTSD in the World Mental Health Surveys#^p28385165-risk|Koenen 2017]] [[P28385165 Koenen 2017 PTSD in the World Mental Health Surveys#^p28385165-caution-recall|Appraisal: Koenen 2017]]

**Course and consequences.** About half of the people with PTSD in the World Mental Health surveys described their symptoms as persistent. [[P28385165 Koenen 2017 PTSD in the World Mental Health Surveys#^p28385165-persistence|Koenen 2017]] The reference chapter lists chronic impairment, co-occurring psychiatric illness and a raised risk of suicide among the consequences. [[P32644555 Torrico 2024 Posttraumatic stress disorder#^p32644555-definition|Torrico 2024]]

**First-line treatment at class level.** Across 14 international guidelines, most count both psychological and pharmacological therapies as first-line; all but one name cognitive behavioural therapy as the first-line psychological approach and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors as the first-line drug class. [[P34575284 Martin 2021 Treatment guidelines for PTSD#^p34575284-firstline|Martin 2021]] Recommendations for core symptoms varied little between guidelines, while nightmares were seldom addressed. [[P34575284 Martin 2021 Treatment guidelines for PTSD#^p34575284-nightmares|Martin 2021]] Public guidance describes the psychological side as trauma-focused psychotherapy, and exposure-based therapy such as prolonged exposure draws on laboratory research on fear conditioning and extinction. [[F88 NIMH Post-traumatic stress disorder#^f88-treatment|NIMH: Post-traumatic stress disorder]] [[P39032727 Maples-Keller 2025 Treatments derived from fear extinction#^p39032727-model|Maples-Keller 2025]] In trials, psychological treatment worked as well after multiple traumatic events as after a single event. [[P38219762 Hoppen 2024 Single versus multiple trauma treatment#^p38219762-conclusion|Hoppen 2024]] These are recommendations about treatment classes for populations; this note gives no drug choice, dose or individual treatment advice. [[P34575284 Martin 2021 Treatment guidelines for PTSD#^p34575284-caution-classlevel|Appraisal: Martin 2021]]

**Access is uneven.** Treatment seeking was about 54% in high-income countries but roughly 23-29% in lower- and upper-middle-income countries, and in the Dutch sample about half of people with probable lifetime PTSD had sought help and about a third had received first-line treatment. [[P28385165 Koenen 2017 PTSD in the World Mental Health Surveys#^p28385165-treatment|Koenen 2017]] [[P39808949 Hoeboer 2025 PTSD and CPTSD epidemiology Netherlands#^p39808949-care|Hoeboer 2025]]

**Relation to mechanism and to complex PTSD.** Because current definitions are symptom-based, trauma type or duration alone does not decide the diagnosis; the stress physiology and learning that follow an event are described in [[Trauma and stress responses]] and [[Fear learning and extinction]], and they occur whether or not the criteria are met. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|VA: Complex PTSD history and definitions]] ICD-11 complex PTSD requires the PTSD criteria plus affect-regulation problems, a persistently negative self-concept and relationship difficulties, whereas DSM-5 has no separate complex PTSD diagnosis. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-dso|VA: Complex PTSD history and definitions]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-systems|VA: Complex PTSD history and definitions]] In representative samples from settings without war exposure and with developed economies, ICD-11 complex PTSD (about 4%) was more common than PTSD (about 2%). [[P40652792 Fung 2025 ICD-11 PTSD and CPTSD prevalence#^p40652792-pooled|Fung 2025]] The DSM-5 dissociative subtype, built around depersonalisation and derealisation, is a PTSD construct rather than a version of complex PTSD. [[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-definition|Lanius 2012]] [[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-limit|Lanius 2012]]

**Reading rule.** Before comparing rates, name the manual, the instrument and whether a figure is lifetime, past-year or current; before comparing treatments, name the comparator. The same population yields different PTSD groups under different rules. [[P40859920 Pettrich 2025 DSM-5 versus ICD-11 PTSD prevalence#^p40859920-agreement|Pettrich 2025]] [[P39808949 Hoeboer 2025 PTSD and CPTSD epidemiology Netherlands#^p39808949-caution-systems|Appraisal: Hoeboer 2025]]

## Evidence and status

Prevalence rests on large population surveys using structured lay interviews or self-report questionnaires, and estimates move with the diagnostic system and instrument. [[P28385165 Koenen 2017 PTSD in the World Mental Health Surveys#^p28385165-limit|Koenen 2017]] [[P39808949 Hoeboer 2025 PTSD and CPTSD epidemiology Netherlands#^p39808949-caution-systems|Appraisal: Hoeboer 2025]] Course evidence held here is a single persistence figure from retrospective reports. [[P28385165 Koenen 2017 PTSD in the World Mental Health Surveys#^p28385165-caution-recall|Appraisal: Koenen 2017]] Treatment recommendations rest on guideline syntheses that agree on first-line classes, though many guidelines were out of date when reviewed. [[P34575284 Martin 2021 Treatment guidelines for PTSD#^p34575284-guidelines|Martin 2021]] [[P34575284 Martin 2021 Treatment guidelines for PTSD#^p34575284-caution-classlevel|Appraisal: Martin 2021]]

## Uncertainties

- Why most exposed people do not develop PTSD while some do; social-disadvantage markers describe group risk only. [[P28385165 Koenen 2017 PTSD in the World Mental Health Surveys#^p28385165-risk|Koenen 2017]]
- Whether the narrower ICD-11 or the broader DSM definition better identifies people who are impaired and need treatment. [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-impairment|Brewin 2017]]
- How to treat nightmares, which most guidelines barely address. [[P34575284 Martin 2021 Treatment guidelines for PTSD#^p34575284-nightmares|Martin 2021]]
- How far PTSD trial results transfer to complex PTSD, since multiple-event exposure in trials is not a complex PTSD diagnosis. [[P38219762 Hoppen 2024 Single versus multiple trauma treatment#^p38219762-caution-category|Appraisal: Hoppen 2024]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Population survey (Journal of anxiety disorders).** Measures the gap between exposure and disorder in one country and finds that only about a third of people with probable lifetime PTSD had received first-line treatment. [[P39808949 Hoeboer 2025 PTSD and CPTSD epidemiology Netherlands#^p39808949-care|Hoeboer 2025]]
- **2025 · Representative population survey (Epidemiology and psychiatric sciences).** Shows two manuals agreeing on how many people have PTSD while disagreeing on who they are. [[P40859920 Pettrich 2025 DSM-5 versus ICD-11 PTSD prevalence#^p40859920-agreement|Pettrich 2025]]
- **2024 · Meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials (The lancet. Psychiatry).** Finds no loss of psychological-treatment efficacy after multiple rather than single traumatic events. [[P38219762 Hoppen 2024 Single versus multiple trauma treatment#^p38219762-efficacy|Hoppen 2024]]

- [[ICD-11 versus DSM-5 classification]] - why the manual must be named: the two define PTSD differently and identify partly different people.
- [[Fear learning and extinction]] - the laboratory model behind exposure-based therapy, the psychological first-line class, and behind relapse after it.
- [[Memory and trauma]] - intrusive memories are a core symptom in both manuals; that note sets out what is established about memory for traumatic events.
- [[Antidepressant mechanisms]] - how the drug class that guidelines name first-line acts at the synapse, and why clinical change lags behind the pharmacology.
- [[PTSD biology and CPTSD evidence limits]] - the biological findings attached to this diagnosis, which are group-level and do not transfer automatically to complex PTSD.
- [[Adverse childhood experiences]] - childhood adversity as a population risk factor, measured by counts that cannot predict an individual's outcome.
- [[Adrenergic blockers in psychiatry]] - prazosin and clonidine for hyperarousal and nightmares, and propranolol tried as prevention after trauma, each with discordant or weak evidence.
