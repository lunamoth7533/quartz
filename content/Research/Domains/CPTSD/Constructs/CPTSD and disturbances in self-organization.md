# CPTSD and disturbances in self-organization

**Definition.** Complex PTSD in ICD-11 combines the core PTSD symptom clusters with disturbances in self-organization. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-dso|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]

## Supported claims

- The self-organization disturbances cover affect regulation, a persistently negative self-concept and relational difficulties. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-dso|Complex PTSD: History and Definitions]]
- The construct is intended to describe the more complex reactions typical of chronic trauma exposure. [[P32345416 Cloitre 2020 ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial#^p32345416-aim|ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial]]
- The three domains come from the ICD-11 criteria and the VA overview rather than from the introductory editorial. [[P32345416 Cloitre 2020 ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial#^p32345416-caution-domains|Appraisal: ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial]]

## How it works

**Core PTSD plus DSO.** Complex PTSD adds disturbances in self-organisation to the core PTSD symptoms - problems with affect regulation, a persistently negative self-concept and difficulties in relationships - and it requires that the PTSD criteria are met in addition. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-dso|Complex PTSD: history and definitions]] ICD-11 specifies the essential features and allows specification of course, so the construct is described at the level of symptom clusters rather than severity ratings. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]]

**Qualifying exposure.** Exposure to a traumatic event remains necessary, but current definitions are symptom-based: trauma type or duration alone does not determine the diagnosis. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-exposure-required|Complex PTSD: history and definitions]] [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|Complex PTSD: history and definitions]]

**The rationale and its provenance.** The editorial introducing ICD-11 complex PTSD describes it as covering the more complex reactions typical of individuals exposed to chronic trauma and expects the distinction to provide greater precision - a rationale rather than a body of evidence, since the editorial presents no data. The three self-organisation domains come from the ICD-11 criteria and the VA overview, not from that editorial. [[P32345416 Cloitre 2020 ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial#^p32345416-aim|ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial]] [[P32345416 Cloitre 2020 ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial#^p32345416-caution-domains|Appraisal: ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial]] [[P32345416 Cloitre 2020 ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial#^p32345416-limit|ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial]]

**Evidence for the structure.** The review of studies testing the ICD-11 proposals found general support for the proposed symptom structures - three factors for PTSD, six for complex PTSD - and for the distinction between the disorders, and reported that complex PTSD identifies a group with more multiple and sustained traumas and greater functional impairment. It was written before the final release of ICD-11. [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-structure|ICD-11 PTSD proposals review]] [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-impairment|ICD-11 PTSD proposals review]]

**Measurement carries the boundary.** The International Trauma Questionnaire (ITQ) was developed as a self-report measure of ICD-11 PTSD and complex PTSD, with items selected through item response theory in trauma-exposed samples, and the optimised 12-item version showed a latent structure consistent with prior findings in the studied samples. The authors frame the instrument as an evolving standard with sample-dependent properties: diagnostic rates vary across samples and validation studies, so instrument performance should not be assumed to transfer to a new population, and where the boundary between PTSD and complex PTSD falls remains sensitive to measurement development. [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-development|International Trauma Questionnaire]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-structure|International Trauma Questionnaire]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-limits|International Trauma Questionnaire]] [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-caution-boundary|Appraisal: ICD-11 PTSD proposals review]]

**Reading rule.** Name the system, the instrument and the sample. Nothing here carries diagnostic weight for an individual, and the dissociative subtype is a separate PTSD construct rather than a CPTSD component. [[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-definition|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]] [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-purpose|ICD-11 CDDR]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** The DSO constructs (affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, disturbances in relationships) sit at the boundary with the clinical-psychiatry and psychology domains' work on emotion regulation and interpersonal function - which is why CPTSD evidence has to be read against those domains rather than treated as a standalone category. [[Emotion regulation]] [[PTSD biology and CPTSD evidence limits]]

## Limitation or common misconception

The three self-organization domains overlap in practice, and instruments operationalize them differently across studies.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Asian journal of psychiatry).** In representative general-population samples, pooled prevalence was about 4% for complex PTSD and 2% for PTSD where there was no war exposure and economies were developed, and about 15% and 16% in war-exposed or less economically developed settings, so the construct is common outside clinics and its rate follows context. [[P40652792 Fung 2025 ICD-11 PTSD and CPTSD prevalence#^p40652792-pooled|Fung 2025]] [[P40652792 Fung 2025 ICD-11 PTSD and CPTSD prevalence#^p40652792-context|Fung 2025]] The estimates rest on few studies and on probable caseness from a single questionnaire. [[P40652792 Fung 2025 ICD-11 PTSD and CPTSD prevalence#^p40652792-limit|Fung 2025]] [[P40652792 Fung 2025 ICD-11 PTSD and CPTSD prevalence#^p40652792-caution-instrument|Appraisal: Fung 2025]]
- **2024 · Systematic review (Clinical psychology & psychotherapy).** Across assessment studies the ITQ dominated, and complex PTSD was consistently more severe and impairing than PTSD in young people as well as adults; no gold-standard assessment could be recommended, and the authors stress adaptation to culture and age. [[P38894553 Sarr 2024 Assessment of ICD-11 CPTSD#^p38894553-measures|Sarr 2024]] [[P38894553 Sarr 2024 Assessment of ICD-11 CPTSD#^p38894553-differentiation|Sarr 2024]] [[P38894553 Sarr 2024 Assessment of ICD-11 CPTSD#^p38894553-adaptation|Sarr 2024]] Heavy reliance on one questionnaire means part of that consistency is shared measurement. [[P38894553 Sarr 2024 Assessment of ICD-11 CPTSD#^p38894553-caution-circularity|Appraisal: Sarr 2024]]

## Connections

- [[ICD-11 versus DSM-5 classification]] - the system this construct belongs to: complex PTSD is an ICD-11 diagnosis that DSM-5 does not use, so a finding here cannot be read across to DSM-5 samples without naming the system.
- [[Emotion regulation]] - the general psychology behind the affect-dysregulation cluster; the complex-trauma literature treats affect regulation as one of the three self-organisation domains.
- [[Trauma and stress responses]] - the step before the construct: exposure and stress physiology are its context, but a symptom-based diagnosis is a separate object from the response to an event.
- [[Emotion dysregulation across conditions]] - places the affect-dysregulation cluster alongside the dysregulation described in ADHD, bipolar disorder and autism, as one expression of a shared dimension.
- [[Dissociative constructs]] - the dissociative subtype and related constructs, which this note keeps outside the self-organisation clusters.
- [[Self-concept and identity]] - develops the negative self-concept cluster: the memory and identity theory, treatment effects on self-concept, and the contrast with borderline identity disturbance.
- [[Shame and guilt]] - the self-conscious emotions closest to the negative self-concept cluster; trauma-related shame tracks distress and trauma-related guilt tracks PTSD severity.
- [[Borderline personality disorder]] - the construct most often set against complex PTSD; comparisons mostly find distinct profiles, while people meeting both report more interpersonal trauma.
- [[Post-traumatic stress disorder]] - the diagnosis this construct is built on: the PTSD criteria must be met before self-organisation disturbances are added, so PTSD prevalence and treatment evidence are the baseline.
