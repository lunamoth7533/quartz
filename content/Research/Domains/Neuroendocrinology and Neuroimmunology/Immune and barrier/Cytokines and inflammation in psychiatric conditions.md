# Cytokines and inflammation in psychiatric conditions

## Definition

Cytokines are immune signalling molecules that influence the central nervous system as well as peripheral immune function. The hypothesis under discussion is that inflammatory signalling contributes to psychiatric symptoms in some people, not that inflammation explains these conditions generally.

## How it works

**The physiology.** Peripheral immune signals reach the brain through routes that include cytokine signalling, altered tryptophan metabolism and vagal transmission, and they produce sickness behaviour - a coordinated set of changes in activity, appetite, sleep and mood. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-mechanism|Inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-sickness|From inflammation to sickness and depression]] The brain's interface is selective rather than open: brain endothelial cells are joined by continuous tight junctions, and ions, nutrients and larger molecules depend on specific transporters, which is how a peripheral signal can act on the central nervous system without free access. [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-tight|Blood-brain barrier]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-transport|Blood-brain barrier]]

**The clinical hypothesis.** Prolonged inflammatory signalling is proposed to sustain depressive symptoms in vulnerable individuals, and inflammatory responses are framed as potentially adaptive in short bursts, with costs when sustained. [[P18073775 Dantzer 2008 Inflammation to sickness and depression#^p18073775-depression|From inflammation to sickness and depression]] [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-adaptive|The role of inflammation in depression]]

**Why the subset framing is the field's honesty move.** Reviews argue that inflammation is relevant to a subgroup identifiable by markers rather than to depression as a whole, and for targeting that marker-identified subset rather than treating inflammation as a general mechanism. [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-subset|The role of inflammation in depression]] Early inflammation-in-depression claims failed as population claims; the current framing - a subgroup identifiable by elevated markers, with experimental medicine evidence that inflammatory challenges can produce depressive-like changes - is narrower and better supported. [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-treatment|The role of inflammation in depression]]

**Where this sits in research frameworks.** In RDoC terms, immune and arousal/regulatory processes are units and systems to be studied across conditions rather than the property of one diagnosis, which is why the same marker literature is cited in several disorder domains in this library. [[F121 NIMH RDoC#^f121-matrix|RDoC]] [[F122 NIMH RDoC constructs#^f122-domains|RDoC constructs]]

**Reading rule.** Separate marker findings, mechanistic models and treatment claims. The first is empirical, the second is a proposal, and the third is experimental and requires that a person belongs to the relevant subset - a determination no cross-sectional group average can make. [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-limit|The role of inflammation in depression]]

## Evidence and status

A systematic review pooled studies of endogenous cytokine concentrations in bipolar disorder across affective states and reported elevated TNF-alpha, soluble TNF receptor type 1 and soluble interleukin-2 receptor in the included studies; these meta-analytic averages describe group differences rather than a cause or a test. [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-finding|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]] [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-caution|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]]

Group differences in circulating markers are reproducible in some conditions: elevated group-level cytokine concentrations survive meta-analysis in bipolar disorder and subgroup analyses in depression, and they support exactly that - a group-level difference in a peripheral marker. Individual-level prediction and causal direction are not established. The chain from marker to mechanism requires direction (cause or consequence), specificity (which signalling) and timing (state or trait), and the sources themselves state that circulating markers are affected by infection, sleep, medication, body composition and illness state, so group differences are not usable individual tests. [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-finding|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]] [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-subset|The role of inflammation in depression]] [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-caution|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]]

Anti-inflammatory strategies are discussed as experimental directions requiring targeting and patient selection, not as established practice: they follow the same narrowing, investigated for marker-defined subgroups rather than offered as general antidepressants. [[P26711676 Miller 2016 Inflammation in depression#^p26711676-treatment|The role of inflammation in depression]]
