# Circadian clock biology

## Definition

Circadian rhythms are approximately 24-hour cycles generated endogenously rather than driven only by external light. The molecular clock is a transcriptional feedback loop, and the system is distributed across tissues.

## How it works

**The feedback loop.** The core clock is a transcriptional-translational feedback loop: clock proteins accumulate and repress their own transcription over roughly 24 hours, producing a self-sustaining oscillation from gene expression rather than from a rhythmic input. The period is set by the kinetics of the feedback itself, and variants in clock genes shift period length - the mechanistic basis for heritable timing differences and for animal models of altered period. The period is intrinsic; light and behaviour reset its phase rather than generating it. That is what makes the clock a genuine biological oscillator rather than a response to rhythmic input: the rhythm persists without cues and is only corrected by them. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]]

**Entrainment is the bridge between intrinsic period and world time.** The molecular loop runs at an intrinsic period slightly off 24 hours, and daily light input to the suprachiasmatic nucleus adjusts its phase - advancing or delaying it - so the internal day stays locked to the external one. Because the adjustment is limited per day, large or repeated shifts (jet lag, shift work) take several cycles to accommodate, during which internal and external time disagree: that transient disagreement is the biological content of "jet lag" and of shift-work maladaptation. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-outputs|Circadian clock architecture]]

**Distributed, not centralised.** Most tissues contain their own clocks coordinated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus, so circadian organisation is distributed rather than located in one structure, and timing information can be measured in peripheral tissues as well as the brain. That architecture explains why peripheral clocks can run out of step with the central clock - the phenomenon behind shift-work and meal-timing studies - and why a single measure of 'the clock' is ambiguous. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-tissue|Circadian clock architecture]]

**Outputs.** Clock output influences metabolism, physiology and behaviour, including immune function and hormone release, which is why disrupted timing has wide-ranging rather than sleep-specific consequences. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-outputs|Circadian clock architecture]]

**Peripheral desynchrony is the health-relevant version.** Because most tissues run their own clocks, central and peripheral timing can dissociate when light-feeding-activity cues conflict - the suspected mechanism behind metabolic consequences of shift work. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-tissue|Circadian clock architecture]] [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain basics: understanding sleep]]

**What generates rhythmic behaviour.** Sleep timing results from circadian rhythms interacting with homeostatic sleep drive - how long the person has been awake and what the body clock expects. The two systems are dissociable, which is why shift work and jet lag are not simply sleep deprivation, and behaviour-level rhythm and molecular rhythm are different measurements linked by physiology. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]] [[F98 OpenStax Psychology 2e stages of sleep#^f98-stages|Stages of Sleep]]

**Reading rule.** The molecular account is primarily from animal models and describes mechanisms; the human claims are about phase, amplitude and their association with outcomes. Keeping the two apart prevents a mouse clock gene from becoming a statement about someone's sleep schedule. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-limit|Circadian clock architecture]]

## Evidence and status

The molecular clock is established in model organisms and human tissue. For peripheral desynchrony the claim's strength varies by outcome: glucose and hormone rhythms shift robustly in controlled studies, while long-term disease outcomes rest on observational occupational literature with its own confounds. [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-tissue|Circadian clock architecture]] [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain basics: understanding sleep]]

The practical effects of chronic misalignment on psychiatric outcomes are studied observationally, with the usual limits on causal inference, and the connection to mood is proposed rather than established: the social-zeitgeber review reports limited direct support for its causal version and considers an internal-pacemaker alternative. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-evaluation|The social zeitgeber theory]] [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-status|The social zeitgeber theory]]
