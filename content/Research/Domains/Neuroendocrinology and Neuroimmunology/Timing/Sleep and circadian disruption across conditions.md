# Sleep and circadian disruption across conditions

## Definition

Sleep and circadian disruption covers changes in how long, how continuously and when a person sleeps, and in the body clock that times sleep, because sleep regulation combines a circadian clock with homeostatic sleep pressure. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]] It is reported in bipolar disorder, ADHD, autism and PTSD, and this framework asks of each finding whether disturbed sleep is a cause, a consequence or a marker of shared vulnerability. [[P39210713 Tonon 2024 Sleep and circadian disruption in bipolar disorders#^p39210713-allstates|Tonon 2024]] [[P37657199 Liang 2023 Objective sleep continuity in ADHD#^p37657199-actigraphy|Liang 2023]] [[P37469173 Kim 2023 Sleep in medication-naive autistic youth#^p37469173-objective|Kim 2023]] [[P40862670 Porten 2025 Sleep after trauma-focused treatment#^p40862670-background|Porten 2025]]

## How it works

**Two systems, several measurements.** Because timing and sleep pressure are separate systems, and sleep itself cycles through REM and non-REM stages, 'disrupted sleep' can mean altered timing, shorter duration, broken continuity or changed architecture, each measured differently. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-rhythm|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]] [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]] The four conditions are studied with different emphases: modelled circadian phase in bipolar time series, latency and efficiency in ADHD and autism, and nightmares and sleep quantity in PTSD. [[P38579366 Song 2024 Circadian phase and mood symptom dynamics#^p38579366-design|Song 2024]] [[P37657199 Liang 2023 Objective sleep continuity in ADHD#^p37657199-actigraphy|Liang 2023]] [[P37469173 Kim 2023 Sleep in medication-naive autistic youth#^p37469173-objective|Kim 2023]] [[P39376120 Reffi 2024 Trauma-related nightmares and PTSD symptoms#^p39376120-prospective|Reffi 2024]] [[P36706561 Zalta 2023 Sleep timing chronotype and PTSD#^p36706561-timing|Zalta 2023]]

**Bipolar I: disturbance in every mood state and before episodes.** A recent review reports changes in sleep phase, structure and duration in all mood states of bipolar disorder, linked with relapse, suicide risk and impaired cognition. [[P39210713 Tonon 2024 Sleep and circadian disruption in bipolar disorders#^p39210713-allstates|Tonon 2024]] Most patients in a current episode recalled sleep disturbance beforehand once asked specifically, and onset was reported earlier before depression than before mania. [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-prevalence|Basquin 2024]] [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-timing|Basquin 2024]] In long wearable records, modelled circadian phase disturbance preceded mood symptoms in bipolar I and major depression but not in bipolar II, and sleep-phase disturbance showed no such lead. [[P38579366 Song 2024 Circadian phase and mood symptom dynamics#^p38579366-direction|Song 2024]] The social zeitgeber account that motivated rhythm-based treatment has limited direct support, so these findings test a plausible mechanism rather than confirm it. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-evaluation|The social zeitgeber theory]]

**ADHD: complaints outrun objective differences, and timing may be delayed.** In children and adolescents with ADHD, actigraphy shows longer sleep latency and lower sleep efficiency, whereas polysomnography differences are not significant. [[P37657199 Liang 2023 Objective sleep continuity in ADHD#^p37657199-actigraphy|Liang 2023]] Medication, comorbidity and the number of recorded nights move these estimates. [[P37657199 Liang 2023 Objective sleep continuity in ADHD#^p37657199-moderators|Liang 2023]] Delayed sleep-wake phase disorder, the commonest circadian disorder of adolescence, is often associated with ADHD and autism. [[P38054481 Narala 2024 Delayed sleep-wake phase disorder in adolescents#^p38054481-prevalence|Narala 2024]] The overlap with inattention runs forward in time: parent-reported sleep problems at 8-9 years predicted higher inattention and other ADHD scores two years later. [[P37721582 González-Safont 2023 Childhood sleep problems and later ADHD symptoms#^p37721582-prospective|González-Safont 2023]]

**Autism: frequent insomnia and a possibly different clock.** Sleep disturbance, insomnia above all, is much more common in autistic youth than in the general population. [[P38302207 Johnson 2024 Autism spectrum disorder and sleep#^p38302207-insomnia|Johnson 2024]] Medication-naive autistic children show longer latency, lower efficiency and shorter total sleep on objective measures, with moderate effects. [[P37469173 Kim 2023 Sleep in medication-naive autistic youth#^p37469173-objective|Kim 2023]] An atypical wrist-temperature rhythm, most marked with co-occurring ADHD, is read by its authors as support for atypical melatonin secretion. [[P38813763 Martinez-Cayuelas 2024 Circadian functioning in autism and ADHD#^p38813763-temperature|Martinez-Cayuelas 2024]]

**PTSD and CPTSD: nightmares, arousal and persistent complaints.** PTSD's symptom pattern includes intrusive memories and altered arousal. [[F88 NIMH Post-traumatic stress disorder#^f88-pattern|Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder]] After traumatic injury, trauma-related nightmares were more common following interpersonal violence and predicted more severe PTSD symptoms a month later. [[P39376120 Reffi 2024 Trauma-related nightmares and PTSD symptoms#^p39376120-violence|Reffi 2024]] [[P39376120 Reffi 2024 Trauma-related nightmares and PTSD symptoms#^p39376120-prospective|Reffi 2024]] Bed and wake times are only weakly related to PTSD, with shorter sleep weakly linked to more symptoms. [[P36706561 Zalta 2023 Sleep timing chronotype and PTSD#^p36706561-timing|Zalta 2023]] In women with PTSD related to childhood abuse, subjective sleep improved with trauma-focused therapy while actigraphy did not change and most remained above a clinical cut-off. [[P40862670 Porten 2025 Sleep after trauma-focused treatment#^p40862670-subjective|Porten 2025]] [[P40862670 Porten 2025 Sleep after trauma-focused treatment#^p40862670-objective|Porten 2025]]

**Cause, consequence or shared vulnerability.** A reciprocal model expects influence in both directions: disturbed sleep can precipitate or worsen symptoms, and symptoms can disturb sleep and clock-controlled processes. [[P38394243 Meyer 2024 Sleep-circadian interface in mental disorders#^p38394243-reciprocal|Meyer 2024]] Genetic designs give some leverage on direction: in one Mendelian randomization study a genetically later activity peak was linked to both autism and ADHD, while the nominal ADHD sleep association ran from the condition to more fragmented sleep. [[P38869021 Dai 2025 Mendelian randomization circadian traits autism ADHD#^p38869021-chronotype|Dai 2025]] [[P38869021 Dai 2025 Mendelian randomization circadian traits autism ADHD#^p38869021-sleepquality|Dai 2025]] Variants in core clock genes correlate with risk for various conditions, one candidate route to shared vulnerability. [[P38871615 Janoski 2024 Clock gene polymorphisms in human pathologies#^p38871615-variants|Janoski 2024]] Sleep disturbance that persists after trauma-focused treatment argues against reading it as a mere by-product of PTSD symptoms. [[P40862670 Porten 2025 Sleep after trauma-focused treatment#^p40862670-caution-persistence|Appraisal: Porten 2025]]

**Reading rule.** For any sleep finding in a condition, name the dimension (timing, duration, continuity or architecture), the measure (report, diary, actigraphy or polysomnography) and the design, then ask which of cause, consequence or shared vulnerability that design can actually test. [[P37657199 Liang 2023 Objective sleep continuity in ADHD#^p37657199-caution-measure|Appraisal: Liang 2023]] [[P38579366 Song 2024 Circadian phase and mood symptom dynamics#^p38579366-caution-precedence|Appraisal: Song 2024]]

## Evidence and status

Descriptive evidence is the strongest layer: meta-analyses establish objective sleep differences in autistic and ADHD youth, and reviews describe disturbance across bipolar mood states. [[P37469173 Kim 2023 Sleep in medication-naive autistic youth#^p37469173-objective|Kim 2023]] [[P37657199 Liang 2023 Objective sleep continuity in ADHD#^p37657199-actigraphy|Liang 2023]] [[P39210713 Tonon 2024 Sleep and circadian disruption in bipolar disorders#^p39210713-allstates|Tonon 2024]] Directional evidence is thinner and rests on single designs: a three-cohort study in ADHD, a wearable time-series study in mood disorders, one Mendelian randomization analysis and a small prospective nightmare study. [[P37721582 González-Safont 2023 Childhood sleep problems and later ADHD symptoms#^p37721582-prospective|González-Safont 2023]] [[P38579366 Song 2024 Circadian phase and mood symptom dynamics#^p38579366-direction|Song 2024]] [[P38869021 Dai 2025 Mendelian randomization circadian traits autism ADHD#^p38869021-chronotype|Dai 2025]] [[P39376120 Reffi 2024 Trauma-related nightmares and PTSD symptoms#^p39376120-prospective|Reffi 2024]] Each has a known gap - shared parent raters, precedence without proof, instrument assumptions and a small sample - so none yet settles direction for a condition. [[P37721582 González-Safont 2023 Childhood sleep problems and later ADHD symptoms#^p37721582-caution-rater|Appraisal: González-Safont 2023]] [[P38579366 Song 2024 Circadian phase and mood symptom dynamics#^p38579366-caution-precedence|Appraisal: Song 2024]] [[P38869021 Dai 2025 Mendelian randomization circadian traits autism ADHD#^p38869021-caution-assumptions|Appraisal: Dai 2025]] [[P39376120 Reffi 2024 Trauma-related nightmares and PTSD symptoms#^p39376120-caution-small|Appraisal: Reffi 2024]] Prodromal reports in bipolar disorder add recall bias to the list. [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-caution-recall|Appraisal: Basquin 2024]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether circadian markers such as melatonin onset or temperature rhythms differ consistently enough in any of the four conditions to work as markers rather than group tendencies.
- Whether the direction of the sleep-symptom link really differs between conditions, as one Mendelian randomization study suggests for autism and ADHD, once larger case samples are available.
- None of the studies used here separated ICD-11 complex PTSD from PTSD; the CPTSD link rests on childhood-abuse samples.
- How much of the sleep difference in ADHD and autism reflects the measure chosen (report, actigraphy or polysomnography) rather than physiology.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Secondary analysis of a randomised trial (European Journal of Psychotraumatology).** Tests whether PTSD treatment resolves sleep problems: reported sleep improved but actigraphy did not, and most women still scored above a clinical cut-off. [[P40862670 Porten 2025 Sleep after trauma-focused treatment#^p40862670-subjective|Porten 2025]] [[P40862670 Porten 2025 Sleep after trauma-focused treatment#^p40862670-objective|Porten 2025]]
- **2025 · Mendelian randomization (Autism).** The first genetic test of direction for autism and ADHD in this note, with only the late-activity finding surviving correction for multiple tests. [[P38869021 Dai 2025 Mendelian randomization circadian traits autism ADHD#^p38869021-chronotype|Dai 2025]] [[P38869021 Dai 2025 Mendelian randomization circadian traits autism ADHD#^p38869021-sleepquality|Dai 2025]] Its direction claims are hypotheses to replicate. [[P38869021 Dai 2025 Mendelian randomization circadian traits autism ADHD#^p38869021-caution-assumptions|Appraisal: Dai 2025]]
- **2024 · Prospective observational cohort (EBioMedicine).** The time-order evidence for bipolar I, built from more than 40,000 days of wearable and mood data. [[P38579366 Song 2024 Circadian phase and mood symptom dynamics#^p38579366-design|Song 2024]]

## Connections

- [[Circadian clock biology]] - the molecular clock whose timing this note follows across conditions; clock-gene variants are one candidate route to shared vulnerability.
- [[Circadian rhythms and sleep]] - supplies the two-system account, clock plus sleep pressure, that decides which sleep dimension a condition-level claim is about.
- [[Circadian and social rhythm hypotheses]] - the bipolar proposal that disrupted routines destabilise rhythms and trigger episodes, which the prodrome and time-series findings here test.
- [[Sleep disorders]] - the clinical categories, including circadian rhythm disorders, into which a condition's sleep complaint has to be sorted before it is interpreted.
- [[Psychoeducation and social rhythms]] - where rhythm regularity becomes a bipolar treatment component, with trial evidence about the package rather than the causal theory.
- [[Adenosine signalling]] - the best-supported molecular candidate for homeostatic sleep pressure, the second system alongside the clock in every finding above.
- [[Trauma and stress responses]] - the trauma domain's account of stress physiology across HPA, autonomic and inflammatory signalling, the wider frame for the PTSD sleep findings here.
- [[Neural oscillations]] - the EEG rhythms that define sleep stages, and the gap between the timing and continuity findings here and oscillation measures such as spindles.
