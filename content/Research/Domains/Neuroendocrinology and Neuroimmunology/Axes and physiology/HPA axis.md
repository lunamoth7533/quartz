# HPA axis

## Definition

The HPA axis is a hormonal control loop: hypothalamic corticotropin-releasing hormone drives pituitary adrenocorticotropic hormone, which drives cortisol from the adrenal cortex, and cortisol feeds back to restrain the loop.

## How it works

**The control loop.** Hypothalamic corticotropin-releasing hormone reaches the pituitary through the portal circulation and drives adrenocorticotropic hormone release, and adrenocorticotropic hormone in turn drives cortisol from the adrenal cortex. Cortisol feeds back on hypothalamus and pituitary to limit further release, which is what makes the axis a loop rather than a chain, and the feedback is what terminates a response rather than merely damping it. [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-feedback|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-drive|The Adrenal Glands]]

**Where the feedback acts.** Releasing-factor secretion responds to circulating corticosteroid levels at the hypothalamus, and corticotroph cells of the pituitary respond directly; central sites including the hippocampus and prefrontal regions participate in the regulation of the axis. Because negative feedback is distributed across several sites, a change measured in circulating cortisol can reflect altered drive, altered feedback sensitivity or altered adrenal output, and a static value cannot distinguish them. [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-feedback|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|Glucocorticoids and stress]]

**Two receptor populations, named and located.** In rat brain, autoradiographic binding distinguished two corticosteroid receptor systems: a mineralocorticoid-like (corticosterone) receptor and a glucocorticoid receptor. The mineralocorticoid-like population is concentrated in the lateral septum and hippocampus - highest in the subiculum plus CA1 field and in the dentate gyrus - and binds corticosterone with high affinity (Kd about 0.5 nM); the glucocorticoid receptor is more widely distributed, including the paraventricular nucleus, and binds with roughly six- to ten-fold lower affinity. Occupancy follows from those affinities: the high-affinity population was about 89.5% occupied even at the daily trough of pituitary-adrenal activity, while the lower-affinity population is recruited mainly at peak and stress levels. The same circulating concentration therefore engages different receptors at different times of day, which is a mechanism about location and affinity rather than about the hormone having two identities. [[P2998738 Reul 1985 Two receptor systems for corticosterone in rat brain#^p2998738-systems|Two receptor systems for corticosterone in rat brain]] [[P2998738 Reul 1985 Two receptor systems for corticosterone in rat brain#^p2998738-affinity|Two receptor systems for corticosterone in rat brain]] [[P2998738 Reul 1985 Two receptor systems for corticosterone in rat brain#^p2998738-occupation|Two receptor systems for corticosterone in rat brain]]

**Adrenal output is not one hormone.** The adrenal cortex produces mineralocorticoids, glucocorticoids and androgens from different zones, so 'adrenal output' is not one signal but a family of signals whose relative contributions depend on the stimulus. Statements about 'the stress hormone' describe the glucocorticoid arm of one axis, not the adrenal gland's whole output. [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-cortex|The Adrenal Glands]]

**Two rhythms, two timescales.** Cortisol is secreted in pulses and follows a daily rhythm, so a single sample mixes the pulse, the time of day and the individual's state. Its effects depend on context and timing and run on different timescales - rapid membrane-associated effects within minutes and slower genomic effects over hours - which is why the same hormone can be described both as an acute responder and as a medium-term regulator, and why the review of glucocorticoid action proposes a four-way classification rather than a single function. [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-timing|Glucocorticoids and stress]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-mediators|Stress mediators]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|Glucocorticoids and stress]]

**Interaction with the fast arm.** The sympathetic nervous system responds within seconds; the hormonal arm takes minutes to hours, so the two handle different phases of a demand. Because the two arms run on different timescales, autonomic and cortisol measurements diverge in the same individual and neither can stand in for the other; they are coordinated rather than redundant, and each can be active while the other is not. ## Evidence and status

Axis anatomy and feedback are well established; the functional interpretation of cortisol in behaviour and psychiatric conditions is less settled, and single measurements are weak proxies. [[F08 OpenStax What is stress#^f08-limit|What is Stress]]

**What the receptor map does not claim.** The rat mapping shows where the two receptor systems sit and how they bind; it does not say what a given cortisol value is doing in a person. The review that organises glucocorticoid actions by permissive, suppressive, stimulatory and preparative categories is explicit that these categories are criteria generated from heterogeneous findings rather than a settled taxonomy. [[P2998738 Reul 1985 Two receptor systems for corticosterone in rat brain#^p2998738-limit|Two receptor systems for corticosterone in rat brain]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-four|Glucocorticoids and stress]] [[P10696570 Sapolsky 2000 Glucocorticoids and stress#^p10696570-contested|Glucocorticoids and stress]]

**Four different things that get called 'chronic stress'.** Because single measures are weak indices, the time-integrated framework used here is allostatic load: the cumulative cost of repeated or sustained adjustment through mediators such as cortisol and catecholamines, built from several measurements rather than from any one sample. [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-load|Stress mediators]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-allostasis|Stress mediators]] Allostatic load is one way to index multisystem burden, not a required vocabulary: the construct, its operationalisation and any cut-off are choices that studies make differently, and it is not the only defensible one. Four things have to be kept apart in any chronic-stress claim - the exposure the environment supplies, the stress the person reports, the physiological mediators that are measured, and the multisystem burden those mediators are taken to index - because they are measured with different instruments on different timescales and they can disagree. A study that measures one of the four supports a conclusion about that one until it supplies evidence linking them. [[Allostasis and chronic stress]] [[P17290796 McEwen 2006 Stress mediators#^p17290796-mediators|Stress mediators]]

A stress claim in any of those domains has to state which axis arm is being measured and how, which is the measurement discipline the methods domain supplies. ## Uncertainties

- Chronic axis activity is difficult to quantify, and each sampling matrix answers a different question. For saliva, the methodological review of field measurement concludes that sampling time must be registered and included in the analysis, that longitudinal collections control season, that food and vigorous exercise be avoided beforehand, and that results differ between laboratory techniques - with several recommended controls explicitly resting on judgement rather than hard evidence. [[P18609093 Hansen 2008 Salivary cortisol sources of variation#^p18609093-recommendations|Salivary cortisol sources of variation]] [[P18609093 Hansen 2008 Salivary cortisol sources of variation#^p18609093-controls|Salivary cortisol sources of variation]] Hair sits at the opposite end of the window: cortisol incorporated into growing hair is treated as a retrospective index of integrated secretion over months, with accumulating evidence on validity, reliability and robustness to a range of confounders, and it measures that integrated window rather than a moment. [[P22366690 Stalder 2012 Analysis of cortisol in hair#^p22366690-window|Analysis of cortisol in hair]] [[P22366690 Stalder 2012 Analysis of cortisol in hair#^p22366690-evidence|Analysis of cortisol in hair]] The matrices change the construct being measured rather than removing measurement problems, so a single value should not be reported as an individual's 'stress level'.
