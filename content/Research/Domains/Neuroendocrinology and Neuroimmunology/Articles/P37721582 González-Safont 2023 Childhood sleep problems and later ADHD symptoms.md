# Sleep problems at ages 8-9 and ADHD symptoms at ages 10-11: evidence in three cohorts from INMA study.

**Authors.** González-Safont L, Rebagliato M, et al. (8 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37721582/)

**Published.** 2023 in European journal of pediatrics.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1007/s00431-023-05145-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00431-023-05145-3) · PMID [37721582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37721582/) · PMCID [PMC10640481](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10640481/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Parent-reported sleep problems at 8-9 years were associated with higher parent-rated ADHD scale scores two years later, with incidence rate ratios of about 1.14 to 1.20 for opposition, inattention, hyperactivity and the ADHD index.
^p37721582-prospective

The associations changed little after full adjustment and in sensitivity analyses excluding children with earlier symptoms, preterm birth or extreme values; only hyperactivity weakened slightly when children with ADHD at age 9 were excluded.
^p37721582-adjusted

## Scope as reported

Three population cohorts; both sleep (sleep items of the Child Behaviour Checklist) and ADHD symptoms (Conners' parent scale) were parent-reported.
^p37721582-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The same parents rated sleep and symptoms, so shared rater bias and unmeasured family factors remain possible; two-year precedence supports a risk marker more firmly than a cause.
^p37721582-caution-rater
