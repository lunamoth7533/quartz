# Subjective and objective sleep alterations in medication-naïve children and adolescents with autism spectrum disorder: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Kim H, Kim JH, et al. (14 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37469173/)

**Published.** 2023 in Epidemiology and psychiatric sciences.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1017/S2045796023000574](https://doi.org/10.1017/S2045796023000574) · PMID [37469173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37469173/) · PMCID [PMC10387490](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10387490/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

On actigraphy and polysomnography, medication-naive autistic young people had longer sleep latency, lower sleep efficiency and shorter time in bed and total sleep time than typically developing peers, with moderate effect sizes.
^p37469173-objective

On questionnaires they had more daytime sleepiness, longer latency, more difficulty initiating and maintaining sleep and more night sweating; age, sex and intellectual disability moderated some differences, and publication bias was possible for several polysomnography measures.
^p37469173-subjective

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis of observational studies searched to March 2021; restricting to medication-naive participants removes one confounder but narrows the samples.
^p37469173-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Pooled group differences with possible publication bias describe how autistic samples differ on average; they neither estimate how many autistic children have insomnia nor explain why.
^p37469173-caution-bias
