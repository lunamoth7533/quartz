# Risk of Postpartum Relapse in Bipolar Disorder and Postpartum Psychosis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

**Authors.** Wesseloo R, Kamperman AM, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26514657/)

**Published.** 2016 in The American journal of psychiatry (first online 2015-10-30).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1176/appi.ajp.2015.15010124](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2015.15010124) · PMID [26514657](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26514657/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across 37 studies (5,700 deliveries in 4,023 women at high risk), about 35% of deliveries were followed by a postpartum relapse (95% CI 29-41%).
^p26514657-overall

Severe postpartum episodes were less frequent in women with bipolar disorder (17%) than in women with a history of postpartum psychosis (29%).
^p26514657-severe

Among women with bipolar disorder, relapse occurred in 66% of those who were medication-free in pregnancy and 23% of those taking prophylactic medication.
^p26514657-medication

## Scope as reported

A PRISMA-guided meta-analysis of studies of women with bipolar disorder and/or prior postpartum psychosis or mania diagnosed by DSM, ICD or Research Diagnostic Criteria; relapse could not be estimated for women with both bipolar disorder and previous postpartum episodes.
^p26514657-limit
