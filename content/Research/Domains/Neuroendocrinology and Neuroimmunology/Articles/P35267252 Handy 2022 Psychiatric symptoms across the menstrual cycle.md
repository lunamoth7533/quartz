# Psychiatric Symptoms Across the Menstrual Cycle in Adult Women: A Comprehensive Review.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across disorders, the premenstrual and menstrual phases were the most consistent times of worsening, with strong evidence that psychotic, manic and depressive symptoms, suicide attempts and drinking all increase then.
^p35267252-phases

Anxiety, stress and binge eating tended to be raised across the whole luteal phase, and people felt the effects of smoking and cocaine less in that phase.
^p35267252-luteal

Patterns were less consistent for panic disorder, PTSD symptoms and borderline personality disorder, and data were too sparse to judge several other conditions.
^p35267252-inconsistent

Proposed explanations include behavioural, psychological and neuroendocrine influences.
^p35267252-mechanisms

## Scope as reported

A comprehensive narrative synthesis across single-disorder literatures on symptom change over the menstrual cycle in adult women, not a meta-analysis; the authors call for standard ways of identifying cycle phase.
^p35267252-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Cycle phase is often estimated from reported dates rather than measured hormones, so "premenstrual worsening" ties symptoms to timing without identifying which hormonal change, if any, is responsible.
^p35267252-caution-phase
