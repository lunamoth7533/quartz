# The role of circadian rhythms and sleep in the aetiology of autism spectrum disorder and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: New evidence from bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization analysis.

**Authors.** Dai X, Williams GJ, et al. (8 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38869021/)

**Published.** 2025 in Autism : the international journal of research and practice (first online 2024-06-13).
**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613241258546](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613241258546) · PMID [38869021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38869021/) · PMCID [PMC11656626](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11656626/)

**Verification.** Full text read via PubMed Central (PMC11656626) on 2026-09-27, with metadata matched by PMID; figures and supplementary material were not examined. Read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked full text)

Genetically predicted later timing of the most active ten hours of the day, an indicator of late chronotype, was associated with higher odds of both autism and ADHD after correction for multiple testing.
^p38869021-chronotype

Higher genetically predicted sleep efficiency was associated with lower odds of autism, and genetic liability to ADHD with more nocturnal sleep episodes, but both associations were nominal and did not survive correction; no instrument indicated that sleep disruption causes ADHD.
^p38869021-sleepquality

## Scope as reported

European-ancestry samples only, no sex-stratified data, lifetime-average genetic effects and few outcome cases; the authors note that the most-active-hours measure may not capture circadian misalignment itself.
^p38869021-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Mendelian randomization assumes instruments act only through the exposure; with few cases and nominal secondary findings, the direction claims are hypotheses to replicate rather than settled causal facts.
^p38869021-caution-assumptions
