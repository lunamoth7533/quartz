## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review reports that changes in sleep phase, structure and duration occur in every mood state of bipolar disorder, and links them with poorer quality of life, higher suicide risk, impaired cognition and more frequent relapse.
^p39210713-allstates

It traces assessment from questionnaires toward ecological momentary assessment and digital phenotyping, and lists open questions including clock-gene mechanisms, environmental effects on rhythms and the two-way relationship between sleep and mood regulation.
^p39210713-methods

## Scope as reported

Narrative review without a systematic search or pooled estimates; it flags standardisation and privacy issues for digital monitoring.
^p39210713-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Disturbance present in all mood states fits a trait vulnerability, an episode feature and a consequence of illness at once, and the review itself leaves the direction of the sleep-mood relationship open.
