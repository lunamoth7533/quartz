## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review describes the mammalian clock as a transcription-translation feedback loop in which a CLOCK-BMAL1 activator complex is opposed by a repressive complex of PER, CRY and casein kinase 1 delta/epsilon, with output pathways that make many processes rhythmic.
^p37708890-loop

Metabolism is described as a main target of clock output that also feeds back to modulate the clock, which makes clock components candidate drug targets, although the authors note that drugs acting on the clock remain rare.
^p37708890-metabolism

## Scope as reported

Narrative review centred on molecular mechanism and chemical biology, largely from cell and animal work.

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Showing that a clock component can be targeted chemically in cells or animals does not show that altering it would change a human sleep or mood outcome.
