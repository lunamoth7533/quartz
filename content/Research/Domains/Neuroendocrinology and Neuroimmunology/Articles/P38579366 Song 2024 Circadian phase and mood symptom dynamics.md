# Causal dynamics of sleep, circadian rhythm, and mood symptoms in patients with major depression and bipolar disorder: insights from longitudinal wearable device data.

**Authors.** Song YM, Jeong J, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38579366/)

**Published.** 2024 in EBioMedicine.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.ebiom.2024.105094](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2024.105094) · PMID [38579366](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38579366/) · PMCID [PMC11002811](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11002811/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Wearable sleep records were used to estimate each patient's daily circadian phase with a mathematical model, and these series were paired with smartphone mood ratings over more than 40,000 days and analysed with transfer entropy, a non-linear test of directional dependence.
^p38579366-design

Circadian phase disturbance appeared to precede mood symptoms in most patients with long records who had major depression or bipolar I disorder, but not in bipolar II, and disturbance of sleep phase itself showed no directional link to mood.
^p38579366-direction

## Scope as reported

Single-country prospective observational study of young adults; circadian phase was modelled from sleep data rather than measured with a physiological marker, and the directional results rest mainly on patients with records longer than 600 days.
^p38579366-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Temporal precedence in observational series is compatible with causation but does not prove it; an unmeasured process driving both rhythm and mood could produce the same pattern.
^p38579366-caution-precedence
