# Exploring the interplay between posttraumatic stress disorder, gut microbiota, and inflammatory biomarkers: a comprehensive meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Petakh P, Oksenych V, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38410510/)

**Published.** 2024 in Frontiers in immunology.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.3389/fimmu.2024.1349883](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1349883) · PMID [38410510](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38410510/) · PMCID [PMC10895958](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10895958/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across nine studies, none of the pooled inflammatory biomarkers differed between people with PTSD and comparison groups.
^p38410510-inflammation

Of six microbiota studies, two reported lower alpha diversity in PTSD, but the pooled Shannon index difference was not significant, and two studies reported lower abundance of Lachnospiraceae.
^p38410510-microbiota

## Scope as reported

Small evidence base searched to December 2023; the authors call for work on causal relationships and other influences on immune function.
^p38410510-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

With six microbiota studies and no pooled differences, the evidence cannot support a PTSD-specific microbial signature, and at this size the absence of a pooled difference is weak evidence of absence.
^p38410510-caution-sparse
