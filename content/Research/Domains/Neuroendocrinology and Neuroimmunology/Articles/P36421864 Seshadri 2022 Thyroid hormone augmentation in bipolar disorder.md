# Thyroid Hormone Augmentation for Bipolar Disorder: A Systematic Review.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review notes a hypothesis that too little thyroid hormone in the brain drives rapid cycling, while the actual mechanism in bipolar disorder is unclear.
^p36421864-rationale

Open-label studies of levothyroxine at above-replacement levels reported improved depression in bipolar depression and fewer recurrences in rapid cycling, but a randomised trial of that approach showed no advantage over placebo; a trial comparing levothyroxine, T3 and placebo found benefit only in women with rapid cycling.
^p36421864-trials

Study differences precluded meta-analysis; the authors describe mixed, low-quality evidence in which open-label promise was not consistently replicated, with no significant thyrotoxicosis reported.
^p36421864-quality

## Scope as reported

A PRISMA-guided systematic review of eight studies of mixed design, found through Ovid MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO and Cochrane searches; no pooled estimate was possible.
^p36421864-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Eight heterogeneous studies with one subgroup-positive randomised trial cannot establish benefit, and improvement in open-label studies is the pattern that expectation and regression to the mean would also produce. This record describes research status, not a treatment recommendation.
^p36421864-caution-evidence
