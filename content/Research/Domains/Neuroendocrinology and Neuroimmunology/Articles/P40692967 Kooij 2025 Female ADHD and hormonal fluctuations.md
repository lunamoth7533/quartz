## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review concludes that hormonal transitions - puberty, the menstrual cycle, pregnancy and (peri)menopause - make ADHD symptoms and mood problems worse, while drug studies and tailored treatments for women are lacking.
^p40692967-transitions

It discusses how oestrogen and progesterone interact with dopamine pathways and suggests that phases of lower oestrogen may affect cognition.
^p40692967-oestrogen

Women with undiagnosed ADHD are described as more vulnerable to premenstrual dysphoric disorder and postpartum depression, and to cardiovascular disease in perimenopause; late diagnosis is linked to more comorbidity and poorer functioning.
^p40692967-risks

## Scope as reported

An expert narrative review by the Eunethydis Special Interest Group on Female ADHD, synthesising published studies and women's self-reported experiences; it calls for longitudinal, sex-specific studies that record hormonal status.
^p40692967-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Much of the evidence behind the conclusion is cross-sectional or self-reported, so "hormonal transitions worsen ADHD" summarises associations and accounts; within-person studies with measured hormones are needed before a hormonal mechanism is assumed.
^p40692967-caution-evidence
