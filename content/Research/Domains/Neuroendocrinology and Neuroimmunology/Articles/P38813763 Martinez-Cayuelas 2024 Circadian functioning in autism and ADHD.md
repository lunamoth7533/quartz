# Sleep problems and circadian rhythm functioning in autistic children, autism with co-occurring attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, and typically developing children: A comparative study.

**Authors.** Martinez-Cayuelas E, Moreno-Vinués B, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38813763/)

**Published.** 2024 in Autism : the international journal of research and practice.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613241254594](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613241254594) · PMID [38813763](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38813763/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Wrist-worn ambulatory circadian monitoring recorded more motor activity during sleep in autistic children, and most in those with co-occurring ADHD.
^p38813763-motor

Wrist temperature followed an atypical pattern, highest in neurotypical children, lower in autistic children and lowest with co-occurring ADHD; because distal temperature is closely tied to sleep-wake state and melatonin, the authors read this as supporting atypical melatonin secretion in autism.
^p38813763-temperature

## Scope as reported

Single-country comparative study with a small neurotypical group; melatonin itself is not among the measures summarised in the abstract.
^p38813763-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Skin temperature is a proxy for circadian and melatonin physiology, so the finding supports a hypothesis about melatonin secretion rather than measuring it.
^p38813763-caution-proxy
