# Objectively measured sleep continuity in children and adolescents with ADHD: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Liang X, Qiu H, et al. (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37657199/)

**Published.** 2023 in Psychiatry research.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115447](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115447) · PMID [37657199](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37657199/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Actigraphy showed longer sleep latency and moderately lower sleep efficiency in young people with ADHD than in typically developing peers, whereas polysomnography showed no significant differences.
^p37657199-actigraphy

Medication status and comorbid psychiatric conditions moderated the sleep-efficiency difference and the number of actigraphy nights moderated the latency difference; the authors contrast this with consistently higher reported sleep problems in earlier work.
^p37657199-moderators

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis of observational studies and intervention baselines searched to September 2022.
^p37657199-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A difference that appears with actigraphy but not polysomnography, and that shifts with medication, comorbidity and recording length, shows how much the finding depends on method and sample.
