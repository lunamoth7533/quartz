# The role of combined modifiable lifestyle behaviors in the longitudinal association between stressful life events and allostatic load in Australian adults.

**Authors.** Siew RVK, Bowe SJ, et al. (8 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36610209/)

**Published.** 2023 in Psychoneuroendocrinology.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.psyneuen.2022.106021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psyneuen.2022.106021) · PMID [36610209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36610209/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Latent profile analysis of five lifestyle behaviours identified least, moderately and most healthy clusters, and exposure to stressful life events did not predict which cluster participants belonged to.
^p36610209-profiles

Only the most healthy cluster was associated with lower allostatic load, and neither cluster carried a significant indirect effect, so lifestyle combinations did not explain the 12-year association between stressful life events and three allostatic load measures.
^p36610209-mediation

## Scope as reported

One national cohort; allostatic load was operationalised with three composite measures, and the authors call for replication in other longitudinal samples.
^p36610209-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A null behavioural mediation leaves a physiological route as the remaining explanation only by default; it does not demonstrate that route, and the load composites are themselves study-specific choices.
