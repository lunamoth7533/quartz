# From inflammation to sickness and depression: when the immune system subjugates the brain

**Authors.** Dantzer R, O'Connor JC, Freund GG, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18073775/)

**Published.** 2008.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/nrn2297](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2297) · PMID [18073775](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18073775/) · PMCID [PMC2919277](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2919277/)

## Reported findings (checked full text)

Peripheral immune signals reach the brain and produce sickness behaviour, a coordinated set of changes in activity, appetite, sleep and mood.
^p18073775-sickness

The review proposes that prolonged inflammatory signalling can contribute to depressive symptoms in vulnerable individuals, as a pathophysiological hypothesis.
^p18073775-depression

Proposed routes include cytokine signalling, altered tryptophan metabolism and vagal transmission, which the authors present as interacting rather than exclusive.
^p18073775-mechanism

## Scope as reported

A mechanistic review proposing a pathway from inflammation to mood. The causal claim is a model the review advances, not an established mechanism for depression generally.
^p18073775-limit
