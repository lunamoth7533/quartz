## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across 25 population-based studies, hypothyroidism was associated with clinical depression (OR 1.30, 95% CI 1.08-1.57); for thyroid autoimmunity alone the result was inconclusive (OR 1.24, 95% CI 0.89-1.74).
^p34524390-association

The association was stronger for overt hypothyroidism (OR 1.77) than for subclinical hypothyroidism (OR 1.13), and sensitivity analyses gave more conservative estimates.
^p34524390-overt

In a post hoc analysis the association held in women (OR 1.48) but not in men (OR 0.71).
^p34524390-sex

The authors judge the link considerably weaker than had been assumed.
^p34524390-size

## Scope as reported

Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of epidemiological, population-based studies with laboratory or ICD-coded hypothyroidism and depression defined by operationalised criteria or rating-scale cut-offs; study quality was rated with the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale.
^p34524390-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

An observational association cannot show direction: fatigue and low mood overlap with hypothyroid symptoms, and people with depression may be tested more often. A modest pooled odds ratio describes populations, not the cause of any one person's low mood.
^p34524390-caution-direction
