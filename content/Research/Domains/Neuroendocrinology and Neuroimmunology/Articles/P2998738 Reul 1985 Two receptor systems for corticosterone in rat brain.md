# Two receptor systems for corticosterone in rat brain: microdistribution and differential occupation

**Authors.** Reul JM, de Kloet ER. · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2998738/)

**Published.** 1985.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1210/endo-117-6-2505](https://doi.org/10.1210/endo-117-6-2505) · PMID [2998738](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2998738/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Two corticosteroid receptor systems are distinguished in rat brain: a mineralocorticoid-like receptor (also called the corticosterone receptor) and the glucocorticoid receptor.
^p2998738-systems

The mineralocorticoid-like system concentrates in lateral septum and hippocampus - highest in subiculum plus CA1 and in the dentate gyrus - with very high corticosterone affinity (Kd about 0.5 nM); the glucocorticoid receptor is more widespread and binds with six- to ten-fold lower affinity (Kd about 2.5-5 nM).
^p2998738-affinity

The high-affinity population was about 89.5% occupied at the morning trough and stayed highly occupied at the diurnal peak and after restraint stress, so the two populations are engaged at different circulating concentrations.
^p2998738-occupation

## Scope as reported

Receptor binding and occupation measured in rat brain, with microdistribution mapped by autoradiography. The affinity and distribution figures are rodent values and the paper does not report human tissue or clinical outcomes.
