# Positive Controls in Adults and Children Support That Very Few, If Any, New Neurons Are Born in the Adult Human Hippocampus.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Dividing progenitors beside immature doublecortin-positive cells are easy to find in the infant hippocampus, become scarcer through childhood, and are few or absent in adults.
^p33762407-decline

The authors argue that most doublecortin-stained cells in the adult dentate gyrus look mature, that doublecortin also marks adult neurons in the amygdala and nearby cortex, that detecting it needs no special antigen-retrieval step, and that adults show no germinal zone of dividing precursors.
^p33762407-markers

They read available RNA-sequencing data as consistent with rare or absent adult neurogenesis and call for direct imaging of dividing precursors or single-cell dating of neurons.
^p33762407-rnaseq

## Scope as reported

Dual Perspectives review presenting one side of a published debate, with new post-mortem data and reanalysed transcriptomic datasets; no systematic search is reported.
^p33762407-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Failing to detect a cell type in post-mortem tissue is weaker evidence than detecting it, and doubts about marker specificity cut against both sides. The article narrows what current markers can show; it does not prove that adult human neurogenesis is absent.
^p33762407-caution-absence
