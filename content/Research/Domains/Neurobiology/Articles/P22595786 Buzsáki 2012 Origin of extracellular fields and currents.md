## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Active neurons pass currents across their membranes that can be picked up in the fluid around them, and synaptic currents make the largest contribution to this extracellular signal.
^p22595786-synaptic

Sodium and calcium spikes, ion flow through voltage-gated and ligand-gated channels, and intrinsic oscillations of the membrane can also shape the field considerably.
^p22595786-sources

Recordings from dense electrode arrays in animals and from electrode grids on the human cortex, analysed with newer tools and models, can estimate how neurons act together, the average synaptic input they receive and the spikes they emit.
^p22595786-inference

## Scope as reported

Narrative review of the biophysics behind extracellular recordings at several scales (spikes, local field potentials, electrocorticography and EEG), drawing on animal work, invasive human recordings and computational modelling.
^p22595786-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

If synaptic currents dominate the extracellular signal, a scalp rhythm mainly reflects the summed, time-aligned synaptic activity of large neuronal populations; it is not a direct readout of spiking in any one region, and the same rhythm can arise from different circuit arrangements.
