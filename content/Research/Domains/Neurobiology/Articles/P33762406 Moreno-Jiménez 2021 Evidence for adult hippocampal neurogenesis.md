## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Rodents make new hippocampal neurons throughout life; in humans direct evidence is described as hard to obtain, though earlier studies that birth-dated new cells and validated markers of young neurons are cited as demonstrating it.
^p33762406-background

The authors read their own data as showing that adult hippocampal neurogenesis continues into the tenth decade of life and is markedly reduced in Alzheimer's disease.
^p33762406-persistence

They report that the way post-mortem tissue is processed and analysed can suppress markers of new neurons until they become undetectable, and they list methodological controls that human studies should apply.
^p33762406-methods

## Scope as reported

Dual Perspectives review presenting one side of a published debate and drawing heavily on the authors' own post-mortem work; no systematic search is reported.
^p33762406-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

This is an advocate's summary published alongside an opposing article. The tissue-processing argument explains why negative studies might miss new neurons, but it does not by itself establish how many new neurons adults make or what they contribute.
^p33762406-caution-debate
