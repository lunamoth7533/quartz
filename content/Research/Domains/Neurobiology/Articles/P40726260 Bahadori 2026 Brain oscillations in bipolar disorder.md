# Brain Oscillations in Bipolar Disorder: Insights from Quantitative EEG Studies.

**Authors.** Bahadori AR, Naghavi E, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40726260/)

**Published.** 2026 in Clinical EEG and neuroscience (first online 2025-07-29).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/15500594251360059](https://doi.org/10.1177/15500594251360059) · PMID [40726260](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40726260/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across 20 studies with 475 patients, raised beta and gamma activity was the most consistent quantitative EEG finding in bipolar disorder.
^p40726260-bands

Alpha and theta changes ran in different directions, varying with region and with mood state, and delta changes were more marked in bipolar I disorder.
^p40726260-variable

Power in the gamma, theta and alpha bands fell with treatment. The authors report that quantitative EEG separated bipolar disorder from major depression and schizophrenia, and they call for standardised methods and validation.
^p40726260-treatment

## Scope as reported

Systematic review following PRISMA 2020, with four databases searched to 30 October 2024; the abstract reports no pooled estimate and notes methodological variability between studies.
^p40726260-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Twenty mostly small studies with differing methods, medication and mood states cannot establish a diagnostic EEG signature. The reported separation from other diagnoses would need prospective testing in independent samples before it could mean anything for an individual.
^p40726260-caution-diagnostic
