# Increased number of excitatory synapsis and decreased number of inhibitory synapsis in the prefrontal cortex in autism.

**Authors.** Vakilzadeh G, Maseko BC, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38696601/)

**Published.** 2024 in Cerebral cortex (New York, N.Y. : 1991).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1093/cercor/bhad268](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhad268) · PMID [38696601](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38696601/) · PMCID [PMC11065106](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11065106/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Compared with control cases, autistic brains had more excitatory synapses in the upper cortical layers and fewer inhibitory synapses in every cortical layer of the prefrontal cortex.
^p38696601-counts

Synapses were counted as co-localised pairs of presynaptic and postsynaptic markers: VGlut1 with PSD-95 for excitatory contacts, and the vesicular GABA transporter with gephyrin for inhibitory contacts.
^p38696601-markers

The authors place the result after earlier reports of more excitatory pyramidal cells and fewer parvalbumin-positive chandelier interneurons in the same region, and suggest the shift could disturb prefrontal network function.
^p38696601-context

## Scope as reported

Postmortem histology of prefrontal cortex in 10 autistic and 10 control cases, with co-localised marker puncta quantified by automated image analysis. One cortical region was examined.
^p38696601-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Ten cases per group from one region, in postmortem tissue, is a small base for a general claim. A count of co-localised markers is a structural proxy: it does not measure synaptic strength, plasticity or excitation-inhibition balance as it operated in life.
^p38696601-caution-proxy
