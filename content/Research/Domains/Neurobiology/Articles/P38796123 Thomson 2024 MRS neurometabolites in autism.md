# Neurometabolite differences in Autism as assessed with Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Pooling spectroscopy studies, the review found lower GABA and lower N-acetyl aspartate in autistic groups than in comparison groups; the authors read the GABA result as a sign of disturbed excitation-inhibition balance and the NAA result as a sign of altered neural integrity.
^p38796123-gaba

Both differences were largest in children and in limbic brain regions.
^p38796123-age

Study outcomes varied with demographic and methodological factors, and the authors call for standardised consensus designs and transparent reporting.
^p38796123-methods

## Scope as reported

Systematic review and meta-analysis of in vivo proton spectroscopy studies in autism covering glutamate and GABA plus glutathione (oxidative stress), glutamine and creatine (energy metabolism), and NAA, choline and myo-inositol (markers of neuronal and glial integrity). Data were grouped by metabolite and brain region before standardised effect sizes were calculated. The abstract does not report the number of included studies.
^p38796123-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A spectroscopy value is a concentration averaged over a voxel of tissue, and it cannot by itself separate transmitter used for signalling from transmitter in metabolic pools. Lower GABA at group level is compatible with reduced inhibition but is a regional measure, not a measurement of synaptic inhibition or of any individual's brain.
^p38796123-caution-measure
