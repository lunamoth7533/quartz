# A decade of EEG Theta/Beta Ratio Research in ADHD: a meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Arns M, Conners CK, et al. (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23086616/)

**Published.** 2013 in Journal of attention disorders (first online 2012-10-19).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/1087054712460087](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054712460087) · PMID [23086616](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23086616/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The pooled effect size for a raised theta/beta ratio in ADHD was 0.75 at ages 6-13 and 0.62 at ages 6-18, but heterogeneity stayed significant, so the authors treat both figures as overestimates.
^p23086616-effect

The ADHD-control difference shrank across the decade of studies because the ratio rose in the comparison groups.
^p23086616-drift

The authors conclude that a raised ratio is not a reliable way to diagnose ADHD, while a sizeable subgroup does deviate on it, for whom they suggest a prognostic rather than diagnostic use.
^p23086616-diagnosis

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis of nine studies of eyes-open recordings at one central electrode (Cz) in 6-18-year-olds; the finding about the shrinking difference came from a post hoc analysis.
^p23086616-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A marker whose group difference drifts over time and varies between studies cannot serve as a diagnostic test. The subgroup's prognostic value is proposed in the conclusion rather than established by the pooled analysis, and the evidence covers children and adolescents only.
^p23086616-caution-marker
