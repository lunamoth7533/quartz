# Molecular cascades and cell type-specific signatures in ASD revealed by single-cell genomics.

**Authors.** Wamsley B, Bicks L, et al. (12 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38781372/)

**Published.** 2024 in Science (New York, N.Y.).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1126/science.adh2602](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2602) · PMID [38781372](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38781372/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Single-nucleus RNA sequencing of autism cortex, backed by chromatin accessibility and spatial transcriptomic data, showed transcriptomic changes that were mostly specific to particular cell types and spread across several of them.
^p38781372-celltype

The most prominent changes involved callosal-projecting neurons, interneurons of the superficial layers, and distinct reactive states of oligodendrocytes, microglia and astrocytes.
^p38781372-glia

Gene regulatory network drivers altered in autism, and their targets, were enriched for both rare and common genetic risk variants.
^p38781372-risk

## Scope as reported

Postmortem study combining single-nucleus RNA sequencing, single-nucleus chromatin accessibility sequencing and spatial transcriptomics. Sample sizes are not given in the abstract.
^p38781372-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A postmortem transcriptomic state is a snapshot at death. It cannot show whether reactive glial states drive autistic development, follow from it, or reflect co-occurring conditions, medication or the circumstances of death, and it does not describe any living person's brain.
^p38781372-caution-snapshot
