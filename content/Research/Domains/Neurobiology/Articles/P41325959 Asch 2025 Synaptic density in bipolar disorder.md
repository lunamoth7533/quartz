# Neural Signatures of Bipolar Disorder and Psychotropic Medication Effects: A Multimodal Positron Emission Tomography-Magnetic Resonance Imaging Study.

**Authors.** Asch RH, Fan S, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41325959/)

**Published.** 2025 in Biological psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.biopsych.2025.11.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2025.11.018) · PMID [41325959](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41325959/) · PMCID [PMC12981349](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12981349/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

PET imaging of synaptic vesicle glycoprotein 2A with [11C]UCB-J, used as a marker of synaptic density, showed lower frontolimbic synaptic density in the bipolar group than in controls.
^p41325959-sv2a

Exploratory analyses suggested the lower density was driven by participants taking psychotropic medication; grey matter volume differed only once medication status was considered, being higher in medicated than unmedicated participants.
^p41325959-medication

Synaptic density and grey matter volume were positively related in controls but not in the bipolar group, where a lower ratio of the two went with greater impulsivity.
^p41325959-coupling

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional study of 19 people with bipolar disorder (13 with type I, 6 with type II) and 26 controls combining synaptic density PET with structural and functional MRI of frontolimbic regions. Medication analyses were exploratory.
^p41325959-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

With 19 patients split further by medication status, and medication not assigned at random, the study cannot separate illness effects from treatment effects or from the reasons some participants were treated. It is a first in vivo signal to replicate, not evidence that any medication reduces synapses.
^p41325959-caution-confound
