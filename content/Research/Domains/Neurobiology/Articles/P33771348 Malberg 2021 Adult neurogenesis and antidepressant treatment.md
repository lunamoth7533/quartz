# Adult Neurogenesis and Antidepressant Treatment: The Surprise Finding by Ron Duman and the Field 20 Years Later.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review recounts the finding that prolonged antidepressant treatment increases the birth of new neurons in the adult hippocampus, in the subgranular zone, which gave rise to a neurogenic account of antidepressant action tied to neurotrophic and synaptic-plasticity ideas about depression.
^p33771348-increase

Antidepressant treatment is described as counteracting, and sometimes reversing, what stress does to the brain and to newly formed hippocampal cells, perhaps through neurotrophic factors.
^p33771348-stress

Preclinical ablation studies are cited as showing that neurogenesis may be needed for some antidepressant effects, and recently approved antidepressants are reported to act on neurogenesis and plasticity as well.
^p33771348-ablation

## Scope as reported

Commemorative narrative review of one research programme, covering animal models, human studies and implications for drug discovery; no systematic search or pooled estimate is reported.
^p33771348-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The evidence that neurogenesis is necessary comes from animal ablation experiments, and the human side depends on post-mortem counts whose ability to detect new neurons is itself disputed. The review supports a hypothesis about mechanism, not a demonstrated route by which antidepressants help people.
^p33771348-caution-translation
