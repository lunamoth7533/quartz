# TSPO PET brain inflammation imaging: A transdiagnostic systematic review and meta-analysis of 156 case-control studies.

**Authors.** De Picker LJ, Morrens M, et al. (11 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37543251/)

**Published.** 2023 in Brain, behavior, and immunity.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.bbi.2023.07.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2023.07.023) · PMID [37543251](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37543251/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Pooled across illness categories, cortical grey matter showed a higher TSPO signal in patients than in controls, but within categories the cortical increase was significant only for Alzheimer's disease and other neurodegenerative disorders.
^p37543251-cortex

Cortico-limbic increases were most prominent in neurodegenerative disorders, mild cognitive impairment, mood disorders and multiple sclerosis, while main results for schizophrenia, substance use disorders, viral infections and several other categories were not significant.
^p37543251-mood

The quantification method explained about a quarter of between-study variance, with reference-tissue approaches giving larger case-control differences than distribution-volume approaches; patient age and radioligand generation also contributed.
^p37543251-method

## Scope as reported

Systematic review of all English-language, peer-reviewed case-control TSPO PET studies of the central nervous system in living humans - 156 studies with 2,626 patients and 2,381 controls, 139 of them meta-analysable in 11 illness categories - with random-effects meta-analysis of standardised mean differences in cortical grey matter, thalamus and cortico-limbic circuitry, and subgroup and meta-regression analyses of heterogeneity.
^p37543251-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

TSPO binding is an indirect index of an inflammatory or glial response: a case-control difference does not say which cell type changed, whether the change is a cause or a consequence of illness, or anything about one person. The size of the method effect also means that studies using different quantification approaches cannot be compared at face value.
^p37543251-caution-signal
