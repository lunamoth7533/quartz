# Neural oscillations

## Definition

Neural oscillations are rhythmic fluctuations in the electrical activity of neuronal populations, recorded as extracellular fields in which synaptic currents are the largest contributor. [[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-synaptic|Buzsáki 2012]] Scalp EEG measures these rhythms by their frequency and amplitude with millisecond timing but little spatial detail, and studies report power in conventional bands named, from slow to fast, delta, theta, alpha, beta and gamma. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-eeg|Brain imaging]] [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-bands|Bahadori 2026]] [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-variable|Bahadori 2026]]

## How it works

**Where the signal comes from.** Active neurons pass currents across their membranes that can be recorded in the surrounding fluid, and synaptic currents dominate that signal. [[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-synaptic|Buzsáki 2012]] Sodium and calcium spikes, ion flow through voltage-gated and ligand-gated channels, and intrinsic membrane oscillations of single neurons also shape the field. [[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-sources|Buzsáki 2012]] A rhythm large enough to reach the scalp therefore reflects the summed, time-aligned synaptic activity of large populations; it is not a direct readout of spiking in any one region, and different circuit arrangements can produce the same rhythm. [[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-caution-scalp|Appraisal: Buzsáki 2012]]

**What different recordings add.** Dense electrode arrays in animals and electrode grids on the human cortex, analysed with modern tools and models, can estimate how neurons act together, the average synaptic input they receive and the spikes they emit; these invasive recordings are the bridge between cellular mechanism and the scalp signal. [[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-inference|Buzsáki 2012]] [[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-limit|Buzsáki 2012]] Scalp EEG gives up that spatial detail in exchange for timing, so its disagreements with imaging are not automatically errors: the methods measure different quantities. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-caution-modality|Appraisal: Brain imaging]]

**Sleep is staged by its rhythms.** Sleep is described in cycles of non-REM and REM stages distinguished by their brain-wave patterns, with slow-wave sleep concentrated early in the night and longer REM periods later; in REM sleep brain activity resembles waking while voluntary muscle activity is suppressed. [[F98 OpenStax Psychology 2e stages of sleep#^f98-stages|Stages of Sleep]] [[F98 OpenStax Psychology 2e stages of sleep#^f98-rem|Stages of Sleep]] [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Understanding Sleep]] Most condition findings sourced here concern the timing and continuity of sleep rather than the oscillations themselves. In young people with ADHD, actigraphy showed longer sleep latency and lower efficiency, but EEG-based polysomnography found no significant differences. [[P37657199 Liang 2023 Objective sleep continuity in ADHD#^p37657199-actigraphy|Liang 2023]] In bipolar disorder, changes in sleep phase, structure and duration occur in every mood state. [[P39210713 Tonon 2024 Sleep and circadian disruption in bipolar disorders#^p39210713-allstates|Tonon 2024]]

**ADHD and the theta/beta ratio.** In nine studies of 6-18-year-olds, pooled effect sizes for a raised theta/beta ratio (slow theta power divided by faster beta power) in ADHD were 0.75 at ages 6-13 and 0.62 at ages 6-18, but heterogeneity was significant and the authors treat both as overestimates. [[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-limit|Arns 2013]] [[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-effect|Arns 2013]] The ADHD-control difference shrank across a decade of studies because the ratio rose in comparison groups, and the authors conclude that a raised ratio cannot reliably diagnose ADHD, while a subgroup deviates on it and may carry prognostic information. [[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-drift|Arns 2013]] [[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-diagnosis|Arns 2013]] Training EEG rhythms as a treatment has not held up on blinded measures either: when symptoms were rated by people probably unaware of allocation, neurofeedback did not significantly reduce total ADHD symptoms across 20 trials, and the authors found no meaningful group-level benefit. [[P39661381 Westwood 2025 Neurofeedback with blinded outcomes#^p39661381-blinded|Westwood 2025]] [[P39661381 Westwood 2025 Neurofeedback with blinded outcomes#^p39661381-conclusion|Westwood 2025]]

**Bipolar disorder.** Across 20 quantitative EEG studies with 475 patients, raised beta and gamma activity was the most consistent finding; alpha and theta changes ran in different directions with region and mood state, and delta changes were more marked in bipolar I disorder. [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-bands|Bahadori 2026]] [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-variable|Bahadori 2026]] Power in several bands fell with treatment, and the authors report that quantitative EEG separated bipolar disorder from depression and schizophrenia. [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-treatment|Bahadori 2026]] Mostly small studies with varying methods, medication and mood states cannot establish a diagnostic signature, and that separation needs prospective testing in independent samples. [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-caution-diagnostic|Appraisal: Bahadori 2026]]

**Reading rule.** Before accepting an oscillation claim, name the band and how it was defined, the electrodes, whether eyes were open or closed, the state (awake, sleep stage or mood state) and medication, and whether the result is band power, a ratio or a sleep-stage measure; a group difference in band power is neither a diagnostic marker nor evidence about which circuit produced it. [[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-caution-marker|Appraisal: Arns 2013]] [[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-caution-scalp|Appraisal: Buzsáki 2012]]

## Evidence and status

That extracellular fields and EEG mainly reflect synaptic currents rests on animal recordings, invasive human recordings and biophysical modelling, and is the best-established layer here. [[P22595786 Buzsáki 2012 Origin of extracellular fields and currents#^p22595786-limit|Buzsáki 2012]] Condition findings are weaker. The ADHD theta/beta difference is heterogeneous, has shrunk over time and does not diagnose; neurofeedback shows no benefit on blinded outcomes. [[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-caution-marker|Appraisal: Arns 2013]] [[P39661381 Westwood 2025 Neurofeedback with blinded outcomes#^p39661381-caution-blinding|Appraisal: Westwood 2025]] Bipolar findings come from a systematic review of small, methodologically varied studies without a pooled estimate. [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-limit|Bahadori 2026]] [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-caution-diagnostic|Appraisal: Bahadori 2026]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether a theta/beta subgroup in ADHD has real prognostic value: it is proposed in a conclusion rather than established by pooled analysis. [[P23086616 Arns 2013 Theta-beta ratio in ADHD meta-analysis#^p23086616-caution-marker|Appraisal: Arns 2013]]
- Whether bipolar band-power differences reflect trait, mood state or treatment, given that alpha and theta findings change direction with state and power falls with treatment. [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-variable|Bahadori 2026]] [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-treatment|Bahadori 2026]]
- Finer sleep oscillations, such as spindles and slow waves, have not yet been sourced in this library for ADHD, bipolar disorder, autism or PTSD.

## Recent research

- **2026 · Systematic review (Clinical EEG and Neuroscience).** Twenty studies point to raised beta and gamma activity in bipolar disorder, with alpha and theta findings that depend on region and mood state; the claimed diagnostic separation still needs independent validation. [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-bands|Bahadori 2026]] [[P40726260 Bahadori 2026 Brain oscillations in bipolar disorder#^p40726260-caution-diagnostic|Appraisal: Bahadori 2026]]
- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (JAMA Psychiatry).** Neurofeedback for ADHD showed no significant benefit on probably blinded symptom ratings, with a small effect only for standard protocols. [[P39661381 Westwood 2025 Neurofeedback with blinded outcomes#^p39661381-blinded|Westwood 2025]] [[P39661381 Westwood 2025 Neurofeedback with blinded outcomes#^p39661381-protocols|Westwood 2025]]
- **2024 · Narrative review (Psychiatry and Clinical Neurosciences).** Sleep phase, structure and duration change in every bipolar mood state and are linked with relapse, cognition and suicide risk. [[P39210713 Tonon 2024 Sleep and circadian disruption in bipolar disorders#^p39210713-allstates|Tonon 2024]]
- **2023 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Psychiatry Research).** In ADHD, actigraphy and EEG-based polysomnography disagreed about sleep continuity, a reminder that the measure decides the finding. [[P37657199 Liang 2023 Objective sleep continuity in ADHD#^p37657199-actigraphy|Liang 2023]]
