# Circuit development and homeostasis

## Definition

Circuit development is the construction and refinement of connected neuronal populations; circuit homeostasis is the set of processes that keep activity within a functional range once the circuit exists. Development and homeostasis use overlapping machinery.

## How it works

**An ordered but overlapping sequence.** Brain circuit development runs through proliferation, migration, differentiation, synapse formation and experience-dependent refinement. The processes overlap across regions rather than completing one after another, which is why structure-function relationships shift with age and why an intervention at one age works differently at another. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-sequence|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]]

**Growth and loss together.** Overproduced synapses and connections are subsequently eliminated, so normal development involves deletion as much as addition. At the neuromuscular junction the outcome of that competition is fewer inputs per muscle fibre but greater complexity and strength in the survivors, which is the clearest illustration that pruning is a constructive process rather than a failure of maintenance. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-pruning|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]

**How inputs are selected.** Elimination runs through activity-dependent competition, which favours the more active inputs, and through direct competition, which favours axons that capture limited trophic support supplied by the target cell. Both mechanisms run at once in most systems, which is why the textbook shorthand 'use it or lose it' is directionally right and mechanistically incomplete. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-competition|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-limit|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]

**Sensitive windows have machinery.** Some refinement windows open only when local inhibitory circuits mature: in primary visual cortex, activity-dependent development is triggered by the functional maturation of local inhibitory connections, and structural consolidation of competing inputs involves proteolytic reorganisation of the extracellular matrix during the critical period. Sensitive periods are therefore windows with identifiable components, not merely convenient ages. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]] [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-consolidation|Critical period plasticity]]

**Different systems, different schedules.** Different tracts and functions mature on different timetables, so there is no single age at which the brain is 'finished': the age at which experience has its largest effect differs across functions, and myelination continues well past the period when synapse numbers peak. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]] [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]

**What 'homeostasis' adds.** The term is used here for the set of negative-feedback adjustments that keep activity within a usable range while the circuit is being refined - opposing the runaway strengthening that Hebbian rules alone would produce. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-lasting|Synaptic Plasticity]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Long-term potentiation and depression]] Mature circuits adjust excitability to keep activity in range, which is why removing input produces compensatory changes rather than silence. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-excitatory|Amino acid transmitters]]

**Experience is an input, not a script.** Early experience interacts with genetically guided development, so variation in outcome is the expected case. That interaction is the reason developmental accounts that assign a single cause to a single outcome rarely survive contact with data. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-limit|The basics of brain development]]

## Evidence and status

Sensitive periods and refinement are established in animal models with direct manipulation; human evidence is largely from imaging trajectories, lesion timing and clinical observation. The critical-period review argues that reactivating sensitive-period machinery could support recovery of function, explicitly as a research strategy rather than an established treatment. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-reactivation|Critical period plasticity]]

Homeostasis is a framework with specific proposed mechanisms rather than a single measured quantity, and its cellular demonstrations come mainly from reduced preparations. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-lasting|Synaptic Plasticity]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Long-term potentiation and depression]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether sensitive-period mechanisms can be safely reopened in humans is a research question with clinical implications, not an established treatment. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-reactivation|Critical period plasticity]]
- Individual differences in developmental timing are large, so group averages can mislead about any one child. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]]
