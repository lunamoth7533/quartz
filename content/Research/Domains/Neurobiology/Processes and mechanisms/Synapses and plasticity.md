# Synapses and plasticity

**Definition.** At a chemical synapse the electrical signal becomes transmitter release and then a signal in the target cell; electrical synapses also exist. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-types|Communication Between Neurons]]

## Supported claims

- Transmitter is released by calcium-dependent vesicle fusion and cleared by degradation or by neuronal or glial reuptake. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-release|Communication Between Neurons]]
- The postsynaptic effect depends on the receptor rather than the transmitter alone. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]]
- Metabotropic signalling can modify ion channels and gene transcription, which the textbook links cautiously to stronger connections and possibly to learning and memory. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]

## How it works

**The release step and the base state.** At a chemical synapse the arriving spike opens voltage-gated calcium channels, and calcium entry into the terminal is the key trigger for vesicle fusion and transmitter release. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-calcium|Mechanisms of Neurotransmitter Release]] Release occurs in quantal units, which is the classical evidence that transmitter is packaged and released in discrete events. [[F115 Neuroscience Online transmitter release#^f115-quanta|Transmitter release]] Transmission continues through receptor binding and postsynaptic current and ends with clearance of transmitter from the cleft. Each step is a place where the strength of the connection can be changed, which is why 'synaptic strength' is a summary of several distinct variables rather than one quantity. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-release|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-receptor|Cells of the Nervous System]]

**Receptor logic.** What a transmitter does is decided by the receptor, not by the molecule. Ionotropic receptors open an ion channel directly and act on a millisecond timescale; metabotropic receptors act through second messengers and can modify channels, excitability or transcription over seconds to hours. [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-receptor|Communication Between Neurons]] [[F04 OpenStax Communication between neurons#^f04-metabotropic|Communication Between Neurons]]

**Three timescales of change.** Release probability varies over hundreds of milliseconds: short-term plasticity works through residual calcium and vesicle availability, so facilitation makes a second spike release more transmitter and depression makes it release less, depending on the synapse's release probability. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-facilitation|Synaptic Plasticity]] Receptor number and composition can change over minutes to hours, and changes in gene expression and structure persist for days or longer: lasting change additionally requires second-messenger cascades and, for durable forms, new protein synthesis - the territory covered in [[Long-term potentiation and depression]]. A single word - plasticity - covers all three timescales, and specifying the timescale is what makes a plasticity claim testable. [[F100 Neuroscience Online synaptic plasticity#^f100-lasting|Synaptic plasticity]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-mechanism|Long-term potentiation and depression]]

**The associative rule.** The defining property of the Hebbian family is coincidence detection: change depends on the relative timing of pre- and postsynaptic activity rather than on activity magnitude alone, so a synapse that is active when its target cell is depolarised is the one that changes. That specificity is what allows a network to store patterns rather than merely to get stronger. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-properties|Long-term potentiation and depression]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-timing|Long-term potentiation and depression]]

**Developmental context.** Synapses are not only modified but created and removed: during development, activity-dependent competition and trophic support decide which inputs survive. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse formation and elimination]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-competition|Synapse formation and elimination]] Adult plasticity and developmental refinement therefore use overlapping machinery to answer different questions.

**Structure follows function, sometimes.** Spine cytoskeletal elements are responsible for the shape changes observed in some forms of plasticity, so in those cases a functional change is accompanied by an anatomical one, and dendritic spine shape changes are one visible correlate of plasticity that links the molecular story to something that can be imaged. The converse - inferring function from anatomy alone - does not follow, which is why spine density is a suggestive rather than decisive measure and the inference from spine morphology to 'learning' remains a correlate-level claim. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-spines|Organization of Cell Types]]

**Where the chain is inferred rather than demonstrated.** Slice and animal preparations establish mechanism; human non-invasive measures produce LTP-like changes without demonstrating the defining features; and behaviour is related to plasticity by inference. Keeping the three levels separate prevents the most common error in this area, which is to treat a slice result as an explanation of learning. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Long-term potentiation and depression]] [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-caution-inference|Appraisal: Long-term potentiation and depression]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Synaptic plasticity is the cellular substrate the neuroanatomy, genetics-neurodevelopment and computational-brain-theories domains each describe at their own scale - developmental pruning, learning rules and lasting change all reduce to this material when they are made concrete. [[Synaptic pruning and myelination]] [[Long-term potentiation and depression]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Activity-dependent strengthening and weakening of synapses (long-term potentiation and depression) is not covered by a verified source in this library yet, so it stays on the advanced study queue rather than being taught here.

## Recent research

- **2025 · PET-MRI case-control study (Biological Psychiatry).** In 19 adults with bipolar disorder (13 with type I) and 26 controls, imaging of synaptic vesicle glycoprotein 2A showed lower frontolimbic synaptic density, which exploratory analyses linked to psychotropic medication use. [[P41325959 Asch 2025 Synaptic density in bipolar disorder#^p41325959-limit|Asch 2025]] [[P41325959 Asch 2025 Synaptic density in bipolar disorder#^p41325959-sv2a|Asch 2025]] [[P41325959 Asch 2025 Synaptic density in bipolar disorder#^p41325959-medication|Asch 2025]] With a small sample and treatment that was not randomised, illness and medication effects cannot yet be separated. [[P41325959 Asch 2025 Synaptic density in bipolar disorder#^p41325959-caution-confound|Appraisal: Asch 2025]]
- **2024 · Postmortem case-control study (Cerebral Cortex).** Prefrontal tissue from 10 autistic and 10 control brains had more excitatory synapses in upper layers and fewer inhibitory synapses in all layers, counted as co-localised pre- and postsynaptic markers. [[P38696601 Vakilzadeh 2024 Prefrontal excitatory and inhibitory synapses in autism#^p38696601-limit|Vakilzadeh 2024]] [[P38696601 Vakilzadeh 2024 Prefrontal excitatory and inhibitory synapses in autism#^p38696601-counts|Vakilzadeh 2024]] [[P38696601 Vakilzadeh 2024 Prefrontal excitatory and inhibitory synapses in autism#^p38696601-markers|Vakilzadeh 2024]] Such counts are an anatomical correlate of the kind this note calls suggestive rather than decisive: they do not measure synaptic strength or plasticity in life. [[P38696601 Vakilzadeh 2024 Prefrontal excitatory and inhibitory synapses in autism#^p38696601-caution-proxy|Appraisal: Vakilzadeh 2024]]
