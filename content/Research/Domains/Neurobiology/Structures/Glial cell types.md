# Glial cell types

## Definition

'Glia' is an umbrella term for several non-neuronal cell populations in the nervous system with different jobs. The classical list - astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia and ependymal cells - is a division by cell type, not by function, and each type has subtypes with different properties. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]]

## How it works

**The inventory.** The nervous system contains neurons plus several glial populations: astrocytes, oligodendrocytes and their peripheral counterpart Schwann cells, microglia, and the ependymal cells lining the ventricular system. Glial cells provide scaffolding, insulation, nutrient transport, waste handling and immune mediation. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]]

**Astrocytes.** Astrocytes contact synapses and blood vessels, influence the extracellular environment by buffering ions and transmitters, and contribute to the blood-brain barrier; they are described as required for barrier development and maintenance of its properties. That their processes contact both synapses and vessels is the anatomical basis for the claim that they couple neuronal activity to local blood flow. [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-support|Blood Brain Barrier]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]]

**Oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells.** These produce myelin: in some axons glial cells form a sheath that insulates the axon and increases conduction speed by restricting current flow to the nodes of Ranvier, where the membrane is exposed and ion channels cluster. Oligodendrocytes wrap central axons - one oligodendrocyte can myelinate many of them - whereas Schwann cells myelinate a single peripheral segment each, a difference that matters for regeneration and for disease patterns. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-limit|Nervous Tissue]]

**Microglia.** Microglia are the resident immune cells, described as highly dynamic surveillants of the brain parenchyma that respond immediately to damage: in vivo imaging showed their processes extending and retracting constantly in uninjured cortex, sampling the local environment, and a switch to shielding the injured site after barrier disruption. That surveillance role reframes them from passive scavengers to active participants in circuit maintenance, and it is also why microglial involvement appears in many disease contexts. [[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-surveillance|Microglial surveillance]] [[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-response|Microglial surveillance]]

**Ependymal cells and cerebrospinal fluid.** Ependymal cells line the ventricles and are part of the cerebrospinal fluid system: the ventricular system produces and circulates cerebrospinal fluid, which is reabsorbed so that obstruction changes pressure and function. The cells lining it are part of the barrier and transport system rather than bystanders, which is why the tissue chapter treats them with the other glial families. [[F30 OpenStax Circulation and the central nervous system#^f30-ventricles|Circulation and the Central Nervous System]]

**Where the support metaphor fails.** 'Support cell' implies passivity that the evidence does not support: astrocytes gate extracellular signalling and the barrier, microglia survey and remodel contacts, and oligodendrocyte lineage cells are themselves activity-responsive. The accurate summary is that these cells create and maintain the conditions under which neurons signal, and in several cases participate in signalling directly - while still avoiding the opposite error of treating each glial population as a second nervous system. [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-support|Blood Brain Barrier]] [[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-surveillance|Microglial surveillance]] [[F105 Neuroscience Online blood-brain barrier#^f105-limit|Blood Brain Barrier]]

## Evidence and status

Cellular anatomy is well established from microscopy and tracer work; the physiological roles of each type are established in varying degrees. Oligodendrocyte function is tightly constrained by conduction evidence, including the functional consequences of myelin loss. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-mechanism|Multiple Sclerosis]] Microglial surveillance is demonstrated by direct in vivo imaging. [[P15831717 Nimmerjahn 2005 Microglial surveillance#^p15831717-revision|Microglial surveillance]] Astrocyte contributions to synaptic physiology are the most actively revised area. The frequently quoted neuron-to-glia ratio is an average rather than a universal constant: current estimates put the glia-to-neuron ratio at roughly one to one in humans - a correction to the older claim that glia vastly outnumber neurons. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]]

## Uncertainties

- How much of synaptic signalling depends on astrocyte activity in the intact human brain remains contested, with most evidence from slice and animal preparations.
- Microglial states are heterogeneous, and the field has moved from a binary resting/activated model to multi-state descriptions that are still being standardised.
- Human-specific glial biology is difficult to study, so cross-species generalisation is a real limitation.
