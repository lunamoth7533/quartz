# Nervous tissue and myelin

**Definition.** Grey matter concentrates cell bodies and local processing while white matter contains long-range myelinated axons. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Basic Structure and Function of the Nervous System]]

## Supported claims

- In some axons a myelin sheath insulates the fibre and increases conduction speed, interrupted by the nodes of Ranvier. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]
- Grey and white matter describe tissue composition rather than a clean functional split in real circuits. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Basic Structure and Function of the Nervous System]]

## How it works

**Grey and white matter.** Grey matter is dominated by cell bodies, dendrites and local synapses and is associated with local processing; white matter is dominated by myelinated axons carrying long-range connections between regions. The shorthand is useful and imperfect, because real circuits do not observe a strict boundary: grey matter contains axons and some deep grey structures contain long-range fibres, while white-matter tracts contain glia and interstitial fluid. The distinction describes the dominant tissue and is a useful first approximation for imaging and for anatomy, not a partition rule. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure and Function]]

**Tissue composition.** Nervous tissue is built from neurons and from several glial populations whose contributions include scaffolding, insulation, nutrient transport, waste handling and immune mediation; the same source notes that a roughly one-to-one glia-to-neuron ratio corrects the older impression that glia dominate numerically. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-glia|Nervous Tissue]]

**Myelin as an engineering trade.** Myelin is a specialised, lipid-rich extension of glial membrane - a wrap formed by oligodendrocytes centrally and Schwann cells peripherally - rather than a secreted substance, which is why myelin damage is a structural injury and not a chemical imbalance. It raises conduction velocity by restricting where current can cross the membrane: voltage-gated channels cluster at nodes of Ranvier, and the spike effectively jumps between them in a process called saltatory conduction. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] Conduction speed therefore depends on two geometric properties - axon diameter and internode spacing - which is why demyelination slows or blocks conduction while leaving the axon physically present.

**Myelination is not finished at birth.** Cortical association tracts continue to myelinate through adolescence and into adulthood, and the timing differs between tracts. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-sequence|Normal development of brain circuits]] This is one biological basis for describing adolescence as a period of continuing structural change rather than a completed system.

**Regional specialisation of the tissue.** Cortical grey matter is laminated and columnar; white matter is organised into tracts with named origins and destinations; and the same cell type can adopt different morphologies in different regions. This is why anatomical language carries direction and level information - the same axon's function depends on where it is. [[F05 OpenStax The central nervous system#^f05-parts|The Central Nervous System]] [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure and Function]]

**Why the tissue needs a privileged supply.** The central nervous system has an extensive, tightly regulated blood supply separated from neural tissue by a selective barrier; because neural tissue depends on continuous perfusion and holds little reserve, interruption produces focal deficits. Tissue organisation, vascular supply and barrier function are therefore one system: white matter tracts run alongside the vessels that supply them, and a lesion's effect depends on what is destroyed, what is merely deprived, and for how long. [[F30 OpenStax Circulation and the central nervous system#^f30-privilege|Circulation and the Central Nervous System]] [[F30 OpenStax Circulation and the central nervous system#^f30-stroke|Circulation and the Central Nervous System]]

**Disease illustration.** Multiple sclerosis is described as an immune-mediated attack on central myelin that disrupts signalling, and it is a useful existence proof that myelin integrity is load-bearing for function rather than a timing optimisation. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-mechanism|Multiple Sclerosis]] Imaging changes can resemble MS without the disease, which is why the diagnosis is clinical rather than radiological. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-diagnosis|Multiple Sclerosis]]

**Clinical translation of tissue organisation.** Localisation reasoning reads symptoms back to tissue: which tract, which level, which side. That reasoning is powerful and probabilistic - fibres of passage and compensation limit it - which is why neurological diagnosis combines examination findings with imaging rather than treating either as decisive. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-limit|Nervous System Structure and Function]] [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Myelin and tissue organisation are the substrate that the neuroanatomy and neurology domains rely on when they describe conduction, tract and lesion findings; a white-matter difference on MRI is a change in the tissue described here, so reading imaging findings without this material skips a step. ## Limitation or common misconception

A volume or thickness difference on a scan does not map onto 'more' or 'better' tissue.
