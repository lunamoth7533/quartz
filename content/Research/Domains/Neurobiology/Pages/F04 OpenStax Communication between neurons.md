# Communication Between Neurons

**Publisher.** OpenStax, Anatomy and Physiology 2e, section 12.5

**Source.** <https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-5-communication-between-neurons>

**Verification.** Official page opened and checked on 2026-09-24; educational material, not primary research.

## Key points (as published)

There are two kinds of connection between electrically active cells: chemical synapses, where a neurotransmitter carries the signal across the synaptic cleft, and electrical synapses, where ions pass directly through gap junctions.
^f04-types

At a chemical synapse the action potential opens voltage-gated calcium channels, vesicles fuse with the presynaptic membrane, and transmitter diffuses across the cleft to bind specific receptors; transmitter is cleared by enzymatic degradation or by neuronal or glial reuptake.
^f04-release

The postsynaptic effect depends on the receptor rather than on the transmitter alone: glutamate receptors depolarise adult postsynaptic cells while glycine and GABA receptors hyperpolarise them, and dopamine D1 receptors are excitatory while D2 receptors are inhibitory.
^f04-receptor

Graded potentials summate at the initial segment of the axon, where voltage-gated sodium channels are dense; for most neurons the axon hillock is that initial segment, and receptor potentials in sensory cells trigger transmitter release without a threshold.
^f04-initial-segment

Cholinergic synapses include the neuromuscular junction and autonomic sites; nicotinic receptors depolarise the postsynaptic cell while muscarinic receptors can depolarise or hyperpolarise depending on subtype.
^f04-cholinergic

Metabotropic receptors act through second messengers that can modify ion channels and gene transcription, changes the page links cautiously to stronger connections between cells and possibly to learning and memory.
^f04-metabotropic
