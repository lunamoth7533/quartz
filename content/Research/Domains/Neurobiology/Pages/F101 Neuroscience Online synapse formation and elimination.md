# Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination

## Key points (as published)

At the neuromuscular junction, synapses compete for innervation; the result is fewer inputs per muscle fibre but greater complexity and strength in the surviving inputs.
^f101-elimination

Activity-dependent competition favours more active inputs, and direct competition favours axons that capture trophic factors supplied by the target cell.
^f101-competition

Receptor subunit composition at the junction changes during development, so the same synapse has different molecular properties at different ages.
^f101-receptors

## Scope and limitations

A textbook chapter on developmental synapse formation and elimination, based on animal preparations.
