# ADHD across the lifespan

**Definition.** ADHD can persist from childhood into adulthood, with changing presentation and continuing impairment for many people. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-base|ADHD in adults review]]

## Supported claims

- Estimated worldwide adult prevalence is around 2.5%, and up to 70% of childhood-onset cases continue with impairing symptoms. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-base|ADHD in adults review]]
- The consensus statement compiles evidence on course, causes, impairments and treatment. [[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-scope|World Federation ADHD consensus statement]]
- Open questions include possible late-onset ADHD, emotional dysregulation and how impairment is defined. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults review]]

## How it works

**What changes and what persists.** ADHD is a developmental pattern of inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity that interferes with functioning or development. Its symptoms change form across childhood, adolescence and adulthood while the difficulties persist - hyperactivity may become inner restlessness - so the functional difficulty, not the surface behaviour, remains the criterion. [[F86 NIMH ADHD#^f86-definition|ADHD]] [[F86 NIMH ADHD#^f86-lifespan|ADHD]]

**The adult numbers and the threshold problem.** Estimated adult prevalence is around 2.5%, and up to 70% of people with childhood-onset ADHD keep impairing symptoms as adults, sometimes without meeting full criteria. A categorical threshold applied to a fluctuating developmental presentation will therefore misclassify people in both directions. Adult evidence is also younger and smaller than child evidence, and diagnostic thresholds and informant availability differ between ages, which moves prevalence estimates. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-base|ADHD in adults: evidence base]]

**Validity is argued, not assumed.** The adult review addresses descriptive, predictive and concurrent validity, and lists late-onset ADHD, emotional dysregulation as a candidate core symptom, the definition of functional impairment, comorbidity persistence and objective diagnostic measures among its open questions. Those are the live questions rather than settled positions. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults: evidence base]] [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-caution-biomarker|Appraisal: ADHD in adults: evidence base]]

**Cognitive profiles vary within and across ages.** Timing, inhibitory control and delay-related deficits behaved as independent components rather than one core deficit in a school-age sample - a heterogeneity finding that also complicates lifespan comparison, because the same person's profile can change while the diagnosis remains. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-finding|Beyond the dual pathway model]] [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-implication|Beyond the dual pathway model]]

**Measurement and outcome.** Self-report, informant report and clinician rating produce different estimates, which is why guidelines describe assessment as multi-source. [[F86 NIMH ADHD#^f86-definition|ADHD]] The World Federation consensus statement curates 208 assertions from large studies and meta-analyses about course and outcome, while a functioning framework reminds readers that activity and participation - education, employment, relationships - are not symptom counts and should be measured separately. Lifespan claims should therefore name the age, the informant and the outcome. [[P33549739 Faraone 2021 World Federation ADHD consensus statement#^p33549739-scope|ADHD consensus statement]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-caution-impairment|Appraisal: International Classification of Functioning]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** The same measurement problem appears in the developmental-psychology material: a categorical threshold applied to a changing presentation across time inherits all the longitudinal-inference and measurement-invariance limits the methods domain describes. [[Longitudinal and within-person inference]] [[Measurement invariance]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Course and outcome differ between clinical and community samples, so prevalence and prognosis depend on who was studied.

## Recent research

- **2023 · Prospective cohort study (JAMA Network Open).** Extends the lifespan picture into old age: in a cohort of older adults from one Israeli health organisation, an adult ADHD diagnosis was associated with a higher later risk of dementia, with no clear increase among people who had received psychostimulants. [[P37847497 Levine 2023 Adult ADHD and dementia risk#^p37847497-limit|Levine 2023]] [[P37847497 Levine 2023 Adult ADHD and dementia risk#^p37847497-dementia|Levine 2023]] [[P37847497 Levine 2023 Adult ADHD and dementia risk#^p37847497-subgroups|Levine 2023]] The association is observational, and a diagnosis first made in late life may partly capture early cognitive decline. [[P37847497 Levine 2023 Adult ADHD and dementia risk#^p37847497-caution-causal|Appraisal: Levine 2023]]
- **2023 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry).** Extends it backwards: activity level, sustained attention and negative emotionality measured in infancy and toddlerhood prospectively predicted childhood ADHD with moderate pooled associations, so the developmental course can be traced before diagnosis. [[P36599815 Joseph 2023 Infant temperament and later ADHD#^p36599815-temperament|Joseph 2023]] Associations of this size describe group risk, not a forecast for an individual infant. [[P36599815 Joseph 2023 Infant temperament and later ADHD#^p36599815-caution-prediction|Appraisal: Joseph 2023]]

- [[ADHD medication evidence]] - applies the lifespan split to treatment: child and adult trials differ in raters and results, so an age group must be named before any medication claim transfers.
- [[Heterogeneity and presentations in ADHD]] - sibling construct note: developmental change is one source of the heterogeneity described there, so shifts across ages and profile differences within ages are read together.
- [[Functional outcomes and measurement]] - supplies the outcome frame used here: participation in education, employment and relationships is measured separately from symptom counts, so persistence claims should name which outcome persisted.
- [[Sex hormones and mood across the lifespan]] - adds the female lifespan: puberty, the menstrual cycle, pregnancy and perimenopause as reported windows of worse ADHD symptoms and mood.
