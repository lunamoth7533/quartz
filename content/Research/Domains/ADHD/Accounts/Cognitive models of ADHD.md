# Cognitive models of ADHD

## Definition

Cognitive models of ADHD explain its symptoms through a few underlying information-processing differences rather than through the symptom list itself. [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-theory|Willcutt 2005]] The main families are executive-function accounts, dual pathway accounts that add a motivational route built around delay, state-regulation accounts about matching arousal and effort to the task, and default-mode accounts about intrusion from internally directed brain activity. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-model|Sonuga-Barke 2010]] [[P23062355 Metin 2012 Event rate effects in ADHD#^p23062355-model|Metin 2012]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-size|Norman 2023]] Each explains part of the average difference between ADHD and comparison groups, and none has proved necessary for the diagnosis. [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-necessary|Willcutt 2005]] [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-implication|Sonuga-Barke 2010]]

## How it works

**Executive-function accounts.** The most prominent account holds that ADHD stems from a primary deficit in executive functions, the processes that keep a problem-solving set active toward a later goal. [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-theory|Willcutt 2005]] Across 83 studies, ADHD groups did worse on every executive task, with medium effects that were most consistent for response inhibition, vigilance, working memory and planning; the weaknesses held in clinic and community samples and were not explained by IQ, academic achievement or other disorders' symptoms. [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-effects|Willcutt 2005]] [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-samples|Willcutt 2005]] Because deficits were absent in many individuals, the same meta-analysis judged executive weakness an important component of ADHD but not a necessary or sufficient cause. [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-necessary|Willcutt 2005]] A medium difference leaves the two groups' score distributions largely overlapping, so many people with ADHD score in the typical range on any single task. [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Willcutt 2005]] How far executive dysfunction belongs in the definition of adult ADHD is still listed as an open question. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|Cortese 2025]]

**Dual pathway accounts and delay aversion.** The dual pathway model replaced a single deficit with two separable routes, one cognitive and one motivational, each affecting only some patients. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-model|Sonuga-Barke 2010]] The motivational route centres on delay aversion and links to reinforcement accounts: the spontaneously hypertensive rat prefers immediate reinforcement and shows a steep delay-of-reinforcement gradient, which the dopamine transfer deficit hypothesis attributes to weak transfer of dopamine responses from rewards to predictive cues. [[P38384667 Tripp 2024 Dopamine transfer deficit in ADHD#^p38384667-rodent|Tripp 2024]] [[P38384667 Tripp 2024 Dopamine transfer deficit in ADHD#^p38384667-dtd|Tripp 2024]] That support comes largely from one animal strain and still needs direct human tests. [[P38384667 Tripp 2024 Dopamine transfer deficit in ADHD#^p38384667-caution-translation|Appraisal: Tripp 2024]] A third, timing route was tested in children with ADHD, their siblings and controls on nine tasks: inhibitory, delay-related and temporal-processing measures formed independent components, and the ADHD group differed from controls on all three. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-design|Sonuga-Barke 2010]] [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-finding|Sonuga-Barke 2010]] The authors read this as heterogeneity rather than one core deficit, from a single clinic-referred sample. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-implication|Sonuga-Barke 2010]] [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-limit|Sonuga-Barke 2010]]

**State regulation.** The state regulation deficit model moves the problem from control capacity to energetic state: performance in ADHD should depend on how fast events arrive, with fast rates over-activating and adding commission errors, and slow rates under-activating and producing slow, variable responses and omissions. [[P23062355 Metin 2012 Event rate effects in ADHD#^p23062355-model|Metin 2012]] A meta-analysis of Go/No-Go studies found the predicted pattern for speed and errors: reaction times slowed disproportionately at slow event rates, and commission-error differences grew at fast rates. [[P23062355 Metin 2012 Event rate effects in ADHD#^p23062355-rate|Metin 2012]] Two results limit it: event rate did not change reaction-time variability, and ADHD-control differences persisted once event-rate effects were accounted for. [[P23062355 Metin 2012 Event rate effects in ADHD#^p23062355-variability|Metin 2012]] State therefore explains part of the gap on one task family without becoming the cause of ADHD. [[P23062355 Metin 2012 Event rate effects in ADHD#^p23062355-caution-task|Appraisal: Metin 2012]]

**Default-mode interference.** The default mode network is a set of brain regions tied to thinking about oneself and others, recalling personal events and spontaneous mind-wandering. [[P37167968 Menon 2023 Twenty years of the default mode network#^p37167968-functions|Menon 2023]] Default-mode interference accounts propose that in ADHD this internally directed activity is not kept separate enough from the networks serving external tasks. [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-size|Norman 2023]] In a mega-analysis of 1,301 children with ADHD matched to 1,301 controls, ADHD went with weaker anticorrelation between the default mode network and salience/ventral attention, somatomotor and dorsal attention networks, and ADHD traits in a sample of 10,113 showed the same pattern. [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-anticorrelation|Norman 2023]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-traits|Norman 2023]] The effects were small (Cohen's d about 0.14-0.17), and resting-state connectivity cannot show that weaker segregation causes lapses during tasks. [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-size|Norman 2023]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-caution-effect|Appraisal: Norman 2023]]

**Where the accounts meet.** The accounts overlap more than they compete: executive weakness, delay-related choice, timing and state effects each separate groups on average, and each is missing in many people with the diagnosis. [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-necessary|Willcutt 2005]] [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-finding|Sonuga-Barke 2010]] Brain-based subgroups show the same pattern at another level: clustering resting-state connectivity gave replicable subgroups that differed in hyperactivity-impulsivity and intelligence but did not line up with diagnoses. [[P36912839 Vandewouw 2023 Replicable transdiagnostic brain subgroups#^p36912839-subgroups|Vandewouw 2023]] [[P36912839 Vandewouw 2023 Replicable transdiagnostic brain subgroups#^p36912839-transdiagnostic|Vandewouw 2023]] The reinforcement-learning and catecholamine accounts, kept in their own notes, formalise the motivational and neurochemical levels of the same picture. [[Reinforcement learning accounts of ADHD]] [[ADHD and prefrontal catecholamines]]

**Reading rule.** For any cognitive claim about ADHD, name the process, the task that measured it and the comparison group, and ask whether the result is an average difference or a statement about everyone with the diagnosis; in this literature each account explains part of the group difference and none is necessary for the diagnosis. [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-necessary|Willcutt 2005]] [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Willcutt 2005]]

## Evidence and status

Each account has group-level support from a meta-analysis or a large pooled sample: medium executive-function differences across 83 studies, event-rate effects across Go/No-Go studies, and small default-mode network differences in more than 2,600 children. [[P15950006 Willcutt 2005 Executive function theory of ADHD#^p15950006-effects|Willcutt 2005]] [[P23062355 Metin 2012 Event rate effects in ADHD#^p23062355-rate|Metin 2012]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-size|Norman 2023]] The multi-pathway evidence held here rests on one clinic-referred sample, and the delay mechanism draws largely on a single rodent strain. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-limit|Sonuga-Barke 2010]] [[P38384667 Tripp 2024 Dopamine transfer deficit in ADHD#^p38384667-caution-translation|Appraisal: Tripp 2024]] None of the accounts has produced an individual diagnostic test, and objective diagnostic measures remain an open question in adult ADHD. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|Cortese 2025]] [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-caution-biomarker|Appraisal: Cortese 2025]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether the accounts describe distinct subgroups of people or facets present to different degrees in everyone with ADHD. [[P20410727 Sonuga-Barke 2010 Beyond the dual pathway#^p20410727-implication|Sonuga-Barke 2010]]
- What reaction-time variability indexes: it did not change with event rate, which weakens its use as a marker of state regulation. [[P23062355 Metin 2012 Event rate effects in ADHD#^p23062355-variability|Metin 2012]]
- Whether weaker default-mode segregation produces attentional lapses, accompanies them or follows from them. [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-caution-effect|Appraisal: Norman 2023]]
- How specific any of these processes is to ADHD, given brain-based subgroups that cut across diagnoses. [[P36912839 Vandewouw 2023 Replicable transdiagnostic brain subgroups#^p36912839-transdiagnostic|Vandewouw 2023]]

## Recent research

- **2023 · Mega-analysis of resting-state fMRI (Neuropsychopharmacology).** Tests the default-mode account in large pooled cohorts: weaker separation between default mode and task networks in diagnosed children and along ADHD traits, with small effects that fit a continuum. [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-traits|Norman 2023]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-size|Norman 2023]]
- **2024 · Narrative review (Personality neuroscience).** Restates the motivational pathway as a dopamine transfer deficit, drawing mainly on one rodent strain, so the hypothesis still awaits direct human tests. [[P38384667 Tripp 2024 Dopamine transfer deficit in ADHD#^p38384667-dtd|Tripp 2024]] [[P38384667 Tripp 2024 Dopamine transfer deficit in ADHD#^p38384667-caution-translation|Appraisal: Tripp 2024]]
- **2023 · Case-control clustering study (JAMA network open).** Finds replicable brain-connectivity subgroups that ignore diagnostic boundaries, arguing against any network model specific to ADHD. [[P36912839 Vandewouw 2023 Replicable transdiagnostic brain subgroups#^p36912839-interpretation|Vandewouw 2023]] [[P36912839 Vandewouw 2023 Replicable transdiagnostic brain subgroups#^p36912839-caution-subgroups|Appraisal: Vandewouw 2023]]
