# ADHD and substance use

## Definition

This framework covers two linked questions: how far childhood ADHD raises the later risk of substance use and substance use disorders, and whether ADHD medication changes that risk. [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-disorder|Lee 2011]] [[P23754458 Humphreys 2013 Stimulant medication and substance outcomes#^p23754458-question|Humphreys 2013]] The evidence is observational and population-level, so it describes risk in groups rather than offering guidance for anyone who uses substances. [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Lee 2011]] [[P28659039 Quinn 2017 ADHD medication and substance-related problems#^p28659039-caution-within|Appraisal: Quinn 2017]]

## How it works

**Childhood ADHD predicts later substance problems.** In a meta-analysis restricted to prospective studies that tracked children with and without ADHD to their teens or adult years, those with ADHD were more likely to have tried nicotine and other substances, while alcohol use did not differ. [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-use|Lee 2011]] [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-limit|Lee 2011]] They were also more likely to develop abuse or dependence involving nicotine, alcohol, marijuana, cocaine or other substances, and the associations did not vary significantly with sex, age, race, sample source, publication year or DSM edition. [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-disorder|Lee 2011]] [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-moderators|Lee 2011]]

**Why the association is hard to read causally.** Too few studies separated ADHD from co-occurring disruptive behaviour disorders, and the authors judge that this comorbidity clouds any claim about ADHD's own effect. [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-dbd|Lee 2011]] A prospective design fixes the order of events but not their cause: conduct problems, family adversity and shared genetic liability could each produce both ADHD and later substance problems. [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Lee 2011]] Trait-level work suggests one transdiagnostic route: in non-clinical young adults, the link between ADHD symptoms and heavier drinking ran entirely through alexithymia and impulsivity. [[P38552929 Lyvers 2024 ASD and ADHD symptoms and alcohol use#^p38552929-alcohol|Lyvers 2024]] That was a cross-sectional path analysis of symptom scales, not a causal demonstration in diagnosed ADHD. [[P38552929 Lyvers 2024 ASD and ADHD symptoms and alcohol use#^p38552929-caution-design|Appraisal: Lyvers 2024]]

**The worry about stimulant treatment.** A long-running controversy asked whether childhood stimulant treatment raises later susceptibility to substance problems, after earlier reports had suggested that it lowers the risk. [[P23754458 Humphreys 2013 Stimulant medication and substance outcomes#^p23754458-question|Humphreys 2013]] The concern draws on the reward account of addiction, in which addictive drugs flood reward circuitry with dopamine far beyond natural rewards, while repeated exposure reshapes circuits for reward, stress and self-control. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-reward|NIDA: Drugs and the brain]] [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-adaptation|NIDA: Drugs and the brain]]

**Between-person evidence: no increase, no protection.** Across 15 longitudinal studies (2,565 participants) in which treatment came before outcomes were measured, children with and without a stimulant history had comparable rates of later use and of abuse or dependence for each substance examined. [[P23754458 Humphreys 2013 Stimulant medication and substance outcomes#^p23754458-null|Humphreys 2013]] [[P23754458 Humphreys 2013 Stimulant medication and substance outcomes#^p23754458-limit|Humphreys 2013]] The authors concluded that stimulant treatment neither protects against nor adds to the risk of later substance use disorders. [[P23754458 Humphreys 2013 Stimulant medication and substance outcomes#^p23754458-conclusion|Humphreys 2013]] Treated and untreated children differ in severity, family resources and access to care, so similar outcomes could hide offsetting effects, and each substance outcome rested on few studies. [[P23754458 Humphreys 2013 Stimulant medication and substance outcomes#^p23754458-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Humphreys 2013]]

**Registry evidence: within-person and emulated-trial designs.** In US claims for nearly 3 million adolescents and adults with ADHD, the same people had lower odds of substance-related emergency visits in medicated than in unmedicated months (odds ratio 0.65 in men, 0.69 in women), and lower odds two years after medicated periods, a long-term result less consistent in women. [[P28659039 Quinn 2017 ADHD medication and substance-related problems#^p28659039-concurrent|Quinn 2017]] [[P28659039 Quinn 2017 ADHD medication and substance-related problems#^p28659039-later|Quinn 2017]] Comparing people with themselves removes stable confounders but not changing circumstances, such as a crisis, that affect both medication and substance use. [[P28659039 Quinn 2017 ADHD medication and substance-related problems#^p28659039-caution-within|Appraisal: Quinn 2017]] In a Swedish national cohort that emulated a trial of starting medication within three months of diagnosis, initiation was associated with lower two-year mortality, a difference driven by unnatural causes including accidental poisoning. [[P38470385 Li 2024 ADHD medication and mortality#^p38470385-allcause|Li 2024]] [[P38470385 Li 2024 ADHD medication and mortality#^p38470385-causes|Li 2024]] An emulated trial makes the causal question explicit but cannot remove confounding by indication. [[P38470385 Li 2024 ADHD medication and mortality#^p38470385-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Li 2024]]

**What randomised evidence covers.** Randomised trials of ADHD medication are short, and a network meta-analysis found insufficient data at 26 and 52 weeks. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|Cortese 2018]] Long-term substance outcomes therefore come only from observational designs, each open to a different kind of confounding. [[P23754458 Humphreys 2013 Stimulant medication and substance outcomes#^p23754458-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Humphreys 2013]] [[P28659039 Quinn 2017 ADHD medication and substance-related problems#^p28659039-caution-within|Appraisal: Quinn 2017]] [[P38470385 Li 2024 ADHD medication and mortality#^p38470385-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Li 2024]]

**Reading rule.** Ask whether a claim concerns risk (does ADHD predict later substance problems), treatment (does medication change that risk) or mechanism, and which design produced it: prospective cohorts, between-person comparisons, within-person comparisons and emulated trials each remove different confounders, and none is a randomised test of long-term substance outcomes. [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Lee 2011]] [[P28659039 Quinn 2017 ADHD medication and substance-related problems#^p28659039-caution-within|Appraisal: Quinn 2017]] [[P38470385 Li 2024 ADHD medication and mortality#^p38470385-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Li 2024]]

## Evidence and status

That childhood ADHD predicts later substance use disorders rests on a meta-analysis of prospective cohorts, with the causal share unresolved because co-occurring conduct problems were rarely separated. [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-disorder|Lee 2011]] [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-dbd|Lee 2011]] On treatment, a meta-analysis of between-person longitudinal studies found no association in either direction, while within-person and emulated-trial registry analyses found fewer substance-related emergencies and unnatural deaths during or after treatment. [[P23754458 Humphreys 2013 Stimulant medication and substance outcomes#^p23754458-null|Humphreys 2013]] [[P28659039 Quinn 2017 ADHD medication and substance-related problems#^p28659039-concurrent|Quinn 2017]] [[P38470385 Li 2024 ADHD medication and mortality#^p38470385-causes|Li 2024]] The designs converge against the idea that treatment raises risk; they do not establish that it prevents substance use disorders. [[P28659039 Quinn 2017 ADHD medication and substance-related problems#^p28659039-conclusion|Quinn 2017]] [[P23754458 Humphreys 2013 Stimulant medication and substance outcomes#^p23754458-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Humphreys 2013]]

## Uncertainties

- How much of the ADHD-substance association is causal rather than due to co-occurring conduct problems or shared liability. [[P21382538 Lee 2011 Childhood ADHD and later substance use#^p21382538-dbd|Lee 2011]]
- Whether lower rates during treatment persist after treatment stops, and whether the longer-term reduction holds for women. [[P28659039 Quinn 2017 ADHD medication and substance-related problems#^p28659039-later|Quinn 2017]]
- Whether the associations differ by substance, age at starting treatment or type of medication; the sources held here do not separate these.
- No source held here addresses ADHD treatment in people who already have a substance use disorder.

## Recent research

- **2024 · Nationwide cohort with target trial emulation (JAMA).** Adds an emulated-trial estimate: starting medication soon after diagnosis went with fewer deaths from unnatural causes, including accidental poisoning, over two years. [[P38470385 Li 2024 ADHD medication and mortality#^p38470385-causes|Li 2024]] Two years of follow-up says nothing about decades of use. [[P38470385 Li 2024 ADHD medication and mortality#^p38470385-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Li 2024]]
- **2024 · Cross-sectional survey with path analysis (Alcohol).** Points to transdiagnostic traits, alexithymia and impulsivity, as the carriers of the link between ADHD symptoms and drinking in non-clinical adults. [[P38552929 Lyvers 2024 ASD and ADHD symptoms and alcohol use#^p38552929-alcohol|Lyvers 2024]] [[P38552929 Lyvers 2024 ASD and ADHD symptoms and alcohol use#^p38552929-caution-design|Appraisal: Lyvers 2024]]
