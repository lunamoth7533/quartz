# ADHD medication evidence

**Definition.** Medication evidence in ADHD comes mainly from short randomized trials aggregated into network meta-analyses that compare many drugs at once. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]

## Supported claims

- In adults, stimulants and atomoxetine improved core symptoms on both self- and clinician-rated scales at 12 weeks. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-stimulants|Comparative efficacy of ADHD interventions in adults]]
- In children and adolescents, all included drugs beat placebo on clinicians' ratings, but only methylphenidate and modafinil did on teachers' ratings. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication network meta-analysis]]
- Aggregate dose-response curves cannot set a dose for an individual. [[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-caution-individual|AI synthesis: ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]

## How it works

**The short-term picture.** A network meta-analysis of 133 double-blind randomised trials found that at 12 weeks every drug beat placebo on clinicians' ratings in children and adolescents, while on teachers' ratings only methylphenidate and modafinil did; in adults, amphetamines, methylphenidate, bupropion and atomoxetine beat placebo and modafinil did not. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

**Tolerability is age-specific.** Amphetamines were less well tolerated than placebo in both age groups, guanfacine only in children, and atomoxetine, methylphenidate and modafinil only in adults; head-to-head comparisons favoured amphetamines on clinician-rated efficacy. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-tolerability|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-corrigendum|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

**Reading the two syntheses together.** The 2018 network meta-analysis in children and adolescents and the 2025 adult syntheses differ in population, comparators and outcomes, so they are complementary rather than confirmatory: the children's finding that all drugs beat placebo on clinician ratings but few on teacher ratings is a measurement result as much as a drug result, and the adult finding that stimulants and atomoxetine improved core symptoms on both rater types is its adult counterpart. Reading either without its rater structure overstates it. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-acceptability|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

**Comparative evidence has a quality ceiling.** Most indirect comparisons carried low or very low confidence while some direct comparisons were high or moderate, and the adult synthesis reported confidence between very low and moderate for efficacy estimates, found medications not efficacious on additional outcomes such as quality of life, and left longer-term evidence under-investigated. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-limit|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-limit|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

**Where the long-term question stands.** Data at 26 and 52 weeks were insufficient in the 2018 synthesis, whose authors called for long-term research; the 2025 adult review keeps long-term effects among its open questions; and quality-of-life outcomes did not separate from placebo in the adult synthesis. A broader synthesis of pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions again found short-term advantages with limited long-term data. The correct reading is that long-term benefit has not been demonstrated, not that it has been excluded. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults review]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

**Dose-effect modelling adds a second dimension.** A 2026 synthesis found systematic dose-effect relationships for some agents: median efficacy plateaued above study-specific doses for methylphenidate, amphetamines and guanfacine in children and for amphetamines in adults, while discontinuation for adverse events rose with dose for amphetamines and methylphenidate above study-specific ranges. The authors read this as challenging both therapeutic inertia and uncritical dose escalation. [[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-doseeffect|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]] [[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-interpretation|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]

**Nothing here is individual guidance.** Group-level rankings and head-to-head averages are not prescribing guidance for a person, aggregate dose-response curves cannot set a dose, and this library reproduces no dosing or titration information - a deliberate boundary rather than an omission. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-caution-choice|AI appraisal: ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-caution-individual|AI appraisal: ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]

**Reading rule.** State the age group, the rater, the comparator and the follow-up window. All four changed the answer in the same literature, which is why a bare statement that medications work for ADHD is not a finding. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-stimulants|Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Group rankings and averages are not prescribing guidance, and the longest-term questions carry the least evidence.
