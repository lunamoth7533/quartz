# Cross-Cultural Adult ADHD Assessment in 42 Countries Using the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale Screener.

**Authors.** Lewczuk K, Marcowski P, et al. (69 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38180045/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of attention disorders.

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The screener showed good reliability and validity and partial measurement invariance across languages, countries and genders.
^p38180045-invariance

About one in five respondents screened as at risk for adult ADHD (21.4% of women, 18.1% of men); scores were highest in English-speaking Western countries and lower in East Asian and non-English-speaking European countries.
^p38180045-atrisk

The authors read the results as consistent with the low specificity of adult ADHD screening instruments.
^p38180045-specificity

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional analysis of 72,627 adults from a large research project spanning 42 countries; "at risk" status comes from screener scores rather than clinical assessment, and associations with age, mental and sexual health and socioeconomic status were examined.
^p38180045-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A screen-positive rate near 20% is far above adult prevalence estimates, which shows why a positive screen is a prompt for assessment rather than a diagnosis; partial invariance also means raw score comparisons between countries or genders need caution.
^p38180045-caution-screening
