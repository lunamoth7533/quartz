## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review describes the spontaneously hypertensive rat as preferring immediate reinforcement, weighting single reinforcers more than reinforcement history, showing a steeper delay-of-reinforcement gradient and developing less incentive approach to reward-predicting cues.
^p38384667-rodent

It argues that a dopamine transfer deficit, a weak transfer of dopamine responses from rewards to the cues that predict them, would produce these features and the altered response to reinforcement seen in ADHD.
^p38384667-dtd

## Scope as reported

Theoretical review built mainly on one rodent strain, which also proposes that variation in dopamine transfer may underlie personality dimensions linked to reinforcement sensitivity.
^p38384667-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The dopamine transfer deficit is a hypothesis: its support here comes largely from a single animal model, and carrying the strain's behaviour over to children with ADHD, or to personality, needs direct human tests.
^p38384667-caution-translation
