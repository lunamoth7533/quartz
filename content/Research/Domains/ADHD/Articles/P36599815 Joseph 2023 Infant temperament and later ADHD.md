# Research Review: A systematic review and meta-analysis of infant and toddler temperament as predictors of childhood attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder.

**Authors.** Joseph HM, Lorenzo NE, et al. (8 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36599815/)

**Published.** 2023 in Journal of child psychology and psychiatry, and allied disciplines.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/jcpp.13753](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.13753) · PMID [36599815](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36599815/) · PMCID [PMC10404471](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10404471/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Higher activity level in infancy and toddlerhood had the strongest pooled association with later childhood ADHD (moderate size); lower sustained attention and higher negative emotionality also showed moderate associations.
^p36599815-temperament

Activity level and negative emotionality predicted all three symptom dimensions, whereas early sustained attention was associated only with combined symptoms.
^p36599815-dimensions

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis of prospective longitudinal studies that measured four theoretically chosen temperament constructs (sustained attention, activity level, inhibition, negative emotionality) between birth and 36 months against preschool or childhood ADHD outcomes. The authors note that relatively few studies examined early sustained attention (9) or inhibition (15).
^p36599815-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Pooled correlations of this size describe group-level risk gradients and leave wide overlap between infants who do and do not later meet ADHD criteria, so early temperament cannot serve as an individual predictor; where one informant rates both temperament and later symptoms, shared-rater variance could also inflate the associations.
^p36599815-caution-prediction
