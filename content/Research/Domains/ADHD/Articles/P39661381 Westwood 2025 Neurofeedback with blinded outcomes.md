# Neurofeedback for Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

**Authors.** Westwood SJ, Aggensteiner PM, et al. (18 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39661381/)

**Published.** 2025 in JAMA psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.3702](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.3702) · PMID [39661381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39661381/) · PMCID [PMC11800020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11800020/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

When symptoms were rated by people probably unaware of treatment allocation, neurofeedback did not significantly reduce total ADHD symptoms (20 trials, 1,214 participants).
^p39661381-blinded

A small benefit appeared only when analyses were limited to trials using established standard protocols, and of five neuropsychological outcomes only processing speed improved, slightly.
^p39661381-protocols

The authors conclude that neurofeedback showed no meaningful group-level clinical or neuropsychological benefit.
^p39661381-conclusion

## Scope as reported

Primary analyses used probably blinded symptom reports or neuropsychological tests; searches ran to July 2023, risk of bias was assessed with RoB 2, and heterogeneity was described as generally low to moderate.
^p39661381-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The analysis choice is the point for this library: by restricting to raters probably unaware of allocation, it isolates the effect that survives blinding, and a benefit seen only by informed raters would be compatible with expectation rather than treatment.
^p39661381-caution-blinding
