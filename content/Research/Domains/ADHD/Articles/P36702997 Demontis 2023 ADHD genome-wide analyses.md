# Genome-wide analyses of ADHD identify 27 risk loci, refine the genetic architecture and implicate several cognitive domains.

**Authors.** Demontis D, Walters GB, et al. (49 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36702997/)

**Published.** 2023 in Nature genetics.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41588-022-01285-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-022-01285-8) · PMID [36702997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36702997/) · PMCID [PMC10914347](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10914347/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The meta-analysis identified 27 genome-wide significant loci and 76 candidate risk genes, enriched among genes expressed especially in early brain development.
^p36702997-loci

Common-variant ADHD risk mapped onto several neuron types found only in the brain, including dopamine neurons of the midbrain.
^p36702997-dopaminergic

Modelling put the share of ADHD-influencing variants that also act in other psychiatric disorders at 84-98%, and common-variant risk was linked to poorer complex cognition and executive functions, including attention.
^p36702997-shared

## Scope as reported

Case-control genome-wide association meta-analysis combined with exome sequencing, which found an excess of rare protein-truncating variants in a set of risk genes and pointed to SORCS3 through both common and rare variants.
^p36702997-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Enrichment in midbrain dopaminergic neurons is compatible with catecholamine accounts but does not single out a transmitter deficit: the signal is polygenic, spread across neuronal types and largely shared with other disorders, and it describes populations rather than any individual's biology.
^p36702997-caution-polygenic
