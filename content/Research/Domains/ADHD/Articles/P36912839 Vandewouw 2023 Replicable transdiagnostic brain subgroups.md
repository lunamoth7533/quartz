# Identifying Replicable Subgroups in Neurodevelopmental Conditions Using Resting-State Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging Data.

**Authors.** Vandewouw MM, Brian J, et al. (14 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36912839/)

**Published.** 2023 in JAMA network open.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.2066](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.2066) · PMID [36912839](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36912839/) · PMCID [PMC10011941](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10011941/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Clustering of resting-state connectome measures, run separately in each data set, produced subgroups with similar brain characteristics in both.
^p36912839-subgroups

The subgroups differed in intelligence and in hyperactivity-impulsivity traits but did not line up with diagnoses: the proportion of each diagnosis did not differ between subgroups in either data set.
^p36912839-transdiagnostic

The authors conclude that neurobiological similarity crosses diagnostic boundaries and tracks behavioural characteristics instead.
^p36912839-interpretation

## Scope as reported

Clustering of resting-state functional connectivity in two independently collected data sets from Ontario and New York; one reported subgroup difference in hyperactivity-impulsivity scores had an eta-squared of 0.02.
^p36912839-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Replication across two data sets strengthens the clustering result, but subgroups defined from a single resting scan in a cross-sectional sample describe heterogeneity rather than distinct causes, and a small behavioural separation means subgroup membership would not guide a decision for an individual.
^p36912839-caution-subgroups
