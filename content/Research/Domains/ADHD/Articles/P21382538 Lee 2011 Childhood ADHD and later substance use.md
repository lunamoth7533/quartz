# Prospective association of childhood attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and substance use and abuse/dependence: a meta-analytic review.

**Authors.** Lee SS, Humphreys KL, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21382538/)

**Published.** 2011 in Clinical psychology review (volume 31, issue 3, pages 328-41) (first online 2011-01-20).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.cpr.2011.01.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2011.01.006) · PMID [21382538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21382538/) · PMCID [PMC3180912](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3180912/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Children with ADHD were more likely than peers to have tried nicotine and other substances at some point, while alcohol use did not differ.
^p21382538-use

They were also more likely to develop abuse or dependence involving nicotine, alcohol, marijuana, cocaine or unspecified substances.
^p21382538-disorder

Moderator tests (sex, age, race, sample source, publication year, DSM edition) showed no significant influence on the associations whose effect sizes varied.
^p21382538-moderators

Too few studies separated ADHD from co-occurring disruptive behaviour disorders for a formal analysis, and the authors judge that this comorbidity clouds claims about ADHD's specific effect.
^p21382538-dbd

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis restricted to prospective longitudinal studies of children with and without ADHD, chosen to strengthen causal inference; studies of comorbid disruptive behaviour disorders were summarised qualitatively.
^p21382538-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A prospective design fixes the order of events but not their cause: conduct problems, family adversity and shared genetic liability could each produce both ADHD and later substance problems, so the association marks risk rather than proving that ADHD itself causes substance disorders.
^p21382538-caution-confounding
