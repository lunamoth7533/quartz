# Sex differences in attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder diagnosis and clinical care: a national study of population healthcare records in Wales.

**Authors.** Martin J, Langley K, et al. (8 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38864317/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of child psychology and psychiatry, and allied disciplines (volume 65, issue 12, pages 1648-1658) (first online 2024-06-12).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/jcpp.13987](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.13987) · PMID [38864317](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38864317/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Males outnumbered females 3.9 to 1 among diagnosed young people; the ratio was 4.8 to 1 for diagnoses before age 12 and 1.9 to 1 for diagnoses after 18.
^p38864317-ratio

Males were diagnosed younger on average (10.9 versus 12.6 years) and were more likely to be prescribed ADHD medication.
^p38864317-timing

Before their ADHD diagnosis, females more often had recorded anxiety, depression or other mental health diagnoses and antidepressant prescriptions; the authors suggest diagnostic overshadowing or initial misdiagnosis as partial explanations.
^p38864317-overshadowing

## Scope as reported

Cohort built from routine primary and secondary care records, including ethnicity, deprivation and social-services contact; the sex differences were largely stable across these demographic groups.
^p38864317-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Records capture diagnoses and prescriptions, not symptoms: an earlier anxiety or depression diagnosis may reflect a missed ADHD presentation, a genuine co-occurring condition or both, and the data cannot tell these apart or count people who were never diagnosed.
^p38864317-caution-records
