# Are We Measuring ADHD or Anxiety? Examining the Factor Structure and Discriminant Validity of the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale in an Adult Anxiety Disorder Population.

**Authors.** Alarachi A, Merrifield C, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38288573/)

**Published.** 2024 in Assessment.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/10731911231225190](https://doi.org/10.1177/10731911231225190) · PMID [38288573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38288573/) · PMCID [PMC11409565](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11409565/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

A correlated three-factor model of inattention, impulsivity and hyperactivity, with one item moved from hyperactivity to impulsivity, fitted better than one-factor, two-factor and the traditional three-factor models.
^p38288573-factors

Discrimination from anxiety and distress measures was fair to good overall, but some hyperactivity items, such as difficulty relaxing and feeling driven, loaded more strongly on clinical anxiety factors than on ADHD factors.
^p38288573-overlap

## Scope as reported

Comparison of competing factor models and of the screener's discriminant properties in one clinical sample of 618 adults with anxiety disorders, where symptom overlap and ADHD rates are described as high.
^p38288573-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Items that load with anxiety show that a presentation built from self-reported hyperactivity can partly reflect co-occurring anxiety; results from an anxiety clinic may not transfer to other adult samples.
^p38288573-caution-overlap
