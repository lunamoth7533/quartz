# Evidence from "big data" for the default-mode hypothesis of ADHD: a mega-analysis of multiple large samples.

**Authors.** Norman LJ, Sudre G, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36100657/)

**Published.** 2023 in Neuropsychopharmacology (volume 48, issue 2, pages 281-289) (first online 2022-09-13).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41386-022-01408-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41386-022-01408-z) · PMID [36100657](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36100657/) · PMCID [PMC9751118](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9751118/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Children with diagnosed ADHD, matched one-to-one with controls on head motion and demographics, showed weaker anticorrelation of the default mode network with salience/ventral attention, somatomotor and dorsal attention networks.
^p36100657-anticorrelation

ADHD traits in the large multi-cohort sample showed the same pattern, most clearly between default mode and dorsal attention networks, and case-control results survived adjustment for co-occurring problems and stimulant use.
^p36100657-traits

Effect sizes were small (Cohen's d about 0.14-0.17); the authors read the results as subtle group differences that fit default-mode interference models and an ADHD continuum.
^p36100657-size

## Scope as reported

Mega-analysis pooling several large cohorts processed through one standardised pipeline, with a categorical case-control analysis and a separate dimensional analysis of ADHD traits.
^p36100657-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Effects this small mean heavily overlapping distributions, so the result supports the network account at group level without offering an individual marker; resting-state connectivity also cannot show that weaker network segregation produces attentional lapses during tasks.
^p36100657-caution-effect
