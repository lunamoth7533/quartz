# ADHD Medication and Substance-Related Problems.

**Authors.** Quinn PD, Chang Z, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28659039/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Comparing each patient's medicated months with their unmedicated months, the odds of a substance-related emergency visit were lower while on stimulants or atomoxetine (odds ratio 0.65 in men, 0.69 in women).
^p28659039-concurrent

Odds were also lower two years after medicated periods (0.81 in men, 0.86 in women), although sensitivity analyses supported the long-term result less consistently in women.
^p28659039-later

The authors conclude that ADHD medication is unlikely to raise the risk of substance-related problems in adolescence or adulthood.
^p28659039-conclusion

## Scope as reported

Within-individual analysis of US commercial claims from 2005 to 2014; the outcome was emergency department visits related to substance use disorders.
^p28659039-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Comparing people with themselves removes stable confounders such as genes and upbringing, but not circumstances that change over time and affect both medication use and substance use, such as a period of crisis; claims data also miss substance problems that never reach an emergency department.
^p28659039-caution-within
