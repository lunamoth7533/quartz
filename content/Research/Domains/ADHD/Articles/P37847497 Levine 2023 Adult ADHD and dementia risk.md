# Adult Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder and the Risk of Dementia.

**Authors.** Levine SZ, Rotstein A, et al. (8 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37847497/)

**Published.** 2023 in JAMA network open.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.38088](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.38088) · PMID [37847497](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37847497/) · PMCID [PMC10582792](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10582792/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

An adult ADHD diagnosis, modelled as time-varying, was associated with a higher risk of incident dementia after weighting for 18 potential confounders (adjusted hazard ratio about 2.8), and most of 14 complementary analyses did not weaken this.
^p37847497-dementia

The authors found no clear increase in dementia risk among people who had received psychostimulant medication, and only mild evidence of reverse causation.
^p37847497-subgroups

## Scope as reported

Cohort of older adults in one Israeli health organisation, with ADHD and dementia identified from ICD-9 and ICD-10 diagnoses; 7,726 participants received a dementia diagnosis during follow-up from 2003 to 2020.
^p37847497-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A diagnosis first recorded in late life is observational and rare in this cohort; it may partly capture early cognitive decline or differences in help-seeking, so the association does not show that ADHD causes dementia, and the medication subgroup result is not evidence of protection.
^p37847497-caution-causal
