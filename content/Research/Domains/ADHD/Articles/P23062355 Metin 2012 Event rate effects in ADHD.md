# A meta-analytic study of event rate effects on Go/No-Go performance in attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder.

**Authors.** Metin B, Roeyers H, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23062355/)

**Published.** 2012 in Biological psychiatry (volume 72, issue 12, pages 990-6) (first online 2012-10-11).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.08.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.08.023) · PMID [23062355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23062355/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The state regulation deficit model predicts that stimulus pace shapes ADHD performance: fast event rates over-activate and add commission errors, while slow rates under-activate and yield slow, variable responses and omissions.
^p23062355-model

In ADHD groups, reaction times slowed disproportionately when events came slowly, and commission-error differences grew when events came fast, which the authors take as support for the model.
^p23062355-rate

Event rate did not alter reaction-time variability, and ADHD-control differences on every measure persisted once event-rate effects were taken into account.
^p23062355-variability

## Scope as reported

Systematic literature search with two analyses: within-study contrasts between event-rate conditions, and meta-regression of event rate as a moderator of ADHD-control differences across Go/No-Go studies.
^p23062355-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The support comes from one task family and from group averages. Showing that energetic state explains part of the performance gap does not make state regulation the cause of ADHD, and the general differences that remain leave room for other accounts.
^p23062355-caution-task
