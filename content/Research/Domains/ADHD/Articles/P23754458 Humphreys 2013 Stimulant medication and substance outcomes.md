## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review addressed a live controversy: whether childhood stimulant treatment raises later susceptibility to substance problems, given earlier reports that it lowers that risk.
^p23754458-question

Children with and without a history of stimulant treatment had comparable rates of lifetime use and of abuse or dependence for alcohol, cocaine, marijuana, nicotine and non-specific drugs.
^p23754458-null

The authors conclude that stimulant treatment for ADHD neither protects against nor adds to the risk of later substance use disorders.
^p23754458-conclusion

## Scope as reported

Random-effects meta-analysis of studies published from 1980 to February 2012 using odds ratios supplied or extracted per substance outcome; each outcome drew on 3 to 11 studies, and moderators were examined.
^p23754458-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Children who receive stimulants differ from those who do not in severity, family resources and access to care, so similar outcomes could conceal offsetting effects; with few studies per substance, modest differences in either direction could also go undetected.
^p23754458-caution-comparison
