# Definition of Response in Randomized Controlled Trials of Medications for Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Across the Lifespan: A Systematic Review.

**Authors.** Roy S, Colacicco G, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40365735/)

**Published.** 2025 in Journal of child and adolescent psychopharmacology.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1089/cap.2025.0029](https://doi.org/10.1089/cap.2025.0029) · PMID [40365735](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40365735/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Of 167 trials, 88 defined treatment response as a percentage fall in rating-scale scores, most often a reduction of at least 30% on the ADHD Rating Scale; other trials used 25%, 40% or 50% cut-offs, sometimes on other adult and child scales.
^p40365735-thresholds

The other 79 trials specified no response threshold, and the authors call for a consensus definition of response for each rating scale.
^p40365735-consensus

## Scope as reported

Systematic review of how response was defined across the trials compiled in the MED-ADHD database, which draws on multiple bibliographic databases plus unpublished information from manufacturers and study authors.
^p40365735-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Because a responder rate depends on a cut-off applied to a rating scale, the same data can produce different responder percentages under different thresholds, so response rates from trials with different definitions are not directly comparable.
^p40365735-caution-threshold
