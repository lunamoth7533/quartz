# A systematic review and meta-analysis comparing the severity of core symptoms of attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder in females and males.

**Authors.** Young S, Uysal O, et al. (13 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39494848/)

**Published.** 2024 in Psychological medicine (volume 54, issue 14, pages 3763-3784) (first online 2024-11-04).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1017/S0033291724001600](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291724001600) · PMID [39494848](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39494848/) · PMCID [PMC11578919](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11578919/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

With rating-scale and interview data pooled across the lifespan, males with ADHD had more severe hyperactivity-impulsivity than females; on rating scales alone, boys scored higher than girls on that dimension.
^p39494848-hyperactivity

On adult rating scales men were rated more inattentive than women, with no sex difference in hyperactivity-impulsivity.
^p39494848-adults

Every significant difference was small, and clinical interview data showed no sex difference in severity for children or adults.
^p39494848-interview

## Scope as reported

Systematic review with 15 meta-analyses of studies published 1996-2021 under DSM-IV, DSM-IV-TR or DSM-5 criteria, analysing rating-scale and interview data separately as well as together.
^p39494848-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Differences that appear on rating scales but not in interviews may reflect how scales work as much as the condition itself; and because every sample was already diagnosed, girls and women who were never recognised are missing by design, so similar severity among the diagnosed does not show similar presentation in everyone with ADHD.
^p39494848-caution-diagnosed
