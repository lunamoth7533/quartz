# Instrumental learning and behavioral persistence in children with attention-deficit/hyperactivity-disorder: does reinforcement frequency matter?

**Authors.** Hulsbosch AK, Beckers T, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37040877/)

**Published.** 2023 in Journal of child psychology and psychiatry, and allied disciplines.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/jcpp.13805](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.13805) · PMID [37040877](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37040877/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Children with ADHD needed more trials than typically developing children to reach the learning criterion, under both continuous and partial reinforcement.
^p37040877-acquisition

After partial reinforcement they made fewer target responses during extinction, a weaker partial reinforcement extinction effect, while making more responses overall during extinction in both conditions.
^p37040877-extinction

The authors read the results as slower reinforcement learning and lower behavioural persistence, bearing on predictions of the dynamic developmental theory and the dopamine transfer deficit hypothesis.
^p37040877-interpretation

## Scope as reported

A simple instrumental learning task with acquisition under 100% or 20% reinforcement followed by a four-minute extinction phase, analysed with diagnosis-by-condition analyses of variance.
^p37040877-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Group differences in acquisition speed and extinction are behavioural data; on their own they do not identify a learning-rate parameter or a dopamine mechanism, which would need model fitting and comparison against alternative explanations such as lower engagement with the task.
^p37040877-caution-mechanism
