# Tricyclic antidepressants for attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) in children and adolescents.

**Authors.** Otasowie J, Castells X, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25238582/)

**Published.** 2014 in The Cochrane database of systematic reviews (first online 2014-09-19).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/14651858.CD006997.pub2](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006997.pub2) · PMID [25238582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25238582/) · PMCID [PMC11236426](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11236426/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

More young people on a tricyclic than on placebo reached a predefined improvement in core ADHD symptoms; desipramine improved core symptoms on parent, teacher and clinician ratings, and nortriptyline on clinician ratings.
^p25238582-efficacy

No serious adverse effects were found, but diastolic blood pressure and pulse rose slightly, desipramine suppressed appetite and nortriptyline increased weight.
^p25238582-harms

The authors rate the evidence low to very low quality and single out desipramine's cardiovascular effects as an important clinical concern.
^p25238582-conclusion

## Scope as reported

Six small trials, none free of bias, searched to September 2013; two involved children with co-occurring tic disorders, and the benefit reported is short-term.
^p25238582-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The evidence is dated, small and almost entirely about one drug in young people; it cannot be read as current support for tricyclics in ADHD, and it says nothing about adults.
^p25238582-caution-age
