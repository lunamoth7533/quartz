# Validity of the executive function theory of attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: a meta-analytic review.

**Authors.** Willcutt EG, Doyle AE, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15950006/)

**Published.** 2005 in Biological psychiatry (volume 57, issue 11, pages 1336-46).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.02.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.02.006) · PMID [15950006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15950006/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The paper tests the theory that ADHD stems from a primary executive deficit, meaning weak processes for holding a problem-solving set toward a later goal.
^p15950006-theory

ADHD groups did worse than comparison groups on every executive task, with medium effects (0.46-0.69), most consistently for response inhibition, vigilance, working memory and planning.
^p15950006-effects

The weaknesses appeared in clinic and community samples alike and were not explained by IQ, academic achievement or other disorders' symptoms.
^p15950006-samples

Given moderate effects and deficits absent in many individuals, the authors judge executive weakness one important component of ADHD, not necessary or sufficient for every case.
^p15950006-necessary

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis of 83 group-comparison studies of executive-function measures; the abstract does not report age ranges or a publication-bias assessment.
^p15950006-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A medium group difference leaves the two groups' score distributions largely overlapping, so many people with ADHD score in the typical range on any single executive task; the result argues against a single executive core deficit, not against executive difficulties being common.
^p15950006-caution-overlap
