## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Pooled liability correlations were almost perfect for identical twins (.98, 95% CI .96-.99), while fraternal-twin correlations were .53 when autism prevalence was set at 5% and .67 at 1%.
^p26709141-correlations

The resulting heritability estimates were 64-91%; a shared-environment component became significant (7-35%) only as the assumed prevalence fell from 5% to 1%.
^p26709141-estimates

The authors attribute earlier reports of large shared-environment effects mainly to over-inclusion of concordant fraternal pairs, a statistical artefact.
^p26709141-artefact

In a population sample of 6,413 twin pairs, heritability stayed broadly constant across the range of trait scores.
^p26709141-continuum

## Scope as reported

Systematic review of 13 twin studies of autism, 7 of which met a systematic-recruitment criterion and were meta-analysed with corrections for selection and ascertainment; a separate extremes analysis used Childhood Autism Spectrum Test scores from a population twin sample.
^p26709141-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Twin estimates depend on model inputs and assumptions, notably the assumed prevalence and the premise that identical and fraternal pairs share environments to a similar degree; the width of the 64-91% range shows how much the headline figure moves with those choices.
^p26709141-caution-assumptions

How the design is read: identical twins share practically all their DNA sequence and fraternal twins on average half of their segregating variants, so a larger identical-twin correlation is credited to genes and resemblance beyond what genes predict to shared environment. The partition is only as good as the equal-environments assumption behind it.
^p26709141-caution-logic
