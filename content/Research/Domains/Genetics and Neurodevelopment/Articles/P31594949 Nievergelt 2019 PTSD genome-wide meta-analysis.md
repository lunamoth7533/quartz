# International meta-analysis of PTSD genome-wide association studies identifies sex- and ancestry-specific genetic risk loci.

**Authors.** Nievergelt CM, Maihofer AX, et al. (179 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31594949/)

**Published.** 2019 in Nature communications (first online 2019-10-08).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41467-019-12576-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12576-w) · PMID [31594949](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31594949/) · PMCID [PMC6783435](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6783435/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

PTSD risk after trauma is heritable; SNP-based heritability, estimated from common variants, was 5-20% and differed between the sexes.
^p31594949-snp

Three genome-wide significant loci were found (two in European-ancestry and one in African-ancestry analyses), with three more in men-only analyses; associations included PARK2, a gene linked to Parkinson's disease and dopamine regulation.
^p31594949-loci

A PTSD polygenic score significantly predicted re-experiencing symptoms in the Million Veteran Program, although the specific loci did not replicate there.
^p31594949-prs

## Scope as reported

Genome-wide association meta-analysis of multi-ethnic cohorts; the authors stress sex-stratified analyses and the need for samples beyond European ancestry.
^p31594949-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

SNP-based heritability counts only what common genotyped variants capture among unrelated people, so it is expected to sit below twin estimates; the two figures answer different questions, and subtracting one from the other does not size an environmental share.
^p31594949-caution-snp
