# Epigenome-wide association studies identify novel DNA methylation sites associated with PTSD: a meta-analysis of 23 military and civilian cohorts.

**Authors.** Katrinli S, Wani AH, et al. (82 named authors plus consortium authorship) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39696436/)

**Published.** 2024 in Genome medicine.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1186/s13073-024-01417-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13073-024-01417-1) · PMID [39696436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39696436/) · PMCID [PMC11658418](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11658418/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In the main meta-analysis, blood DNA methylation at 11 CpG sites was associated with PTSD, and 14 more sites emerged in analyses split by cohort type, sex and ancestry, including sites in AHRR and CDC42BPB.
^p39696436-cpgs

Many of these sites showed methylation levels correlated between blood and brain, and several were also related to PTSD in brain tissue from multiple regions.
^p39696436-brain

Of nine sites annotated to genes expressed in blood, five showed methylation levels correlated with expression of the annotated gene.
^p39696436-expression

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional meta-analysis by the Psychiatric Genomics Consortium PTSD Epigenetics Workgroup of 5,077 participants (2,156 with PTSD, 2,921 trauma-exposed controls) from 23 civilian and military cohorts, with harmonised diagnosis, blood methylation arrays, adjustment for sex, age, blood cell proportions and ancestry, and follow-up in brain tissue, sorted neuronal nuclei and a prolonged-stress cell model. The authors note the differences could reflect susceptibility, trauma impact or sequelae of PTSD.
^p39696436-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A single cross-sectional blood sample cannot tell which of those possibilities applies at a given site, and blood-brain correlation supports but does not establish that a blood mark tracks brain state. Controls were trauma-exposed, so the contrast concerns PTSD given trauma; PTSD as diagnosed in these cohorts is also not the same construct as complex PTSD.
^p39696436-caution-direction
