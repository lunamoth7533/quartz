# Exploring cross-tissue DNA methylation patterns: blood-brain CpGs as potential neurodegenerative disease biomarkers

**Authors.** Mendonça V, Soares-Lima SC, Moreira MAM. · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39060467/)

**Published.** 2024.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s42003-024-06591-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-06591-x) · PMID [39060467](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39060467/) · PMCID [PMC11282059](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11282059/)

**Verification.** Retrieved as `europepmc-fullTextXML` on 2026-09-25 and checked at the level of the full text (`receipts/source-checks/P39060467.txt`).

## Reported findings (checked full text)

Searching methylation databases for sites whose methylation levels correlate between blood and brain, the authors selected 18,293 blood-brain CpGs as candidate sites for comparing blood measurements across conditions.
^p39060467-bbcpg

Comparing patients with neurodegenerative conditions against controls, 64 of those sites differed: nine in Alzheimer's disease, nine in Parkinson's disease, 28 in multiple sclerosis and 18 in fragile X syndrome.
^p39060467-confirm

The authors present this as support for using blood DNA as a surrogate when the site is shown to correlate across tissues, and as a route to biomarker discovery rather than evidence of causation.
^p39060467-rationale

## Scope as reported

A bioinformatics analysis of public methylation datasets in neurodegenerative disease. Cross-tissue correlation is a property of the individual site, and the design is observational.
^p39060467-limit
