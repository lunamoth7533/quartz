# Return of genetic research results in 21,532 individuals with autism.

**Authors.** Wright JR, Astrovskaya I, et al. (13 named authors plus consortium authorship) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38958063/)

**Published.** 2024 in Genetics in medicine : official journal of the American College of Medical Genetics.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.gim.2024.101202](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gim.2024.101202) · PMID [38958063](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38958063/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Genotyping and exome sequencing identified 1,903 likely pathogenic or pathogenic variants in 1,861 autistic participants (8.6%), and nearly 90% of these variants were previously unknown to the participants.
^p38958063-yield

A returnable result was more likely with cognitive and medically complex features, in female participants, in White compared with non-White participants, and when autism had been diagnosed more than 20 years earlier.
^p38958063-predictors

Returnable results appeared across the autism spectrum, and also in parents who had passed a variant on and had neuropsychiatric features without an autism diagnosis; 589 participants (53% of those contacted) received their result.
^p38958063-parents

## Scope as reported

Microarray genotyping and exome sequencing of 21,532 autistic participants and 17,785 parents in a recontactable research cohort without clinical ascertainment. Findings eligible for return were copy-number changes, whole-chromosome aneuploidies and variants in genes firmly linked to autism and intellectual disability, classified by American College of Medical Genetics criteria.
^p38958063-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The yield depends on which variant classes and gene lists were counted and on who joined the cohort. The difference by ancestry may reflect uneven reference data and gene discovery as much as biology, and a returnable variant is not a complete explanation of a person's autism.
^p38958063-caution-yield
