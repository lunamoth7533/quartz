# Heritability and twin studies

## Definition

Heritability is the share of variation in a trait, within a given population, that is associated with genetic differences among its members; it is a population statistic, not a measure of how strongly genes fix any one person's trait. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-heritability|Human genetics]] Twin studies estimate it from how much more alike identical twins are than fraternal twins, while molecular studies estimate the smaller share that measured common variants capture. [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-correlations|Tick 2016]] [[P31594949 Nievergelt 2019 PTSD genome-wide meta-analysis#^p31594949-snp|Nievergelt 2019]]

## How it works

**The twin comparison.** Identical twins share practically all their DNA sequence and fraternal twins on average half of their segregating variants, so a larger identical-twin correlation is credited to genes and any resemblance beyond that to shared environment. [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-caution-logic|Appraisal: Tick 2016]] The autism twin meta-analysis shows the arithmetic: pooled liability correlations were about .98 for identical pairs and .53-.67 for fraternal pairs, giving heritability estimates of 64-91%. [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-correlations|Tick 2016]] [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-estimates|Tick 2016]]

**Estimates depend on inputs.** In the same meta-analysis a shared-environment component appeared only when the assumed prevalence was lowered from 5% to 1%, and the authors traced earlier large shared-environment estimates to over-inclusion of concordant fraternal pairs. [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-estimates|Tick 2016]] [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-artefact|Tick 2016]] A heritability figure is the output of a model, its assumptions and its sample, so it belongs to a design and a population rather than to the trait itself. [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-caution-assumptions|Appraisal: Tick 2016]] [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-limit|Human genetics]]

**What heritability does not mean.** Genes are expressed in interaction with environment and experience, so a genotype is a set of instructions read differently in different conditions rather than a fixed destiny. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-expression|Human genetics]] A high estimate therefore does not show that a trait is unchangeable, that its causes are known, or that it predicts one person's outcome. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-heritability|Human genetics]] [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-caution-prediction|Appraisal: O'Connell 2025]]

**Estimates for the conditions in this library.** For bipolar disorder, the largest genomic study cites heritability of 60-80% while noting that most of its determinants were unknown before that analysis. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-limit|O'Connell 2025]] For ADHD, family, twin and adoption evidence shows familial clustering and a heritability of 74%. [[P29892054 Faraone 2019 Genetics of ADHD#^p29892054-heritability|Faraone 2019]] For autism, twin estimates run from 64% to 91%, and in a population twin sample heritability stayed broadly constant across the range of trait scores. [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-estimates|Tick 2016]] [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-continuum|Tick 2016]] For PTSD, the risk of developing the disorder after trauma is heritable, and common variants capture 5-20% of the variation, with a difference between the sexes. [[P31594949 Nievergelt 2019 PTSD genome-wide meta-analysis#^p31594949-snp|Nievergelt 2019]]

**The missing-heritability gap.** Measured variants explain less than twin studies imply. In ADHD, genome-wide studies attribute about a third of the heritability to many common variants of small effect, and rare copy-number changes account for a further part. [[P29892054 Faraone 2019 Genetics of ADHD#^p29892054-common|Faraone 2019]] [[P29892054 Faraone 2019 Genetics of ADHD#^p29892054-rare|Faraone 2019]] Across complex traits the gap narrowed as samples grew - height variants explained more than a fifth of heritability by 2014 - because individual common-disease variants often carry odds ratios near 1.01. [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-heritability|10 years of GWAS discovery]] [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-polygenic|10 years of GWAS discovery]] The gap compares two estimation methods: SNP-based figures count only what genotyped common variants capture among unrelated people, so they are expected to sit below twin estimates, and the difference is not an environmental share. [[P31594949 Nievergelt 2019 PTSD genome-wide meta-analysis#^p31594949-caution-snp|Appraisal: Nievergelt 2019]] [[P29892054 Faraone 2019 Genetics of ADHD#^p29892054-caution-gap|Appraisal: Faraone 2019]]

**Shared genetics across conditions.** Family and twin evidence suggests partly shared genetic contributions to autism and ADHD, a statement about overlapping architecture rather than one common cause, and genetic factors also contribute to ADHD's comorbidity with other disorders. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-shared|Leitner 2014]] [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-caution-label|Appraisal: Leitner 2014]] [[P29892054 Faraone 2019 Genetics of ADHD#^p29892054-rare|Faraone 2019]]

**Twins as a tool beyond heritability.** The same relatives can test environmental causes: across twin, sibling, adoption and other quasi-experimental designs, the link between childhood maltreatment and later mental health problems fell from d = 0.56 to d = 0.31 once confounding was addressed, and the smaller link held across designs. [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-attenuation|Baldwin 2023]] [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-designs|Baldwin 2023]] Each design removes some confounding under its own assumptions, so agreement strengthens a causal reading without proving it. [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-caution-designs|Appraisal: Baldwin 2023]]

**Reading rule.** Before quoting a heritability, name the design (twin, family or SNP-based), the population and the interval, and read the figure as a statement about variation in that population - never as one person's genetic loading or a forecast. [[F07 OpenStax Human genetics#^f07-heritability|Human genetics]] [[P31594949 Nievergelt 2019 PTSD genome-wide meta-analysis#^p31594949-caution-snp|Appraisal: Nievergelt 2019]] [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-caution-individual|Appraisal: 10 years of GWAS discovery]]

## Evidence and status

Twin and family estimates are consistently substantial for bipolar disorder, ADHD and autism. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-limit|O'Connell 2025]] [[P29892054 Faraone 2019 Genetics of ADHD#^p29892054-heritability|Faraone 2019]] [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-estimates|Tick 2016]] For PTSD only a SNP-based estimate is sourced here, and it cannot be compared directly with the twin figures for the other conditions. [[P31594949 Nievergelt 2019 PTSD genome-wide meta-analysis#^p31594949-caution-snp|Appraisal: Nievergelt 2019]] Any single figure moves with model assumptions and sampling, as the spread of the autism estimates shows. [[P26709141 Tick 2016 Heritability of autism twin meta-analysis#^p26709141-caution-assumptions|Appraisal: Tick 2016]] The gap between twin and molecular estimates is well documented and narrows with sample size, but in ADHD common variants still account for only about a third of the twin figure. [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-heritability|10 years of GWAS discovery]] [[P29892054 Faraone 2019 Genetics of ADHD#^p29892054-common|Faraone 2019]]

## Uncertainties

- How much of the twin-to-SNP gap reflects rare variants, incomplete tagging of causal variants, or twin-model assumptions such as equal environments. [[P29892054 Faraone 2019 Genetics of ADHD#^p29892054-caution-gap|Appraisal: Faraone 2019]]
- Whether PTSD heritability differs by sex and by type of trauma; the SNP-based estimates already differ by sex. [[P31594949 Nievergelt 2019 PTSD genome-wide meta-analysis#^p31594949-snp|Nievergelt 2019]]
- Whether estimates drawn mostly from European-ancestry samples hold in other populations. [[P31594949 Nievergelt 2019 PTSD genome-wide meta-analysis#^p31594949-limit|Nievergelt 2019]] [[P28686856 Visscher 2017 10 years of GWAS discovery#^p28686856-limit|10 years of GWAS discovery]]
- A twin heritability estimate for PTSD, and any estimate specific to complex PTSD, are not yet sourced in this library.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Multi-ancestry genome-wide association study (Nature).** The largest bipolar study found 298 loci in a disorder with heritability of 60-80%, showing discovery growing with sample size while most determinants remain unexplained. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-loci|O'Connell 2025]] [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-limit|O'Connell 2025]]
- **2023 · Meta-analysis of quasi-experimental studies (American Journal of Psychiatry).** Twin, sibling and adoption designs used to test an environmental cause showed that part of a raw association reflected wider genetic and environmental risk. [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-attenuation|Baldwin 2023]] [[P36628513 Baldwin 2023 Childhood maltreatment quasi-experimental meta-analysis#^p36628513-interpretation|Baldwin 2023]]
