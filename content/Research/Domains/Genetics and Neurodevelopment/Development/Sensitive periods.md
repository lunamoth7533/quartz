# Sensitive periods

## Definition

A sensitive (or critical) period is a developmental window during which experience has unusually strong and lasting effects on circuit organisation. Outside the window, the same experience has less structural effect.

## How it works

**Timing across systems.** Different systems mature on different schedules and development is overlapping rather than strictly staged, which is why age of onset and sensitive windows differ across functions, why an intervention at one age works differently at another, and why 'the' sensitive period is a simplification. The schedule is a property of the developing system rather than of the calendar. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]]

**Trigger: how the window opens.** In primary visual cortex, the best-studied case, activity-dependent development is triggered by functional maturation of local inhibitory connections, including a late-developing subset of interneurons. On this inhibitory-trigger account, the window opens when local inhibitory circuits mature enough to sharpen the activity patterns that drive refinement. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]]

**Closure: how it closes.** Structural consolidation of competing inputs involves proteolytic reorganisation of the extracellular matrix that occurs during the critical period, and the window closes when this reorganisation around the refined contacts stabilises the outcome. Both ends are mechanisms with molecular handles, which is why "critical period" names a manipulable biological process rather than an age on a chart. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-consolidation|Critical period plasticity]] [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]]

**Reactivation.** The review argues that reactivating the process could support recovery of function in conditions such as amblyopia, framed explicitly as a research strategy rather than a treatment. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-reactivation|Critical period plasticity]]

**Experience is an input, not a script.** Early experience interacts with genetically guided development, so variation in outcome is the expected case and a missed window does not determine a trajectory in the way the metaphor suggests. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]]

**Reading rule.** State the function, the species, the measured marker and whether the claim is about mechanism or about intervention; the second does not follow from the first without a trial. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-limit|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]]

## Evidence and status

Visual-cortex sensitive periods are firmly established in animals and supported by clinical observation of amblyopia treatment windows; evidence in humans for other functions is more variable and design-dependent. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-limit|Critical period plasticity]] The critical-period machinery was worked out largely in visual cortex of animals, and the review is explicit that reactivation is a proposal; the human developmental literature describes typical development and generalises across samples. Human development certainly shows schedule-dependent learning, but identifying which human windows have which machinery - and whether they can be reopened - requires human measurement that mostly does not yet exist. Extending a visual-cortex window to language, social development or clinical intervention is therefore an inference, and claims that a human sensitive period "closed" at a given age are usually inferences from behaviour, not measurements of the underlying process. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-limit|Critical period plasticity]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-limit|The basics of brain development]]

## Uncertainties

- Claims about 'reopening' sensitive periods in humans are not clinically established and should not be read as intervention guidance.
