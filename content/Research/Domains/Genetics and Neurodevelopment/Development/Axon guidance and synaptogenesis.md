# Axon guidance and synaptogenesis

## Definition

Axon guidance is the process by which a growing axon navigates to its target using molecular cues; synaptogenesis is the formation of synaptic connections once it arrives.

## How it works

**Four conserved families, one combinatorial code.** Growing axons follow chemical gradients and surface cues to their targets, guided by attractive and repulsive cues that conserved families - netrins, Slits, semaphorins and ephrins - carry. Each family includes attractants and repellents, acts at short and long range, and is deployed in gradients and boundaries along the pathway; a growing axon reads the combination through its receptor complement rather than any single cue. [[P12471249 Dickson 2002 Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance#^p12471249-cues|Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance]]

**Specificity from a small vocabulary.** Regulatory mechanisms ensure the right axon responds to the right cue at the right time, which is how a modest number of guidance factors generates complex wiring: combinatorial coding is how a limited molecular vocabulary specifies the brain's wiring complexity. [[P12471249 Dickson 2002 Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance#^p12471249-precision|Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance]] It predicts that disrupting one cue shifts only the axons that depend on it - which is what the disorder-genetics examples show: mapping examples in the genetics-in-neurology literature show how identifying such molecules changed understanding of specific disorders. [[P12471249 Dickson 2002 Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance#^p12471249-cues|Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance]] [[P33706917 Korb 2021 Axon guidance signalling#^p33706917-context|Axon guidance signalling]] [[F117 Neuroscience Online genetics and neuronal disease#^f117-disease|Genetics and Neuronal Disease]]

**The growth cone is the decision-maker.** Guidance cues act by regulating and remodelling cytoskeletal dynamics in the growth cone at the axon's tip, through signalling pathways that remain incompletely understood. [[P12471249 Dickson 2002 Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance#^p12471249-growthcone|Molecular mechanisms of axon guidance]] The sign of a cue - attraction or repulsion - therefore depends on the cone's current receptor set and internal state, not on the molecule alone. Receptor regulation, including trafficking and modification, is a major source of that context sensitivity, which is why the same cue can be interpreted differently in different contexts and can attract at one stage and repel at another. [[P33706917 Korb 2021 Axon guidance signalling#^p33706917-regulation|Axon guidance signalling]] [[P33706917 Korb 2021 Axon guidance signalling#^p33706917-context|Axon guidance signalling]]

**From contact to working synapse.** Once a growth cone reaches its target, the handshake proceeds through recognisable stages in a defined sequence: adhesion stabilises the contact, pre- and postsynaptic specialisations assemble reciprocally, and receptor maturation continues after the junction is functional, so its molecular composition changes during development. That maturation sequence is why an early synapse is not simply a small adult synapse, why developmental electrophysiology shows silent synapses that are anatomically present but not yet functional in the same way, and why developmental findings do not transfer automatically to adults. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-receptors|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]

**Refinement by activity.** Synapses initially form in supernumerary numbers, and those connections are refined by activity-dependent competition and trophic support; the neuromuscular junction illustrates the outcome: fewer inputs per fibre, each stronger. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-competition|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]

**Reading rule.** Distinguish guidance (molecular, developmental) from refinement (activity-dependent) from maintenance (adult); the three have different evidence bases. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-limit|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]

## Evidence and status

Guidance molecules and their receptors are established in genetic and biochemical work, largely in invertebrate and vertebrate model organisms; human relevance is inferred, with disease associations as supporting context.
