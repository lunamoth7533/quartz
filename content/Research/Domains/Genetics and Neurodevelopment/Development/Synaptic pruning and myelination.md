# Synaptic pruning and myelination

## Definition

Synaptic pruning is the elimination of surplus synapses during development; myelination is the progressive wrapping of axons that increases conduction velocity. Together they reshape connectivity rather than adding to it.

## How it works

**Overproduction, then pruning.** Circuits are built with excess synapses and connections that are subsequently eliminated, so removal is a normal developmental process and loss is part of building a circuit. The textbook shorthand 'use it or lose it' is directionally right and mechanistically incomplete. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-pruning|Normal development of brain circuits]]

**Competition and elimination.** At the neuromuscular junction, synapses compete for innervation: activity-dependent competition favours more active inputs, and direct competition favours axons that capture limited trophic support supplied by the target cell. Both mechanisms run at once in most systems, which is why 'activity' alone does not explain which connections survive. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-competition|Synapse formation and elimination]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-limit|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]] The outcome is fewer inputs per muscle fibre but greater complexity and strength in the surviving connections. [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse formation and elimination]]

**Pruning has a cellular executor and a molecular grammar.** In the developing brain, complement-system signalling tags weak synapses and microglial engulfment removes them, while at the neuromuscular junction the same functional outcome - fewer, stronger inputs - is achieved through axonal retraction. The executor differs by preparation; the principle - activity and trophic signals decide what is kept - is shared, which is why pruning is described as a programme rather than decay. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-pruning|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse formation and elimination]] [[Neuroimmune interactions and microglia]]

**Myelination changes the conduction regime.** In some axons glial cells form a myelin sheath that insulates the axon and increases conduction speed, interrupted by the nodes of Ranvier - the cellular mechanism described in the neurobiology domain. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] Faster conduction changes what a circuit can do without changing its wiring diagram: myelination changes the timing of communication between regions rather than simply accelerating everything. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]]

**Myelination is a schedule, not an event.** Myelination continues beyond infancy and past the period when synapse numbers peak, and it varies by tract: different tracts myelinate on different timetables extending into adulthood, with sensory and motor pathways earlier and prefrontal connections later. Because myelin changes conduction timing between regions, the maturing timetable changes which interactions are efficient at which age, and developmental claims about "late prefrontal maturation" are myelination and refinement claims that inherit this schedule's per-region structure. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[White matter and tracts]] More generally, different systems mature on different schedules, which is why behavioural capabilities emerge at different ages. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]]

**Adult myelin is not static.** Myelin is a target of injury in demyelinating disease and a subject of repair research, which is why the developmental account and the clinical accounts share the same cellular vocabulary; the barrier review's unanswered questions also include how interfaces change with age and disease. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-mechanism|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[P32211826 Profaci 2020 Blood-brain barrier#^p32211826-open|The blood-brain barrier]]

**Reading rule.** Distinguish synapse elimination, axon retraction and myelination; they happen on different schedules, involve different cells, and are supported by different preparations - mostly animal work in the developmental case. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-sequence|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-receptors|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]

## Evidence and status

Pruning is established in animal preparations; human evidence comes from imaging proxies for structure and myelin, which are indirect. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-limit|Normal development of brain circuits]]
