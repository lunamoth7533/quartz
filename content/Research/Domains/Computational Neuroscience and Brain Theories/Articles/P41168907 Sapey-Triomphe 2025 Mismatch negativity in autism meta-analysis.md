# Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Mismatch Negativity in Autism: Insights Into Predictive Mechanisms.

**Authors.** Sapey-Triomphe LA, Bouet R, Mattout J, Sonié S, Schmitz C, Lecaignard F (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41168907/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In designs with several deviant types, autistic children and adolescents showed smaller mismatch negativity amplitudes than neurotypical peers (g = 0.25), whereas autistic adults showed larger amplitudes (g = -0.26).
^p41168907-age

Amplitudes were smaller in autistic participants for phoneme deviants (g = 0.41), latency did not differ between groups across designs, and residual heterogeneity remained.
^p41168907-latency

## Scope as reported

Auditory change-detection paradigms; the authors interpret the results within predictive coding, treating mismatch negativity as a prediction-error response, and call for larger samples, better data reporting and computational modelling of learning dynamics.
^p41168907-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Reading mismatch negativity as prediction error is a model-dependent interpretation, and age-by-design interactions in a meta-analysis are between-study contrasts that longitudinal studies would need to confirm before they describe development.
^p41168907-caution-reading
