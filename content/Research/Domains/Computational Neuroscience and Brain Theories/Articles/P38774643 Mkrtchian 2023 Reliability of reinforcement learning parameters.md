# Reliability of Decision-Making and Reinforcement Learning Computational Parameters.

**Authors.** Mkrtchian A, Valton V, Roiser JP (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38774643/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Over two weeks, the reinforcement-learning model's learning rates for reward and for punishment were reliably re-measured (good), its reward and punishment sensitivities less so (fair), while prospect-theory risk and loss aversion showed good and excellent reliability.
^p38774643-reliability

Both models predicted individuals' later choices above chance, and predictions were better when based on a person's own parameters than on other participants' estimates.
^p38774643-prediction

## Scope as reported

Healthy volunteers only, two commonly used tasks and a two-week retest interval; the authors frame the results as support for the translational potential of these parameters.
^p38774643-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Two-week reliability in 50 healthy adults does not establish reliability in clinical groups, across different tasks or over longer intervals, which is what a claim that a parameter is a trait marker would need.
^p38774643-caution-generalise
