# A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Empirical Evidence for the Simple Bayesian Model of Autism.

**Authors.** Cui K, Lin X, Gao R, Jing S, Luo F, Wang J (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40576893/)

**Published.** 2026 in Neuropsychology review (first online 2025-06-27).
**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1007/s11065-025-09672-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11065-025-09672-8) · PMID [40576893](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40576893/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Pooled across 23 studies, autistic participants showed a small-to-moderate effect (g = 0.37) in the direction the simple Bayesian model predicts: broader priors and/or heightened sensory precision relative to non-autistic controls.
^p40576893-effect

Heterogeneity was large and was not explained by prior type, stimulus type, task setting, cognitive domain or participant characteristics, so the authors see only limited support for a universal simple Bayesian model and recommend testing hierarchical Bayesian accounts.
^p40576893-heterogeneity

## Scope as reported

Described by the authors as the first meta-analysis of this model; a random-effects synthesis of the model's core predictions.
^p40576893-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A pooled effect with large unexplained heterogeneity does not show which parameter differs - broader priors and sharper sensory precision make overlapping predictions in many tasks - so the average supports a direction of difference, not a mechanism.
^p40576893-caution-identifiability
