# Reward and punishment learning deficits among bipolar disorder subtypes.

**Authors.** Pouchon A, Vinckier F, Dondé C, Gueguen MC, Polosan M, Bastin J (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37591352/)

**Published.** 2023 in Journal of affective disorders.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2023.08.075](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2023.08.075) · PMID [37591352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37591352/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

On an instrumental task separating reward-seeking from punishment-avoidance learning, both bipolar subtypes learned less from rewards than controls, which computational modelling captured as lower sensitivity to rewards relative to punishments.
^p37591352-reward

Choice randomness was higher in bipolar II than bipolar I, reflecting a tendency of bipolar I participants to do better at punishment avoidance; the authors read the results as consistent with the reward-hyposensitivity account of bipolar disorder.
^p37591352-subtypes

## Scope as reported

Participants were not antipsychotic-naive and were in syndromic remission rather than euthymic by the International Society for Bipolar Disorders definition.
^p37591352-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A single modest case-control sample on treatment cannot separate illness effects from medication or residual symptoms, and a sensitivity parameter estimated on one task is not yet a stable trait marker.
^p37591352-caution-medication
