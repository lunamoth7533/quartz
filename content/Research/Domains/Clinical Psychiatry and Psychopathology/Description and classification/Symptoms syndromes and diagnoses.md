# Symptoms, syndromes and diagnoses

## Definition

A symptom is a reported or observed feature; a syndrome is a characteristic cluster of features that co-occur; a diagnosis is a named category defined by specified criteria and used for communication, statistics and treatment planning.

## How it works

**From feature to category.** Categories are constructed by selecting features, specifying thresholds and adding duration and impairment requirements - decisions that encode judgement, not measurement. A diagnosis is then a decision, made by a specified process in a specified classification, that a person's presentation meets a named cluster. [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-definition|What Are Psychological Disorders?]]

**Dysfunction and context.** The textbook definition of a psychological disorder is a clinically significant disturbance in cognition, emotion regulation or behaviour, tied to dysfunction and distress rather than to difference alone. Defining disorder therefore requires more than statistical unusualness: harm, dysfunction and cultural context are part of the definition, and cultural expectations shape what counts as disordered, which is why unusual behaviour by itself is not disorder and the same feature can be a problem in one setting and not another. [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-definition|What Are Psychological Disorders?]] [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-harm|What Are Psychological Disorders?]] [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-cultural|What Are Psychological Disorders?]]

**What a classification manual actually contains.** ICD-11's clinical descriptions number each category and state essential features, boundaries with neighbouring categories and course qualifiers, written to support reliable clinical identification, so a label is a compressed statement about a pattern rather than a single symptom. The manual defines categories; it is not evidence about mechanisms or treatment effects, and its rules differ from DSM-5-TR in places, so reading a diagnosis means reading the system it came from. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-purpose|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-use|ICD-11 CDDR]]

**What diagnoses are for.** Labels support communication, service planning and research eligibility; they were not designed to describe mechanisms or to guide individual treatment choices by themselves. [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-purpose|HiTOP]]

**Research frameworks deliberately separate description from mechanism.** NIMH's RDoC organises studies around dimensions of functioning rather than existing diagnostic categories, combining units of analysis - genes, molecules, cells, circuits, physiology, behaviour and self-report - with functional domains such as negative valence, positive valence, cognitive systems, social processes, arousal and regulatory systems, and sensorimotor systems. NIMH frames it as an approach for research on mechanisms across conditions, not a diagnostic system for clinical use. [[F121 NIMH RDoC#^f121-purpose|RDoC]] [[F121 NIMH RDoC#^f121-matrix|RDoC]] [[F122 NIMH RDoC constructs#^f122-domains|RDoC constructs]] [[F121 NIMH RDoC#^f121-scope|RDoC]]

**Reading rule.** For any claim in this domain, separate (a) the descriptive features, (b) the category label and the system that defines it, and (c) any mechanistic or treatment inference. Classifications exist to make communication and research possible; they are instruments, and their boundaries are decisions that can be revised.

## Evidence and status

Diagnostic reliability varies by category and setting, and categorical boundaries are known to be imperfect; that is a documented property of the systems rather than a reason to discard them. [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]] HiTOP argues that categorical systems suffer from arbitrary thresholds, unclear boundaries between disorders, frequent co-occurrence, within-disorder heterogeneity and diagnostic instability, and proposes grouping covarying syndromes into dimensional spectra as a research effort for measurement and aetiology. The critique is about the structure of categories, not about whether people experience impairment.
