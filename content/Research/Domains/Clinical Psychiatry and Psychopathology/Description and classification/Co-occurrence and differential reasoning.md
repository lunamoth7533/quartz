# Co-occurrence and differential reasoning

**Definition.** Conditions that are defined by symptom patterns overlap more often than chance, so assessment has to reason about which pattern fits while holding more than one possibility open. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-definition|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

## Supported claims

- ADHD and autism frequently co-occur, sharing some phenotypic features while remaining distinct diagnostic categories. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-definition|P24808851]]
- The earlier diagnostic system made autism an exclusion for ADHD; the revision allows a comorbid diagnosis, which changed both research and clinical practice. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-history|P24808851]]
- Registry data cited in the review associate co-occurrence with lower quality of life and poorer adaptive functioning than either condition alone. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-frequency|P24808851]]
- Family and twin evidence suggests partly shared familial and genetic contributions between the two conditions. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-shared|P24808851]]
- Current ICD-11 and DSM-5 definitions are symptom-based; trauma type or duration alone does not determine a trauma-related diagnosis. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-definition|VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions]]
- Defining functional impairment remains an open question in adult ADHD, which matters because impairment is part of how the condition is assessed. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base]]
- Co-occurrence is a group-level, definition-dependent observation and does not establish that one condition causes another. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-caution-label|Appraisal: P24808851]]

## How it works

**Frequency.** Conditions co-occur far more often than chance, both within families such as mood and anxiety disorders and across neurodevelopmental conditions: ADHD and autism frequently co-occur, sharing some features while remaining distinct diagnostic categories. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-definition|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]] [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-frequency|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

**Classification history.** Diagnostic systems sometimes prohibit combinations that clinical reality contains. The earlier diagnostic system excluded a dual diagnosis of ADHD with autism that the DSM revision permits - a documented example of a change in rules that changed the measured frequency without changing anyone. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-history|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

**Why co-occurrence happens.** Overlapping criteria, shared risk factors, referral effects and genuine shared mechanisms all produce co-occurrence, and distinguishing them requires design rather than inspection. For autism and ADHD, family and twin evidence suggests partly shared familial and genetic contributions. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-shared|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

**What co-occurrence is associated with.** Registry data associate co-occurrence with lower quality of life and poorer adaptive functioning, and co-occurring presentations show more severe social and adaptive difficulties and may fit single-condition treatments less well. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-frequency|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]] [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-clinical|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

**The structural explanation.** Categorical taxonomies suffer from arbitrary thresholds, unclear boundaries, frequent co-occurrence, within-disorder heterogeneity and diagnostic instability, and dimensional alternatives organise covarying syndromes into spectra with comorbidity represented as overlap. The network approach goes further, treating comorbidity as overlap between symptom networks. Both are research programmes rather than installed clinical systems. [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]] [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-structure|HiTOP]] [[P28127906 Borsboom 2017 Network theory#^p28127906-consequences|Network theory]] [[P28127906 Borsboom 2017 Network theory#^p28127906-status|Network theory]]

**What assessment should do.** The task is not to decide which single label applies but to characterise which features are present, which are primary, and what they imply for support. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-clinical|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]] When overlap is expected, differential assessment covers several domains - neurodevelopmental, mental, physical, communication and sensory - and a single diagnosis may not capture the presentation. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-differential|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]]

**Caution.** Co-occurrence is a definition-dependent, group-level observation; it does not mean one condition causes the other. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-caution-label|Appraisal: Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

**Reading rule.** State the definitions and instruments used, whether the presentations were assessed separately, and whether the claim is about a population or a person. [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-definition|Measurement invariance]] [[P27913917 Network meta-analysis introduction#^p27913917-assumption|Network meta-analysis introduction]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Comorbidity is simultaneously a clinical-psychiatry reasoning problem and a genetics-neurodevelopment question: if conditions share familial and genetic contributions, the same data can be read through either lens, and the taxonomy's structural problems described in the clinical domain affect what a co-occurrence finding means. [[Bipolar genetics and polygenic risk]] [[Autism genetics and rare variants]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Overlap statistics depend on the sample, the informant and the diagnostic instrument, and they describe groups rather than any individual's presentation.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Cohort study (Military medicine).** In combat-deployed veterans, ADHD was about twice as common in those with current PTSD, yet ADHD self-report measures agreed poorly with each other and with prior diagnosis, an overlap that makes co-occurrence hard to separate from misattribution. [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-ptsd|Knight 2025]] [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-measures|Knight 2025]] [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Knight 2025]]
- **2024 · Systematic review (Personality disorders).** Most studies comparing complex PTSD with borderline personality disorder found distinct profiles despite shared affect, self and relationship features, and people with both conditions reported earlier and more frequent interpersonal trauma and more impairment. [[P38753372 Atkinson 2024 CPTSD versus borderline personality disorder#^p38753372-distinct|Atkinson 2024]] [[P38753372 Atkinson 2024 CPTSD versus borderline personality disorder#^p38753372-comorbid|Atkinson 2024]] Group-level distinctness does not settle how one person's presentation is labelled. [[P38753372 Atkinson 2024 CPTSD versus borderline personality disorder#^p38753372-caution-distinct|Appraisal: Atkinson 2024]]
