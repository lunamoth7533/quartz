# Anxiety and fear constructs

## Definition

Fear is a response to immediate threat; anxiety is anticipation of future threat. Both are adaptive in proportion, and anxiety disorders are defined by excessive or persistent fear and worry that interferes with functioning. [[F81 NIMH Anxiety disorders#^f81-family|Anxiety Disorders]] [[F81 NIMH Anxiety disorders#^f81-features|Anxiety Disorders]]

## How it works

**Fear and anxiety are distinguished constructively.** Fear is the response to a detectable threat present now; anxiety is the anticipatory state when threat is uncertain or distant. The distinction organises research programmes - fear conditioning models fear, uncertainty and avoidance models anxiety - and it organises clinical description, because disorders differ in which of the two dominates and what triggers it. [[F82 NIMH Obsessive-compulsive disorder#^f82-cycle|Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder]] [[Fear learning and extinction]]

**The family.** Anxiety disorders are presented as a group that includes generalised anxiety, panic disorder and several phobia and fear-related patterns. Their features are persistent, excessive worry or fear that interferes with daily life, distinct from ordinary situational anxiety; treatment approaches described include psychotherapy, medication and combinations. [[F81 NIMH Anxiety disorders#^f81-family|Anxiety Disorders]] [[F81 NIMH Anxiety disorders#^f81-features|Anxiety Disorders]] [[F81 NIMH Anxiety disorders#^f81-treatment|Anxiety Disorders]] [[F81 NIMH Anxiety disorders#^f81-limit|Anxiety Disorders]]

**Learning account.** Classical conditioning pairs a neutral cue with a meaningful stimulus so that the cue comes to evoke the response on its own - which is how neutral cues acquire threat value - and extinction reduces the conditioned response when the cue is repeatedly presented without the outcome. [[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-pairing|Classical Conditioning]] [[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-extinction|Classical Conditioning]]

**Return of fear.** The extinguished response can return through spontaneous recovery, reinstatement or renewal - context change, reinstatement or the passage of time can overcome extinction learning. That return is the evidence that extinction is new learning competing with the original association rather than erasure; it is the laboratory basis for exposure-based treatment, and it is why relapse after successful exposure is expected in a proportion of cases. [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-newlearning|Fear extinction]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-model|Fear extinction]]

**Avoidance maintains what it reduces.** Avoidance reduces anxiety in the moment, which reinforces the avoidance, prevents disconfirming experience, and thereby preserves the threat belief - the maintenance logic that exposure-based treatments reverse by blocking avoidance and allowing new learning. That single mechanism diagram connects conditioning theory to the treatment literature and explains why reassurance-seeking belongs in the same family as escape behaviour. [[Learning and conditioning]] [[Therapy models overview]]

**The neural basis, with its limits.** Amygdala circuits support threat learning, with cortical and hippocampal contributions to context and regulation; the structure is described through connections with cortical, thalamic, hypothalamic and brain-stem structures, stimulation and lesion evidence, and conditioning paradigms. The evidence comes largely from animal conditioning work and clinical case observation, so it constrains what a human mechanism could look like without describing any individual's anxiety, and translation from consolidation and reconsolidation findings into better treatment has been incomplete. [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-fear|Amygdala]] [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-connections|Amygdala]] [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-limit|Amygdala]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-translation|Fear extinction]]

**Boundaries with other constructs.** Obsessive-compulsive presentations involve anxiety but are classified separately, which illustrates how categories are organised by pattern rather than by shared emotion. Classification places these patterns in named categories with essential features and boundaries, and co-occurrence with depression and other conditions is common rather than exceptional, which is why differential reasoning matters more than category purity. [[F82 NIMH Obsessive-compulsive disorder#^f82-pattern|Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder]] [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]]

**Reading rule.** Separate the descriptive category, the learning model, and the treatment evidence: the model explains why exposure could work, trials describe average effects for named populations, and neither says what will happen for one person. [[F81 NIMH Anxiety disorders#^f81-treatment|Anxiety Disorders]]

## Evidence and status

Conditioning and extinction mechanisms are well established in animals and healthy humans; treatment effects are established in randomised trials with varying effect sizes by condition and comparator.
