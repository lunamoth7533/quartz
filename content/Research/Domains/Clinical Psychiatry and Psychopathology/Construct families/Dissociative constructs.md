# Dissociative constructs

## Definition

Dissociative disorders involve disruption of normally integrated functions of consciousness, memory, identity or perception. Depersonalisation and derealisation are the symptoms most used to identify the trauma-related form. [[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-group|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]]

## How it works

**The group of disorders.** Dissociative disorders are described as a group in which normally integrated functions of consciousness, memory, identity or perception are disrupted. The professional overview stresses the differential from trauma- and stressor-related presentations and from neurological and substance-related causes, because dissociative symptoms are non-specific. [[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-group|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]] [[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-differential|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]]

**The trauma-related subtype.** A dissociative subtype of PTSD was proposed around symptoms of depersonalisation and derealisation, supported by latent class analyses in veteran and civilian samples. The review that supported this argued it from antecedent, concurrent and predictive evidence - the standard basis for claiming a subtype is clinically meaningful - and argued that the distinction may matter for treatment. [[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-definition|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]] [[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-validators|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]] [[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-implication|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]]

**Where the subtype sits in the classification.** The ICD-11 clinical descriptions define complex PTSD through three self-organization domains additional to core PTSD, and ICD-11 differs from DSM-5-TR in places. The dissociative subtype is a PTSD construct rather than an ICD-11 complex PTSD component: the DSO domains come from the ICD-11 criteria, and the editorial introducing the diagnosis states the rationale rather than the criteria. Keeping the two separate prevents a PTSD-subtype finding from being relabelled as a complex-PTSD mechanism. [[P32345416 Cloitre 2020 ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial#^p32345416-aim|ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial]] [[P32345416 Cloitre 2020 ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial#^p32345416-caution-domains|Appraisal: ICD-11 complex PTSD editorial]] [[P23047775 Pitman 2012 Biological studies of PTSD#^p23047775-caution-cptsd|Appraisal: Biological studies of PTSD]]

**Assessment depends on the instrument.** Instruments exist, but self-report of dissociation correlates modestly with interview measures, and both can be influenced by the context of assessment. The International Trauma Questionnaire was developed as a self-report measure of ICD-11 PTSD and complex PTSD, with items selected through item response theory in trauma-exposed community and clinical samples, and its optimised 12-item version showed a latent structure consistent with prior findings in those samples. [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-development|International Trauma Questionnaire]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-structure|International Trauma Questionnaire]] Its authors frame the instrument as an evolving standard with sample-dependent properties: performance in a new population must be established rather than assumed. Any statement about how common a dissociative or complex presentation is therefore has to name the instrument and the sample. [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-limits|International Trauma Questionnaire]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-limit|International Trauma Questionnaire]]

**Reading rule.** Treat 'dissociation' as a set of specified symptom constructs measured by a named instrument in a named sample; the category, the subtype and the mechanism are three different claims, and this literature supports them to different degrees. [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-practice|Measurement invariance]]

## Evidence and status

The dissociative subtype has empirical support and is recognised in classification. [[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-implication|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]] Subtype validity claims remain group-level and evolving: the supporting review acknowledged that evidence on differential treatment response was still developing. [[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-limit|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]]

Functional-imaging findings are described as an active research area; they describe group patterns and are not diagnostic tests. [[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-biology|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]] [[F85 MSD Manual Dissociative disorders#^f85-limit|Overview of Dissociative Disorders]]

The boundaries between dissociative presentations and other trauma-related patterns are debated. Where the boundary between PTSD and complex PTSD should fall remains sensitive to measurement development, and the review of the ICD-11 proposals found general support for the proposed structures while flagging that complex PTSD identifies a group with more multiple and sustained traumas and greater functional impairment. Classification claims here should name the instrument and the system used. [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-structure|ICD-11 PTSD proposals review]] [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-impairment|ICD-11 PTSD proposals review]] [[P29029837 Brewin 2017 ICD-11 PTSD proposals review#^p29029837-caution-boundary|Appraisal: ICD-11 PTSD proposals review]]

## Recent research

- **2024 · Systematic review (Journal of trauma & dissociation).** Studies of ICD-11 complex PTSD used twelve different dissociation measures, and the association between complex PTSD and dissociation was moderate to strong but inconsistently reported. [[P38112306 Hamer 2024 Dissociative symptoms in CPTSD#^p38112306-measures|Hamer 2024]] [[P38112306 Hamer 2024 Dissociative symptoms in CPTSD#^p38112306-relationship|Hamer 2024]] A summary across different instruments does not place dissociation inside the definition. [[P38112306 Hamer 2024 Dissociative symptoms in CPTSD#^p38112306-caution-measure|Appraisal: Hamer 2024]]
- **2024 · Latent class analysis of a national survey (Journal of trauma & dissociation).** In a nationally representative sample, a complex PTSD class without dissociation (26.5%) sat alongside a complex PTSD plus dissociation class (10.0%) with the poorest mental health and functioning, so complex PTSD symptoms can occur without dissociation while the combination marks greater severity. [[P37401797 Hyland 2024 Dissociation and ICD-11 CPTSD#^p37401797-classes|Hyland 2024]] [[P37401797 Hyland 2024 Dissociation and ICD-11 CPTSD#^p37401797-independent|Hyland 2024]] [[P37401797 Hyland 2024 Dissociation and ICD-11 CPTSD#^p37401797-severity|Hyland 2024]] Latent classes are model-based profiles, not prevalence estimates. [[P37401797 Hyland 2024 Dissociation and ICD-11 CPTSD#^p37401797-caution-lca|Appraisal: Hyland 2024]]
