# Mood disorders

## Definition

Mood disorders include depressive disorders and bipolar and related disorders. They are distinguished by the presence of manic or hypomanic episodes and by the pattern of episodes over time.

## How it works

**Depressive constructs.** NIMH describes depression as a common but serious mood disorder that can affect how a person feels, thinks and handles daily activity. [[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-scale|Depression]] It distinguishes major depressive episodes from persistent depressive disorder and from bipolar depression because treatment implications differ. [[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-patterns|Depression]]

**Depressive episodes and disorders are distinguished by course and impairment.** A depressive syndrome - low mood or loss of interest with cognitive and vegetative features - becomes a disorder category through duration, count of features and functional impact, and through exclusion of bereavement-like, substance-related and medical precipitations. The threshold structure is a convention for a continuum, which is why dimensional severity measures sit alongside categorical diagnosis in research and increasingly in care. [[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-patterns|Depression]] [[Categorical versus dimensional classification]]

**Bipolar constructs.** Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania; depressive episodes are common but not required. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]] The clinical picture combines episodes with a lifetime course, so acute and long-term considerations differ. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar disorder]]

**Episode versus disorder.** ICD-11 gives separate descriptions for depressive, manic, mixed and hypomanic episodes before the disorder categories, so an episode label describes a period of illness while the disorder label describes the pattern over time - episode and disorder are different levels of description. Course qualifiers are part of the description, which is how a classification expresses recurrence and persistence without changing the category itself. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]] Episode counting, inter-episode functioning and recurrence risk are central to the description, which is why longitudinal data matter more here than in many other areas.

**The bipolar boundary is the highest-stakes differential.** Depressive presentations that are actually part of bipolar disorder change the treatment implications substantially, which is why history-taking for past elevated or irritable periods is built into mood-disorder assessment, and why the bipolar condition hub exists as its own evidence domain rather than a subsection here. Misclassification in this boundary is the classic costly error of the mood-disorder literature. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]] [[Bipolar depression and mixed features]]

**Overlap.** Mood and anxiety symptoms frequently co-occur, and some features - disturbed sleep, irritability, difficulty concentrating - appear in several categories, which is part of why differential reasoning is difficult. [[F81 NIMH Anxiety disorders#^f81-features|Anxiety Disorders]]

**Research frameworks and the mood family.** RDoC places mood-relevant function in named domains - negative valence and positive valence systems among others - and treats them as dimensions to be studied across conditions rather than as diagnosis-specific mechanisms. That framing makes the boundary question a research design question: a study can sample by category or by dimension, and the choice changes what its results mean. [[F122 NIMH RDoC constructs#^f122-domains|RDoC constructs]] [[F121 NIMH RDoC#^f121-purpose|RDoC]]

**Reading rule.** For any mood-disorder claim, name the episode or the disorder, the phase being treated, and the classification system. The practical distinctions - unipolar versus bipolar depression, mania versus hypomania, mixed features - exist because they change what the evidence can be applied to. [[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-limit|Depression]] [[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-research|Depression]]

## Evidence and status

Episode definitions are reliably applied in research settings; boundaries between categories and the role of subthreshold presentations remain contested.

**Treatment evidence is phase-specific, which is why the boundaries are clinical rather than biological.** The mood disorders share low or elevated mood as a central feature, but their treatment evidence is phase-specific and does not pool: the CANMAT and ISBD guidance translates efficacy, safety and tolerability evidence into separate first-, second- and third-line recommendations for different phases of illness. That structure reflects how the evidence was generated, not only how the categories are drawn. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]
