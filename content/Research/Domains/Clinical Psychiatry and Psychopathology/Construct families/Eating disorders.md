# Eating disorders

## Definition

Eating disorders are defined by disturbed eating behaviour; the main ones are anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge-eating disorder and avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder (ARFID), with a combined lifetime prevalence of 2% to 5% that is higher in females. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-scope|Attia 2025]] They carry medical as well as psychiatric risk, and anorexia nervosa brings nearly six times the mortality of people the same age. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-harms|Attia 2025]]

## How it works

**One family, four disorders.** Current descriptions group ARFID with anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder as the main eating disorders. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-scope|Attia 2025]] Binge eating is shared by bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder and is the target of their behavioural and medication treatments, whereas no medication works for anorexia nervosa. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-treatment|Attia 2025]] Evidence on ARFID is younger and thinner than for the other three. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-caution-average|Appraisal: Attia 2025]]

**The body carries much of the risk.** Complications include electrolyte disturbance, slow heart rate, hormonal change and bone loss, which makes an eating disorder a medical condition as well as a psychiatric one. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-harms|Attia 2025]] Depression and suicide attempts are more common than in people without eating disorders, and a quarter of deaths in anorexia nervosa are by suicide. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-harms|Attia 2025]]

**Treatment at class level.** Nutritional support, psychotherapy and medication are first-line; family-based treatment helps young people with anorexia nervosa, behavioural therapy helps bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder, and some medications reduce binge episodes. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-treatment|Attia 2025]] These are average effects in trial populations, not guidance for any individual. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-caution-average|Appraisal: Attia 2025]]

**Overlap with ADHD.** People with ADHD have higher odds of any eating disorder than controls (pooled odds ratio 3.82), with similarly raised odds for anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder. [[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-risk|Nazar 2016]] The association runs the other way too, with higher odds of ADHD in people with eating disorders (2.57), strongest in binge-eating samples. [[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-reverse|Nazar 2016]] Associations were stronger when ADHD was diagnosed by clinical interview than by self-report. [[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-method|Nazar 2016]] Odds ratios from mostly cross-sectional studies show that the conditions travel together, not which comes first or why. [[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-caution-direction|Appraisal: Nazar 2016]]

**Overlap with autism.** Feeding or eating disorders are among the most frequent co-occurring conditions in autistic people. [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-frequent|Micai 2023]] People with anorexia nervosa score well above healthy controls on measures of autistic traits (Hedges g 0.88), and higher traits go with more severe eating-disorder symptoms, though only modestly (r 0.28). [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-traits|Inal-Kaleli 2025]] [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-severity|Inal-Kaleli 2025]] About 29% of children and adults with anorexia nervosa score above the autism cut-off on the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule, a positive result on an observational assessment rather than a diagnostic prevalence. [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-ados|Inal-Kaleli 2025]] [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-limit|Inal-Kaleli 2025]] Starvation can itself bring rigidity and social withdrawal, so traits measured during acute illness may overstate lasting autistic features. [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-caution-starvation|Appraisal: Inal-Kaleli 2025]]

**Bodily signals as a research thread.** Interoception research sometimes pools eating disorders with anxiety and depression: one preregistered imaging study of such a mixed patient group found an asymmetric pattern of insula activity when people attended to or anticipated bodily sensations. [[P39535878 Adamic 2024 Insula interoception across psychiatric disorders#^p39535878-limit|Adamic 2024]] [[P39535878 Adamic 2024 Insula interoception across psychiatric disorders#^p39535878-asymmetry|Adamic 2024]] Group activation maps give no individual readout and do not carry over to other conditions without their own studies. [[P39535878 Adamic 2024 Insula interoception across psychiatric disorders#^p39535878-caution-scope|Appraisal: Adamic 2024]]

**Reading rule.** For any overlap figure, check how the second condition was established (interview, questionnaire or observation schedule), whether the person was acutely ill when assessed, and whether the design can show direction; keep treatment claims to the disorder and population the trials studied. [[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-method|Nazar 2016]] [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-caution-starvation|Appraisal: Inal-Kaleli 2025]] [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-caution-average|Appraisal: Attia 2025]]

## Evidence and status

The overview figures come from a 2025 clinical review that summarises trials and meta-analyses rather than new data. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-limit|Attia 2025]] The ADHD overlap rests on a meta-analysis of studies in both directions whose between-study differences meta-regression could not explain, and the autism-anorexia overlap on a meta-analysis of 22 studies. [[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-method|Nazar 2016]] [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-limit|Inal-Kaleli 2025]] Neither literature can yet say whether having both conditions changes course or treatment response. [[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-limit|Nazar 2016]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether autistic traits measured in anorexia nervosa persist after recovery of weight or partly reflect starvation. [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-caution-starvation|Appraisal: Inal-Kaleli 2025]]
- Why ADHD and eating disorders travel together; shared traits such as impulsivity are plausible but untested explanations. [[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-caution-direction|Appraisal: Nazar 2016]]
- The overlap between ARFID and autism is not quantified in this library yet; the sourced autism figure covers feeding and eating disorders together. [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-frequent|Micai 2023]]
- How course and treatment response change when autism or ADHD accompanies an eating disorder. [[P27859581 Nazar 2016 Eating disorders comorbid with ADHD#^p27859581-limit|Nazar 2016]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Clinical review (JAMA).** Places ARFID among the main eating disorders, reports anorexia nervosa's excess mortality with a quarter of its deaths by suicide, and notes that no medication works for anorexia nervosa. [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-scope|Attia 2025]] [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-harms|Attia 2025]] [[P40048192 Attia 2025 Eating disorders review#^p40048192-treatment|Attia 2025]]
- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (International Journal of Eating Disorders).** Quantified autistic traits in anorexia nervosa and found about 29% above an observational autism cut-off, sharpening the question of how to assess autism during acute illness. [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-traits|Inal-Kaleli 2025]] [[P39530423 Inal-Kaleli 2025 Autistic traits in anorexia nervosa#^p39530423-ados|Inal-Kaleli 2025]]
- **2023 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews).** Placed feeding or eating disorders among the most frequent co-occurring conditions in autism across about 590,000 participants. [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-frequent|Micai 2023]]
