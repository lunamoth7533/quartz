# Substance-related constructs

## Definition

Substance-related constructs describe patterns of use that produce harm or impaired control. Terms such as dependence and addiction are defined in classification systems, and their criteria differ between them.

## How it works

**The reward framing and its scale.** Addictive drugs can flood reward circuitry with dopamine, driving it much more strongly or faster than natural rewards. That is the starting point of the neurobiological account, and the reason the same learning machinery that supports adaptive behaviour can be recruited by a substance. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-reward|Drugs and the Brain]]

**Adaptation over repeated exposure.** Repeated exposure changes circuits involved in reward, stress and self-control, which is why use can become compulsive. The shift is not a single receptor event: the same overview names reward, stress and control systems together, which is a coordinated-change account rather than a chemical-imbalance claim. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-adaptation|Drugs and the Brain]]

**Habit and control.** Compulsive use resembles the shift from goal-directed to habitual control described in learning research, which is one bridge between behavioural and neural accounts. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal ganglia]]

**Reinforcement learning formalises the learning part.** Reinforcement learning supplies the formal account of how outcomes shape choice: value functions and policies describe how an agent learns from reward signals to choose actions, and exploration-exploitation trade-offs are explicit parts of the framework. [[F123 Sutton and Barto Reinforcement Learning#^f123-framework|Reinforcement Learning]] [[F123 Sutton and Barto Reinforcement Learning#^f123-exploration|Reinforcement Learning]] The dopamine signals that carry prediction errors are also heterogeneous - additional salience and movement signals, different populations with different targets - so 'reward' is a useful approximation rather than a complete description. [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-scope|Reward prediction error]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-heterogeneity|What does dopamine mean?]]

**Variation is the rule.** Response varies with the drug, the route, the amount, genetics and environment, so no single pathway explains all substance-use problems. That statement rules out both a moralised account and a strictly pharmacological one, and it is why this library stays away from individualised predictions. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-variation|Drugs and the Brain]] [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-limit|Drugs and the Brain]] Drugs of abuse converge on reward circuitry while producing dependence through receptor-level adaptation, but because response varies, a single-substance-pathway story predicts neither which exposures become disorders nor which treatments work for whom: the honest mechanism layer is a set of biasing processes, not a destiny. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-variation|Drugs and the Brain]] [[Motivation and reward]]

**The constructs separate use, intoxication, dependence and use disorder.** Use is behaviour; intoxication is a state; the disorder category combines impaired control, social impairment, risky use and pharmacological indicators (tolerance, withdrawal) with severity graded by count. The separation matters because the four travel together imperfectly: dependence pharmacology can exist without social impairment, and problematic use without dependence pharmacology - so screening and treatment planning track the specific constructs, not a single substance-use quantity. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-adaptation|Drugs and the Brain]] [[Receptor adaptation tolerance and dependence|Receptor adaptation, tolerance and dependence]]

**Classification and co-occurrence.** Substance-related presentations are classified as named categories alongside other mental and behavioural disorders, and they co-occur with anxiety, depression and other presentations at rates that make differential reasoning routine rather than exceptional. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-use|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]]

**Reading rule.** Distinguish the acute pharmacology, the learned behaviour, the broader adaptation, and the social and environmental conditions. This note contains no treatment guidance, and the reward account explains plausibility rather than any person's trajectory. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-limit|Drugs and the Brain]]

## Evidence and status

Reward and adaptation mechanisms are supported by animal and human studies; treatment effects vary substantially by substance and by outcome, and relapse is common.
