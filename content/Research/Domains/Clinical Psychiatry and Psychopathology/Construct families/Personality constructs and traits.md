# Personality constructs and traits

## Definition

Personality constructs describe enduring patterns of thinking, feeling and behaving. Personality disorders are defined as inflexible patterns that deviate from expectations and cause distress or impairment. [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|Borderline Personality Disorder]]

## How it works

**Trait approaches are dimensional.** Historical perspectives on personality are traced from temperament ideas through trait and psychodynamic traditions. Trait models describe personality through a few broad dimensions derived from covariation among descriptors rather than through types, which is why modern work uses continuous scores and profiles rather than categories. [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-historical|Personality]] [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-traits|What Is Personality?]]

**Stability is a matter of degree and age.** Enduring disagreements about consistency across situations - how much behaviour is stable across contexts - remain live, and they are the same question raised by dimensional and contextual accounts of psychopathology. Trait rank order stabilises through adulthood, but mean levels shift (maturity trends), and specific patterns respond to environment and to treatment; the picture is neither fixed essence nor unlimited plasticity. That calibrated middle is what the measurement literature supports, and it is what distinguishes personality description from character verdicts. [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-debate|What Is Personality?]] [[Personality models]]

**Temperament, character and disorder are different cuts.** Temperament describes early-appearing dispositions; character describes organised patterns of traits and coping; personality disorder describes inflexible, pervasive patterns causing distress or impairment. The three sit on a developmental and severity gradient rather than being three kinds of thing, and the gradient framing is why adolescent personality pathology is now described with dimensions and provisional patterns rather than fixed categories. [[F45 OpenStax Psychology 2e personality#^f45-traits|What Is Personality?]] [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|Borderline Personality Disorder]]

**Where categorical language persists clinically.** Borderline personality disorder involves long-standing instability in mood, self-image and relationships, with marked sensitivity to rejection and abandonment; features often emerge in adolescence or early adulthood, and diagnosis is possible when criteria are met. [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|Borderline Personality Disorder]] [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-onset|Borderline Personality Disorder]] Evidence-based psychotherapies are described as the primary approach, with medication sometimes used for specific symptoms rather than for the disorder itself. [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-treatment|Borderline Personality Disorder]] That is a categorical clinical label attached to a dimensional underlying construct, which is exactly the hybrid the classification debate describes. [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-limit|Borderline Personality Disorder]]

**Why dimensions keep winning arguments about thresholds.** Research increasingly measures personality pathology dimensionally, mirroring the wider classification debate. [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-structure|HiTOP]] The HiTOP critique - arbitrary thresholds, unclear boundaries, frequent co-occurrence, within-disorder heterogeneity and diagnostic instability - applies with full force to personality pathology, where dimensional models are a mainstream research direction rather than a fringe proposal. [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-problem|HiTOP]] [[P28333488 Kotov 2017 HiTOP#^p28333488-purpose|HiTOP]]

**Measurement before comparison.** Comparing trait or personality-pathology scores across groups or time assumes invariance, and absent invariance changes what a difference means; content validity and reliability are separate properties from predictive usefulness, and invariance plus content validity remain prerequisites for any comparison. [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-definition|Measurement invariance]] [[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-caution-score|AI appraisal: Developing and validating scales]]

**Reading rule.** A trait score is a description of position in a distribution, not a verdict, and the harm-and-dysfunction requirement means unusual behaviour alone is not disorder. Cultural expectations shape what counts as disordered, so criteria are not culture-free. [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-cultural|Psychological Disorders]] [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-harm|Psychological Disorders]]

## Evidence and status

Trait dimensions replicate; personality disorder categories have weaker reliability, and the shift toward dimensional assessment is well advanced in research and in progress in practice.
