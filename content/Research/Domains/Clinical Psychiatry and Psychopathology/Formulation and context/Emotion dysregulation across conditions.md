# Emotion dysregulation across conditions

## Definition

Emotion dysregulation is difficulty in shaping which emotions arise, how strong they become and how they are expressed and recovered from, to a degree that disrupts what a person is trying to do; the process model behind the term is described in [[Emotion regulation]]. It is reported in ADHD, bipolar disorder, autism and complex PTSD, and the one meta-analysis that compares conditions directly reads it as a transdiagnostic continuum rather than a feature owned by a single diagnosis. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-continuum|De Prisco 2023]]

## How it works

**ADHD: a candidate core feature.** Adults with ADHD use non-adaptive regulation strategies more often than people without ADHD symptoms, and their dysregulation tracks symptom severity, executive functioning and co-occurring conditions. [[P36608036 Soler-Gutierrez 2023 Emotion dysregulation in adult ADHD#^p36608036-strategies|Soler-Gutiérrez 2023]] [[P36608036 Soler-Gutierrez 2023 Emotion dysregulation in adult ADHD#^p36608036-correlates|Soler-Gutiérrez 2023]] Whether it belongs to the core of adult ADHD is still listed as an open question, and co-occurrence with dysregulation does not settle it. [[P40948064 Cortese 2025 ADHD in adults evidence base#^p40948064-open|ADHD in adults: evidence base]] [[P36608036 Soler-Gutierrez 2023 Emotion dysregulation in adult ADHD#^p36608036-caution-core|Appraisal: Soler-Gutiérrez 2023]]

**Bipolar disorder: close to depression, with its own edges.** Across comparative studies, people with bipolar disorder resembled people with major depression on most regulation strategies, differing mainly in positive rumination and risk-taking. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-mdd|De Prisco 2023]] Within a large bipolar cohort, affect intensity and affective lability measured as dimensions were associated with markers of more severe illness, including rapid cycling and suicide attempts. [[P40715078 Etain 2025 Polygenic scores and bipolar dimensions#^p40715078-lability|Etain 2025]] Neither source reports bipolar I separately. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-limit|De Prisco 2023]] [[P40715078 Etain 2025 Polygenic scores and bipolar dimensions#^p40715078-limit|Etain 2025]]

**Autism: raised, with autism-specific predictors.** Dysregulation is higher in autistic adults than in nonclinical controls but milder than in borderline personality disorder; sensory sensitivity and camouflaging predicted it specifically in the autistic group. [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-levels|Bemmouna 2023]] [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-specific|Bemmouna 2023]]

**Complex PTSD: written into the diagnosis.** Affect dysregulation is one of the three disturbances in self-organisation that ICD-11 adds to core PTSD, and it is measured as a symptom cluster of the International Trauma Questionnaire. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-dso|Complex PTSD: history and definitions]] [[P30178492 Cloitre 2018 International Trauma Questionnaire#^p30178492-development|International Trauma Questionnaire]] In the phase-comparison meta-analysis, phase-based, multi-phase and exposure interventions improved this cluster more than other designs. [[P41949043 Lee 2026 Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy#^p41949043-outcomes|Phase-based versus non-phase-based trauma therapy]]

**Dysregulation is not a mood episode.** Bipolar I is defined by the occurrence of mania, with depressive episodes common, so the diagnosis rests on sustained episodes with their own criteria. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]] Dysregulation describes how emotions rise and settle within any state, so it is a different object from an episode, and a regulation score taken during an episode partly measures the episode. Mood state at testing is the first thing to check in any bipolar comparison. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-caution-state|Appraisal: De Prisco 2023]]

**Shared and distinct.** Some predictors look shared: alexithymia, emotional vulnerability and borderline traits predicted dysregulation in autistic, borderline and control groups alike, and in bipolar disorder the ADHD polygenic score related to adult affective lability through childhood ADHD symptoms. [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-shared|Bemmouna 2023]] [[P40715078 Etain 2025 Polygenic scores and bipolar dimensions#^p40715078-adhd|Etain 2025]] Others look distinct: borderline personality disorder carries more dysregulation and more maladaptive strategies than bipolar disorder, and autism adds sensory and camouflaging predictors. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-bpd|De Prisco 2023]] [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-specific|Bemmouna 2023]] Borderline traits overlap with dysregulation by definition, so their predictive strength is partly circular. [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Bemmouna 2023]]

**Reading rule.** Name the condition, the instrument and the mood state behind a dysregulation claim: a higher score in one diagnosis is a group difference on a shared dimension, not evidence of a separate mechanism, and most of the evidence here is self-report, whose limits are set out in [[Emotion regulation]]. [[P36608036 Soler-Gutierrez 2023 Emotion dysregulation in adult ADHD#^p36608036-caution-core|Appraisal: Soler-Gutiérrez 2023]]

## Evidence and status

The strongest comparative evidence is one meta-analysis that could pool only the comparisons of bipolar disorder with major depression and with borderline personality disorder; comparisons with ADHD, anxiety disorders, schizophrenia and PTSD were too sparse. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-continuum|De Prisco 2023]] The adult ADHD evidence is a narrative synthesis of scarce, heterogeneous studies, and the autism and bipolar-dimension findings come from single cross-sectional samples, one with unverified self-reported diagnoses. [[P36608036 Soler-Gutierrez 2023 Emotion dysregulation in adult ADHD#^p36608036-limit|Soler-Gutiérrez 2023]] [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-limit|Bemmouna 2023]] [[P40715078 Etain 2025 Polygenic scores and bipolar dimensions#^p40715078-limit|Etain 2025]] Shared-mechanism claims therefore rest on correlation and statistical mediation, which fit a developmental pathway without demonstrating one. [[P40715078 Etain 2025 Polygenic scores and bipolar dimensions#^p40715078-caution-mediation|Appraisal: Etain 2025]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether dysregulation is a core feature of adult ADHD or a correlate of severity and co-occurring conditions.
- How much of the lability measured in bipolar disorder reflects residual mood symptoms rather than a stable trait.
- Whether the affect dysregulation of complex PTSD is a trauma-specific process or the same dimension seen in other conditions.
- Whether similar scores across diagnoses reflect shared mechanisms or shared questionnaires.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Genetic association study (Translational psychiatry).** Adds a developmental route to the bipolar picture: ADHD genetic liability related to adult affective lability through childhood ADHD symptoms, and lability related to more severe bipolar illness. [[P40715078 Etain 2025 Polygenic scores and bipolar dimensions#^p40715078-adhd|Etain 2025]] [[P40715078 Etain 2025 Polygenic scores and bipolar dimensions#^p40715078-lability|Etain 2025]]
- **2023 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Psychological medicine).** The first pooled comparison of regulation in bipolar disorder with other conditions, placing bipolar disorder near major depression and below borderline personality disorder on a shared continuum. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-mdd|De Prisco 2023]] [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-bpd|De Prisco 2023]]
- **2023 · Cross-sectional comparison (Molecular autism).** Separates what autistic adults share with other groups (alexithymia, emotional vulnerability) from what looks specific (sensory sensitivity, camouflaging). [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-shared|Bemmouna 2023]] [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-specific|Bemmouna 2023]]

## Connections

- [[Emotion regulation]] - the general process model (what is regulated, when, by which strategy) that this note applies across diagnoses; its point that self-report, physiology and behaviour diverge applies to every score here.
- [[CPTSD and disturbances in self-organization]] - where dysregulation is written into a diagnosis: affect dysregulation is one of the three self-organisation clusters of ICD-11 complex PTSD.
- [[Bipolar depression and mixed features]] - the episode-level states that dysregulation has to be told apart from; mixed features are episodes, whereas dysregulation describes reactivity within any mood state.
- [[ADHD across the lifespan]] - where emotional dysregulation appears as an open question about the adult ADHD construct.
- [[Categorical versus dimensional classification]] - the argument this note illustrates: a dimension that runs across categories and is described by degree rather than by diagnosis.
- [[Masking and camouflaging]] - camouflaging is an autism-specific predictor of dysregulation, linking the effort of self-presentation to emotional cost.
- [[Trauma and PTSD in autistic and ADHD people]] - where overlap between dysregulation, neurodevelopmental features and trauma reactions complicates assessment.
- [[Co-occurrence and differential reasoning]] - the general method; shared dysregulation is one reason ADHD, bipolar disorder, borderline personality disorder and complex PTSD are hard to tell apart.
- [[Borderline personality disorder]] - the diagnosis in which dysregulation is most central, used as the comparison group in autism studies and the main differential for bipolar II and complex PTSD.
- [[Dialectical behaviour therapy]] - the psychotherapy built around emotional escalation and self-harm, whose effect on suicide attempts in bipolar adolescents ran partly through reduced dysregulation.
- [[Rumination and repetitive negative thinking]] - the regulation strategy most tied to both depressive and (hypo)manic symptoms in bipolar disorder, and to intrusive re-experiencing in PTSD.
