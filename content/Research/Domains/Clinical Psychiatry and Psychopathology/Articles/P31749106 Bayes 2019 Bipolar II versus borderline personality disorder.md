## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Biological comparisons, including genetics, epigenetics, daily rhythms and brain imaging, show suggestive differences that are not yet good enough to guide diagnosis.
^p31749106-biology

The more useful discriminators are family history, developmental antecedents, illness course, how mood states are experienced, personality style and relationship factors.
^p31749106-discriminators

Impulsivity, neuropsychological profile, sex distribution, co-occurring conditions and treatment response separate the two conditions less well.
^p31749106-weak

## Scope as reported

A narrative review of recent studies; the authors call for studies that compare bipolar II specifically, rather than bipolar disorder in general, with borderline personality disorder.
^p31749106-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The discriminators are clinical patterns reported across studies, not validated decision rules with known accuracy; because the two conditions can also co-occur, choosing between them is not always the right question.
^p31749106-caution-rules
