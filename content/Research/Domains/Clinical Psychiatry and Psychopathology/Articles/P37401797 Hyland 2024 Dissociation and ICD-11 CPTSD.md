# Is Dissociation a Fundamental Component of ICD-11 Complex Posttraumatic Stress Disorder?

**Authors.** Hyland P, Hamer R, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37401797/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of trauma & dissociation : the official journal of the International Society for the Study of Dissociation (ISSD) (volume 25, issue 1, pages 45-61) (first online 2023-07-04).
**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1080/15299732.2023.2231928](https://doi.org/10.1080/15299732.2023.2231928) · PMID [37401797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37401797/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Latent class analysis found four classes: low symptoms (48.9%), PTSD (14.7%), complex PTSD (26.5%) and complex PTSD with dissociation (10.0%).
^p37401797-classes

Complex PTSD symptoms occurred without dissociation in a sizeable class, indicating that they can exist independently of dissociative experience.
^p37401797-independent

The complex PTSD with dissociation class had the poorest mental health and the greatest functional impairment, and class membership was related to childhood emotional and physical neglect.
^p37401797-severity

## Scope as reported

Single nationally representative self-report survey; classes were derived statistically.
^p37401797-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Latent classes are model-based summaries of self-report profiles, not diagnoses, so the class sizes should not be read as prevalence estimates.
^p37401797-caution-lca
