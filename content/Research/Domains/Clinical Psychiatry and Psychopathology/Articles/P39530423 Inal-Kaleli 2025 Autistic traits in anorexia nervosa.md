# Investigating the Presence of Autistic Traits and Prevalence of Autism Spectrum Disorder Symptoms in Anorexia Nervosa: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

**Authors.** Inal-Kaleli I, Dogan N, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39530423/)

**Published.** 2025 in The International journal of eating disorders (first online 2024-11-12).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/eat.24307](https://doi.org/10.1002/eat.24307) · PMID [39530423](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39530423/) · PMCID [PMC11784827](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11784827/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

People with anorexia nervosa scored well above healthy controls on measures of autistic traits (Hedges g 0.88).
^p39530423-traits

Higher autistic traits went with more severe eating-disorder symptoms, though the correlation was modest (r 0.28).
^p39530423-severity

About 29% of children and adults with anorexia nervosa scored above the autism cut-off on the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule.
^p39530423-ados

## Scope as reported

Searches of three databases in August 2023, updated in April 2024; the 29% figure is a positive result on an observational assessment, not a diagnostic prevalence.
^p39530423-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Starvation can itself bring rigidity, social withdrawal and reduced flexibility, so traits measured during acute illness may overstate lasting autistic features; cross-sectional data cannot separate the two.
^p39530423-caution-starvation
