# Ten-year course of borderline personality disorder: psychopathology and function from the Collaborative Longitudinal Personality Disorders study.

**Authors.** Gunderson JG, Stout RL, et al. (13 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21464343/)

**Published.** 2011 in Archives of general psychiatry (first online 2011-04-04).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2011.37](https://doi.org/10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2011.37) · PMID [21464343](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21464343/) · PMCID [PMC3158489](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3158489/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Over ten years 85% of participants with borderline personality disorder remitted, more slowly than those with major depression and a little more slowly than those with other personality disorders; 12% relapsed, less often and later than in either comparison group.
^p21464343-remission

All criteria declined at similar rates, but social functioning remained severely impaired, improved only modestly and stayed worse than in both comparison groups; falls in criteria predicted later gains in global functioning.
^p21464343-function

## Scope as reported

Treatment-seeking patients from 19 hospital and outpatient settings in four US cities, followed with repeated standardised interviews for DSM-IV personality disorders, major depression and social functioning.
^p21464343-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Remission here means dropping below the diagnostic threshold, not recovery: a person can stop meeting criteria while work and relationships remain difficult, so remission rates on their own overstate how well people are doing.
^p21464343-caution-remission
