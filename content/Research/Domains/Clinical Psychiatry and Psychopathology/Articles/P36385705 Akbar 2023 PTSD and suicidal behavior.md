# Posttraumatic stress disorder and risk of suicidal behavior: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Akbar R, Arya V, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36385705/)

**Published.** 2023 in Suicide & life-threatening behavior (first online 2022-11-16).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/sltb.12931](https://doi.org/10.1111/sltb.12931) · PMID [36385705](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36385705/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

A PTSD diagnosis was associated with about twice the risk of death by suicide (relative risk 2.09), and about four times the risk among combat veterans.
^p36385705-suicide

PTSD was also strongly associated with suicide attempts (relative risk 4.05) and suicidal ideation (2.91), consistently so in people with other psychiatric conditions and in non-clinical cohorts.
^p36385705-attempts

For suicide death, the pooled relative risk among people with PTSD and co-occurring psychiatric conditions was lower (0.74).
^p36385705-comorbid

## Scope as reported

Stratified meta-analyses with meta-regression; the suicide-death estimate rests on seven studies and has a wide confidence interval (1.11-3.94).
^p36385705-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

These are PTSD samples weighted towards veterans, not ICD-11 complex PTSD samples; the comparison group changes what a relative risk means, and transfer to complex PTSD or to trauma-exposed autistic and ADHD people needs its own studies.
^p36385705-caution-transfer
