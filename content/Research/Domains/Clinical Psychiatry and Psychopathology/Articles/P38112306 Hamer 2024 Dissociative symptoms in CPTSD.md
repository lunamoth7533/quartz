# Dissociative Symptoms in Complex Posttraumatic Stress Disorder: A Systematic Review.

**Authors.** Hamer R, Bestel N, Mackelprang JL · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38112306/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of trauma & dissociation : the official journal of the International Society for the Study of Dissociation (ISSD) (volume 25, issue 2, pages 232-247) (first online 2023-12-19).
**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1080/15299732.2023.2293785](https://doi.org/10.1080/15299732.2023.2293785) · PMID [38112306](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38112306/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Complex PTSD was most often measured with a version of the International Trauma Questionnaire, whereas twelve different instruments were used for dissociation, most commonly the Dissociative Symptoms Scale and the Dissociative Experiences Scale.
^p38112306-measures

Complex PTSD and dissociative symptoms were linked with moderate-to-strong associations, though studies reported them inconsistently.
^p38112306-relationship

The authors call for work on which dissociation measures suit complex PTSD research.
^p38112306-need

## Scope as reported

Systematic review of four databases searched in March 2021, synthesised narratively.
^p38112306-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

With twelve dissociation instruments across seventeen studies, a moderate-to-strong association summarises different constructs; it does not show that dissociation belongs inside the complex PTSD definition.
^p38112306-caution-measure
