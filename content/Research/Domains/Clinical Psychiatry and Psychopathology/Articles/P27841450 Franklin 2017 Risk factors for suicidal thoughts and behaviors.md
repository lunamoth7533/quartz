# Risk factors for suicidal thoughts and behaviors: A meta-analysis of 50 years of research.

**Authors.** Franklin JC, Ribeiro JD, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27841450/)

**Published.** 2017 in Psychological bulletin (first online 2016-11-14).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1037/bul0000084](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000084) · PMID [27841450](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27841450/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across odds-ratio, hazard-ratio and diagnostic-accuracy analyses, risk factors predicted suicidal thoughts, attempts and deaths only slightly better than chance, and no broad category or subcategory did much better.
^p27841450-prediction

Predictive ability had not improved over 50 years, and longer follow-up periods did not predict better.
^p27841450-stagnation

Studies rarely combined risk factors, and five broad categories accounted for close to 80% of all risk-factor tests.
^p27841450-homogeneity

## Scope as reported

A random-effects meta-analysis of longitudinal studies predicting a specific suicidal outcome; the authors note that the narrow methods of past research limited which theories could be tested, and they argue for moving towards algorithms that combine many factors.
^p27841450-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Poor prediction follows partly from low base rates: even a factor that multiplies risk several-fold flags far more people who will not attempt suicide than people who will, so risk factors can direct attention and services without sorting individuals into safe and unsafe groups.
^p27841450-caution-base-rate
