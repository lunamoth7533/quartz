# Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder compared to other mental illnesses: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** De Prisco M, Oliva V, et al. (13 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37842774/)

**Published.** 2023 in Psychological medicine (volume 53, issue 16, pages 7484-7503).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1017/S003329172300243X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S003329172300243X) · PMID [37842774](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37842774/) · PMCID [PMC10951413](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10951413/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

People with bipolar disorder did not differ from those with major depression on most adaptive and maladaptive regulation strategies, apart from modest group differences in dwelling on positive mood and in risk-taking.
^p37842774-mdd

People with borderline personality disorder showed more emotion dysregulation overall and more maladaptive strategies than people with bipolar disorder.
^p37842774-bpd

Data were too sparse for meta-analytic comparison with ADHD, anxiety, schizophrenia or PTSD, and the authors interpret emotion dysregulation as a transdiagnostic continuum.
^p37842774-continuum

## Scope as reported

Searches to April 2022 across four databases, limited to studies using validated instruments; the abstract does not separate bipolar subtypes.
^p37842774-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Emotion regulation reported by people with bipolar disorder depends on current mood state, so trait comparisons need euthymic samples, which the abstract does not describe; the pooled groups are bipolar disorder in general rather than bipolar I specifically.
^p37842774-caution-state
