# The prevalence of traumatic experiences and PTSD according to DSM-5 and ICD-11 in the German general population.

**Authors.** Pettrich A, Nesterko Y, Glaesmer H · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40859920/)

**Published.** 2025 in Epidemiology and psychiatric sciences (volume 34, pages e44).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1017/S2045796025100164](https://doi.org/10.1017/S2045796025100164) · PMID [40859920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40859920/) · PMCID [PMC12450535](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12450535/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

47.2% reported at least one lifetime traumatic event, most often transportation accidents and life-threatening injuries.
^p40859920-exposure

Probable PTSD was 4.7% under both the DSM-5 and the ICD-11 algorithms, and 2.6% with a conservative cut-off.
^p40859920-prevalence

The two algorithms agreed substantially but not perfectly (kappa 0.62), with only moderate agreement on re-experiencing (kappa 0.57), so the systems identified partly different cases.
^p40859920-agreement

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional survey from 2016 in which PTSD was estimated from the PCL-5 using DSM-5 and ICD-11 algorithms and cut-off scores.
^p40859920-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

ICD-11 caseness is approximated here from a DSM-5 checklist rather than an ICD-11 instrument, and self-organisation symptoms were not measured, so the comparison concerns PTSD definitions only.
^p40859920-caution-approximation
