# The biosocial correlates and predictors of emotion dysregulation in autistic adults compared to borderline personality disorder and nonclinical controls.

**Authors.** Bemmouna D, Lagzouli A, Weiner L · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38110995/)

**Published.** 2023 in Molecular autism (volume 14, issue 1, pages 47).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1186/s13229-023-00580-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13229-023-00580-3) · PMID [38110995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38110995/) · PMCID [PMC10726572](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10726572/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Emotion dysregulation and its behavioural correlates, such as self-harm, were higher in autistic adults than in controls but milder than in borderline personality disorder.
^p38110995-levels

Borderline traits, emotional vulnerability and alexithymia strongly predicted emotion dysregulation in all groups.
^p38110995-shared

Sensory sensitivity and autistic camouflaging were specific predictors in the autistic group; autistic women reported more risk factors, including sexual abuse and camouflaging, without higher dysregulation scores.
^p38110995-specific

## Scope as reported

Diagnoses were self-reported and not verified, and the cross-sectional design cannot establish direction or causality, as the authors state.
^p38110995-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Using borderline traits to predict emotion dysregulation risks circularity, because dysregulation is part of the borderline construct; the autism-specific predictors are the more informative result.
^p38110995-caution-overlap
