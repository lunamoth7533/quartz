## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Eating disorders, defined by disturbed eating behaviour, have a lifetime prevalence of 2% to 5%, higher in females; the main ones are anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder and binge-eating disorder.
^p40048192-scope

Complications include electrolyte disturbance, slow heart rate, hormonal change and bone loss; depression and suicide attempts are commoner, and anorexia nervosa brings nearly six times peers' mortality, a quarter of deaths by suicide.
^p40048192-harms

Nutritional support, psychotherapy and medication are first-line; family-based treatment helps youth with anorexia nervosa, behavioural therapy helps bulimia and binge eating, some medications cut binge episodes, and none works for anorexia nervosa.
^p40048192-treatment

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Average effects in trial populations describe what treatments do for groups, not what will help a particular person; the evidence on avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder is younger and thinner than for the other three disorders.
