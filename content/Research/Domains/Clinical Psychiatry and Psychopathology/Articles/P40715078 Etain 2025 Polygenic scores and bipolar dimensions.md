# Polygenic risk scores for severe psychiatric disorders in bipolar disorders: associations with the clinical and dimensional expression, interactions with childhood maltreatment and mediation models.

**Authors.** Etain B, Lajnef M, et al. (20 named authors; the PubMed record also lists 1 unnamed entry) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40715078/)

**Published.** 2025 in Translational psychiatry (volume 15, issue 1, pages 256).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41398-025-03466-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-025-03466-5) · PMID [40715078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40715078/) · PMCID [PMC12297605](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12297605/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

No polygenic score was associated with the clinical expression of bipolar disorder in terms of onset, course or comorbidity.
^p40715078-prs

The ADHD polygenic score correlated with childhood maltreatment, childhood ADHD symptoms and adult affective lability, impulsivity and hostility, with the links to adult dimensions mediated by childhood ADHD symptoms.
^p40715078-adhd

Affect intensity and affective lability were associated with markers of more severe illness, including rapid cycling, suicide attempts and anxiety disorders.
^p40715078-lability

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional analysis of an 852-person bipolar cohort adjusted for age, sex, bipolar type and depressive symptoms; no polygenic score moderated the effects of maltreatment.
^p40715078-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Mediation estimated from cross-sectional data with retrospective childhood measures is compatible with a developmental pathway but does not demonstrate one, and polygenic scores are population-level measures rather than predictors for an individual.
^p40715078-caution-mediation
