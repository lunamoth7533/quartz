# The risk of eating disorders comorbid with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Nazar BP, Bernardes C, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27859581/)

**Published.** 2016 in The International journal of eating disorders (first online 2016-11-15).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/eat.22643](https://doi.org/10.1002/eat.22643) · PMID [27859581](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27859581/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

People with ADHD had higher odds of any eating disorder than controls (pooled odds ratio 3.82), with similarly raised odds for anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder.
^p27859581-risk

People with eating disorders also had higher odds of ADHD (2.57), and the link was strongest in binge-eating samples.
^p27859581-reverse

Associations were stronger when ADHD was diagnosed by clinical interview than by self-report, and meta-regression did not explain the differences between studies.
^p27859581-method

## Scope as reported

A PRISMA-based meta-analysis of studies in both directions; the authors call for work on whether having both conditions changes prognosis, course or treatment response.
^p27859581-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Odds ratios from mostly cross-sectional studies show that the two conditions travel together, not which comes first or why; shared traits such as impulsivity are plausible explanations that the meta-analysis could not test.
^p27859581-caution-direction
