# MRI versus EEG

**Definition.** Structural MRI images anatomy with high spatial resolution while EEG records summed electrical activity with high temporal resolution. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

## Supported claims

- The overview treats imaging and EEG as different modalities measuring different things. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]
- Conventional structural MRI shows anatomy; functional MRI measures activity-related signal change rather than electrical activity itself. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-caution-modality|AI synthesis: Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]
- Structural findings such as group cortical thickness differences describe anatomy, not circuit dynamics. [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

## How it works

**Complementary physics.** Structural MRI reconstructs anatomy and tissue properties from nuclear magnetic resonance signals - the magnetic properties of tissue - with millimetre spatial resolution but resolution-limited time; functional MRI tracks blood flow and oxygenation as a haemodynamic proxy for activity. EEG records the electric fields generated by synchronised neuronal activity - summed electrical activity - with millisecond timing but limited spatial precision, because of volume conduction and the inverse problem. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-mri|Brain imaging]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-fmri|Brain imaging]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-eeg|Brain imaging]]

**What each can answer.** Structural questions (where is the lesion, what tissue is it) go to MRI, which is suited to structure and slow functional change; temporal questions (when does the abnormal activity occur, how does it evolve during a seizure) go to EEG, which is suited to timing and to oscillatory activity - which is why epilepsy evaluation relies on electrophysiology. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-management|Epilepsy]] [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]]

**Why the two are combined in practice.** Combining them is how questions about structure-function relationships are addressed in clinical practice. Epilepsy care is the worked example: MRI looks for the structural lesion, EEG times and characterises the abnormal discharges, and neither alone answers both questions. The combination pattern generalises - space and time are different currencies, and most clinical and research questions need an explicit statement of which currency the claim is made in. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-management|Epilepsy]] [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]]

**Neither measures neurons directly.** MRI contrast reflects tissue properties; EEG reflects summed fields. Claims that a technique shows 'brain activity' should specify the signal's physical basis and its inference distance to neuronal firing. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-caution-modality|AI synthesis: Brain imaging]] That distance differs in kind, not just degree: MRI contrast is one step removed from tissue properties; EEG is several steps from single-neuron firing, through summed post-synaptic currents, volume conduction through tissue and skull, and an inverse problem with no unique solution. Both distances are manageable when named; both produce over-reading when unnamed. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-caution-modality|AI appraisal: Brain imaging]]

**Comparability and analysis dependence.** Because the modalities measure different quantities, agreement is informative and disagreement is not automatically an error. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-caution-modality|AI appraisal: Brain imaging]] Both require preprocessing choices that affect results, and reproducibility in imaging research is a documented concern. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]]

**Costs, settings and patient factors are selection criteria too.** EEG is portable, quiet, contraindication-free and long-recordable; it tolerates movement better and can be used in young children and in prolonged monitoring, but its spatial ambiguity limits localisation claims. MRI is expensive, loud and slow, and excludes or constrains some implanted devices and severe claustrophobia; CT trades resolution for speed and availability in acute settings. A modality-choice claim that omits these practical dimensions is incomplete for clinical purposes, because the best-answerable-question test runs through feasibility as well as through physics, and those population-level constraints are part of the answer to "which test should be used", not a footnote to it. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

**Reading rule.** Match the modality to the question and state the inference; a mismatch produces a finding that cannot bear the interpretation placed on it. [[Structural versus functional measures]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** The research-methods domain treats this as an operationalisation problem: a modality measures an operationalisation of a construct, and modality disagreement is often a disagreement between operationalisations rather than an error. [[Measurement validity and reliability]] [[Structural versus functional measures]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Spatial and temporal resolution trade off, so choosing a modality commits you to the kind of question you can answer.
