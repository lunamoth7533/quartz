# Tic disorders and Tourette syndrome

## Definition

Tourette syndrome, called Tourette disorder in DSM-5-TR, is a common neurodevelopmental condition affecting up to 1% of people, defined by several motor and vocal tics that begin in childhood and classed as a hyperkinetic movement disorder. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-definition|Shekhawat 2026]] Treatment guidance addresses Tourette syndrome and chronic tic disorders together. [[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-limit|Pringsheim 2019]]

## How it works

**A hyperkinetic disorder read through circuits.** The basal ganglia modulate cortical output through direct and indirect pathways rather than generating movement, and basal ganglia dysfunction is the classic substrate of movement disorders, so hyperkinetic phenomena such as tics are interpreted through these circuits. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal ganglia]] [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-disorders|Basal ganglia]] [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Motor disorders]] The pathophysiology and genetic basis of Tourette syndrome itself remain incompletely understood, and the textbook pathway model has been refined since, so it is a starting frame rather than an explanation of tics. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-limit|Shekhawat 2026]] [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-limit|Basal ganglia]]

**Course.** Tics begin in childhood and the later course varies: they may remit by adulthood while associated symptoms persist, many people continue to have tics as adults, and some get worse. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-definition|Shekhawat 2026]] [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-course|Shekhawat 2026]]

**Co-occurrence with ADHD and OCD.** Tourette syndrome frequently co-occurs with ADHD, OCD, anxiety disorders, autism, sleep disorders and oppositional defiant disorder. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-cooccurrence|Shekhawat 2026]] In 1,374 people with Tourette syndrome interviewed for lifetime diagnoses, 85.7% had at least one comorbid psychiatric disorder, 57.7% had two or more, and 72.1% met criteria for OCD or ADHD, with mood, anxiety and disruptive behaviour disorders each near 30%. [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-prevalence|Hirschtritt 2015]] [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-ocdadhd|Hirschtritt 2015]] Most comorbid disorders began between ages 4 and 10. [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-age|Hirschtritt 2015]] The high rate of mood disorders was accounted for by comorbid OCD, and genetic correlations with mood, anxiety and disruptive disorders may run through ADHD and OCD rather than through Tourette syndrome itself. [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-mediation|Hirschtritt 2015]] A sample assembled through family and genetic research is probably more affected than people with tics in the community, so the headline figure describes that sample. [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-caution-sample|Appraisal: Hirschtritt 2015]]

**Treatment at class level.** Mild tics often need no treatment; therapy becomes relevant when tics cause pain, injury, marked distress or disruption of school, work or social life. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-treatment|Shekhawat 2026]] A practice guideline sets out the classes of option - watchful waiting, a behavioural therapy (the Comprehensive Behavioral Intervention for Tics), medication, and deep brain stimulation for adults whose severe tics have not responded to other treatment - with choices shared among patient, caregiver and clinician and weighed against benefits, harms and co-occurring conditions. [[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-options|Pringsheim 2019]] [[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-dbs|Pringsheim 2019]] Its recommendations also cover counselling on the natural history of tics, psychoeducation for teachers and peers, assessment for co-occurring disorders and periodic review of whether treatment is still needed. [[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-recommendations|Pringsheim 2019]] Those options describe what may be offered at population level; they are not a ranking of medicines or a plan for an individual. [[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-caution-scope|Appraisal: Pringsheim 2019]]

**Sudden-onset presentations.** During the COVID-19 pandemic, tic presentations rose sharply, most among adolescent girls. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-pandemic|Shekhawat 2026]] Because this departs from the childhood onset that defines Tourette syndrome, such presentations need their own diagnostic reasoning rather than being pooled with it. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-caution-pandemic|Appraisal: Shekhawat 2026]]

**Reading rule.** Before attributing an outcome to tics rather than to ADHD, OCD or another co-occurring condition, state the age at onset, which co-occurring conditions were assessed and how, and whether the sample came from a clinic, a genetic study or the community. [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-mediation|Hirschtritt 2015]] [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-caution-sample|Appraisal: Hirschtritt 2015]]

## Evidence and status

The clinical picture and course come from a reference chapter rather than from cohort data sourced here. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-limit|Shekhawat 2026]] Co-occurrence rates rest on a large structured-interview study of a research sample. [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-limit|Hirschtritt 2015]] [[P25671412 Hirschtritt 2015 Psychiatric comorbidity in Tourette syndrome#^p25671412-prevalence|Hirschtritt 2015]] Treatment options are framed by a guideline built on a systematic review and a structured panel process; its abstract names the classes of option but not their comparative effect sizes. [[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-limit|Pringsheim 2019]] [[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-options|Pringsheim 2019]]

## Uncertainties

- How tics arise in basal ganglia circuits, and which genetic variants contribute, remain incompletely understood. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-limit|Shekhawat 2026]]
- The comparative effect sizes of behavioural therapy and medication classes are not given in the guideline abstract sourced here. [[P31061208 Pringsheim 2019 Guideline on treatment of tics#^p31061208-options|Pringsheim 2019]]
- How often tics persist into adulthood is described in words, not estimated, in the sources here. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-course|Shekhawat 2026]]
- Whether sudden-onset tic-like presentations in adolescents belong with functional disorders or with primary tic disorders is not settled by these sources. [[P29763133 Shekhawat 2026 Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders#^p29763133-caution-pandemic|Appraisal: Shekhawat 2026]]
