# Seizures and epilepsy

## Definition

A seizure is an episode of abnormal excessive brain activity. Epilepsy, per the ILAE 2014 practical definition, is a disease of the brain defined by any of: at least two unprovoked (or reflex) seizures more than 24 hours apart; one unprovoked (or reflex) seizure with a recurrence risk over the next 10 years similar to the general two-seizure recurrence risk (at least 60%); or a diagnosis of an epilepsy syndrome. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-definition|Epilepsy]] [[F130 Fisher 2014 ILAE epilepsy definition#^f130-criteria|Fisher 2014 ILAE]]

## How it works

**An event, a predisposition and a syndrome are different levels.** A seizure is an event, whereas epilepsy is described as a brain disorder characterised by an enduring predisposition to generate epileptic seizures, which the ILAE 2014 practical definition operationalises through the three routes set out in the definition. A first unprovoked seizure can satisfy that definition in specified high-risk circumstances - a documented ≥60% ten-year recurrence risk - and a syndrome diagnosis is another route, so a single seizure with low recurrence risk is not the same clinical problem as epilepsy, and classification and assessment cannot be reduced to counting events. [[F130 Fisher 2014 ILAE epilepsy definition#^f130-criteria|ILAE practical definition]]

**Causes and triggers.** Causes include head injury, stroke, infection, genetic factors and metabolic disturbance - a heterogeneous list, and the same seizure type can arise from different causes, which is precisely why assessment looks for a cause rather than assuming one. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-triggers|Epilepsy]]

**The excitation-inhibition link is real and specific.** Regional excitability is central: seizures are disruptions of the balance between excitatory and inhibitory signalling, which is why excitation-inhibition ideas appear in both basic and clinical accounts, and many antiseizure drugs act by enhancing inhibition (potentiating GABA) or reducing excitation (blocking sodium channels or glutamate receptors) - the clinical territory where the basic E/I framework earns its keep. The caveat from the autism literature travels here: balance language is meaningful at the circuit level with named mechanisms, not as a general explanation of any condition. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-inhibitory|Amino acid transmitters]] [[Excitation and inhibition balance]]

**The diagnostic pathway is staged.** First-seizure assessment involves specialist referral, history, examination and investigations that include EEG and imaging where indicated, and national guidance covers these referral and diagnostic pathways. An acute symptomatic seizure prompts investigation of the provoking condition, while longer-term decisions also depend on the underlying disease, recurrence risk and the person's circumstances. The organising concept is syndrome: the same event pattern in different syndromes implies different treatment logic, so the guideline organises assessment and treatment by seizure type and syndrome rather than prescribing one response to every event. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]] [[F130 Fisher 2014 ILAE epilepsy definition#^f130-criteria|ILAE practical definition]]

**EEG's role and its limits.** EEG measures the brain's electrical activity through scalp electrodes, reporting frequency and amplitude with millisecond timing; its spatial precision is limited compared with structural imaging, but EEG can contribute to localising epileptiform activity and seizure onset in clinical evaluation, particularly when combined with video monitoring or invasive recording. It is a functional rather than structural measure, which is why it complements rather than replaces imaging. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-eeg|Brain Imaging]] [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological diagnostic tests]]

**Treatment.** Antiseizure medication is chosen by seizure type and syndrome: national guidance recommends specific antiseizure medicines accordingly and sets out when surgery or other interventions are considered, and it lists research recommendations where evidence is insufficient. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-treatment|Epilepsies NG217]] [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-research|Epilepsies NG217]] The consumer overview likewise describes medication first for many people, with surgery, devices and dietary approaches used in specific situations, and links out rather than advising individuals. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-management|Epilepsy]]

**Overlap with psychiatric practice.** A medication history matters when neurological and psychiatric care overlap. General interaction categories explain why one treatment can alter another's exposure or effects; whether a particular drug changes seizure risk requires its specific evidence and prescribing information. A class label alone does not establish that risk or an individual's response. [[F19 FDA Drug interactions what you should know#^f19-types|Drug interaction categories]] [[Drug classes and mechanisms overview]]

**Reading rule.** Name the event type, the syndrome if known, the investigations performed and the treatment phase being discussed. Seizure classification drives drug choice in the guidance, so a claim that names no seizure type has lost the detail that makes it actionable. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

## Evidence and status

Seizure classification and drug efficacy by seizure type are established; prognosis after a first seizure is variable and depends on cause and findings. This note is reference material, not guidance for any individual.
