# Traumatic brain injury

## Definition

Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is damage to the brain caused by external mechanical force - an impact, rapid acceleration and deceleration, or penetration - and closed injuries such as concussion, contusions and intracranial haematomas are the most common form. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-definition|Ginsburg 2025]] Trauma is one of the mechanism classes, alongside vascular, infectious, genetic and degenerative causes, that neurological reasoning uses to interpret a deficit. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-causes|Overview of the neurological exam]]

## How it works

**Severity is graded at the time of injury.** TBI is classed as mild, moderate or severe: mild injury, or concussion, means loss of consciousness under 30 minutes, post-traumatic amnesia under 24 hours, or any altered consciousness, while Glasgow Coma Scale scores of 3-8 mark severe and 9-12 moderate injury. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-severity|Ginsburg 2025]] Mild injuries make up more than 90% of cases. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-severity|Ginsburg 2025]] The grades describe the acute injury, not the outcome, so a mild grade does not rule out lasting difficulties. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-caution-grading|Appraisal: Ginsburg 2025]]

**Persisting symptoms.** Long-term risks include persisting headache, dizziness, poor concentration and depression, and repeated injuries can accumulate into permanent damage, including chronic traumatic encephalopathy. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-persisting|Ginsburg 2025]] Later difficulties may reflect pre-injury problems, the injury itself, or both, which is why attribution needs information from before the injury. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-caution-grading|Appraisal: Ginsburg 2025]] [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-caution-direction|Appraisal: Asarnow 2021]]

**Investigation.** Neurological assessment combines history and examination with CT, MRI, EEG and laboratory tests, and each modality measures something different. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures]]

**Overlap with PTSD.** In civilian studies at low risk of bias, PTSD after TBI ranged from 2.6% to 36% and pooled to 15.6%. [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-prevalence|Van Praag 2019]] Pooled prevalence was similar after mild (13.5%) and after moderate or severe injury (11.8%), and across assessment methods and timings. [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-severity|Van Praag 2019]] Most single comparisons with trauma or healthy controls found no difference, but pooled across studies PTSD was more common after TBI (OR 1.73). [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-controls|Van Praag 2019]] The two conditions share symptoms such as poor concentration, irritability and disturbed sleep, and amnesia for the event complicates trauma-memory items, so prevalence depends on how each instrument separates them. [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Van Praag 2019]]

**Overlap with ADHD.** In paediatric studies, 16.0% of children with TBI had an ADHD diagnosis before the injury, against 10.8% in the general paediatric population. [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-preinjury|Asarnow 2021]] After concussion, mild or moderate injury, later ADHD diagnoses were not significantly more common than in controls; after severe injury they were, and 35.5% of children with severe TBI had ADHD more than a year later. [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-mild|Asarnow 2021]] [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-severe|Asarnow 2021]] Because ADHD raises the chance of injury, only the severe-injury association survives the check against pre-injury status in this synthesis. [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-caution-direction|Appraisal: Asarnow 2021]]

**A three-way knot.** In a veteran cohort, ADHD measures agreed poorly with one another and ADHD was about twice as common in people with current PTSD; because poor concentration also features after concussion, co-occurrence and misattribution are hard to tell apart when all three conditions are in play. [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-measures|Knight 2025]] [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-ptsd|Knight 2025]] [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-persisting|Ginsburg 2025]] [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Knight 2025]]

**Reading rule.** Before crediting a later symptom or diagnosis to a brain injury, name the severity grade and how it was assigned, the time since injury, the comparison group (uninjured or other-injury controls) and whether pre-injury status was known. [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-caution-direction|Appraisal: Asarnow 2021]] [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-controls|Van Praag 2019]] [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-caution-grading|Appraisal: Ginsburg 2025]]

## Evidence and status

Severity grading and the clinical picture of closed injury come from a clinical reference chapter rather than a systematic review. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-limit|Ginsburg 2025]] PTSD after civilian TBI is quantified by a meta-analysis whose estimates vary widely between studies. [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-prevalence|Van Praag 2019]] [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-limit|Van Praag 2019]] The ADHD association is clearest after severe paediatric injury, while milder injuries show no clear increase once controls are considered. [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-mild|Asarnow 2021]] [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-severe|Asarnow 2021]]

## Uncertainties

- Which people develop persisting symptoms after a mild injury, and why, is not answered by severity grades. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-caution-grading|Appraisal: Ginsburg 2025]]
- Whether PTSD after TBI reflects the injury, the traumatic event or measurement overlap; single-study comparisons and pooled estimates disagree. [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-controls|Van Praag 2019]]
- Adult ADHD after TBI, and military samples, fall outside the meta-analyses sourced here. [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-limit|Asarnow 2021]] [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-limit|Van Praag 2019]]
- How often chronic traumatic encephalopathy follows repeated injury is not quantified in the sources here. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-persisting|Ginsburg 2025]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Reference chapter (StatPearls).** A current clinical summary of grading, the predominance of mild injury and the long-term risks of repeated injury. [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-severity|Ginsburg 2025]] [[P32491784 Ginsburg 2025 Traumatic brain injury#^p32491784-persisting|Ginsburg 2025]]
- **2021 · Meta-analysis (JAMA Pediatrics).** High pre-injury ADHD rates account for much of the apparent link, leaving an association after severe injury only. [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-preinjury|Asarnow 2021]] [[P34251435 Asarnow 2021 ADHD and pediatric traumatic brain injury#^p34251435-severe|Asarnow 2021]]
- **2019 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Journal of Neurotrauma).** PTSD after civilian TBI pooled at 15.6%, with no gradient by injury severity. [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-prevalence|Van Praag 2019]] [[P31238819 Van Praag 2019 PTSD after civilian traumatic brain injury#^p31238819-severity|Van Praag 2019]]
