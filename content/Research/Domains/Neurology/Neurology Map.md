# Neurology Map

The neurology layer covers how the nervous system is organized, examined and measured, and how to read group imaging findings honestly.

**Contents.** [[#Reference overview|Overview]], [[#Reference spine|Reference spine]], [[#Concept register|Concept register]], [[#Where this domain connects|Cross-domain links]], [[#Evidence boundaries|Evidence boundaries]], [[#Learning route|Learning route]], [[#Core sequence|Core sequence]]

## Reference overview

Neurology covers diseases of the nervous system and the clinical method used to localise them.
Its reference value for this library is that it supplies the discipline of lesion-based reasoning -
what a deficit implies about where damage sits - together with the honest limits of that reasoning.

**Localisation is probabilistic inference.** History and examination generate hypotheses about
level and side; the reasoning works because tracts and nuclei have characteristic signatures, and
it fails because of fibres of passage, compensation, multiple lesions and non-focal presentations.
That is why the examination is a hypothesis-generating instrument rather than a test. [[History and localisation]] [[Neurological examination]] [[Differential reasoning in neurology]]

**Syndromes are patterns, not causes.** Sensory, motor and autonomic syndromes describe what is
disrupted; the same pattern can arise from different pathologies, and the same pathology can
produce different patterns at different sites. Vascular, demyelinating, neoplastic and degenerative
processes are distinguished by course and distribution as much as by any single finding.
[[Sensory and motor systems]] [[Nervous system divisions]] [[Cerebrovascular disease]]
[[Demyelination and multiple sclerosis]] [[Neurodegeneration and cognitive syndromes]]

**Epilepsy illustrates the network view.** Seizures are episodes of abnormal, excessive synchronous
activity; their classification reflects where activity starts and how it spreads, which is why the
same underlying pathology can present differently across people and why treatment targets circuits
rather than lesions. [[Seizures and epilepsy]]

**Movement and neuromuscular disorders show structure-function mapping at work.** Basal ganglia
circuits shape the initiation and scaling of movement; cerebellar circuits shape coordination and
timing; motor units and peripheral nerves carry the final common path. Each level produces a
characteristic deficit pattern that localisation uses. [[Movement disorders]] [[Basal ganglia]]
[[Peripheral neuropathy and neuromuscular disease]]

**Diagnostics measure proxies with defined limits.** EEG measures summed postsynaptic potentials
over time with poor spatial resolution; structural MRI measures anatomy; functional imaging
measures indirect metabolic signals; and each modality's sensitivity depends on what it is asked to
detect. 'The scan was normal' constrains less than it appears to. [[EEG and other diagnostics]]
[[MRI versus EEG]] [[Structural versus functional measures]] [[Neuroimaging methods]]

**Evidence boundaries.** Localisation rules are clinically validated heuristics rather than
deterministic laws; imaging findings are frequently non-specific; incidental findings create their
own management problems; and this library contains no diagnostic or treatment instructions for
individuals. [[Bipolar MRI findings and their limits]] [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-limit|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

**Where to start.** [[History and localisation]] for the clinical method, [[Cerebrovascular disease]] and [[Seizures and epilepsy]] as worked examples, and [[EEG and other diagnostics]] for what the tests actually measure. The full article list for this domain is in the concept register below; [[Reference Index]] carries A-Z, concept-kind, domain, condition and evidence routes over the whole reference layer.

## Reference spine

Neurology here is organised around localisation and mechanism: using history and examination to infer where a problem is, then using tests selectively to change probabilities.

The examination is a reasoning method. Its domains mirror the functional divisions of the nervous system, and causes are grouped by mechanism - vascular, infectious, genetic, degenerative, traumatic - which frames interpretation before any test is ordered. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-purpose|Overview of the Neurological Exam]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-causes|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

Localisation relies on a few robust rules. Sensory pathways cross, so lateralised deficits point opposite the lesion above the crossing; tract positions and segmental innervation let the level of a spinal lesion be estimated; dissociations between discriminative touch and pain or temperature testing indicate which channel is affected. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-segments|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]] [[F103 Neuroscience Online somatosensory systems#^f103-division|Somatosensory Systems]]

Disorder categories follow their mechanisms. Stroke interrupts perfusion with territory-dependent deficits; demyelination slows or blocks conduction while leaving axons intact; movement disorders map onto basal ganglia and cerebellar circuits; neurodegeneration produces progressive cognitive syndromes whose clinical and pathological diagnoses are not identical. [[F74 MedlinePlus Stroke#^f74-mechanism|Stroke]] [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-mechanism|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-dyskinesias|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[F113 Neuroscience Online CNS aging and Alzheimer's disease#^f113-diagnosis|CNS Aging and Alzheimer's Disease]]

Diagnostics are interpreted against pre-test probability, and no modality substitutes for clinical reasoning: MRI, EEG, nerve conduction studies and cerebrospinal fluid each measure specific properties with specific limits. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-caution-modality|AI appraisal: Brain imaging]]
