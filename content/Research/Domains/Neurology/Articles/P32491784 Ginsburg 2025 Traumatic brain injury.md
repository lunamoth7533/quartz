## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Traumatic brain injury results from external mechanical force (impact, acceleration-deceleration or penetration); closed injuries such as concussion, contusions and intracranial haematomas are the most common.
^p32491784-definition

Severity is graded mild, moderate or severe: mild injury (concussion) means under 30 minutes' loss of consciousness, under 24 hours of post-traumatic amnesia, or any altered consciousness; Glasgow Coma Scale scores of 3-8 mark severe and 9-12 moderate injury. Mild injuries are over 90% of cases.
^p32491784-severity

Long-term risks include persisting headache, dizziness, poor concentration and depression, and repeated injuries can accumulate into permanent damage, including chronic traumatic encephalopathy.
^p32491784-persisting

## Scope as reported

A regularly updated clinical reference chapter centred on closed rather than penetrating head injury; it summarises rather than systematically reviews the evidence.
^p32491784-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Severity grades describe the acute injury, not the outcome: a mild grade can still be followed by persisting symptoms, and later difficulties may reflect pre-injury problems, the injury itself, or both.
