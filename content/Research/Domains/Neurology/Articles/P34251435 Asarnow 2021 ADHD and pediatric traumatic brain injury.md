# Association of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Diagnoses With Pediatric Traumatic Brain Injury: A Meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Asarnow RF, Newman N, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34251435/)

**Published.** 2021 in JAMA pediatrics.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jamapediatrics.2021.2033](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2021.2033) · PMID [34251435](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34251435/) · PMCID [PMC8276124](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8276124/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Before injury, 16.0% of children with TBI already had an ADHD diagnosis, above the 10.8% rate in the general paediatric population.
^p34251435-preinjury

After concussion, mild or moderate injury, the odds of an ADHD diagnosis were not significantly raised compared with uninjured or other-injury controls.
^p34251435-mild

After severe injury the odds were raised against children with other injuries at both time points and against uninjured children beyond one year, and 35.5% of children with severe TBI had ADHD more than a year later.
^p34251435-severe

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis of 24 English-language, peer-reviewed studies (1981-2019) of children aged 4-18, following MOOSE guidance and reporting odds ratios with Bayesian credible intervals; the authors conclude that the high pre-injury rate makes pre-injury functioning essential context.
^p34251435-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Because ADHD raises the chance of being injured, a post-injury ADHD diagnosis cannot be credited to the injury without pre-injury information; in this synthesis only the severe-injury association survives that comparison.
^p34251435-caution-direction
