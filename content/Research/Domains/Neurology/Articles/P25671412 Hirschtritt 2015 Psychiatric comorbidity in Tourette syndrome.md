# Lifetime prevalence, age of risk, and genetic relationships of comorbid psychiatric disorders in Tourette syndrome.

**Authors.** Hirschtritt ME, Lee PC, et al. (17 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25671412/)

**Published.** 2015 in JAMA psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2014.2650](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2014.2650) · PMID [25671412](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25671412/) · PMCID [PMC4446055](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4446055/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Lifetime prevalence of any comorbid psychiatric disorder was 85.7%, and 57.7% had two or more.
^p25671412-prevalence

72.1% met criteria for OCD or ADHD; mood, anxiety and disruptive behaviour disorders each affected roughly 30%.
^p25671412-ocdadhd

Most comorbid disorders began between ages 4 and 10, except eating and substance use disorders, whose onset came in adolescence.
^p25671412-age

The high rate of mood disorders was accounted for by comorbid OCD, and genetic correlations of Tourette syndrome with mood, anxiety and disruptive behaviour disorders may run through ADHD and OCD.
^p25671412-mediation

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional structured DSM-IV-TR interviews (1992-2008) of people with Tourette syndrome and unaffected relatives, with heritability and genetic-correlation analyses.
^p25671412-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A sample assembled through family and genetic research and assessed for lifetime diagnoses is likely to be more affected than people with tics in the community, so the 85.7% figure describes this sample rather than everyone with tics.
^p25671412-caution-sample
