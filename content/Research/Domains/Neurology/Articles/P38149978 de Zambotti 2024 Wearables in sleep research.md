# State of the science and recommendations for using wearable technology in sleep and circadian research.

**Published.** 2024 in Sleep (volume 47, issue 4).

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review reports that, judged against polysomnography, current consumer-grade devices estimate sleep better than traditional actigraphy.
^p38149978-performance

Named limitations include misclassifying wake within the sleep period, poor tracking outside the main night-time sleep, artefacts, and unclear performance in people with certain characteristics or comorbidities; sensor problems linked to skin colour or obesity could widen existing health disparities.
^p38149978-limits

It sets out decision points for research use: device capabilities and performance, the target population and study goals, access to raw versus summary data, and data processing and analysis.
^p38149978-research

## Scope as reported

A Sleep Research Society-commissioned statement on the state of the science: what wearable devices can measure, how accurate their data are, and what researchers should weigh before relying on them.
^p38149978-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The review is not presented as a systematic synthesis, and agreement with polysomnography is a group-level property, so one person's tracker output remains an estimate, least certain in the groups where performance is unclear.
^p38149978-caution-device
