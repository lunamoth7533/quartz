# Regional, circuit and network heterogeneity of brain abnormalities in psychiatric disorders.

**Authors.** Segal A, Parkes L, et al. (24 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37580620/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Normative models showed that each person's deviations in regional grey-matter volume were highly individual: any given region was affected in fewer than 7% of people sharing a diagnosis.
^p37580620-regional

In up to 56% of people those scattered deviations converged on shared functional circuits and networks; the salience-ventral attention system was involved across diagnoses, and other systems in specific conditions.
^p37580620-circuits

The 1,294 cases had one of six diagnoses - schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder, autism or ADHD - against 1,465 matched controls; the condition-specific systems were found in depression, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia and ADHD.
^p37580620-diagnoses

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional grey-matter volume study across six diagnoses with matched controls, framed as a response to case-control research that relies on group mean comparisons and ignores individual heterogeneity.
^p37580620-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

If regional deviations rarely coincide across people with one diagnosis, a group-average map can show a difference that few individuals share, so it should not be read as the profile of a typical patient.
^p37580620-caution-average
