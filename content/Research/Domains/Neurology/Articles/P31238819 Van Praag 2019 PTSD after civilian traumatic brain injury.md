# Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder after Civilian Traumatic Brain Injury: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Prevalence Rates.

**Authors.** Van Praag DLG, Cnossen MC, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31238819/)

**Published.** 2019 in Journal of neurotrauma (first online 2019-08-02).

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In low-risk-of-bias civilian studies, PTSD prevalence after TBI ranged from 2.6% to 36%, pooling to 15.6%.
^p31238819-prevalence

Pooled prevalence after mild injury (13.5%) did not differ from that after moderate or severe injury (11.8%), and estimates were similar across assessment methods and timing.
^p31238819-severity

Most individual studies found no difference from trauma or healthy controls, but across studies PTSD was more common after TBI (OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.21-2.47).
^p31238819-controls

## Scope as reported

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 52 civilian studies (31 at low risk of bias on the MORE checklist), excluding military samples; the authors stress between-study variability and the need for higher-quality studies of risk factors.
^p31238819-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Post-concussive and posttraumatic symptoms overlap (poor concentration, irritability, disturbed sleep), and amnesia for the event complicates trauma-memory items, so prevalence depends on how each instrument separates the two.
^p31238819-caution-overlap
