# A Systematic Evaluation of Machine Learning-Based Biomarkers for Major Depressive Disorder.

**Authors.** Winter NR, Blanke J, et al. (46 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38198165/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across roughly 4 million machine-learning models using structural and functional MRI, diffusion imaging and a polygenic risk score, mean accuracy for classifying individuals as patients or controls ranged from 48.1% to 62.0%.
^p38198165-accuracy

Combining modalities, or stratifying by age, sex, treatment or remission status, did not improve performance, and simulated perfect reliability did not significantly help.
^p38198165-integration

Multivariate markers outperformed univariate ones, yet the authors identified no informative individual-level biomarker for depression.
^p38198165-conclusion

## Scope as reported

Case-control study of adults recruited from primary care and the general population in two German cities (2014-2018); the replication in the second cohort was partial and was observed for structural MRI.
^p38198165-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Near-chance classification does not mean brain differences are absent; it shows that group differences of the observed size did not separate individuals in this design.
