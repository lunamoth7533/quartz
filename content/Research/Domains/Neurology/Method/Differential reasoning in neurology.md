# Differential reasoning in neurology

## Definition

Differential reasoning is the structured comparison of explanations for a presentation. In neurology it works from mechanism, timing, distribution of findings and the patient's context, before tests are chosen.

## How it works

**Localise first, then name.** The exam identifies where function is lost so a deficit can be attributed to a region or pathway, and the causes of deficits are grouped by mechanism as an interpretive frame - vascular, infectious, inflammatory, genetic, degenerative, neoplastic, toxic, traumatic and functional categories - each generating different expectations about speed of onset and course. The two steps together generate a differential: a location plus a time course. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-purpose|Overview of the Neurological Exam]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-causes|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

**Distribution.** Whether findings are focal, multifocal or generalised, and whether they respect a territory or a pathway, narrows the list more than any single sign. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]

**Signs map to circuits.** The motor-disorders literature maps disorders to circuits, so signs are interpreted through the anatomy that produces them rather than as isolated symptoms, and symptoms arising from basal ganglia dysfunction are described as extrapyramidal to distinguish them from pyramidal-tract weakness. Pattern recognition in neurology is circuit reasoning. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-mapping|Motor Disorders]] [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Motor Disorders]]

**Tempo is the master variable.** Vascular disease acts in minutes, inflammatory disease over days to weeks, neoplastic over weeks to months, degenerative over years; a careful timeline does more diagnostic work than most tests, because each mechanism carries a characteristic tempo. That is why history-taking - onset, progression, associated systemic features - remains the core skill even in an imaging-rich environment. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-causes|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

**Localisation plus tempo generates the differential.** The two axes combine: a focal, progressively worsening deficit in one territory generates a different list from a multifocal, relapsing one, and the tests that would confirm each candidate are chosen only after the axes have narrowed them. [[Cerebrovascular disease]]

**Test results are inputs, not verdicts.** Diagnosis combines history, examination, imaging and laboratory findings, and imaging changes resembling multiple sclerosis can appear without the disease; the diagnostic overview covers history and examination, imaging, electroencephalography and laboratory tests and what each modality measures. So a finding has to be interpreted against the pattern that generated the hypothesis. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-diagnosis|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological diagnostic tests]] Tests change probabilities rather than replacing reasoning: they are interpreted against pre-test probability, so a finding that is meaningful at high pre-test probability, or in one presentation, may be incidental at low probability or in another. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-caution-modality|AI appraisal: Neurological diagnostic tests]]

**The psychiatric boundary is a two-way differential.** Symptoms such as fatigue, cognitive complaints, sensory changes and psychomotor changes appear in both neurological and psychiatric conditions, so psychiatric presentations deserve consideration of neurological causes and vice versa; co-occurrence is also frequent, so shared symptoms do not imply shared cause, which is why co-occurrence reasoning is central rather than peripheral. The disciplined move in both directions is the same: full characterisation before category assignment. [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-cultural|What Are Psychological Disorders]] [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-caution-label|AI appraisal: Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]] [[Mood disorders]] The same reasoning appears in psychiatry: differential assessment in adult autism guidance covers neurodevelopmental, mental, physical, communication and sensory conditions because features overlap across categories, and co-occurrence is common enough to be a design consideration rather than an exception. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-differential|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-clinical|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

**Reading rule.** State the location, the time course, the pattern that fits, and what would disconfirm it. Differential reasoning is the disciplined version of holding several explanations at once. ## Evidence and status

Reasoning frameworks are taught and clinically validated by outcome; they are heuristics, and errors are well documented in both directions - missed organic disease and over-investigation.
