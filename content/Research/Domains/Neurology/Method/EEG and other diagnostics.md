# EEG and other diagnostics

## Definition

Neurological diagnostics include electrophysiological tests, laboratory studies and imaging. Each measures a specific property, and none substitutes for clinical assessment.

## How it works

**Each diagnostic answers a named question.** The diagnostic overview covers history and examination, imaging with CT and MRI, electroencephalography and laboratory tests, and what each modality measures. EEG asks what the summed electrical rhythm is doing and when; nerve conduction asks whether the lesion is axonal, demyelinating, nervous or muscular; cerebrospinal fluid asks about inflammation and infection; CT and MRI ask about structure. The diagnostic reasoning is question-first: choose the test whose answer can change the working differential, rather than accumulating tests and reasoning backwards. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]] [[Differential reasoning in neurology]]

**EEG.** Scalp electrodes record summed electrical activity over time with high temporal resolution and limited spatial precision - frequency and amplitude with accuracy within milliseconds but without fine spatial localisation - which makes EEG the tool of choice for detecting epileptiform activity and for sleep staging. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-eeg|Brain imaging]] [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]]

**MRI is a family of methods.** CT and MRI contribute structural information, with the modality chosen for the clinical question. Conventional structural MRI images anatomy, while functional MRI gives an activity-related haemodynamic signal with more spatial detail than EEG but on a slower timescale - a distinction worth stating explicitly because the same acronym covers different measurements. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological diagnostic tests]] [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-caution-modality|AI appraisal: Neurological diagnostic tests]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-fmri|Brain Imaging]] Because these three families measure different quantities, disagreement between them is not automatically error and agreement does not make any one of them a clinical classifier. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-caution-modality|AI appraisal: Brain Imaging]]

**Nerve conduction and electromyography.** These distinguish nerve from muscle and axonal from demyelinating processes, which is why they are used in peripheral presentations. [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-causes|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] Peripheral nerve disorders are a large group affecting nerves outside the brain and spinal cord, with over a hundred recognised types, symptoms including weakness, aching and tingling or numbness beginning in the hands and feet, and causes spanning diabetes, infections, nutritional deficiencies, toxins, trauma and inherited conditions. That breadth is why the overview does not rank causes by frequency and why nerve conduction and related studies are used to characterise a pattern rather than to name a disease. [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-list|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-symptoms|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-causes|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]] [[F78 MedlinePlus Peripheral nerve disorders#^f78-limit|Peripheral Nerve Disorders]]

**Cerebrospinal fluid.** Sampling can identify inflammation or infection, and it is part of the diagnostic pathway for demyelinating disease in some presentations. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-diagnosis|Multiple Sclerosis]]

**Diagnosis is assembled across findings.** In multiple sclerosis, diagnosis combines history, examination, imaging and laboratory findings, and imaging changes resembling MS can appear without the disease; in suspected seizure, guidance specifies referral and specialist assessment after a first event. Both examples make the same point: the test result is one input to a specified combination, and its interpretation depends on the others. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-diagnosis|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]]

**Normal results are probability updates too.** A normal EEG between events does not exclude epilepsy; a normal scan does not exclude a seizure tendency. Interpreting a negative test requires the same pre-test-probability reasoning as a positive one, which is why the guideline structures epilepsy diagnosis around history plus targeted investigation rather than test-first pathways. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-diagnosis|Epilepsies NG217]] [[Seizures and epilepsy]]

**Reading rule.** State the modality, the quantity measured, the finding's sensitivity and specificity where known, and how the finding changes the diagnosis or management. A test that does not change either is being reported for completeness rather than for care. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-limit|Neurological diagnostic tests]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-limit|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

## Evidence and status

Sensitivity and specificity differ substantially across tests and presentations, so test selection and interpretation depend on the pre-test probability, which is the clinical reasoning carried out before ordering anything.
