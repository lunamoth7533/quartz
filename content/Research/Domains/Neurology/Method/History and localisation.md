# History and localisation

## Definition

Localisation is the inference from a clinical presentation to the site of dysfunction. It combines history, examination findings and anatomical knowledge rather than relying on imaging alone.

## How it works

**The purpose of the exam.** The neurological exam is a structured set of tests whose purpose is to identify where function is lost, so that a deficit can be attributed to a region or pathway rather than to a symptom name. History precedes it because the history is what generates the hypotheses the exam tests. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-purpose|Overview of the Neurological Exam]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-limit|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

**History sets the frame.** Timing - sudden, progressive, fluctuating - narrows the mechanism before any test, and vascular, inflammatory, degenerative and structural causes produce different patterns. Causes of neurological deficits are grouped by mechanism - vascular, infectious, genetic, degenerative and traumatic - as an organising frame for interpretation, so time course is part of the pattern: sudden onset and gradual onset point to different mechanism groups, which is why a history of sudden onset carries different weight from a gradual one. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-causes|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

**What each domain samples.** The exam's domains correspond to functional divisions of the nervous system: mental status, cranial nerves, motor, sensory, coordination and reflex testing. The mapping from domain to division is what makes a finding localising rather than merely abnormal. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

**Anatomy supplies the map.** Spinal cord organisation into segments, with sensory afferents entering dorsally and motor neurons leaving ventrally and ascending and descending tracts in characteristic positions, is why the level and pattern of a deficit carry localising information: segmental innervation and tract positions let the examiner estimate the level of a spinal lesion, using dermatome and root rules. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-segments|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-horns|Spinal Cord]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]] Because ascending and descending pathways cross, a lateralised deficit usually points to the opposite side above the crossing point; the sensory pathway's three-neuron chain, with a second-order neuron crossing the midline, explains why some lesions produce contralateral and others ipsilateral deficits. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]]

**Pathway reasoning.** Separating discriminative touch and proprioception from pain and temperature tests different channels, and dissociations between them are informative. [[F103 Neuroscience Online somatosensory systems#^f103-division|Somatosensory systems]] [[F103 Neuroscience Online somatosensory systems#^f103-exam|Somatosensory systems]]

**Test results are inputs, not verdicts.** Imaging changes resembling multiple sclerosis can appear without the disease, so investigation findings are interpreted against the hypothesis the history generated rather than read on their own. [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-diagnosis|Multiple Sclerosis]] [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological diagnostic tests]]

**Reading rule.** Separate the symptom's location, the mechanism suggested by its time course, and the investigation that could confirm it. Localisation is an inference from pattern, and the textbook presentation is a teaching frame rather than a clinical protocol. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-limit|Overview of the Neurological Exam]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-limit|Spinal Cord]]

## Evidence and status

Localisation rules are clinically validated by lesion-outcome correlation; they are heuristics that guide investigation rather than deterministic laws. Fibres of passage, compensation and individual variation mean localisation is probabilistic, and imaging findings must be interpreted in the clinical context rather than treated as definitive.
