# Neurological examination

**Definition.** The neurological evaluation starts with history and examination before technology is used to answer the questions they raise. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

## Supported claims

- The NINDS overview places history and examination alongside imaging, EEG and laboratory testing in the diagnostic process. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]
- Findings are interpreted against norms and context; a normal examination does not exclude every neurological or psychiatric condition. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-caution-modality|AI synthesis: Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]]

## How it works

**Purpose.** The neurological exam is a structured set of tests whose purpose is to identify where function is lost, so a deficit can be attributed to a region or pathway rather than to a symptom name. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-purpose|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

**The examination is a designed instrument.** Its domains - mental status, cranial nerves, motor function, sensation, coordination and reflexes - mirror and partition the functional divisions of the nervous system, so the examination is best read as a battery of targeted probes whose logic mirrors the anatomy: each element samples a specific system at a specific level rather than serving as a general check. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]] [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-modalities|Neurological Diagnostic Tests and Procedures]] Cranial nerve testing samples brainstem and peripheral function; motor testing (tone, power, reflexes, bulk) distinguishes upper from lower motor neuron patterns; sensory testing maps pathways by modality and territory; coordination and gait sample cerebellar and proprioceptive contributions; and mental status samples cortical function. That design is also why incomplete examinations lose information non-randomly - skipping coordination testing removes cerebellar and proprioceptive evidence specifically. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-mapping|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]

**The upper/lower motor neuron distinction is the highest-yield dichotomy.** Increased tone, hyperreflexia and extensor plantar responses indicate upper motor neuron involvement; wasting, fasciculation and reduced reflexes indicate lower motor neuron involvement. Distinguishing them separates tract involvement from cell and nerve involvement - narrowing the lesion to the tract or the cell, which is the examination's main contribution - and so immediately halves the differential and directs imaging and conduction studies. The dichotomy is teachable, reliable enough at the level of pattern, and maps onto anatomically distinct substrates - a rare combination in clinical measurement. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]

**Sectional reasoning.** Localisation uses the plane and level at which a lesion appears, which is why anatomical conventions are part of the method. [[F54 Neuroscience Online nervous system overview#^f54-sections|Overview of the Nervous System]]

**Causes grouped by mechanism.** Deficits are grouped as vascular, infectious, genetic, degenerative and traumatic, providing an organising frame for interpretation rather than a diagnosis. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-causes|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

**Limits and reliability.** A normal examination does not exclude disease, and findings are interpreted against history, timing and context; the exam is a reasoning tool rather than a test with a threshold. Many examination findings have modest inter-rater reliability, and their interpretation depends on the examiner's technique and the patient's cooperation; this is a measurement property, not a criticism of examination as a method. [[F15 NINDS Neurological diagnostic tests and procedures#^f15-caution-modality|AI appraisal: Neurological diagnostic tests]]

**Reading rule.** An examination claim should state the sign, the technique and the patient's state; the same nominal sign can mean different things depending on how it was elicited. [[History and localisation]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** This article is the applied instrument of everything the neuroanatomy domain describes, and its modest inter-rater reliabilities are the methods domain's reminder that a well-designed clinical instrument is still a measurement with error. [[Sensory and motor systems]] [[Measurement validity and reliability]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Examination findings depend on the examiner's technique and on the person's cooperation, so they are evidence rather than certainty.
