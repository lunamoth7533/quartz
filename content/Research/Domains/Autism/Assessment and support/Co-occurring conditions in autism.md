# Co-occurring conditions in autism

## Definition

Co-occurring conditions are diagnoses that autistic people have in addition to autism, and they are common enough that one meta-analysis of about 590,000 autistic children and adults estimated prevalence for dozens of them. [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-scope|Micai 2023]] The most frequent include developmental coordination, sleep-wake, gastrointestinal, ADHD, anxiety, weight and feeding or eating problems, with epilepsy and mood conditions also more common than in the general population. [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-frequent|Micai 2023]] [[P34510916 Liu 2022 Epilepsy prevalence in autism#^p34510916-prevalence|Liu 2022]] [[P31447415 Lai 2019 Co-occurring mental health diagnoses in autism#^p31447415-rates|Lai 2019]]

## How it works

**Mental-health conditions.** In a meta-analysis restricted to clinically confirmed diagnoses, pooled prevalence among autistic people was about 28% for ADHD, 20% for anxiety disorders, 13% for sleep-wake disorders, 11% for depressive disorders, 9% for obsessive-compulsive disorder, 5% for bipolar disorders and 4% for schizophrenia spectrum disorders. [[P31447415 Lai 2019 Co-occurring mental health diagnoses in autism#^p31447415-rates|Lai 2019]] Most estimates were above general-population figures, and clinical samples gave higher figures than population or registry samples. [[P31447415 Lai 2019 Co-occurring mental health diagnoses in autism#^p31447415-moderators|Lai 2019]] Unexplained heterogeneity was extreme, so each figure is the centre of a wide range rather than a single rate. [[P31447415 Lai 2019 Co-occurring mental health diagnoses in autism#^p31447415-limit|Lai 2019]] [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-caution-pooling|Appraisal: Micai 2023]]

**ADHD.** ADHD is the most frequent co-occurring diagnosis in the pooled mental-health data, and an earlier diagnostic rule excluding a dual diagnosis, since revised, held back research on the combination. [[P31447415 Lai 2019 Co-occurring mental health diagnoses in autism#^p31447415-rates|Lai 2019]] [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-history|Leitner 2014]] People with both conditions show more severe social and adaptive difficulties and may fit single-condition treatments less well. [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-clinical|Leitner 2014]]

**Epilepsy.** About one in ten autistic people also have epilepsy, more than in the general population. [[P34510916 Liu 2022 Epilepsy prevalence in autism#^p34510916-prevalence|Liu 2022]] Prevalence is higher in clinical samples, in adolescents and adults, and where more participants are female or have intellectual disability, and lower in countries with a higher human development index. [[P34510916 Liu 2022 Epilepsy prevalence in autism#^p34510916-moderators|Liu 2022]] Because epilepsy tracks intellectual disability, a pooled figure depends on the sample mix, and country differences may reflect detection as much as risk. [[P34510916 Liu 2022 Epilepsy prevalence in autism#^p34510916-caution-mix|Appraisal: Liu 2022]]

**Sleep, gastrointestinal and feeding problems.** Sleep-wake problems, gastrointestinal problems and feeding or eating disorders are among the most frequent co-occurring conditions by point prevalence, with estimates that differ between children and adults and between population and clinical samples. [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-frequent|Micai 2023]] [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-moderators|Micai 2023]]

**Trauma and PTSD.** Pooled point prevalence of diagnosed PTSD is about 1.1% in autistic children and young people and 2.1% in autistic adults, close to general-population estimates but below what screening studies suggest. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-prevalence|Mansour 2025]] [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-discrepancy|Mansour 2025]] The gap could mean that criteria miss how autistic people experience trauma or that screening over-includes, and the meta-analysis cannot decide between them. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-caution-underdiagnosis|Appraisal: Mansour 2025]]

**Why co-occurrence matters for risk.** In Swedish registers, risk of suicide attempt and suicide was highest in autistic people without intellectual disability who also had ADHD, and adjusting for other psychiatric diagnoses weakened the associations. [[P31238998 Hirvikoski 2020 Suicide attempt and suicide in autism#^p31238998-adhd|Hirvikoski 2020]] [[P31238998 Hirvikoski 2020 Suicide attempt and suicide in autism#^p31238998-comorbidity|Hirvikoski 2020]] Part of the excess risk therefore travels with co-occurring conditions rather than with autism alone. [[P31238998 Hirvikoski 2020 Suicide attempt and suicide in autism#^p31238998-comorbidity|Hirvikoski 2020]]

**Diagnostic overshadowing runs both ways.** Overshadowing means attributing a new difficulty to a diagnosis already made, so that a second condition goes unassessed; because features overlap, a co-occurring condition can be missed or double-counted depending on who assessed and how. [[P31447415 Lai 2019 Co-occurring mental health diagnoses in autism#^p31447415-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Lai 2019]] Adult guidance asks assessment to cover coexisting mental and physical disorders and other neurodevelopmental conditions. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-domains|NICE CG142]] In the other direction autism itself can be overshadowed: 54.2% of autistic females and 40.9% of autistic males in Swedish registers had a psychiatric diagnosis, most often ADHD, anxiety or depression, before autism was recognised. [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-preceding|Martini 2025]] Continued care for these earlier diagnoses fell below half for most conditions once autism was diagnosed. [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-stability|Martini 2025]] In an adult referral clinic, affective disorders were common whether or not autism was diagnosed, and phobias and personality disorders were other frequent differentials. [[P37160157 Sappok 2023 Adult autism differentials and AQ utility#^p37160157-differentials|Sappok 2023]]

**Reading rule.** Read every prevalence figure with its sample - clinical, registry or population - and its diagnostic method, and treat a new difficulty in an autistic person as a question for assessment rather than as part of autism by default. [[P31447415 Lai 2019 Co-occurring mental health diagnoses in autism#^p31447415-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Lai 2019]] [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-caution-pooling|Appraisal: Micai 2023]]

## Evidence and status

Prevalence evidence is broad but heterogeneous: two large meta-analyses and an epilepsy meta-analysis agree that many conditions are more common in autistic people, and all show estimates moving with age, sample source, intellectual functioning or country. [[P31447415 Lai 2019 Co-occurring mental health diagnoses in autism#^p31447415-moderators|Lai 2019]] [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-moderators|Micai 2023]] [[P34510916 Liu 2022 Epilepsy prevalence in autism#^p34510916-moderators|Liu 2022]] The Micai and epilepsy abstracts report no pooled intervals or heterogeneity statistics. [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-limit|Micai 2023]] [[P34510916 Liu 2022 Epilepsy prevalence in autism#^p34510916-limit|Liu 2022]] Register studies add outcome and pathway data but miss undiagnosed autism and events that never reached care. [[P31238998 Hirvikoski 2020 Suicide attempt and suicide in autism#^p31238998-caution-register|Appraisal: Hirvikoski 2020]] [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-limit|Martini 2025]]

## Uncertainties

- How much of the apparent excess reflects shared features counted twice rather than distinct conditions. [[P31447415 Lai 2019 Co-occurring mental health diagnoses in autism#^p31447415-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Lai 2019]]
- Whether low recorded PTSD rates reflect under-recognition in autistic people or over-inclusive screening. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-caution-underdiagnosis|Appraisal: Mansour 2025]]
- Which diagnoses made before autism were misattributions and which were genuine conditions that later resolved. [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-caution-misdiagnosis|Appraisal: Martini 2025]]
- Sleep and gastrointestinal figures are sourced here only from a pooled abstract without rates; condition-specific meta-analyses are not yet in this library. [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-limit|Micai 2023]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Nationwide register cohort (Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry).** Autistic people, especially females, often had ADHD, anxiety or depression diagnosed before autism, and most earlier diagnoses were not followed by continued care, a pathway view that separates persistent co-occurring needs from overlapping presentations. [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-preceding|Martini 2025]] [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-stability|Martini 2025]]
- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Clinical Psychology Review).** Diagnosed PTSD in autistic people sat near general-population levels but below screening estimates, keeping open whether trauma is under-recognised. [[P40783299 Mansour 2025 PTSD prevalence in autistic people#^p40783299-discrepancy|Mansour 2025]]
- **2023 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews).** Across about 590,000 participants, developmental coordination, sleep, gastrointestinal, ADHD, anxiety, weight and feeding problems were among the most frequent co-occurring conditions. [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-scope|Micai 2023]] [[P37913872 Micai 2023 Co-occurring conditions in autism#^p37913872-frequent|Micai 2023]]
