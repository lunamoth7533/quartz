# Autistic burnout

## Definition

Autistic burnout is a state that autistic adults describe as long-lasting exhaustion, loss of previously available skills and lowered tolerance of sensory input, arising under chronic load without adequate support. [[P32851204 Raymaker 2020 Defining autistic burnout#^p32851204-features|Raymaker 2020]] [[P32851204 Raymaker 2020 Defining autistic burnout#^p32851204-definition|Raymaker 2020]] A second, co-produced definition describes exhaustion, withdrawal, difficulties with thinking, reduced daily living skills and more visible autistic traits. [[P34088219 Higgins 2021 Defining autistic burnout Delphi#^p34088219-definition|Higgins 2021]] The term came from autistic community discussion and was almost absent from academic and clinical writing before 2020. [[P32851204 Raymaker 2020 Defining autistic burnout#^p32851204-limit|Raymaker 2020]]

## How it works

**Two definitions with a shared core.** The first study built its definition from interviews and public online accounts: a syndrome of pervasive exhaustion, loss of function and reduced stimulus tolerance, typically lasting three months or more. [[P32851204 Raymaker 2020 Defining autistic burnout#^p32851204-definition|Raymaker 2020]] A Delphi study led by autistic adults added withdrawal, thinking difficulties, loss of daily living skills and heightened autistic traits. [[P34088219 Higgins 2021 Defining autistic burnout Delphi#^p34088219-definition|Higgins 2021]] When 141 autistic adults with experience of burnout compared the two, they strongly endorsed the Delphi definition but described both short and long episodes, so no duration criterion is settled. [[P36637293 Arnold 2023 Confirming the nature of autistic burnout#^p36637293-definition|Arnold 2023]] [[P36637293 Arnold 2023 Confirming the nature of autistic burnout#^p36637293-duration|Arnold 2023]]

**The proposed mechanism is load without relief.** In the originating accounts, life stressors add to a cumulative load, barriers to support block relief, and expectations come to exceed abilities; participants linked recovery with acceptance and social support, reduced demands and doing things in an autistic way, including unmasking. [[P32851204 Raymaker 2020 Defining autistic burnout#^p32851204-mechanism|Raymaker 2020]] [[P32851204 Raymaker 2020 Defining autistic burnout#^p32851204-recovery|Raymaker 2020]] Measurement work summarises the state as exhaustion, withdrawal and problems with concentration and thinking, tied to everyday stress. [[P36637292 Arnold 2023 Towards measuring autistic burnout#^p36637292-profile|Arnold 2023]] This is a lived-experience model of demands and resources: no study cited here measures the load or its physiology directly.

**Masking is the most often cited contributor.** Participants in the foundational camouflaging study linked camouflaging to exhaustion and a reduced sense of self. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-consequence|Social camouflaging]] In one survey burnout was associated with masking, yet the burnout scale used there tracked depression rather than masking, and a later validation study found only moderate correlations between burnout measures and camouflaging. [[P36637292 Arnold 2023 Towards measuring autistic burnout#^p36637292-links|Arnold 2023]] [[P36637292 Arnold 2023 Towards measuring autistic burnout#^p36637292-abm|Arnold 2023]] [[P38660943 Mantzalas 2024 Measuring and validating autistic burnout#^p38660943-correlates|Mantzalas 2024]]

**Where it meets depression.** The definitional papers present burnout as distinct from clinical depression and occupational burnout. [[P32851204 Raymaker 2020 Defining autistic burnout#^p32851204-definition|Raymaker 2020]] [[P34088219 Higgins 2021 Defining autistic burnout Delphi#^p34088219-depression|Higgins 2021]] The measured picture is less separate: burnout scores rise with depression, anxiety, stress and fatigue, and tests of overlap with depression were mixed. [[P38660943 Mantzalas 2024 Measuring and validating autistic burnout#^p38660943-correlates|Mantzalas 2024]] [[P38660943 Mantzalas 2024 Measuring and validating autistic burnout#^p38660943-overlap|Mantzalas 2024]] Many participants in one survey reported earlier misdiagnoses such as depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder or borderline personality disorder. [[P36637293 Arnold 2023 Confirming the nature of autistic burnout#^p36637293-misdiagnosis|Arnold 2023]]

**How it is measured.** The 27-item AASPIRE Autistic Burnout Measure showed a four-factor structure under one overarching burnout construct in 238 autistic adults, and it and the emotional-exhaustion subscale of the Copenhagen Burnout Inventory were judged valid preliminary screening tools. [[P38660943 Mantzalas 2024 Measuring and validating autistic burnout#^p38660943-structure|Mantzalas 2024]] [[P38660943 Mantzalas 2024 Measuring and validating autistic burnout#^p38660943-overlap|Mantzalas 2024]] In an earlier sample the same measure did not separate current from past burnout, and newly drafted severity items did not combine cleanly into one score. [[P36637292 Arnold 2023 Towards measuring autistic burnout#^p36637292-abm|Arnold 2023]] [[P36637292 Arnold 2023 Towards measuring autistic burnout#^p36637292-severity|Arnold 2023]]

**Reading rule.** Report which definition and which instrument a burnout claim rests on, and do not read a burnout score as separating burnout from depression: that separation is asserted by lived-experience definitions and not yet shown by measurement. [[P38660943 Mantzalas 2024 Measuring and validating autistic burnout#^p38660943-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Mantzalas 2024]]

## Evidence and status

The evidence base is young: qualitative interviews and online accounts, one Delphi consensus and online surveys of self-selected adults, with the first dedicated study published in 2020. [[P32851204 Raymaker 2020 Defining autistic burnout#^p32851204-limit|Raymaker 2020]] [[P34088219 Higgins 2021 Defining autistic burnout Delphi#^p34088219-limit|Higgins 2021]] [[P36637293 Arnold 2023 Confirming the nature of autistic burnout#^p36637293-limit|Arnold 2023]] The construct has good face validity among people who identify with it, while its discriminant validity against depression and fatigue is unresolved. [[P36637293 Arnold 2023 Confirming the nature of autistic burnout#^p36637293-caution-selfselection|Appraisal: Arnold 2023]] [[P34088219 Higgins 2021 Defining autistic burnout Delphi#^p34088219-caution-consensus|Appraisal: Higgins 2021]] No study cited here tests whether masking causes burnout; the one longitudinal test of camouflaging in this library concerned general mental health and found no clear forward effect over two years. [[P40579935 van der Putten 2025 Camouflaging cause or consequence#^p40579935-conclusion|van der Putten 2025]] [[P40579935 van der Putten 2025 Camouflaging cause or consequence#^p40579935-caution-interval|Appraisal: van der Putten 2025]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether burnout can be separated from depression, anxiety and fatigue in measured data, not only in lived-experience accounts.
- Duration and recurrence: both short and long episodes are reported, and the definitions differ on a time criterion.
- How often clinicians mistake burnout for mood, anxiety or personality conditions; misdiagnosis is reported but not quantified.
- Whether findings from small, online, self-selected samples, with few participants who are not white or who need more support, apply more widely.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Ecological momentary assessment (Autism).** Smartphone reports from 87 autistic adults over 28 days put the masking-stress link that burnout accounts rely on into daily life: masking rose in non-autistic company, fell among autistic peers, and moments with more masking carried more stress. [[P40635406 Scheeren 2025 Masking social context and stress#^p40635406-context|Scheeren 2025]] [[P40635406 Scheeren 2025 Masking social context and stress#^p40635406-stress|Scheeren 2025]] Concurrent reports cannot show which comes first. [[P40635406 Scheeren 2025 Masking social context and stress#^p40635406-caution-concurrent|Appraisal: Scheeren 2025]]
- **2024 · Psychometric validation (Autism Research).** In 238 autistic adults the main burnout measure held together as one overarching construct, but it correlated strongly with depression, anxiety, stress and fatigue and only moderately with camouflaging, which is why the construct's independence remains open. [[P38660943 Mantzalas 2024 Measuring and validating autistic burnout#^p38660943-structure|Mantzalas 2024]] [[P38660943 Mantzalas 2024 Measuring and validating autistic burnout#^p38660943-correlates|Mantzalas 2024]] [[P38660943 Mantzalas 2024 Measuring and validating autistic burnout#^p38660943-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Mantzalas 2024]]
- **2023 · Online surveys (Autism).** Two surveys from the same group, each with 141 autistic adults, endorsed the Delphi definition, left duration open, recorded frequent reported misdiagnoses, and found an early burnout scale that tracked depression but not masking. [[P36637293 Arnold 2023 Confirming the nature of autistic burnout#^p36637293-definition|Arnold 2023]] [[P36637293 Arnold 2023 Confirming the nature of autistic burnout#^p36637293-misdiagnosis|Arnold 2023]] [[P36637292 Arnold 2023 Towards measuring autistic burnout#^p36637292-abm|Arnold 2023]] A scale that follows depression more than masking may not be measuring burnout specifically. [[P36637292 Arnold 2023 Towards measuring autistic burnout#^p36637292-caution-validity|Appraisal: Arnold 2023]]

## Connections

- [[Masking and camouflaging]] - the most often cited contributor; that note covers the strategies and their costs, this one the exhausted state they are said to feed.
- [[Mood disorders]] - the central differential: burnout is defined as distinct from depression, yet burnout measures correlate strongly with depressive symptoms.
- [[Allostasis and chronic stress]] - a physiological framework for cumulative load; burnout accounts describe load and relief in similar terms but have not measured its mediators.
- [[Autistic sensory and communication access]] - reduced stimulus tolerance is a core burnout feature, and that note's environmental adaptations address the same sensory and communicative demands.
- [[Adult autism assessment and differential considerations]] - burnout enters adult assessment as a differential, since people report earlier misdiagnoses with mood, anxiety and personality conditions.
- [[Autism support and services]] - lack of support is part of the proposed mechanism, and reduced demands and support are what participants linked with recovery.
- [[Measurement validity and reliability]] - the open question here is discriminant validity: whether burnout scales measure something other than depression and fatigue.

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Autistic burnout sits where the autism domain's participation literature meets clinical psychiatry's mood constructs and neuroendocrinology's load models: the same exhaustion can be described as a depressive episode, an allostatic cost or a mismatch between demands and support, and each description brings its own measures. Reading it beside [[Mood disorders]], [[Allostasis and chronic stress]] and [[Measurement validity and reliability]] keeps the lived-experience definition, the psychiatric differential and the measurement question apart.
