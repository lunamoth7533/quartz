# Monotropism

## Definition

Monotropism is a theory of autism, first set out by Murray, Lesser and Lawson, which proposes that atypical ways of allocating attention are central to autism. [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-attention|Murray 2005]] Later work describes it as an autistic-developed account that characterises autism by a tendency to hyperfocus on interests. [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-account|Dwyer 2025]] Its claims have so far been set out in argument, explored in interviews and tested in one small laboratory study. [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-limit|Murray 2005]] [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-limit|Rapaport 2024]] [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-limit|Dwyer 2025]]

## How it works

**Attention as the organising principle.** The founding paper drew on research, the DSM-IV and ICD-10 criteria and autistic first-person accounts to argue that the first two diagnostic criteria, covering social interaction and communication, derive from the narrowed interests described in the third. [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-criteria|Murray 2005]] On this account, differences in how attention is distributed come first, and the social and communication features follow from them. [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-attention|Murray 2005]] [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-criteria|Murray 2005]] Deriving the criteria from one principle makes the theory coherent, but it is not evidence that the principle causes the features. [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-caution-derivation|Appraisal: Murray 2005]]

**An origin in autistic scholarship.** The founding argument drew on autistic first-person experience, including one author's own, and current research describes monotropism as developed by autistic people. [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-criteria|Murray 2005]] [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-account|Dwyer 2025]] That places it among the insider accounts that autistic people and, later, non-autistic researchers have asked autism science to take seriously. [[P34730840 Pellicano 2022 Neurodiversity in autism science#^p34730840-paradigm|Pellicano 2022]]

**Immersion, flow and their costs.** In interviews with 24 autistic adults, descriptions of becoming absorbed in tasks closely matched existing ideas of flow, autistic hyperfocus and monotropism, and immersion was described as all-encompassing. [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-immersion|Rapaport 2024]] Participants valued it as a state in which they could be themselves, but found it hard to balance with the rest of life, naming exhaustion and neglected self-care among the costs. [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-balance|Rapaport 2024]] The authors frame task immersion as potentially valuable and as something that may need support if it is to help autistic people flourish. [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-support|Rapaport 2024]]

**What measurement shows so far.** In the one laboratory test sourced here, autistic adolescents and their caregivers reported more hyperfocus than comparison families, and both judged its overall effect modestly positive. [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-questionnaires|Dwyer 2025]] Event-related potential markers of hyperfocus and cross-modal attention capture did not differ between groups, and attention measures of different kinds were mostly unrelated to one another. [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-lab|Dwyer 2025]] [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-convergence|Dwyer 2025]] Autistic participants had lower visual working memory capacity, which the authors suggest may reflect difficulty attending to several targets, and self-reported hyperfocus went with misophonia and sensory hyperreactivity. [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-lab|Dwyer 2025]] [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-convergence|Dwyer 2025]]

**Reading rule.** Treat monotropism as a descriptive theory that fits many autistic accounts rather than a tested mechanism: ask whether a study compared autistic and non-autistic people, whether its attention measure was a questionnaire or a task, and whether the two agreed. [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Rapaport 2024]] [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-caution-power|Appraisal: Dwyer 2025]]

## Evidence and status

The founding statement is a theoretical argument from criteria, literature and personal experience, with no systematic search and no new data. [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-limit|Murray 2005]] Qualitative evidence shows that the theory's descriptions resonate with autistic adults, but the interview study had no comparison group and a sample of mostly White, well-educated, employed women who used spoken communication. [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-limit|Rapaport 2024]] [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Rapaport 2024]] The only quantitative test sourced here studied 40 adolescents and found that questionnaire and laboratory measures of hyperfocus did not converge, which leaves the theory neither confirmed nor refuted. [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-limit|Dwyer 2025]] [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-caution-power|Appraisal: Dwyer 2025]] Compared with predictive-processing or social-cognition accounts of autism, empirical testing of monotropism is thin, and a theory built by deriving criteria needs predictions that could fail. [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-caution-derivation|Appraisal: Murray 2005]]

## Uncertainties

- No validated measure of monotropism is sourced in this library, and hyperfocus questionnaires and laboratory tasks disagree. [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-convergence|Dwyer 2025]]
- Whether monotropic attention distinguishes autistic from non-autistic people has not been tested with a design that could fail. [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Rapaport 2024]]
- Whether social and communication differences really follow from attention, as the founding argument proposes, rather than arising alongside it. [[P15857859 Murray 2005 Attention and monotropism#^p15857859-caution-derivation|Appraisal: Murray 2005]]
- How deep immersion can be supported without the exhaustion and neglected self-care that participants described. [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-balance|Rapaport 2024]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Case-control laboratory study (Research in Autism).** Elevated self- and caregiver-reported hyperfocus in autistic adolescents was not matched by group differences on event-related potentials or attention-capture tasks, and the measures barely related to one another. [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-questionnaires|Dwyer 2025]] [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-convergence|Dwyer 2025]] With 40 participants, the null laboratory results are weak evidence either way. [[P42318288 Dwyer 2025 Hyperfocus and monotropism measures#^p42318288-caution-power|Appraisal: Dwyer 2025]]
- **2024 · Qualitative interview study (Autism in Adulthood).** Autistic adults' accounts of task immersion matched flow, hyperfocus and monotropism, and brought out both the pleasure and the costs of being absorbed. [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-immersion|Rapaport 2024]] [[P39371355 Rapaport 2024 Autistic flow and task immersion#^p39371355-balance|Rapaport 2024]]

## Connections

- [[Attention]] - the general psychology of selection and control that monotropism recasts as the organising difference in autism.
- [[Attention and executive function]] - the task-versus-everyday measurement gap recurs here, since questionnaire and laboratory measures of hyperfocus did not agree.
- [[Predictive processing accounts of autism]] - a rival account of autistic perception and attention with more experimental testing and mixed results.
- [[Social cognition accounts]] - the accounts monotropism reverses, deriving social and communication differences from attention rather than treating them as primary.
- [[Neurodiversity paradigm]] - the framework that values insider theories such as this one and resists reading intense interests as deficits.
- [[Autistic burnout]] - exhaustion was among the costs of deep immersion that participants described, and burnout accounts describe exhaustion under sustained load.
- [[Qualitative designs]] - most of the evidence for monotropism comes from first-person and interview material, with the strengths and limits that brings.
