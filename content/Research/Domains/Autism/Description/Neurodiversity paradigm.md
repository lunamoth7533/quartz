# Neurodiversity paradigm

## Definition

The neurodiversity paradigm treats autism as one variant among many kinds of human mind, and it has been proposed as an alternative to the medical framing that has shaped autism research since autism was first described. [[P34730840 Pellicano 2022 Neurodiversity in autism science#^p34730840-paradigm|Pellicano 2022]] Its approaches to disability are contested partly because the terms are used in different senses; careful statements of it hold that individual characteristics and society both contribute to disability. [[P36158596 Dwyer 2022 Neurodiversity approaches for researchers#^p36158596-meaning|Dwyer 2022]] [[P36158596 Dwyer 2022 Neurodiversity approaches for researchers#^p36158596-social-model|Dwyer 2022]]

## How it works

**A case against conventional autism science.** The paradigm's research argument starts from three limits it attributes to the medical approach: a heavy focus on deficits, attention to the individual rather than the context around them, and a narrow range of perspectives shaping the questions. [[P34730840 Pellicano 2022 Neurodiversity in autism science#^p34730840-challenges|Pellicano 2022]] The proposal is that core elements of the neurodiversity paradigm can answer each limit, changing what autism research asks and how it is done. [[P34730840 Pellicano 2022 Neurodiversity in autism science#^p34730840-implications|Pellicano 2022]] The case was pressed first by autistic people and later taken up by non-autistic researchers. [[P34730840 Pellicano 2022 Neurodiversity in autism science#^p34730840-paradigm|Pellicano 2022]]

**The social model, strong and moderate.** One reading ties the paradigm to a strong social model in which society alone creates disability; a conceptual review calls that position controversial and joins literature holding that individual characteristics and society both contribute. [[P36158596 Dwyer 2022 Neurodiversity approaches for researchers#^p36158596-social-model|Dwyer 2022]] In a survey of 504 autism community members, the society-only view was common but not universal, and even people holding it endorsed some help aimed at the individual, such as teaching adaptive skills or treating depression and epilepsy. [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-social-model|Dwyer 2025]]

**What supporters ask of interventions.** Backing the neurodiversity movement went with favouring social change and more accommodating environments, and with less support for normalising interventions, although most participants still endorsed teaching adaptive skills. [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-support|Dwyer 2025]] Autistic adults looking back on early interventions endorsed well-being, adult support, accessibility and reducing harmful behaviours as outcomes, did not endorse reducing autistic traits, and described harm from being taught to hide autistic characteristics. [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-outcomes|Nosova 2026]] [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-masking|Nosova 2026]]

**How it has shaped priorities and outcomes.** Across 15 studies of goals for supporting autistic children, autistic people and families gave more weight to flexibility, self-advocacy and strengths, while professionals leaned toward skill acquisition and compliance with behavioural expectations. [[P42003394 Jordan 2026 Goals and outcomes of support#^p42003394-differences|Jordan 2026]] Reviews in this area now call for co-design with autistic people and families, for tools that measure neurodiversity-affirming outcomes, and for early interventions that are autism-affirming and foster self-determination. [[P42003394 Jordan 2026 Goals and outcomes of support#^p42003394-recommendations|Jordan 2026]] [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-conclusion|Nosova 2026]] The same concern about outcomes appears in trial evidence: in a meta-analysis of early interventions, most effects lost significance once caregiver- or teacher-reported and detection-biased outcomes were set aside, leaving one effect on measures of diagnostic characteristics. [[P37963634 Sandbank 2023 Project AIM early intervention meta-analysis#^p37963634-bias|Sandbank 2023]] A surviving effect on diagnostic characteristics is not the same as an outcome autistic people prioritise. [[P37963634 Sandbank 2023 Project AIM early intervention meta-analysis#^p37963634-caution-outcomes|Appraisal: Sandbank 2023]]

**The main critiques.** The most persistent dispute concerns scope: to whom the approaches apply, and what they imply for diagnostic categories. [[P36158596 Dwyer 2022 Neurodiversity approaches for researchers#^p36158596-scope|Dwyer 2022]] Clarifying terms does not settle how to balance individual support against social change for people with intellectual disability and high support needs, and surveys of engaged community members may under-represent exactly those people. [[P36158596 Dwyer 2022 Neurodiversity approaches for researchers#^p36158596-caution-scope|Appraisal: Dwyer 2022]] [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-caution-sample|Appraisal: Dwyer 2025]] A second critique is that a paradigm changes which questions are asked without showing that the resulting research improves lives. [[P34730840 Pellicano 2022 Neurodiversity in autism science#^p34730840-caution-paradigm|Appraisal: Pellicano 2022]] Advocates answer that oversimplified rhetoric misstates their position, which combines strong support for autistic leadership and social reform with openness to individual support. [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-leadership|Dwyer 2025]] [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-social-model|Dwyer 2025]]

**Reading rule.** When a paper invokes neurodiversity, ask which version it uses (society-only or interactional), which outcomes it measures and who chose them, and whether its sample includes people with high support needs; the label tells you a study's values, not its results. [[P36158596 Dwyer 2022 Neurodiversity approaches for researchers#^p36158596-caution-scope|Appraisal: Dwyer 2022]] [[P42003394 Jordan 2026 Goals and outcomes of support#^p42003394-caution-priorities|Appraisal: Jordan 2026]]

## Evidence and status

The paradigm is a framework rather than a testable hypothesis, and its core statements are position-taking and conceptual reviews that argue rather than test. [[P34730840 Pellicano 2022 Neurodiversity in autism science#^p34730840-limit|Pellicano 2022]] [[P36158596 Dwyer 2022 Neurodiversity approaches for researchers#^p36158596-limit|Dwyer 2022]] The empirical work sourced here measures attitudes and stated priorities - a cross-sectional community survey and reviews of stakeholder goals and of retrospective views - rather than whether neurodiversity-aligned support produces better long-term outcomes. [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-limit|Dwyer 2025]] [[P42003394 Jordan 2026 Goals and outcomes of support#^p42003394-limit|Jordan 2026]] [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-limit|Nosova 2026]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether neurodiversity-affirming support improves long-term well-being, participation or quality of life compared with other approaches; no trial of this is sourced in this library. [[P42003394 Jordan 2026 Goals and outcomes of support#^p42003394-caution-priorities|Appraisal: Jordan 2026]]
- How the paradigm applies to autistic people with intellectual disability and little or no spoken language, whose views are least represented in surveys. [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-caution-sample|Appraisal: Dwyer 2025]]
- How often researchers and services adopt the interactional rather than the society-only reading in practice. [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-social-model|Dwyer 2025]]

## Recent research

- **2026 · Systematic review (Autism).** Stakeholders shared communication, social inclusion and well-being as goals for young autistic children but split on normative skill and compliance targets, and the authors call for tools that capture neurodiversity-affirming outcomes. [[P42003394 Jordan 2026 Goals and outcomes of support#^p42003394-shared|Jordan 2026]] [[P42003394 Jordan 2026 Goals and outcomes of support#^p42003394-recommendations|Jordan 2026]]
- **2025 · Mixed-methods survey (Autism).** Among 504 community members, neurodiversity supporters rejected normalisation while accepting individualised supports, and the society-only view of disability was not universal even among supporters. [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-support|Dwyer 2025]] [[P39291753 Dwyer 2025 Community views of neurodiversity and intervention#^p39291753-social-model|Dwyer 2025]]
- **2022 · Annual Research Review (Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry).** Sets out the case for moving autism science toward neurodiversity around three limits of the medical approach: deficit focus, individual focus and narrow perspective. [[P34730840 Pellicano 2022 Neurodiversity in autism science#^p34730840-challenges|Pellicano 2022]]
