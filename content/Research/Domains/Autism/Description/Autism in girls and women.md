# Autism in girls and women

## Definition

Autism in girls and women names the question of why autism is recognised less often and later in females, and whether that gap reflects fewer autistic females, a different presentation, or assessment that misses them. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-conclusion|Fyfe 2026]] [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-conclusion|Cruz 2025]] The recorded male-to-female ratio has fallen as diagnosis reaches older ages, and autistic females more often carry another psychiatric diagnosis before autism is recognised. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-ratio|Fyfe 2026]] [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-preceding|Martini 2025]]

## How it works

**The sex ratio is a moving target.** In a Swedish cohort of about 2.76 million births followed to 2022, 2.8% received an autism diagnosis, with incidence peaking at ages 10-14 in males but at 15-19 in females. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-incidence|Fyfe 2026]] The male-to-female ratio fell with later age at diagnosis and, beyond age 10, across calendar periods, reaching a cumulative 1.2 by age 20 in 2022 with parity projected by 2024. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-ratio|Fyfe 2026]] A ratio of recorded diagnoses mixes any true sex difference with differences in who is referred and recognised, so it cannot by itself show that underlying prevalence is equal. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-caution-recognition|Appraisal: Fyfe 2026]]

**Camouflaging is more common in females.** Camouflaging covers compensation, masking and assimilation behaviours used both for self-protection and for connection, and a systematic review found females more likely to camouflage. [[P39370528 Klein 2025 Social camouflaging systematic review#^p39370528-motives|Klein 2025]] [[P39370528 Klein 2025 Social camouflaging systematic review#^p39370528-gender|Klein 2025]] A separate meta-analysis of 10 studies found that females reported more compensation and masking strategies than males. [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-camouflage|Cruz 2025]] Camouflaging is linked with mental health and with age at diagnosis in ways that may run in both directions, and largely cross-sectional designs leave the causal order untested. [[P39370528 Klein 2025 Social camouflaging systematic review#^p39370528-direction|Klein 2025]] [[P39370528 Klein 2025 Social camouflaging systematic review#^p39370528-caution-direction|Appraisal: Klein 2025]]

**Standard measures may be tuned to male presentations.** Across 67 studies, autistic males scored as more affected on standard clinical measures of core features and social interaction, whereas autistic females had more cognitive and behavioural difficulties; the authors read this as a male bias in clinical procedures. [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-phenotype|Cruz 2025]] [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-conclusion|Cruz 2025]] A lower female score on an instrument built mainly in male samples cannot be read as milder autism until the instrument is shown to measure both sexes the same way, which is a testable property. [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-caution-instruments|Appraisal: Cruz 2025]] [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-practice|Measurement invariance]]

**Other diagnoses often come first.** Among 72,331 autistic people born in Sweden, 54.2% of females and 40.9% of males had a psychiatric diagnosis before autism, most often ADHD, anxiety or depression. [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-preceding|Martini 2025]] Females had higher odds than males of most earlier diagnoses except psychotic disorders and ADHD, and females with an earlier diagnosis reached their autism diagnosis at an older age than males with the same condition. [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-sex|Martini 2025]] Continued care for most earlier diagnoses lapsed after autism was recognised, but females more often kept care for anxiety, sleep disorders and self-harm. [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-stability|Martini 2025]] A lapsed diagnosis may have been a misattribution or a real condition that resolved, so not every earlier label was a mistake. [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-caution-misdiagnosis|Appraisal: Martini 2025]]

**The borderline personality question.** A conceptual analysis argues that autistic girls and women without intellectual disability are often diagnosed late and after misdiagnoses, with borderline personality disorder or traits a common mislabel, and that similar behaviour can have different drivers. [[P40018062 McQuaid 2024 Borderline personality and missed autism diagnosis#^p40018062-misdiagnosis|McQuaid 2024]] [[P40018062 McQuaid 2024 Borderline personality and missed autism diagnosis#^p40018062-distinctions|McQuaid 2024]] In a comparison with borderline personality disorder and controls, autistic women reported more risk factors for emotion dysregulation than autistic men, including sexual abuse and camouflaging, without higher dysregulation scores. [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-specific|Bemmouna 2023]] The conceptual argument explains why the presentations are confusable but supplies no misdiagnosis rates. [[P40018062 McQuaid 2024 Borderline personality and missed autism diagnosis#^p40018062-caution-conceptual|Appraisal: McQuaid 2024]]

**Outcomes differ as well.** Among autistic people without intellectual disability in Swedish registers, females generally had a higher risk of suicidal behaviour than males, with the highest risk in either sex when ADHD co-occurred. [[P31238998 Hirvikoski 2020 Suicide attempt and suicide in autism#^p31238998-sex|Hirvikoski 2020]]

**Reading rule.** Before crediting a sex difference to autism itself, ask whether the sample was found through existing diagnoses or through screening, whether the instrument was validated in females, at what age people were diagnosed, and which diagnoses they carried first. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-caution-recognition|Appraisal: Fyfe 2026]] [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-caution-instruments|Appraisal: Cruz 2025]] [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-caution-misdiagnosis|Appraisal: Martini 2025]]

## Evidence and status

The firmest evidence is register epidemiology: a population birth cohort shows the recorded ratio narrowing with age at diagnosis and over time, and a nationwide cohort shows more earlier psychiatric diagnoses and later autism diagnosis in females; both count recorded diagnoses rather than screening everyone. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-limit|Fyfe 2026]] [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-limit|Martini 2025]] Phenotype and camouflaging evidence comes from meta-analyses and syntheses whose abstracts report no pooled effect sizes or risk-of-bias grading. [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-limit|Cruz 2025]] [[P39370528 Klein 2025 Social camouflaging systematic review#^p39370528-limit|Klein 2025]] The misdiagnosis-as-borderline argument is conceptual, and the dysregulation comparison relied on unverified self-reported diagnoses. [[P40018062 McQuaid 2024 Borderline personality and missed autism diagnosis#^p40018062-limit|McQuaid 2024]] [[P38110995 Bemmouna 2023 Emotion dysregulation in autistic adults#^p38110995-limit|Bemmouna 2023]]

## Uncertainties

- How much of the historical male excess reflects under-recognition and how much a real difference in prevalence; register ratios cannot separate the two. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-caution-recognition|Appraisal: Fyfe 2026]]
- Whether a distinct female autism phenotype exists, or whether standard instruments measure females differently; invariance testing across sex is rarely reported. [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-caution-instruments|Appraisal: Cruz 2025]]
- Whether camouflaging delays diagnosis and harms mental health, or follows from them. [[P39370528 Klein 2025 Social camouflaging systematic review#^p39370528-caution-direction|Appraisal: Klein 2025]]
- Most evidence here is framed by binary sex; gender-diverse autistic people appear mainly where camouflaging syntheses included all gender identities. [[P39370528 Klein 2025 Social camouflaging systematic review#^p39370528-limit|Klein 2025]]

## Recent research

- **2026 · Population birth cohort (BMJ).** Following 2.76 million Swedish births, the recorded male-to-female ratio fell with later age at diagnosis and over calendar time, to 1.2 by age 20 in 2022. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-ratio|Fyfe 2026]] The authors turn the question from how many autistic females there are to why they are diagnosed later. [[P41638711 Fyfe 2026 Male to female ratio in autism incidence#^p41638711-conclusion|Fyfe 2026]]
- **2025 · Nationwide register cohort (Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry).** Autistic females more often had psychiatric diagnoses before autism and reached an autism diagnosis later than males with the same earlier condition, a direct record of the diagnostic pathway. [[P40017336 Martini 2025 Psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism by sex#^p40017336-sex|Martini 2025]]
- **2025 · Systematic review with two meta-analyses (Neuropsychology Review).** Standard measures rated males as more affected while females had more cognitive and behavioural difficulties and reported more camouflaging, which the authors read as male bias in diagnostic procedures. [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-phenotype|Cruz 2025]] [[P38285291 Cruz 2025 Male bias in autism diagnosis#^p38285291-camouflage|Cruz 2025]]
