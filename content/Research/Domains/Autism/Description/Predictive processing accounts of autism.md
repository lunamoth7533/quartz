# Predictive processing accounts of autism

## Definition

Predictive processing accounts propose that autistic perception differs in how prior expectations and incoming sensory evidence are weighted during perceptual inference. [[P22959875 Pellicano 2012 Bayesian explanation of autistic perception#^p22959875-hypopriors|Pellicano 2012]] [[P24860482 Lawson 2014 Aberrant precision account of autism#^p24860482-precision|Lawson 2014]] They apply the framework described in [[Bayesian inference and predictive processing]] to autism, and they form a family of related accounts rather than one theory, since predictive coding itself names several distinct algorithms. [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-plural|Predictive coding algorithms]]

## How it works

**Weaker priors.** The first Bayesian account proposed attenuated priors, or hypo-priors, so that perception follows the incoming signal more closely and prior experience shapes it less; its authors offered this as an explanation for many sensory and other non-social features of autism. [[P22959875 Pellicano 2012 Bayesian explanation of autistic perception#^p22959875-hypopriors|Pellicano 2012]] [[P22959875 Pellicano 2012 Bayesian explanation of autistic perception#^p22959875-scope|Pellicano 2012]]

**Aberrant precision.** A predictive-coding reply located the difference in precision - an imbalance between the weight given to sensory evidence and the weight given to prior beliefs - and argued that a range of behavioural and neurobiological findings fit it. [[P24860482 Lawson 2014 Aberrant precision account of autism#^p24860482-precision|Lawson 2014]] [[P24860482 Lawson 2014 Aberrant precision account of autism#^p24860482-evidence|Lawson 2014]] In the general framework, precision sets how strongly each prediction-error signal counts against the priors. [[Bayesian inference and predictive processing]]

**Inflexible prediction errors.** A third version holds that autistic people give prediction errors a uniformly high, inflexible weight instead of learning when an error can be discounted as noise; its authors derive executive, theory-of-mind and central-coherence differences from this, and read insistence on sameness as a way of securing predictable input. [[P25347312 Van de Cruys 2014 Predictive coding in autism#^p25347312-hippea|Van de Cruys 2014]] [[P25347312 Van de Cruys 2014 Predictive coding in autism#^p25347312-unifying|Van de Cruys 2014]] [[P25347312 Van de Cruys 2014 Predictive coding in autism#^p25347312-sameness|Van de Cruys 2014]] This is where the predictive account enters the territory of [[Social cognition accounts]]: it treats mentalising differences as a consequence of error handling rather than as a separate deficit.

**Volatility and learning.** One experiment found autistic adults less surprised by violated expectations, with lower surprise linked to more severe symptoms; modelling attributed this to over-learning about how volatile the environment was, and modelled surprise tracked pupil size, which the authors read as altered noradrenergic gain. [[P28758996 Lawson 2017 Autistic adults overestimate environmental volatility#^p28758996-surprise|Lawson 2017]] [[P28758996 Lawson 2017 Autistic adults overestimate environmental volatility#^p28758996-volatility|Lawson 2017]] [[P28758996 Lawson 2017 Autistic adults overestimate environmental volatility#^p28758996-pupil|Lawson 2017]] This moves the claim from a fixed imbalance to how learning rates adapt when the world changes.

**The versions are not interchangeable.** Predictive-coding algorithms differ in their generative models and optimisation methods, so evidence for one is not evidence for another. [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-difference|Predictive coding algorithms]] The autism accounts differ in which term carries the difference - the prior, the sensory precision, or the flexibility with which errors are weighted - and because weaker priors and sharper sensory precision make overlapping predictions, a task that cannot separate those terms cannot favour one version. [[P24860482 Lawson 2014 Aberrant precision account of autism#^p24860482-caution-imbalance|Appraisal: Lawson 2014]] [[P40576893 Cui 2026 Simple Bayesian model of autism meta-analysis#^p40576893-caution-identifiability|Appraisal: Cui 2026]]

**Reading rule.** Before counting a result for or against a predictive account of autism, name the version it tests, the kind of prior involved (explicit, learned during the task, or long-standing) and the model term the design can actually isolate. [[P36581168 Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023 Bayesian theories of autism review#^p36581168-learned|Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023]]

## Evidence and status

A systematic review of the first decade of experiments found highly mixed results, with a slight majority of studies showing no group difference in prior integration, frequent low statistical power and inconsistent methods; reduced prior influence appeared more often for priors learned during an experiment than for priors acquired beforehand. [[P36581168 Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023 Bayesian theories of autism review#^p36581168-mixed|Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023]] [[P36581168 Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023 Bayesian theories of autism review#^p36581168-methods|Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023]] [[P36581168 Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023 Bayesian theories of autism review#^p36581168-learned|Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023]] The newer meta-analyses and the direct test listed under Recent research keep that picture: a small average effect with large unexplained heterogeneity, and intact integration of explicit priors. [[P40576893 Cui 2026 Simple Bayesian model of autism meta-analysis#^p40576893-heterogeneity|Cui 2026]] [[P39806435 Fazioli 2025 Intact Bayesian integration in autism#^p39806435-intact|Fazioli 2025]] The accounts are therefore a family of live hypotheses rather than an established mechanism, and the versions that remain most plausible concern how priors are learned and updated rather than a general weakness of priors. [[P36581168 Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023 Bayesian theories of autism review#^p36581168-caution-counting|Appraisal: Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023]] [[P39806435 Fazioli 2025 Intact Bayesian integration in autism#^p39806435-caution-explicit|Appraisal: Fazioli 2025]]

**Why it is still debated.** Studies are often underpowered and methodologically inconsistent. [[P36581168 Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023 Bayesian theories of autism review#^p36581168-methods|Angeletos Chrysaitis 2023]] Effects vary across tasks and people more than any tested moderator explains, which fits the heterogeneity described in [[Autism heterogeneity and support needs]]. [[P40576893 Cui 2026 Simple Bayesian model of autism meta-analysis#^p40576893-heterogeneity|Cui 2026]] A candidate neural marker of prediction error points in opposite directions in autistic children and autistic adults, which a static account struggles to explain. [[P41168907 Sapey-Triomphe 2025 Mismatch negativity in autism meta-analysis#^p41168907-age|Sapey-Triomphe 2025]] And a framework broad enough to accommodate most outcomes invites the general criticism made of unifying predictive theories in [[Bayesian inference and predictive processing]].

## Uncertainties

- Which version, if any, survives designs that separate prior strength, sensory precision and learning rate within the same participants.
- Whether differences concentrate in priors learned during a task, in volatile environments or in social prediction, rather than in perception generally.
- How the child-adult reversal in mismatch responses fits any account, given that it is a contrast between studies rather than a finding from following people over time. [[P41168907 Sapey-Triomphe 2025 Mismatch negativity in autism meta-analysis#^p41168907-caution-reading|Appraisal: Sapey-Triomphe 2025]]
- Whether laboratory measures of prior weighting relate to the everyday sensory experiences autistic people report.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Autism Research).** Across 76 amplitude and 62 latency effects, mismatch responses were smaller in autistic children and adolescents but larger in autistic adults in multifeature designs, smaller for phoneme deviants, and no different in latency - a developmental pattern that neither confirms nor rules out one predictive account. [[P41168907 Sapey-Triomphe 2025 Mismatch negativity in autism meta-analysis#^p41168907-age|Sapey-Triomphe 2025]] [[P41168907 Sapey-Triomphe 2025 Mismatch negativity in autism meta-analysis#^p41168907-latency|Sapey-Triomphe 2025]] [[P41168907 Sapey-Triomphe 2025 Mismatch negativity in autism meta-analysis#^p41168907-caution-reading|Appraisal: Sapey-Triomphe 2025]]
- **2026 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Neuropsychology Review).** Pooled across 23 studies, the difference the simple Bayesian model predicts appeared on average (g = 0.37) but with large heterogeneity that no moderator explained, so the authors judge support limited and point to hierarchical models. [[P40576893 Cui 2026 Simple Bayesian model of autism meta-analysis#^p40576893-effect|Cui 2026]] [[P40576893 Cui 2026 Simple Bayesian model of autism meta-analysis#^p40576893-heterogeneity|Cui 2026]] [[P40576893 Cui 2026 Simple Bayesian model of autism meta-analysis#^p40576893-caution-identifiability|Appraisal: Cui 2026]]
- **2025 · Psychophysics experiment (Molecular Autism).** With explicit priors and varied sensory uncertainty, 126 participants showed intact and equally suboptimal integration in autistic and non-autistic adults, which argues against a general prior deficit while leaving learned priors open. [[P39806435 Fazioli 2025 Intact Bayesian integration in autism#^p39806435-intact|Fazioli 2025]] [[P39806435 Fazioli 2025 Intact Bayesian integration in autism#^p39806435-suboptimal|Fazioli 2025]] [[P39806435 Fazioli 2025 Intact Bayesian integration in autism#^p39806435-caution-explicit|Appraisal: Fazioli 2025]]

## Connections

- [[Bayesian inference and predictive processing]] - the parent framework: priors, likelihood and precision-weighted prediction error, with the caution that predictive-coding algorithms are not interchangeable.
- [[Social cognition accounts]] - the rival mechanism-level account of social differences; the inflexible-error version claims to explain theory-of-mind differences, so both face the same task-versus-everyday test.
- [[Perception]] - the constructive view of perception that makes differences in prior weighting a meaningful hypothesis about autistic sensory experience.
- [[Model comparison and identifiability]] - the central method problem here: telling prior strength, sensory precision and learning rate apart needs models that can be distinguished from each other.
- [[Noradrenaline signalling]] - the neuromodulatory system invoked when modelled surprise tracked pupil size in autistic adults in the volatility study.
- [[Excitation and inhibition balance]] - a sibling circuit-level hypothesis in this domain; both are partial accounts, stated at different levels and tested separately.
- [[Autistic sensory and communication access]] - the everyday sensory differences these accounts try to explain; access recommendations rest on service design rather than on any one mechanism.
- [[Computational psychiatry]] - the wider field that fits such models to patients; its reliability problems apply to prior and precision estimates in autism as much as to learning rates.
- [[Monotropism]] - an autistic-developed rival account that puts the allocation of attention first, so far with much less experimental testing than predictive accounts.

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** This note is where the computational-theories domain's inference models meet the autism domain's sensory and social descriptions. Stating the level of each claim - computational, algorithmic or neural - and holding it to the methods domain's standards for comparing models is what separates a tested version of the account from a redescription of autistic traits in Bayesian vocabulary.
