# Masking and camouflaging

## Definition

Masking or camouflaging refers to strategies used to manage or hide autistic features in social situations. Strategies range from deliberate performance to habitual compensation.

## How it works

**Reported strategies.** The foundational study used an online survey with a large sample of autistic adults to characterise deliberate and less deliberate strategies for managing self-presentation. Reported strategies grouped around masking, compensation and assimilation, and participants described both effort and perceived benefits and costs. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-method|Social camouflaging]] [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-strategies|Social camouflaging]]

**Reported costs.** Camouflaging was described as demanding and linked by participants to exhaustion and a reduced sense of self, which is why it is treated as a substantial burden rather than a harmless social skill. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-consequence|Social camouflaging]] A systematic review of autistic adults' retrospective views of early interventions reports negative consequences from being taught to hide autistic characteristics, which the review discusses under camouflaging or masking. [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-masking|Autistic adults' views on early interventions]]

**Why it makes assessment harder.** Adult guidance expects assessment to be considered when autistic features accompany functional difficulties or a relevant service history, and an AQ-10 score of six or more leads to assessment rather than diagnosis. Comprehensive assessment is team-based and uses informants or documents where possible, covering childhood features, functioning, coexisting disorders and other neurodevelopmental conditions, with differential assessment spanning neurodevelopmental, mental, physical, communication and sensory conditions. Because features may be concealed in a clinical setting, assessment should consider informant history and context sensitivity, which carry information that a single observation cannot. Biological, genetic and neuroimaging tests are not routine. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-identification|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-screening|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-comprehensive|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-domains|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-differential|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-tests|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]]

**Screens are not diagnoses.** A screening score can produce false positives and false negatives, so it neither confirms nor excludes autism - it routes to assessment. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-caution|AI appraisal: Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] The equivalent caution in the research literature is about measurement: comparing scores across groups assumes measurement invariance, and content validity problems in quality-of-life instruments for autistic adults include domains that do not match lived experience and tools that may overestimate quality of life while under-detecting vulnerability. [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-practice|Measurement invariance]] [[P41993846 Fernandez 2026 Quality-of-life assessment in autistic adults#^p41993846-validity|Quality-of-life assessment in autistic adults]]

**The communication and environment side.** The same guidance asks services to respect partnership and autonomy, explain language and check understanding, offer communication aids or interpreters, and adapt space, visual supports, lighting and noise while varying assessment length. A recommendation to adapt an environment is a design principle rather than a trial result: whether a specific accommodation helps a specific person is an empirical and individual question, and access to services varies. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f25-partnership|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f25-communication|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f25-environment|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f25-caution|AI appraisal: Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]]

## Evidence and status

This is well documented self-reported experience; prevalence figures vary with instrument, and causal direction between camouflaging and distress is unresolved. The foundational study remains a survey of self-reported experience: it documents what participants describe and does not establish prevalence or clinical causality. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-limit|Social camouflaging]] Camouflaging has been associated with later identification and with mental health burden in subsequent research, though designs are largely cross-sectional.

**What the adult-views finding is.** The report that being taught to hide autistic characteristics had negative consequences is a stated-experience finding that shapes what counts as a good outcome; it is not a measurement of whether any specific intervention works. [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-caution|AI appraisal: Autistic adults' views on early interventions]]

## Uncertainties

- No validated cross-cultural measure of camouflaging exists, which limits comparison across samples. [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-practice|Measurement invariance]]
