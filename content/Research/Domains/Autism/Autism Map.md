# Autism Map

Autism is heterogeneous in features, development and support needs. This map keeps that heterogeneity central while noting where genetic and neuroscience evidence is strongest.

**Contents.** [[#Reference overview|Overview]], [[#Reference spine|Reference spine]], [[#Concept register|Concept register]], [[#Where this domain connects|Cross-domain links]], [[#Evidence boundaries|Evidence boundaries]], [[#Learning route|Learning route]], [[#Domains|Domains]]

## Reference overview

Autism is a developmental condition defined by differences in social communication and by
restricted, repetitive patterns of behaviour and interest, with sensory features and support needs
varying widely between people. The domain's reference value lies in how carefully it has to be
written: the same evidence supports very different conclusions depending on whose outcomes and
whose perspectives are counted.

**Heterogeneity is the first fact.** Presentation, language, sensory profile, co-occurring
conditions and support needs vary to a degree that makes average descriptions misleading, and the
definition itself has changed across diagnostic systems. Support is individualised rather than
programme-based, and assessments differ substantially between children and adults. [[Autism heterogeneity and support needs]] [[Adult autism assessment and differential considerations]]

**Biology describes architecture, not destiny.** Rare de novo and inherited variants of larger
effect implicate specific genes in a minority of cases; common variation contributes polygenically;
identified risk genes cluster in gene regulation and neuronal communication and are expressed
early in excitatory and inhibitory lineages. Risk-gene findings are population-level and do not
determine an individual's traits or support needs. [[Autism genetics and rare variants]] [[Common and rare variants]] [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-caution-risk|AI appraisal: Autism exome sequencing]]

**Psychological accounts compete rather than stack.** Social-cognition deficits, the double-empathy
proposal that locates difficulties in mutual understanding between autistic and non-autistic
people, predictive-processing proposals and excitation-inhibition hypotheses each explain part of
the picture; the E/I account is explicitly a research framework, and predictive-processing
applications are not sourced in this library. [[Social cognition accounts]] [[Double empathy]]
[[Excitation and inhibition balance]] [[Bayesian inference and predictive processing]]

**Masking and access are evidence, not commentary.** Camouflaging research documents the effort
that goes into managing self-presentation and the exhaustion participants link to it; sensory and
communication access shapes both participation and what measurements capture; and intervention
outcomes look different when autistic perspectives are included in the choice of outcomes.
[[Masking and camouflaging]] [[Autistic sensory and communication access]] [[Intervention outcomes and autistic perspectives]] [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-consequence|Social camouflaging]]

**Evidence boundaries.** Diagnostic instruments have limited adult validation; most intervention
research measures short-term behavioural change rather than long-term participation; under-represented
adult populations and non-Western samples are thin; and this hub contains no intervention
guidance. [[Measurement validity and reliability]] [[Qualitative designs]]

**Where to start.** [[Autism heterogeneity and support needs]] for the construct, [[Autism genetics and rare variants]] for biology, [[Masking and camouflaging]] and [[Autistic sensory and communication access]] for the participation literature. ## Reference spine

Autism is a developmental condition that varies widely between people in sensory, social and behavioural features. The reference literature is organised around heterogeneity first, then mechanisms, then assessment and support, because generalising from one presentation is the most common error in this area.

Genetic architecture involves common variants of small effect and rare variants of larger effect, including de novo mutations, with genes implicated in synaptic and regulatory functions. A risk variant is not a diagnosis, a prognosis or a mechanism, and most cases are not explained by one variant. [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-model|Autism exome sequencing]] [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-genes|Autism exome sequencing]] [[P31981491 Satterstrom 2020 Autism exome sequencing#^p31981491-caution-risk|AI appraisal: Autism exome sequencing]]

Cognitive accounts are partial. Mentalising tasks show group differences but correlate weakly with everyday outcomes, which is why social-cognition accounts now sit alongside relational framings. [[F20 NIMH Autism spectrum disorder#^f20-development|Autism spectrum disorder]] [[P31949163 Lord 2020 Autism spectrum disorder primer#^p31949163-construct|Autism spectrum disorder primer]] The double-empathy account reframes misunderstanding as bidirectional, and camouflaging research documents the effort and cost autistic adults describe in managing self-presentation. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-strategies|Social camouflaging]] [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-consequence|Social camouflaging]] The excitation-inhibition ratio remains a hypothesis under test rather than an established cause. [[P14606691 Rubenstein 2003 Excitation-inhibition model#^p14606691-proposal|Excitation-inhibition model]] [[P14606691 Rubenstein 2003 Excitation-inhibition model#^p14606691-status|Excitation-inhibition model]]

Assessment is multi-source and multi-domain, with screening scores leading to assessment rather than diagnosis, and differential assessment covering other neurodevelopmental, mental, physical, communication and sensory conditions. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-screening|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-differential|Autism in adults: diagnosis and management]] Support is individualised, and autistic adults' accounts of early interventions emphasise long-term wellbeing and autonomy over normalisation. [[F20 NIMH Autism spectrum disorder#^f20-supports|Autism spectrum disorder]] [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-outcomes|Autistic adults' views on early interventions]]

## Evidence gaps

- Evidence in children often does not generalize to adults.
- Rare-variant findings describe risk contributions, not individual prediction.
- Outcome measures frequently reflect caregiver or clinician priorities rather than autistic quality of life.
