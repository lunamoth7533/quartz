# Sex differences in psychiatric diagnoses preceding autism diagnosis and their stability post autism diagnosis.

**Authors.** Martini MI, Kuja-Halkola R, et al. (12 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40017336/)

**Published.** 2025 in Journal of child psychology and psychiatry, and allied disciplines (first online 2025-02-28).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/jcpp.14130](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.14130) · PMID [40017336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40017336/) · PMCID [PMC12267683](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12267683/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Before autism was diagnosed, 54.2% of females and 40.9% of males had already received a psychiatric diagnosis, most often ADHD, anxiety or depression.
^p40017336-preceding

Females had higher odds than males of most earlier diagnoses except psychotic disorders and ADHD, a pattern stable across 2010-2020, and for most conditions, females with an earlier diagnosis reached their autism diagnosis at an older age than males who had the same earlier condition.
^p40017336-sex

Continued care for earlier diagnoses after autism was recognised ranged from 23.1% to 88.9%, below half for most; females kept care more often for anxiety, sleep disorders and self-harm.
^p40017336-stability

## Scope as reported

Linear and logistic regression in Swedish national registers; stability meant a further diagnosis or medication within five years of the autism diagnosis. People with undiagnosed autism are not captured.
^p40017336-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

An earlier diagnosis that lapses after autism is recognised may have been a misattribution, a genuine co-occurring condition that resolved, or a change in how care was recorded; register data cannot separate these, so earlier diagnoses should not all be read as misdiagnoses.
^p40017336-caution-misdiagnosis
