# Autism intervention meta-analysis of early childhood studies (Project AIM): updated systematic review and secondary analysis.

**Authors.** Sandbank M, Bottema-Beutel K, Crowley LaPoint S, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37963634/)

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1136/bmj-2023-076733](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076733) · PMID [37963634](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37963634/) · PMCID [PMC10644209](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10644209/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The update covered 252 studies with 13,304 young autistic children; restricted to randomised trials, several intervention types showed small-to-moderate summary effects, for example naturalistic developmental behavioural interventions on social communication (g = 0.35).
^p37963634-rcts

When caregiver- or teacher-reported outcomes and outcomes at high risk of detection bias were also excluded, only one summary effect stayed significant: naturalistic developmental behavioural interventions on measures of diagnostic characteristics (g = 0.30).
^p37963634-bias

Adverse events were poorly monitored but possibly common.
^p37963634-harms

## Scope as reported

Search updated to November 2021; effects estimated with robust variance estimation and stratified by Cochrane risk of bias, across outcome domains such as social communication, adaptive behaviour, play and language.
^p37963634-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Effects that shrink once reporter-rated and detection-biased outcomes are removed suggest that much of the apparent benefit rests on measures raters can influence; a surviving effect on diagnostic-characteristic measures is also not the same as an outcome autistic people prioritise.
^p37963634-caution-outcomes
