# Barriers to healthcare and a 'triple empathy problem' may lead to adverse outcomes for autistic adults: A qualitative study.

**Authors.** Shaw SC, Carravallah L, Johnson M, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37846479/)

**Published.** 2024 in Autism : the international journal of research and practice (volume 28, issue 7; first online 2023-10-17).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613231205629](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613231205629) · PMID [37846479](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37846479/) · PMCID [PMC11191657](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11191657/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Autistic adults' written accounts described five linked themes: early barriers, communication mismatch, doubt in themselves and from doctors, helplessness and fear, and healthcare avoidance with adverse health outcomes.
^p37846479-themes

The authors, all autistic and mostly medical doctors, built a model connecting these barriers to known health consequences and framed the encounter problem as a triple empathy problem.
^p37846479-model

## Scope as reported

Qualitative analysis of free-text survey data; the abstract does not give participant numbers, sampling frame or country breakdown.
^p37846479-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Free-text survey data show which barriers people report and how they connect them; they cannot estimate how often each barrier occurs or show that a barrier caused a specific health outcome.
