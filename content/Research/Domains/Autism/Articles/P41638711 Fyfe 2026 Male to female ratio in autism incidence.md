# Time trends in the male to female ratio for autism incidence: population based, prospectively collected, birth cohort study.

**Authors.** Fyfe C, Winell H, et al. (12 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41638711/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

By the end of follow-up 2.8% of the cohort had an autism diagnosis. Incidence rose through childhood and across calendar periods and birth cohorts, peaking in 2020-2022 at ages 10-14 in males and 15-19 in females.
^p41638711-incidence

The male-to-female ratio fell with later age at diagnosis and, beyond age 10, across calendar periods; in 2022 the cumulative ratio by age 20 was 1.2, with parity projected by 2024.
^p41638711-ratio

The authors suggest the ratio may be far lower than assumed and ask why females are diagnosed later.
^p41638711-conclusion

## Scope as reported

A registry cohort of every live birth in Sweden from 1985 to 2020, analysed by age, calendar period and birth cohort. The outcome is a recorded clinical diagnosis, not a screen of the whole population.
^p41638711-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A ratio of recorded diagnoses mixes any true sex difference in autism with differences in who is referred, recognised and diagnosed, and when; a ratio near parity by age 20 in one high-awareness country does not show equal underlying prevalence elsewhere.
^p41638711-caution-recognition
