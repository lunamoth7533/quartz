## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Responding to calls from autistic people and, more recently, non-autistic researchers, the review presents neurodiversity, in which autism is one variant among many kinds of mind, as a candidate alternative to the medical paradigm that has framed autism since its first description.
^p34730840-paradigm

It names three limits of conventional autism science: a heavy focus on deficits, attention to the individual rather than the context around them, and a narrow range of perspectives.
^p34730840-challenges

The authors argue that core elements of the neurodiversity paradigm can answer each limit and consider what the shift would mean for autism research.
^p34730840-implications

## Scope as reported

A narrative, position-taking review; it does not sample studies systematically or measure whether neurodiversity-aligned research changes outcomes.
^p34730840-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A paradigm argument changes which questions get asked and whose knowledge counts; whether the research it produces improves autistic people's lives is a separate empirical question that the review does not test.
^p34730840-caution-paradigm
