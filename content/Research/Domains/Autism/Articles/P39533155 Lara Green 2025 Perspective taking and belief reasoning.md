# Investigating visual perspective taking and belief reasoning in autistic adults: A pre-registered online study.

**Authors.** Lara Green R, Joanne Carrington S, Shaw DJ, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39533155/)

**Published.** 2025 in Autism : the international journal of research and practice (volume 29, issue 4; first online 2024-11-12).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613241290880](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613241290880) · PMID [39533155](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39533155/) · PMCID [PMC11967077](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11967077/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Autistic adults were not slower or less accurate than non-autistic adults at judging another person's visual perspective or belief, and they made significantly fewer errors, including fewer intrusions from the other's viewpoint.
^p39533155-performance

The clearest group difference was a steeper increase in response time as the angle between self and other grew in the autistic group.
^p39533155-rotation

The authors discuss the pattern as a difference in self-other control or strategy rather than a deficit.
^p39533155-interpretation

## Scope as reported

Online testing with self-reported diagnosis. The non-autistic group reproduced the task's known pattern of slower belief than perspective trials, which supports the task's behaviour in this sample.
^p39533155-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Self-reported diagnosis and an online, cognitively able sample limit generalisation; a pre-registered result on one task narrows, but does not settle, claims about mentalising in autistic adults in general.
^p39533155-caution-sample
