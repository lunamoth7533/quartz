# Prevalence of co-occurring conditions in children and adults with autism spectrum disorder: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Micai M, Fatta LM, Gila L, et al. (13 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37913872/)

**Published.** 2023 in Neuroscience and biobehavioral reviews (volume 155; first online 2023-10-31).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105436](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105436) · PMID [37913872](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37913872/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review pooled 340 publications with roughly 590,000 participants and estimated prevalence for 38 co-occurring conditions as point prevalence and 27 as lifetime prevalence.
^p37913872-scope

The most frequent point-prevalence conditions included developmental coordination disorder, sleep-wake problems, gastrointestinal problems, ADHD, anxiety disorders, overweight or obesity and feeding or eating disorders.
^p37913872-frequent

Estimates differed between children or adolescents and adults and between population or registry samples and clinical samples.
^p37913872-moderators

## Scope as reported

Random-effects meta-analysis with subgroup analyses by age group and study design; the search strategy drew on guidelines, diagnostic manuals, experts, carers and autistic people. The abstract does not report pooled percentages or heterogeneity statistics.
^p37913872-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Pooled prevalence across heterogeneous samples tells a service what to look for, not how common a condition is in a particular clinic; because estimates depend on design, clinical-sample figures should not be read as population rates.
^p37913872-caution-pooling
