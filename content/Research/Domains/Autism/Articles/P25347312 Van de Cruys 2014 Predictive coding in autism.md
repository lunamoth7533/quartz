# Precise minds in uncertain worlds: predictive coding in autism.

**Published.** 2014 in Psychological review (volume 121, issue 4).

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The authors argue that autistic people give prediction errors a uniformly high and inflexible weight, instead of learning when errors can be discounted as noise in a changing, uncertain world.
^p25347312-hippea

They propose that differences in executive function, theory of mind and central coherence follow from this one difference in processing violated expectations.
^p25347312-unifying

They read insistence on sameness and repetitive behaviour as attempts to secure a sense of predictive success.
^p25347312-sameness

## Scope as reported

A theoretical integration without new data.

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

An account that derives many features from one mechanism is attractive but hard to falsify unless it specifies which tasks should show inflexible error weighting and by how much.
