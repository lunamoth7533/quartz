# "Having All of Your Internal Resources Exhausted Beyond Measure and Being Left with No Clean-Up Crew": Defining Autistic Burnout.

**Authors.** Raymaker DM, Teo AR, Steckler NA, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32851204/)

**Published.** 2020 in Autism in adulthood (volume 2, issue 2; first online 2020-06-10).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1089/aut.2019.0079](https://doi.org/10.1089/aut.2019.0079) · PMID [32851204](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32851204/) · PMCID [PMC7313636](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7313636/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Autistic adults placed three things at the centre of autistic burnout: long-lasting exhaustion, lost skills, and a lowered tolerance for sensory input.
^p32851204-features

They attributed it to life stressors adding to a cumulative load while barriers to support blocked relief, until expectations exceeded abilities.
^p32851204-mechanism

The authors define it as a syndrome of pervasive, long-term (typically three months or more) exhaustion, loss of function and reduced stimulus tolerance, and judge it distinct from occupational burnout and clinical depression.
^p32851204-definition

Participants linked recovery with acceptance and social support, time off or lowered expectations, and doing things in an autistic way, including unmasking.
^p32851204-recovery

## Scope as reported

An exploratory study with a small convenience sample. The authors note that the concept, much discussed by autistic adults, was almost absent from academic and clinical literature, that few participants were not white or had higher support needs, and that measurement, prevention and treatment still need research.
^p32851204-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A definition built from lived experience establishes what the construct should capture; its separation from depression and occupational burnout is a proposal from these accounts, and testing it needs measures and comparison samples.
^p32851204-caution-distinct
