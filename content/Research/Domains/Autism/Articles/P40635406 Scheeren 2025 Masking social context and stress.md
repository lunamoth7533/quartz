# Masking, social context and perceived stress in autistic adults: An ecological momentary assessment study.

**Authors.** Scheeren AM, Nieuwenhuis S, Crane L, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40635406/)

**Published.** 2025 in Autism : the international journal of research and practice (volume 29, issue 12; first online 2025-07-09).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613251353358](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613251353358) · PMID [40635406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40635406/) · PMCID [PMC12618727](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12618727/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Over 28 days of smartphone reports, 87 autistic adults masked less when alone than with others, and more with non-autistic company than with autistic company.
^p40635406-context

Periods with more masking were accompanied by higher perceived stress at the same time.
^p40635406-stress

Participants reported that they could be more themselves among autistic peers.
^p40635406-peers

## Scope as reported

Repeated real-time reports over four weeks reduce the recall problems of retrospective surveys; masking and stress were both self-rated for the same periods.
^p40635406-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Concurrent within-person associations show that masking and stress rise together in daily life; they do not show which comes first, since a stressful situation may both demand masking and raise stress.
^p40635406-caution-concurrent
