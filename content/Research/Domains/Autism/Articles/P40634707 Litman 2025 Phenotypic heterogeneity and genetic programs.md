# Decomposition of phenotypic heterogeneity in autism reveals underlying genetic programs.

**Authors.** Litman A, Sauerwald N, Green Snyder L, et al. (9 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40634707/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Generative mixture modelling of core, associated and co-occurring traits in a large genotyped autism cohort identified robust, clinically relevant classes, which were validated and replicated in an independent cohort.
^p40634707-classes

Each class showed its own pattern of common, de novo and inherited variation and its own set of disrupted biological pathways.
^p40634707-genetics

Classes differed in the developmental timing of affected genes, in line with their differences in clinical outcome.
^p40634707-timing

## Scope as reported

A modelling study that links data-driven phenotypic classes in children with autism to genetic and molecular programmes. The abstract gives no class count, sample size or effect size, and the authors present the biological findings as mechanistic hypotheses.
^p40634707-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Mixture-model classes are statistical groupings whose number and boundaries depend on the traits entered and the sample; they describe population structure and generate hypotheses, not categories that assign an individual's biology or prognosis.
