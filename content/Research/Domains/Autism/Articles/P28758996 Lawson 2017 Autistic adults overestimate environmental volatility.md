# Adults with autism overestimate the volatility of the sensory environment.

**Authors.** Lawson RP, Mathys C, Rees G. (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28758996/)

**Published.** 2017 in Nature neuroscience (volume 20, issue 9; first online 2017-07-31).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/nn.4615](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4615) · PMID [28758996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28758996/) · PMCID [PMC5578436](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5578436/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Behavioural and pupil measures showed autistic adults were less surprised than neurotypical adults when expectations were violated, and lower surprise went with greater symptom severity.
^p28758996-surprise

A hierarchical Bayesian learning model suggested that autistic adults over-learned about volatility when the environment changed, which reduced learning from probabilistically unexpected events.
^p28758996-volatility

Modelled surprise tracked pupil size in the autistic group, which the authors read as heightened noradrenergic responsivity consistent with altered neural gain.
^p28758996-pupil

## Scope as reported

One associative-learning paradigm in adults combining behaviour, pupillometry and a hierarchical Bayesian learning model.
^p28758996-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Model-based conclusions depend on the model family compared; a volatility-learning difference in one task is a specific, testable claim, not evidence for predictive-processing accounts of autism in general.
