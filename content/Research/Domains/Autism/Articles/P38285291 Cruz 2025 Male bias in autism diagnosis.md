# Is There a Bias Towards Males in the Diagnosis of Autism? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

**Authors.** Cruz S, Zubizarreta SC, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38285291/)

**Published.** 2025 in Neuropsychology review (first online 2024-01-29).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1007/s11065-023-09630-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11065-023-09630-2) · PMID [38285291](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38285291/) · PMCID [PMC11965184](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11965184/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across 67 studies, autistic males scored as more affected than autistic females on standard clinical measures of core features and social interaction, whereas females had more cognitive and behavioural difficulties.
^p38285291-phenotype

In a second meta-analysis of 10 studies, females reported using more compensation and masking strategies than males.
^p38285291-camouflage

The authors read these results as support for a male bias in clinical procedures and argue that diagnosis should consider a distinct female presentation, possibly shaped by camouflaging.
^p38285291-conclusion

## Scope as reported

Two meta-analyses drawing on four databases; the camouflaging analysis rests on 10 studies. The abstract reports no pooled effect sizes, heterogeneity statistics or risk-of-bias grading.
^p38285291-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Instruments developed and validated mainly in male samples may measure females differently; without invariance testing, a lower female score on a standard measure cannot be read as milder autism, and a sex difference on it may partly reflect the instrument.
^p38285291-caution-instruments
