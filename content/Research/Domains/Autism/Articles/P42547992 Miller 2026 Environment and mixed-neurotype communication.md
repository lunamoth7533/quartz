# Navigating Mixed-Neurotype Dialogue: Environmental and Affective Influences on Autistic Speakers.

**Authors.** Miller ZA, Conner CM, Chang JC, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42547992/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Most respondents said crowding, noise and familiarity affected their communication at least somewhat; noisy or unpredictable settings were tied to word loss, scripting and withdrawal, quiet familiar spaces to openness.
^p42547992-environment

Conversations with non-autistic partners were described as needing more conscious effort, masking and cognitive load than conversations with autistic partners.
^p42547992-partner

Stress, frustration and anxiety were reported to make communication harder with either partner, and calm or happy states to make it easier.
^p42547992-emotion

## Scope as reported

Retrospective self-reports of past conversations by young autistic adults recruited to an online survey, with no observed interaction and no comparison group.
^p42547992-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Recalled experience shows which conditions autistic adults link to easier or harder communication; it does not measure communication quality, and a young, survey-responding sample may not represent people who need more support.
^p42547992-caution-recall
