# Confirming the nature of autistic burnout.

**Authors.** Arnold SR, Higgins JM, Weise J, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36637293/)

**Published.** 2023 in Autism : the international journal of research and practice (volume 27, issue 7; first online 2023-01-13).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613221147410](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613221147410) · PMID [36637293](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36637293/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Among 141 autistic adults who had experienced autistic burnout, participants strongly agreed with the definition published by Higgins and colleagues when two published definitions were compared.
^p36637293-definition

How long and how often burnout lasts remained unclear, because participants described both short and long episodes.
^p36637293-duration

Exhaustion and a need to withdraw from people and from autism-unfriendly places were prominent.
^p36637293-features

Many participants reported having previously been misdiagnosed with conditions such as depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder or borderline personality disorder.
^p36637293-misdiagnosis

## Scope as reported

An online survey of people with self-identified burnout experience. The abstract available through PubMed is a plain-language summary, and the authors call for larger studies.
^p36637293-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Agreement with a definition among people who already identify with the construct supports its face validity; reported misdiagnoses are recalled experiences and do not establish how often burnout is mistaken for other conditions.
^p36637293-caution-selfselection
