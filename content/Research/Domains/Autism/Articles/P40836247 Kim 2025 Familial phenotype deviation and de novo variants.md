# Evaluation of familial phenotype deviation to measure the impact of de novo mutations in autism.

**Authors.** Kim SW, Lee H, Song DY, et al. (16 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40836247/)

**Published.** 2025 in Genome medicine (volume 17, issue 1; first online 2025-08-20).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1186/s13073-025-01532-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13073-025-01532-7) · PMID [40836247](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40836247/) · PMCID [PMC12366145](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12366145/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

When phenotype scores were expressed relative to unaffected family members, probands carrying disruptive de novo variants showed more behavioural symptoms and lower adaptive functioning than their relatives.
^p40836247-within-family

This family-relative measure gave clearer associations with de novo variants than raw scores and increased gene discovery, adding 18 newly associated genes.
^p40836247-discovery

Eleven genes showed high variability in phenotypic effect between families, which the authors relate to where mutations fall within functional domains or exons.
^p40836247-variability

## Scope as reported

Analysis of existing autism cohorts in which unaffected family members' scores are subtracted before standardising; the abstract does not give effect sizes for the symptom and adaptive-functioning differences.
^p40836247-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Family-relative scores reduce confounding by family background but still describe average effects of variant classes; the between-family variability the study reports is itself evidence that a given variant does not fix a given presentation.
^p40836247-caution-individual
