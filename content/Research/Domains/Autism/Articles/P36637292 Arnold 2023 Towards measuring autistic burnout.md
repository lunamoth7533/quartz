# Towards the measurement of autistic burnout.

**Authors.** Arnold SR, Higgins JM, Weise J, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36637292/)

**Published.** 2023 in Autism : the international journal of research and practice (volume 27, issue 7; first online 2023-01-13).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613221147401](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613221147401) · PMID [36637292](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36637292/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In 141 autistic adults with experience of burnout, autistic burnout was associated with both masking and depression.
^p36637292-links

The AASPIRE Autistic Burnout Measure correlated with depression but not with masking, and it was poor at distinguishing people currently in burnout from those describing a past episode.
^p36637292-abm

Newly drafted Autistic Burnout Severity Items may not combine validly from their subscales into one score.
^p36637292-severity

The authors summarise burnout as exhaustion, withdrawal and problems with concentration and thinking, linked to everyday stress.
^p36637292-profile

## Scope as reported

An online survey developed with an autistic researcher. The abstract available through PubMed is a plain-language summary, and the authors call for more work on telling burnout apart from other conditions and on assessment tools.
^p36637292-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Early psychometric work in a self-selected online sample shows the construct can be measured, but not yet cleanly; a scale that tracks depression more than masking raises the question of whether it measures burnout specifically.
^p36637292-caution-validity
