# Investigating the Outcomes and Quality of Support Programs for Autistic Young Adults in Higher Education: A Systematic Review.

**Authors.** Koops I, Duckett P, Gerace A. (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41050147/)

**Published.** 2025 in Autism in adulthood (volume 7, issue 4; first online 2025-08-11).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1089/aut.2024.0078](https://doi.org/10.1089/aut.2024.0078) · PMID [41050147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41050147/) · PMCID [PMC12493019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12493019/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across 16 studies, mentoring was the most common support; mentored students showed gains in well-being and academic outcomes such as connection, belonging and academic self-efficacy.
^p41050147-mentoring

Multimodal programmes with a psychosocial focus were associated with less anxiety and loneliness and higher self-esteem.
^p41050147-multimodal

The authors recommend co-designed programmes tailored to individual students, with a focus on environmental and systemic change.
^p41050147-recommendation

## Scope as reported

Sixteen studies appraised with the Joanna Briggs Institute tool and rated against Australasian Society for Autism Research quality criteria; the abstract reports no pooled effect sizes and no count of controlled designs.
^p41050147-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Without pooled estimates or clear control conditions, the review shows which supports have been evaluated and in which direction outcomes moved, not how large or lasting the benefits are.
^p41050147-caution-effects
