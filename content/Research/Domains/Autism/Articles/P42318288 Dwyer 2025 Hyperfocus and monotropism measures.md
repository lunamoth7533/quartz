# Investigating autistic hyperfocus and monotropism: Limited convergence of event-related potentials, laboratory tasks, and questionnaire responses.

**Authors.** Dwyer P, Sillas A, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42318288/)

**Published.** 2025 in Research in autism (first online 2025-08-16).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.reia.2025.202683](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.reia.2025.202683) · PMID [42318288](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42318288/) · PMCID [PMC13274634](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13274634/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The study frames monotropism as an autistic-developed account that characterises autism by hyperfocus on interests, and tests it against self-report, caregiver-report and laboratory measures.
^p42318288-account

Autistic adolescents and their caregivers reported more hyperfocus than comparison families, and both judged its overall effect modestly positive.
^p42318288-questionnaires

Event-related potential markers of hyperfocus and cross-modal attention capture did not differ between groups; autistic participants had lower visual working memory capacity, possibly reflecting difficulty attending to several targets.
^p42318288-lab

Attention measures of different kinds were mostly unrelated, and self-reported hyperfocus went with misophonia and sensory hyperreactivity.
^p42318288-convergence

## Scope as reported

A small cross-sectional study; the authors note that controlled laboratory tasks with explicit instructions may not capture everyday autistic attention and call for work aligning laboratory and questionnaire measures.
^p42318288-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

With 40 participants, the absence of laboratory group differences is weak evidence of no difference; the firmer result is that questionnaire and laboratory measures of hyperfocus do not yet index the same thing.
^p42318288-caution-power
