# [Autism Spectrum Disorders in Adulthood: Empirical Findings on Comorbidities, Differential Diagnoses, and the Clinical Utility of the AQ].

**Authors.** Sappok T, Bayer M, Tarasova D, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37160157/)

**Published.** 2023 in Psychiatrische Praxis (volume 50, issue 7; first online 2023-05-09).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1055/a-2044-8540](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2044-8540) · PMID [37160157](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37160157/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Among 106 adults referred with suspected autism, affective disorders were common whether or not autism was diagnosed (48%), and phobias (33%) and personality disorders (22%) were other frequent differential diagnoses.
^p37160157-differentials

Against an interdisciplinary case-conference judgement, the AQ hardly separated adults with and without autism (area under the curve 0.527; sensitivity 70%, specificity 35%).
^p37160157-aq

The authors favour interdisciplinary assessment with standardised scales over AQ-based screening in this setting.
^p37160157-conclusion

## Scope as reported

A single German outpatient sample of 106 referred adults with differential diagnoses and comorbidities recorded on standardised scales; the reference standard was an interdisciplinary case conference. The article is in German and its English abstract was checked.
^p37160157-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Screening accuracy in a referred sample, where most people already show autistic-like features, is expected to be lower than in a community sample; the result bears on triage inside specialist clinics, not on population screening.
^p37160157-caution-spectrum
