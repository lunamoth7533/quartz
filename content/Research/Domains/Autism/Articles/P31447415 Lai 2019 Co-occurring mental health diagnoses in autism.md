# Prevalence of co-occurring mental health diagnoses in the autism population: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Lai MC, Kassee C, et al. (8 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31447415/)

**Published.** 2019 in The lancet. Psychiatry (first online 2019-08-22).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/S2215-0366(19)30289-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(19)30289-5) · PMID [31447415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31447415/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Pooled prevalence of clinically diagnosed conditions in autistic people was about 28% for ADHD, 20% for anxiety disorders, 13% for sleep-wake disorders, 12% for disruptive, impulse-control and conduct disorders, 11% for depressive disorders, 9% for obsessive-compulsive disorder, 5% for bipolar disorders and 4% for schizophrenia spectrum disorders.
^p31447415-rates

Clinical-sample studies gave higher figures than population or registry studies, most estimates exceeded general-population figures in a post hoc comparison, and age, gender, intellectual functioning and country explained some but far from all of the variation.
^p31447415-moderators

## Scope as reported

Random-effects meta-analysis restricted to studies confirming both autism and the co-occurring condition with DSM or ICD criteria; trauma- and substance-related conditions and gender dysphoria were summarised narratively. Unexplained heterogeneity stayed very high (all I² above 95%).
^p31447415-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Pooled rates across very different samples show what assessment should look for, not the chance that a particular autistic person has a condition; and because features overlap, a condition may be missed or double-counted depending on who assessed and how.
^p31447415-caution-overlap
