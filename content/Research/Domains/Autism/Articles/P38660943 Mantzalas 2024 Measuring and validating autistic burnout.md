# Measuring and validating autistic burnout.

**Authors.** Mantzalas J, Richdale AL, Li X, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38660943/)

**Published.** 2024 in Autism research : official journal of the International Society for Autism Research (volume 17, issue 7; first online 2024-04-25).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/aur.3129](https://doi.org/10.1002/aur.3129) · PMID [38660943](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38660943/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In 238 autistic adults, factor analyses gave a four-factor structure for the 27-item Autistic Burnout Measure with an overarching burnout construct, and a two-subscale structure for the Copenhagen personal burnout scale.
^p38660943-structure

Both measures correlated strongly with depression, anxiety, stress and fatigue, but only moderately with camouflaging and well-being.
^p38660943-correlates

Tests of overlap with depression gave mixed results; the authors judge the Autistic Burnout Measure and the Copenhagen emotional-exhaustion subscale valid preliminary screening tools.
^p38660943-overlap

## Scope as reported

A single sample of 238. The authors call for larger and more diverse autistic samples to test the measure further and to clarify how burnout relates to depression and masking.
^p38660943-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Strong correlations with depression and fatigue are as consistent with overlap as with a distinct construct; until discriminant validity is shown in independent samples, scores are best read as the severity of a burnout-like state, not as a diagnosis.
^p38660943-caution-overlap
