# Priorities and Perspectives Regarding Goals and Outcomes of Support for Autistic Children Under 12 Years: A Systematic Review.

**Authors.** Jordan P, Waddington H, Hammond M, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42003394/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across 15 studies, communication, social inclusion and child well-being were priorities shared by autistic people, family members and professionals.
^p42003394-shared

Professionals leaned toward normative targets such as acquiring skills and complying with behavioural expectations, while autistic people and families gave more weight to flexibility, self-advocacy and building on strengths.
^p42003394-differences

The authors call for co-design with autistic people and families, cultural responsiveness, and tools that assess neurodiversity-affirming outcomes consistently.
^p42003394-recommendations

## Scope as reported

Fifteen studies published over roughly the past decade, appraised with Joanna Briggs Institute checklists; a synthesis of stated priorities rather than of intervention effects.
^p42003394-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The review maps whose goals differ and how; it does not show which goals lead to better long-term lives, which is a separate empirical question.
^p42003394-caution-priorities
