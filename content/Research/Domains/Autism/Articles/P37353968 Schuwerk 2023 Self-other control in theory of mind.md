# Differences in self-other control as cognitive mechanism to characterize theory of mind reasoning in autistic and non-autistic adults.

**Authors.** Schuwerk T, Sodian B. (2 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37353968/)

**Published.** 2023 in Autism research : official journal of the International Society for Autism Research (volume 16, issue 9; first online 2023-06-23).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/aur.2976](https://doi.org/10.1002/aur.2976) · PMID [37353968](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37353968/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across repeated unexpected-location false-belief trials, autistic adults showed relatively more difficulty flexibly taking the other person's perspective and less interference from that perspective when reporting their own.
^p37353968-selfother

The authors interpret this as altered self-other control and conclude that theory-of-mind differences in autistic adults exist but are subtle.
^p37353968-subtle

## Scope as reported

A laboratory false-belief paradigm scored on accuracy and reaction time; the abstract reports neither sample sizes nor effect sizes.
^p37353968-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A reaction-time and accuracy pattern in one task identifies a candidate mechanism; whether it relates to everyday interaction difficulties is an inference the task does not test.
^p37353968-caution-task
