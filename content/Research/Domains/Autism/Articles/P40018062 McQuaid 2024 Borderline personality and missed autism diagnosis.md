## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The authors argue that autistic girls and women without intellectual disability tend to be diagnosed later, often after one or more misdiagnoses, with borderline personality disorder or borderline traits a particularly common mislabel.
^p40018062-misdiagnosis

They describe presentations more common in autistic women that are prone to being read as borderline personality, set out distinctions between the two, and note that similar behaviour can have different underlying drivers.
^p40018062-distinctions

## Scope as reported

A conceptual analysis of existing literature focused on cisgender girls and women; it does not estimate how often misdiagnosis occurs.
^p40018062-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A conceptual review can show why two presentations are confusable and what to examine; it cannot supply diagnostic accuracy figures, and its proposed distinctions need testing in clinical samples.
