# Suboptimal but intact integration of Bayesian components during perceptual decision-making in autism.

**Authors.** Fazioli L, Hadad BS, Denison RN, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39806435/)

**Published.** 2025 in Molecular autism (volume 16, issue 1; first online 2025-01-13).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1186/s13229-025-00639-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13229-025-00639-3) · PMID [39806435](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39806435/) · PMCID [PMC11731163](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11731163/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In an orientation categorisation task with 126 participants, autistic adults integrated prior probability and sensory uncertainty into their decisions, indistinguishably from non-autistic adults.
^p39806435-intact

Both groups shifted their decision criteria in a suboptimal way.
^p39806435-suboptimal

The authors take this as strong evidence against a general, basic alteration of prior integration in autism.
^p39806435-conclusion

## Scope as reported

Explicit priors in a perceptual categorisation task with adults described as high-functioning. The authors state that the design cannot rule out reduced prior effects for implicitly learned priors, social decisions or other levels of functioning.
^p39806435-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A clean null for explicit priors narrows the hypo-prior claim but leaves implicitly learned priors open, as the authors themselves note.
^p39806435-caution-explicit
