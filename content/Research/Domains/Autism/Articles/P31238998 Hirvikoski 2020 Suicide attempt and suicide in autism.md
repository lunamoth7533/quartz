# Individual risk and familial liability for suicide attempt and suicide in autism: a population-based study.

**Authors.** Hirvikoski T, Boman M, et al. (8 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31238998/)

**Published.** 2020 in Psychological medicine (first online 2019-06-26).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1017/S0033291719001405](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291719001405) · PMID [31238998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31238998/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Compared with matched controls, autistic people without intellectual disability who also had ADHD showed the highest risk of suicide attempt (odds ratio 7.25) and suicide death (13.09); risk was raised less in autistic people with intellectual disability (suicide 2.31).
^p31238998-adhd

Without intellectual disability, autistic females generally had higher risk than autistic males, and in both sexes risk peaked with co-occurring ADHD.
^p31238998-sex

Adjusting for other psychiatric diagnoses weakened the associations, and relatives of autistic people also had raised risk of suicidal behaviour.
^p31238998-comorbidity

## Scope as reported

Conditional logistic regression on Swedish register data linking diagnoses, suicide attempts recorded in care and suicide deaths; relatives of autistic people were compared with control relatives.
^p31238998-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Registers capture diagnosed autism and attempts that reached care, so undiagnosed autism and unrecorded attempts are missing; large relative risks also sit alongside low absolute risk for most individuals, which limits their use for forecasting any one person's outcome.
^p31238998-caution-register
