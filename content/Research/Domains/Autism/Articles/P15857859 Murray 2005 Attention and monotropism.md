## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The authors propose that atypical ways of allocating attention are central to autism.
^p15857859-attention

Drawing on research, the DSM-IV and ICD-10 criteria and autistic first-person accounts, including one author's own, they argue that the first two diagnostic criteria derive from the narrowed interests described in the third.
^p15857859-criteria

## Scope as reported

A theoretical argument built from published research, diagnostic manuals and personal experience; the abstract reports no systematic search and no new data, and it points to implications for practice.
^p15857859-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Showing that the diagnostic criteria can be derived from one attentional principle makes the account coherent; it is not evidence that the principle causes the features, which needs measures and predictions that could fail.
^p15857859-caution-derivation
