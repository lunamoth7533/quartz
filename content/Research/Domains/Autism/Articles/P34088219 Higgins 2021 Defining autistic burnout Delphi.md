# Defining autistic burnout through experts by lived experience: Grounded Delphi method investigating \#AutisticBurnout.

**Authors.** Higgins JM, Arnold SR, Weise J, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34088219/)

**Published.** 2021 in Autism : the international journal of research and practice (volume 25, issue 8; first online 2021-06-04).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/13623613211019858](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613211019858) · PMID [34088219](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34088219/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Using a grounded Delphi method with autistic adults treated as experts, the study co-produced a definition of autistic burnout built from exhaustion, social withdrawal, difficulty thinking, a drop in everyday living skills and more visible autistic traits.
^p34088219-definition

The authors emphasise that clinicians should recognise autistic burnout as different from depression and call for studies that test whether it can be told apart from depression, chronic fatigue and the burnout described in non-autistic people.
^p34088219-depression

## Scope as reported

A consensus method in which autistic voices led the definition. The abstract notes that only one earlier study existed and that nobody had yet shown it to be distinct from depression, chronic fatigue or burnout in non-autistic people.
^p34088219-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Consensus among people who identify with a construct establishes its lived-experience definition; it does not establish discriminant validity, which needs burnout, depression and fatigue measured in the same people.
^p34088219-caution-consensus
