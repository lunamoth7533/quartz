# Psychology Map

The psychology layer covers attention, learning, memory, emotion regulation and the therapy evidence that uses them.

**Contents.** [[#Reference overview|Overview]], [[#Reference spine|Reference spine]], [[#Concept register|Concept register]], [[#Where this domain connects|Cross-domain links]], [[#Evidence boundaries|Evidence boundaries]], [[#Learning route|Learning route]], [[#Core sequence|Core sequence]]

## Reference overview

Psychology in this library covers the processes that sit between a stimulus and a response -
perception, attention, memory, learning, emotion, motivation, language, social cognition and
personality - and the treatment models built on them. The domain's discipline is measurement:
almost every concept here is operationalised through tasks, ratings or self-report, and each of
those brings its own error structure.

**Attention and executive control are constructs built from tasks.** Selective attention, working
memory and inhibitory control show up as correlated but dissociable factors, which is why a single
task score cannot be read as a general capacity. Laboratory measures correlate weakly with everyday
function, and the weak correlation is a finding rather than a nuisance. [[Attention]] [[Working memory]] [[Attention and executive function]]

**Learning and memory are plural.** Conditioning, habituation and habit formation obey different
rules from episodic recall; procedural and episodic systems depend on partly different circuits;
and retrieval is reconstructive rather than a replay. Confusing the types produces most of the
errors in everyday memory talk. [[Learning and conditioning]] [[Memory processes]] [[Episodic memory]] [[Procedural memory and habit]]

**Emotion and motivation are processes, not states.** Emotion regulation names the strategies that
modify which emotion arises, when it peaks and how it is expressed; motivation and reward research
distinguishes anticipatory value from consummatory pleasure and from learning signals. Both
literatures warn against treating self-report, physiology and behaviour as interchangeable
measures of one underlying quantity. [[Emotion regulation]] [[Motivation and reward]]
[[Decision-making]]

**Social and linguistic capacities are developmentally embedded.** Language and social cognition
develop within relationships; attachment and temperament frameworks describe the developmental
scaffolding; personality models describe stable individual differences at the level of traits
rather than categories. [[Language]] [[Social cognition]] [[Developmental perspectives and attachment]] [[Personality models]]

**Therapy models are testable claims about mechanisms.** Behavioural and cognitive approaches
specify what they change; psychodynamic and humanistic approaches specify relational and
self-directed processes; and the evidence varies by orientation and condition. In the adult-ADHD
trials reviewed by Cochrane, estimates depended on the comparator - large against waiting list,
not statistically significant against supportive psychotherapy - and certainty stayed low or very
low, which is the pattern that makes 'does psychotherapy work' an under-specified question.
[[Therapy models overview]] [[CBT and its evidence base]]

**Evidence boundaries.** Measurement problems are structural, not incidental: blinding participants
to a psychological treatment is impossible, expectancy and allegiance effects are built in, raters
disagree systematically in both directions, and laboratory tasks are proxies for constructs rather
than the constructs themselves. [[Measurement validity and reliability]] [[Rater and measurement effects in ADHD]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

**Where to start.** [[Attention and executive function]] and [[Memory processes]] for the cognitive core, [[Emotion regulation]] for affect, and [[CBT and its evidence base]] for how psychological treatments are tested. ## Reference spine

Psychology here covers the processes between neural activity and behaviour - perception, attention, learning, memory, emotion, motivation, language, social cognition - together with the developmental and therapeutic frameworks that organise them.

Perception begins with detection and ends with interpretation: sensation is receptor-level detection while perception is construction, which is why illusions are evidence about processing rather than curiosities. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-sensation|Sensation versus Perception]] [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-illusions|Sensation versus Perception]] Attention selects what is processed further, and its limits appear as interference and switching costs. [[F109 Neuroscience Online association and executive processing#^f109-areas|Association and Executive Processing]]

Learning has three well-characterised routes: classical conditioning gives cues predictive value, operant conditioning shapes behaviour through consequences, and observational learning transmits behaviour without direct reinforcement. [[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-pairing|Classical Conditioning]] [[F39 OpenStax Psychology 2e operant conditioning#^f39-consequences|Operant Conditioning]] [[F99 OpenStax Psychology 2e observational learning#^f99-modeling|Observational Learning]] Memory is best described through encoding, storage and retrieval across systems, with reconstruction treated as a normal property rather than a failure. [[F41 OpenStax Psychology 2e how memory functions#^f41-three|How Memory Functions]] [[F11 Noba Memory encoding storage retrieval#^f11-reconstructive|Memory encoding storage retrieval]]

Motivation and emotion supply the value and state context. Expectancy and self-efficacy shape persistence, and emotion is described through physiological, cognitive, behavioural and expressive components that do not always agree. [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-efficacy|Motivation]] [[F44 OpenStax Psychology 2e emotion#^f44-components|Emotion]] Social cognition adds the interpretation of other minds and the situational emphasis that distinguishes social psychology from dispositional explanation. [[F46 OpenStax Psychology 2e social psychology#^f46-attribution|What Is Social Psychology?]]

Therapy models apply these processes deliberately: exposure uses extinction logic, cognitive work targets appraisals, and combined treatment is common rather than exceptional. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-behavioural|Types of Treatment]] [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-biomedical|Types of Treatment]]

## Evidence gaps

- Many trials measure symptoms over weeks, leaving durability and function under-studied.
- Psychological constructs overlap, so instruments often measure related things under different names.
