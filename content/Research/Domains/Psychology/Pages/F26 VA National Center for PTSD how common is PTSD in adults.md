# How Common is PTSD in Adults?

**Publisher.** US Department of Veterans Affairs, National Center for PTSD · [PTSD by the numbers](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand/common/common_adults.asp)

**Source.** <https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand/common/common_adults.asp> (page states last updated September 2026)

**Verification.** Official page opened and checked on 2026-09-24; the blocks below paraphrase its prevalence section. The page reports U.S. population estimates, not individual risk.

## Key points (as published)

Most people experience a traumatic event, and most who do will not develop PTSD.
^f26-exposure

To be at risk, the event must be shocking and dangerous, involving perceived threat to life or safety; not every stressor qualifies.
^f26-requirement

Best U.S. estimates are about six in one hundred adults over a lifetime and five in one hundred in a given year.
^f26-prevalence

Direct exposure or injury is associated with higher risk, and men and women tend to experience different types of traumatic events.
^f26-risk

Precise counts are difficult because no single study covers everyone and estimates change with events.
^f26-limits

## Scope and limitations

An official public education page about U.S. adult prevalence. It reports population estimates and risk factors, not a diagnostic test, and the figures describe the United States rather than any other country or any individual.
^f26-scope

## Analyst cautions (AI synthesis)

These paragraphs are this library's own appraisal, not statements from the VA.

Population estimates cannot tell anyone whether they have PTSD; assessment requires the diagnostic criteria applied by a qualified clinician, and exposure is a precondition rather than a diagnosis.
^f26-caution
