# Learning and conditioning

**Definition.** Conditioning research explains how cues and consequences shape behaviour and why some associations persist after they stop being useful. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-classical|Conditioning and Learning]]

## Supported claims

- Classical conditioning pairs a neutral cue with a meaningful stimulus until the cue evokes a response. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-classical|Conditioning and Learning]]
- Reinforcement makes behaviour more likely and punishment less likely, with different side effects. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-operant|Conditioning and Learning]]
- Extinction is new learning that inhibits an old association rather than erasure, which is why responses can return. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-extinction|Conditioning and Learning]]

## How it works

**Classical conditioning.** A neutral cue paired with a meaningful stimulus comes to evoke the response, and the strength of that association depends on contingency, timing and salience. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-classical|Conditioning and learning]] [[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-pairing|Classical Conditioning]] Contingency, not contiguity, is the modern core: mere pairing is insufficient, because learning tracks how predictive the cue is, so a cue that appears equally often without the outcome produces little or no learning. That contingency requirement is why the paradigm is the clearest experimental demonstration that organisms learn predictive structure rather than merely responding to stimuli, why extinction is new learning rather than unlearning, and why the paradigm generalises to cue-competition effects that simple association models missed. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-classical|Conditioning and learning]]

**Operant conditioning.** Consequences change behaviour: reinforcement increases the likelihood of a behaviour and punishment decreases it, with schedule, delay and magnitude all affecting the result. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-operant|Conditioning and learning]] [[F39 OpenStax Psychology 2e operant conditioning#^f39-consequences|Operant Conditioning]] Reinforcement schedules produce characteristic response patterns - variable schedules produce persistence that outlasts fixed ones - which is the behaviour-level reason some behaviours resist extinction and why schedule is part of any complete operant account. [[F39 OpenStax Psychology 2e operant conditioning#^f39-schedules|Operant Conditioning]] Because reinforcement can be delivered contingently and measured, this paradigm underwrites most behavioural treatment logic. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-operant|Conditioning and learning]]

**Extinction is new learning.** Extinction reduces the response when the cue no longer predicts the outcome, but it does not erase the original association: the conditioned response can return with time, context change or re-exposure to the outcome, which is the evidence that extinction is new learning rather than erasure and why relapse after successful exposure treatment is expected rather than anomalous. The mechanisms are developed in [[Fear learning and extinction]]. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-extinction|Conditioning and learning]] [[F38 OpenStax Psychology 2e classical conditioning#^f38-extinction|Classical Conditioning]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-newlearning|Fear extinction]]

**Other routes to learning.** The two paradigms describe different relations (stimulus-stimulus and behaviour-consequence), and neither covers all learning: habituation, observational learning and skill acquisition each have their own accounts. Observational learning acquires behaviour by watching others, through attention, retention, reproduction and motivation. [[F99 OpenStax Psychology 2e observational learning#^f99-modeling|Observational Learning]] [[F99 OpenStax Psychology 2e observational learning#^f99-steps|Observational Learning]]

**Where the paradigms stop.** Contingency learning in humans also involves instruction, inference and social context, so behaviour that looks like conditioning may be rule-governed; and the laboratory preparations used to establish the mechanisms are simpler than the situations to which they are applied. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-limit|Conditioning and learning]] [[Procedural memory and habit]] Pure behaviourism gave way to cognitive accounts that include expectations and representations, and the conditioning paradigms themselves became tools for studying those representations - superseded and absorbed rather than discarded. [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-extinction|Conditioning and learning]] [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-limit|Conditioning and learning]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Pavlovian vocabulary is often stretched over complex behaviour; real reinforcement schedules are messy and hard to observe.
