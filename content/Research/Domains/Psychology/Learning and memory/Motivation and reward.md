# Motivation and reward

## Definition

Motivation concerns the initiation and persistence of goal-directed behaviour. Reward describes outcomes that increase the likelihood of the behaviour that produced them, together with the subjective experience of wanting and liking.

## How it works

**The psychological traditions.** Motivation is described through several traditions - instinct and drive theories such as drive reduction, arousal theory and expectancy accounts - and the traditions differ in what they treat as the explanatory unit. [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-theories|Motivation]]

**Expectancy-value is the bridge construct.** Self-efficacy and goal-setting research links beliefs about capability to persistence and performance: beliefs about capability and beliefs about outcome value jointly predict effort and persistence, which is why expectancy-value models organise the motivation literature and why interventions that change only one arm - capability beliefs or outcome value - often underperform. The construct is also where motivation meets clinical presentation: apathy, anhedonia and amotivation are describable as changes in the expectancy or value arms, which is more precise than a global "motivation deficit". [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-efficacy|Motivation]]

**Social motives.** Belonging and achievement function as organised motivations rather than separate drives, which matters when motivation is described clinically. [[F43 OpenStax Psychology 2e motivation#^f43-social|Motivation]]

**Reinforcement.** Operant conditioning changes behaviour through consequences: reinforcement increases and punishment decreases the likelihood of a behaviour, with schedules producing characteristic persistence. That is the learning-level account of why a rewarding outcome shapes what a person does next. [[F39 OpenStax Psychology 2e operant conditioning#^f39-consequences|Operant Conditioning]] [[F10 Noba Conditioning and learning#^f10-operant|Conditioning and Learning]]

**The neural candidate and its limits.** Midbrain dopamine neurons respond to reward-prediction error, which maps onto reinforcement learning's teaching signal. But dopamine carries additional and heterogeneous signals, including salience and movement-related information, and different populations carry different signals, so population identity matters and 'reward' is an approximation rather than a complete description. [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-rpe|Reward prediction error]] [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-scope|Reward prediction error]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-heterogeneity|What does dopamine mean?]]

**Wanting, liking and learning come apart.** The dopamine literature separates the appetitive pull of a cue from the pleasure of consumption and from the learning signal itself; they co-occur in healthy cases, which hides their separability, but pharmacology and addiction research split them cleanly. That split is why "reward" is not one thing in this library: a reward-prediction error is a learning signal, and treating it as a happiness meter is a category error the primary literature itself warns against. [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-rpe|Reward prediction error]] [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-complexity|What does dopamine mean?]]

**The pharmacology shows the same structure.** Drugs can flood reward circuitry with dopamine, producing effects much larger or faster than natural rewards, and repeated exposure changes circuits involved in reward, stress and self-control - while response varies with the drug, route, amount, genetics and environment, so no single pathway explains all substance-use problems. [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-reward|Drugs and the Brain]] [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-adaptation|Drugs and the Brain]] [[F87 NIDA Drugs and the brain#^f87-variation|Drugs and the Brain]]

**Reading rule.** Separate what a person reports wanting, what a behaviour reveals they value, and what a dopamine measurement shows. The three are related but are measured differently, and a claim that treats one as a proxy for another has lost a level; keeping them separate is the discipline this literature requires. [[P29760524 Berke 2018 What does dopamine mean#^p29760524-caution|What does dopamine mean?]]

## Evidence and status

Behavioural effects of reinforcement are robust; translating reward constructs into measurements of anhedonia or apathy in clinical populations is much less settled.
