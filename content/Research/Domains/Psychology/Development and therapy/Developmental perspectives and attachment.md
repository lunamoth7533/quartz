# Developmental perspectives and attachment

## Definition

Developmental psychology describes change across the lifespan; attachment theory concerns the early formation of expectations about caregiving relationships and their later correlates.

## How it works

**Stage theories and their limits.** Stage accounts describe development as a sequence, but the evidence for strict stages is limited and change is often continuous and culturally variable. [[F42 OpenStax Psychology 2e lifespan theories#^f42-stage|Lifespan Theories]] Erikson's psychosocial stages extend development across life rather than ending in childhood. [[F42 OpenStax Psychology 2e lifespan theories#^f42-psychosocial|Lifespan Theories]]

**The developmental frame.** Brain development is described as processes that overlap in time across regions, with different schedules across systems - the biological counterpart to the psychological timeline - and early experience interacts with genetically guided development, so variation in outcome is expected rather than exceptional. That framing applies to psychological development too, which is why single-cause accounts of adult outcomes rarely survive contact with data. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-overlap|The basics of brain development]] [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-experience|The basics of brain development]]

**Sensitive windows are windows with machinery.** In the best-studied case, activity-dependent refinement is triggered by maturation of local inhibitory connections, and consolidation involves reorganisation of the extracellular matrix. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-trigger|Critical period plasticity]] [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-consolidation|Critical period plasticity]]

**What attachment research can and cannot carry.** Attachment classifications come from structured observation of caregiver-child interaction: they are moderately stable under stable caregiving and can change when caregiving changes. Their predictive value is real but probabilistic - they predict population-level patterns rather than individual outcomes - and the popular extension, reading an adult relationship as the direct expression of an infant classification, outruns the instruments and the designs. [[F42 OpenStax Psychology 2e lifespan theories]] Their stability also depends on caregiving conditions and on the measure used.

**Heterogeneity is the norm.** Autism research describes a construct covering early-emerging differences that vary widely between people, and co-occurrence between neurodevelopmental conditions is frequent - so developmental formulations are profile descriptions rather than predictions about a person's trajectory. [[F20 NIMH Autism spectrum disorder#^f20-development|Autism spectrum disorder]] [[P24808851 Leitner 2014 Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD#^p24808851-definition|Co-occurrence of autism and ADHD]]

**Retrospective accounts are reconstructions.** Memory is reconstructive and retrieval depends on cues and context, so retrospective reports of early relationships are reconstructions shaped by current state. [[F11 Noba Memory encoding storage retrieval#^f11-reconstructive|Memory encoding storage retrieval]] Trauma-related developmental claims need the same discipline: the dissociative subtype literature adds that trauma-related presentations are heterogeneous and that evidence on differential response was still developing when the subtype was proposed. [[P22431063 Lanius 2012 Dissociative subtype of PTSD#^p22431063-implication|The dissociative subtype of PTSD]]

**Reading rule.** Distinguish a developmental mechanism demonstrated in one system, a generalisation across samples, and a claim about an individual's history. The third cannot be derived from the first two. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-limit|The basics of brain development]] [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-limit|Normal development of brain circuits]]

## Evidence and status

Longitudinal studies support associations between early caregiving and later outcomes with modest effect sizes and substantial individual variation; deterministic readings are not supported. Prospective designs dominate the strong findings: retrospective accounts of early experience are shaped by current mood and context, and prospective longitudinal designs, though expensive, are what allow developmental claims to survive confounding and recall bias, so they are stronger evidence than recalled accounts. This is the same inference hierarchy the methods domain teaches, applied to the most emotionally loaded subject in the discipline - which is precisely why the discipline needs it here. [[P18509902 Shiffman 2008 Ecological momentary assessment#^p18509902-rationale|Ecological momentary assessment]] [[Longitudinal and within-person inference]]

The sensitive-window machinery comes from visual-cortex work: reactivating it is proposed as a research strategy rather than a treatment, and applying it to attachment or social development is an extrapolation. [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-reactivation|Critical period plasticity]] [[P16261181 Hensch 2005 Critical period plasticity#^p16261181-limit|Critical period plasticity]]
