# EMDR

## Definition

Eye movement desensitisation and reprocessing (EMDR) is a trauma-focused psychotherapy in which a person brings a distressing memory to mind while making eye movements, and whether those eye movements add anything to recalling the memory has been tested directly. [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-therapy|Lee 2013]] With trauma-focused CBT it is treated as one of the two best-evidenced treatments for PTSD in young people. [[P39450446 Phillips 2024 Social outcomes of youth trauma therapy#^p39450446-treatments|Phillips 2024]] In adults with PTSD it outperforms waitlist or usual care and has not differed significantly from trauma-focused CBT, usually over shorter courses. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-comparators|Simpson 2025]] [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-duration|Simpson 2025]]

## How it works

**What it targets.** EMDR is aimed at traumatic memories and the PTSD symptoms tied to them, and PTSD symptom severity is the outcome its trials are built around. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-comparators|Simpson 2025]] In the one bipolar trial sourced here, the target was current trauma-related symptoms in people with bipolar disorder, while the primary outcome was affective relapse. [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-relapse|Hogg 2024]]

**The eye-movement question.** Dismantling trials compare EMDR with and without its eye movements: the full procedure came out moderately ahead (d = 0.41), and treatment fidelity moderated the difference. [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-therapy|Lee 2013]] Outside therapy, recalling a distressing memory while moving the eyes made the memory feel markedly less vivid, the largest effect in the laboratory studies. [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-lab|Lee 2013]] The authors read this as a sign that EMDR partly works through processes other than those of exposure-based therapy. [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-limit|Lee 2013]] Vividness ratings are laboratory proxies and dismantling trials are small, so the component's contribution is plausible rather than settled. [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-caution-dismantling|Appraisal: Lee 2013]]

**Delivery.** EMDR courses were generally shorter and asked less of patients' time than the comparison treatments, which is one reason a single modelling study ranked it the most cost-effective option. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-duration|Simpson 2025]] As an add-on in bipolar disorder it was given over 20 sessions against the same number of supportive-therapy sessions. [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-limit|Hogg 2024]]

**Adult PTSD: the main evidence base.** Across 16 recent trials combined with those behind the 2018 NICE guideline, EMDR beat waitlist or usual care and was statistically indistinguishable from trauma-focused CBT. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-comparators|Simpson 2025]] Most trials were small, and all but one were at moderate or high risk of bias. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-bias|Simpson 2025]] A null contrast of that kind is not a designed non-inferiority result. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-caution-null|Appraisal: Simpson 2025]]

**Children and young people.** In trials of trauma-focused CBT or EMDR for PTSD in people aged 5 to 25, pooled change in social skills and functioning was small and not significant, and few trials measured social outcomes at all. [[P39450446 Phillips 2024 Social outcomes of youth trauma therapy#^p39450446-pooled|Phillips 2024]] [[P39450446 Phillips 2024 Social outcomes of youth trauma therapy#^p39450446-reporting|Phillips 2024]] That review concerns young people with PTSD, not adults with complex PTSD. [[P39450446 Phillips 2024 Social outcomes of youth trauma therapy#^p39450446-caution-age|Appraisal: Phillips 2024]]

**Bipolar disorder.** In 77 trauma-exposed people with bipolar disorder, relapse over 24 months did not differ between EMDR and supportive therapy. [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-relapse|Hogg 2024]] At 12 months EMDR was ahead on depressive and manic symptoms and on functioning, trauma symptoms fell in both arms, and trauma-focused work was not followed by more relapse or dropout. [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-symptoms|Hogg 2024]] [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-safety|Hogg 2024]] Those are secondary results after a negative primary outcome. [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-caution-primary|Appraisal: Hogg 2024]]

**Reading rule.** Name the comparator (waitlist, usual care or trauma-focused CBT), check the risk of bias behind any pooled estimate, and ask whether the effect is on PTSD symptoms, on functioning or, as in bipolar disorder, on a secondary outcome after a null primary one. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-bias|Simpson 2025]] [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-caution-primary|Appraisal: Hogg 2024]]

## Evidence and status

For adult PTSD the direction is consistent - EMDR beats waitlist and usual care and matches trauma-focused CBT - but the trials beneath that verdict are mostly small and at moderate or high risk of bias. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-comparators|Simpson 2025]] [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-bias|Simpson 2025]] The case that eye movements add something rests on a moderate pooled effect from dismantling trials and a larger laboratory effect on memory vividness, from a synthesis that predates recent trials. [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-therapy|Lee 2013]] [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-caution-dismantling|Appraisal: Lee 2013]] In young people the best-evidenced trauma therapies did not measurably improve social functioning, and in bipolar disorder there is one multicentre trial whose primary outcome was negative. [[P39450446 Phillips 2024 Social outcomes of youth trauma therapy#^p39450446-pooled|Phillips 2024]] [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-relapse|Hogg 2024]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether eye movements are an active ingredient or a marker of a well-delivered protocol, since treatment fidelity moderated their effect. [[P23266601 Lee 2013 Eye movements and emotional memories#^p23266601-therapy|Lee 2013]]
- Whether EMDR's parity with trauma-focused CBT survives larger, low-bias trials designed to test non-inferiority. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-caution-null|Appraisal: Simpson 2025]]
- Whether the symptom gains in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder replicate and translate into fewer mood episodes. [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-caution-primary|Appraisal: Hogg 2024]]
- No EMDR trial whose sample is defined by ICD-11 complex PTSD, autism or ADHD is sourced in this library yet.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Systematic review and Bayesian meta-analysis (British journal of psychology).** Updated the adult evidence after the 2018 NICE guideline: EMDR beat waitlist and usual care, did not differ from trauma-focused CBT, and took less patient time, with most trials at moderate or high risk of bias. [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-comparators|Simpson 2025]] [[P40616777 Simpson 2025 EMDR for adult PTSD#^p40616777-bias|Simpson 2025]]
- **2024 · Multicentre randomised controlled trial (Spanish journal of psychiatry and mental health).** The first multicentre test of trauma-focused therapy for bipolar relapse missed its primary outcome but showed that trauma work can be added without more relapses. [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-relapse|Hogg 2024]] [[P38061553 Hogg 2024 EMDR in trauma-exposed bipolar disorder#^p38061553-safety|Hogg 2024]]
- **2024 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (European journal of psychotraumatology).** Showed that trials of trauma-focused CBT and EMDR in young people rarely measure social outcomes and move them little when they do. [[P39450446 Phillips 2024 Social outcomes of youth trauma therapy#^p39450446-reporting|Phillips 2024]]
