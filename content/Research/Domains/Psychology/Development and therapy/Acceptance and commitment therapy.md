# Acceptance and commitment therapy

## Definition

Acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) is a third-wave cognitive behavioural therapy built on the psychological flexibility model. [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-third-wave|Rowe-Johnson 2025]] [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-components|Levin 2012]] The model names six components - acceptance, defusion, self as context, committed action, values and contact with the present moment - and laboratory studies have tested several of them one at a time. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-components|Levin 2012]]

## How it works

**What it targets.** ACT's stated target is psychological flexibility as its model defines it, so its claims come in two layers: that the components change the processes the model specifies, and that changing those processes changes symptoms or functioning. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-components|Levin 2012]] The first layer has direct support: component effects were larger on the outcomes the model predicts than on others. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-experiential|Levin 2012]]

**The components.** In 66 laboratory studies, acceptance, defusion, present-moment, values and mixed mindfulness components each beat inactive comparisons, as did values combined with mindfulness. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-effects|Levin 2012]] The abstract reports no separate significant effect for self as context or committed action, so the six-part model is not evenly tested. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-limit|Levin 2012]] Components taught through experiential methods such as metaphors or exercises did better than an explanation alone, which matters for how the therapy is delivered. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-experiential|Levin 2012]] Brief laboratory manipulations show that components move their target processes, not that those processes carry change across a full course of treatment. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-caution-lab|Appraisal: Levin 2012]]

**Trauma-related symptoms.** Across 11 studies in adults with trauma symptoms after one or more events, people who received ACT reported fewer trauma symptoms at the end of treatment than comparison groups, a moderate effect with low heterogeneity. [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-effect|Rowe-Johnson 2025]] The estimate covers immediate post-test only, and the abstract gives no risk-of-bias rating or diagnostic breakdown. [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-limit|Rowe-Johnson 2025]] With mixed comparison groups, the pooled effect does not show how ACT performs against established trauma-focused treatment, and trauma symptoms are not an ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis. [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-caution-comparators|Appraisal: Rowe-Johnson 2025]]

**Autism and ADHD.** A review of 30 studies of ACT in neurodevelopmental conditions, mostly autism, ADHD and learning disabilities, found 20 group designs relying on questionnaires and 10 single-case designs that also recorded behaviour directly. [[P34154376 Garcia 2022 ACT in neurodevelopmental disorders#^p34154376-designs|Garcia 2022]] Improvements were mixed on both kinds of measure, and the group studies carried moderate or serious risk of bias. [[P34154376 Garcia 2022 ACT in neurodevelopmental disorders#^p34154376-results|Garcia 2022]] Because studies of parents and support staff were reviewed together with studies of autistic and ADHD people themselves, the review shows that ACT has been tried in these groups rather than that it works for them. [[P34154376 Garcia 2022 ACT in neurodevelopmental disorders#^p34154376-limit|Garcia 2022]] [[P34154376 Garcia 2022 ACT in neurodevelopmental disorders#^p34154376-caution-mixed|Appraisal: Garcia 2022]]

**Reading rule.** Keep three kinds of ACT evidence apart - laboratory component studies, process questionnaires such as flexibility scales, and clinical outcomes against a named comparator - because any one of them can be positive while the others are missing. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-caution-lab|Appraisal: Levin 2012]] [[P34154376 Garcia 2022 ACT in neurodevelopmental disorders#^p34154376-designs|Garcia 2022]] [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-caution-comparators|Appraisal: Rowe-Johnson 2025]]

## Evidence and status

ACT has a laboratory evidence base for most of its components and a moderate pooled effect on trauma-related symptoms at the end of treatment, but no sourced comparison against established trauma-focused treatment and no follow-up estimate. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-effects|Levin 2012]] [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-effect|Rowe-Johnson 2025]] [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-limit|Rowe-Johnson 2025]] In neurodevelopmental conditions the evidence is a mix of group and single-case studies at moderate or serious risk of bias, some of them treating caregivers or staff rather than the autistic or ADHD person. [[P34154376 Garcia 2022 ACT in neurodevelopmental disorders#^p34154376-results|Garcia 2022]] [[P34154376 Garcia 2022 ACT in neurodevelopmental disorders#^p34154376-limit|Garcia 2022]] Measured against the orientation-level picture in this library, ACT sits where most newer therapies sit: a named mechanism with partial laboratory support and clinical effects that still depend on the comparator. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-caution-lab|Appraisal: Levin 2012]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether the processes that move in short laboratory tasks are the ones that carry clinical change over a full course of ACT. [[P23046777 Levin 2012 Psychological flexibility component studies#^p23046777-caution-lab|Appraisal: Levin 2012]]
- How ACT compares with trauma-focused treatments for PTSD, and whether its effects last beyond the end of treatment. [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-limit|Rowe-Johnson 2025]]
- Whether ACT helps autistic and ADHD people themselves, as distinct from their caregivers, once lower-bias trials exist. [[P34154376 Garcia 2022 ACT in neurodevelopmental disorders#^p34154376-caution-mixed|Appraisal: Garcia 2022]]
- No ACT trial in bipolar disorder is sourced in this library yet.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Psychological trauma : theory, research, practice and policy).** The first pooled estimate of ACT for trauma-related symptoms in adults found a moderate post-treatment effect with low heterogeneity, leaving comparisons with trauma-focused treatment and follow-up open. [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-effect|Rowe-Johnson 2025]] [[P39374151 Rowe-Johnson 2025 ACT for trauma-related symptoms#^p39374151-third-wave|Rowe-Johnson 2025]]
- **2022 · Systematic review (Behavior modification).** Mapped ACT across autism, ADHD and learning disabilities and their caregivers, finding mixed results and moderate or serious risk of bias. [[P34154376 Garcia 2022 ACT in neurodevelopmental disorders#^p34154376-results|Garcia 2022]]
