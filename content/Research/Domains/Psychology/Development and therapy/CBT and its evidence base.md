# CBT and its evidence base

**Definition.** Cognitive behavioural therapy is a structured, skills-based approach that targets the relationship between thoughts, behaviour and mood. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]

## Supported claims

- CBT with pharmacotherapy was more effective than pharmacotherapy alone for ADHD core symptoms in adults. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|CBT for ADHD (Cochrane review)]]
- Adjunctive manualized psychotherapy was linked to lower bipolar recurrence than control. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]
- Retention was higher for family or conjoint therapy and brief psychoeducation. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-retention|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

## How it works

**The approach and its model.** Psychotherapy approaches are grouped by orientation - psychodynamic, behavioural, cognitive and humanistic - and the types-of-treatment overview distinguishes what each targets. Cognitive behavioural approaches hold that thoughts, behaviour and feelings interact, and that changing the first two changes the third; skill acquisition and practice between sessions are central. CBT combines the cognitive and behavioural strands, which is why it appears across several condition domains in this library. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-psychotherapy|Types of Treatment]]

**The mechanism claim borrowed from learning research.** Behavioural therapies apply conditioning principles directly, and exposure-based methods, developed for fear and avoidance, use extinction logic deliberately: repeated contact without the feared outcome builds competing learning. [[F49 OpenStax Psychology 2e types of treatment#^f49-behavioural|Types of Treatment]] Extinction is new learning that competes with the original fear memory and can return through spontaneous recovery, reinstatement or renewal, so treatment design includes context and relapse planning rather than assuming erasure. [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-newlearning|Fear extinction]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-translation|Fear extinction]]

**How effects are established.** Effects are established through randomised trials with waitlist or active comparators, and what the strongest synthesis reports is narrower than the reputation. The Cochrane review of CBT for adults with ADHD found that adding CBT to medication beat medication alone on clinician-rated and self-rated core symptoms, and that CBT beat a waiting list on self-reported symptoms - with certainty low or very low for most comparisons because trials were small and outcome measures heterogeneous, and with estimates that depended on the comparator (large against a waiting list, not statistically significant against supportive psychotherapy). [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-symptoms|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-certainty|Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]]

**The bipolar literature shows a different shape.** There, adding manualised psychotherapy to medication was associated with lower recurrence, and CBT was linked to stabilising depressive symptoms with family and interpersonal therapy pointing the same way less certainly. The outcome that moves differs by condition, which is why 'CBT works' is not a finding. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-symptoms|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]]

**Delivery matters.** Format, therapist training, dose and adherence all influence outcomes, which is why guideline recommendations specify both method and delivery. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-longterm|Bipolar disorder CG185]]

**Limits of the design.** Blinding participants to a psychotherapy is impossible, so expectancy and allegiance effects are structural features of the design rather than artefacts that can be removed.

**Reading rule.** Name the condition, the comparator, the rater and the outcome; then check the certainty rating. Guideline documents sit on top of that evidence and are service documents rather than measurements. [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|Appraisal: Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]] [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-framing|Bipolar disorder CG185]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Therapy evidence is clinical-trials evidence, so the certainty, reporting and synthesis conventions described in the methods domain (GRADE, PRISMA, publication-bias checks) apply directly here; reading this article alongside the methods material makes the evidence base interpretable rather than merely reported. [[Replication and publication bias]] [[Meta-analysis and review limits]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Evidence is protocol- and population-specific: 'CBT' covers many manuals and delivery formats.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Systematic review and component network meta-analysis (The Lancet Psychiatry).** In randomized trials of adults with ADHD, CBT - like cognitive remediation, mindfulness and psychoeducation - was better than placebo only on clinician-reported measures, extending the rater dependence described above to a larger evidence base. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-nonpharm|Ostinelli 2025]] Confidence in the estimates ranged from very low to moderate. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-limit|Ostinelli 2025]]
- **2023 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (World Psychiatry).** Across 409 trials in depression, CBT had moderate-to-large effects against waiting list or usual care but only a small, fragile advantage over other psychotherapies; it matched medication in the short term, and combined treatment beat medication alone but not CBT alone. [[P36640411 Cuijpers 2023 CBT for depression comprehensive meta-analysis#^p36640411-control|Cuijpers 2023]] [[P36640411 Cuijpers 2023 CBT for depression comprehensive meta-analysis#^p36640411-comparators|Cuijpers 2023]] The comparator sets the effect size, and depression lies outside this library's four conditions. [[P36640411 Cuijpers 2023 CBT for depression comprehensive meta-analysis#^p36640411-caution-comparator|Appraisal: Cuijpers 2023]]
