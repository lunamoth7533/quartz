# Social cognition

## Definition

Social cognition covers the processes by which people perceive and reason about others: attribution, mentalising, emotion recognition, and the influence of group membership.

## How it works

**Situations and interpretations.** Social psychology examines how situations and interpretations shape behaviour, contrasting this with accounts that locate behaviour in stable dispositions - the framing within which social-cognition research operates. [[F46 OpenStax Psychology 2e social psychology#^f46-situational|What Is Social Psychology?]]

**Attribution is where everyday explanation lives.** People explain behaviour using dispositional and situational causes, and the fundamental attribution error describes over-weighting the former for others. [[F46 OpenStax Psychology 2e social psychology#^f46-attribution|What Is Social Psychology?]] [[F46 OpenStax Psychology 2e social psychology#^f46-situational|What Is Social Psychology?]] The attribution literature documents this as a systematic asymmetry - other people's behaviour is explained by disposition, one's own by situation - that is measurable, appears across cultures with varying strength, and matters beyond the laboratory because it shapes judgements about responsibility and blame, which is why the construct appears in clinical formulation and stigma research rather than only in social-psychology textbooks. [[F46 OpenStax Psychology 2e social psychology]]

**Mentalising as a construct.** Social-cognition accounts propose that differences in inferring others' mental states explain social difficulty. Theory-of-mind tasks, the best-known paradigms, measure the ability to infer others' beliefs, and differences on such tasks have been used to explain autistic social differences. [[F20 NIMH Autism spectrum disorder#^f20-development|Autism spectrum disorder]] The critique recorded in this library is that task performance correlates weakly with everyday social outcomes and many people pass the tasks, so the account explains a subgroup rather than the construct. [[P31949163 Lord 2020 Autism spectrum disorder primer#^p31949163-construct|Autism spectrum disorder primer]]

**What mentalising tasks measure, exactly.** False-belief and related tasks measure performance on a specific inference under specific demands. Group differences on them are findings about those tasks; extending them to a general claim about a group's capacity for understanding minds requires ruling out task demands, language load, motivation and interest - the standard measurement critique, and the reason the double-empathy framing could emerge as a competing account rather than a denial. [[F20 NIMH Autism spectrum disorder]] [[Double empathy]]

**The relational reframing.** Later work argues that social difficulties are often bidirectional: misunderstanding arises between two people whose expectations differ, which is the core of the double-empathy account and reframes the finding as interactional rather than as a deficit in one person. Autistic adults' accounts of managing self-presentation and compensating for others' expectations are consistent with that framing. [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-strategies|Social camouflaging]] [[P28527095 Hull 2017 Social camouflaging#^p28527095-consequence|Social camouflaging]]

**Cultural context.** Cultural expectations shape what counts as appropriate social behaviour, which is why social-cognitive findings cannot be read as culture-free norms. [[F47 OpenStax Psychology 2e psychological disorders#^f47-cultural|What Are Psychological Disorders?]]

**Reading rule.** Name the construct, the task, the comparison group and the informant, and state whether the claim is about capacity, performance or interaction. Most disputes in this area are disputes about which of the three a study measured. [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-difference|Predictive coding algorithms]] [[P31949163 Lord 2020 Autism spectrum disorder primer#^p31949163-caution-adult|AI appraisal: Autism spectrum disorder primer]]

## Evidence and status

Attribution and mentalising effects are established in laboratory paradigms; their explanatory power for real-world social outcomes is more limited than the paradigms suggest. Studies that measure autistic people against non-autistic norms find differences by construction, so designs should include both directions of comparison. Comparing scores across groups also assumes measurement invariance - a testable property rather than an automatic one, and one that changes what a difference means - and self-report and proxy report carry different limitations. [[P27942093 Putnick 2016 Measurement invariance#^p27942093-definition|Measurement invariance]] [[P41993846 Fernandez 2026 Quality-of-life assessment in autistic adults#^p41993846-report|Quality-of-life assessment in autistic adults]]
