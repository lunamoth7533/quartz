# Language

## Definition

Language is a structured system for communication, described through phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics and pragmatics. Its neural organisation is distributed and only partly localised.

## How it works

**The components.** Language is described through phonemes, morphemes, syntax, semantics and pragmatics. The components are separable in principle and dissociable in aphasia, which is why linguistic description and lesion evidence developed together, and why 'language ability' is several measurements rather than one: a person can have intact vocabulary and impaired pragmatics, or the reverse. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-components|Language]]

**Development.** Language develops through a sequence of overlapping stages with substantial variation in timing, and the timetable is treated as descriptive rather than fixed. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-development|Language]] Late-talking children frequently catch up - facts that constrain any claim equating a timetable with a mechanism. The developmental sequence is also evidence of dissociation: components can develop unevenly, which supports treating phonology, syntax and semantics as separable systems rather than facets of one capacity. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language]]

**The classical localisation.** Broca's 1861 work localised a language function to a frontal region, and the classical Wernicke model connects a receptive and an expressive centre through a pathway. [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-history|Language]] [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-model|Language]] The model - production separated from comprehension, lesion patterns producing distinct syndromes - came from the correlation between specific lesions and specific language losses, and that aphasia evidence established language's partial separability. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language]]

**Modern qualification.** The classical model is historically decisive but incomplete: language functions are distributed, modern work describes networks rather than two centres, and individual lesions do not map cleanly to syndromes. [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-limits|Language]] Lesions compare coarse regions with fine functions, recovery redistributes function, and imaging in intact people shows widely distributed networks rather than two boxes, so the classical model survives as a first-order map with its exceptions named. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language]]

**Association cortex supplies the framework.** Association areas link information from primary and unimodal regions, and anterior association cortex supports executive processing; language networks are a special case of that organisation rather than a separate principle. [[F109 Neuroscience Online association and executive processing#^f109-areas|Association and Executive Processing]]

## Evidence and status

Lesion-deficit associations are well established; fine-grained functional localisation from imaging is less stable across individuals and tasks. Language imaging inherits the analytic flexibility and power problems documented for neuroimaging, so a group difference in activation during a language task is a sample-level description; lesion evidence and network descriptions remain the stronger basis for localisation claims. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]] [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-limits|Language]]

**Language and thought remain an open question.** The relationship between language and thought is presented as a long-running question with evidence on both sides - so claims that language determines thought, or that it is irrelevant to it, both outrun the sources. [[F40 OpenStax Psychology 2e language#^f40-thought|Language]]
