# Self-concept and identity

## Definition

In clinical research, self-concept is studied mainly as negative self-concept - dysfunctional beliefs about oneself - and identity mainly as identity disturbance, a lasting instability in how people see themselves that models describe as breaks in the coherence, stability and continuity of the self. [[P36325255 Banz 2022 PTSD treatment and negative self-concept#^p36325255-definition|Banz 2022]] [[P42731903 Mullins 2026 Identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder#^p42731903-definition|Mullins 2026]] [[P42731903 Mullins 2026 Identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder#^p42731903-types|Mullins 2026]] Both are written into diagnoses: a persistently negative self-concept is one of the self-organisation disturbances of ICD-11 complex PTSD, and an unstable self-image is part of how borderline personality disorder is described. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-dso|VA: complex PTSD]] [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|NIMH: borderline personality disorder]]

## How it works

**Negative and unstable are different problems.** The complex PTSD wording describes a self-view that is persistently negative, whereas the borderline wording stresses a self-image that keeps shifting; one concerns the content of the self-concept, the other its stability. [[F17 VA National Center for PTSD Complex PTSD history and definitions#^f17-dso|VA: complex PTSD]] [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|NIMH: borderline personality disorder]] A review comparing the two conditions starts from their shared features, including negative self-concept, and finds that most studies separate them by symptom profile, while noting that they can co-occur. [[P38753372 Atkinson 2024 CPTSD versus borderline personality disorder#^p38753372-overlap|Atkinson 2024]] [[P38753372 Atkinson 2024 CPTSD versus borderline personality disorder#^p38753372-distinct|Atkinson 2024]] [[P38753372 Atkinson 2024 CPTSD versus borderline personality disorder#^p38753372-comorbid|Atkinson 2024]] Group-level separation does not settle how one person's presentation should be labelled. [[P38753372 Atkinson 2024 CPTSD versus borderline personality disorder#^p38753372-caution-distinct|Appraisal: Atkinson 2024]]

**Complex PTSD: the memory and identity theory.** The memory and identity theory proposes that trauma exposure interacts with individual vulnerability to produce intrusive, sensation-based trauma memories and negative identities, which together generate PTSD and self-organisation symptoms. [[P37338431 Hyland 2023 Memory and identity theory of CPTSD#^p37338431-model|Hyland 2023]] In a wartime survey, new measures of memory and identity disturbance performed adequately, and almost all the associations the theory predicts were observed. [[P38991292 Hyland 2024 Testing memory and identity theory#^p38991292-measures|Hyland 2024]] [[P38991292 Hyland 2024 Testing memory and identity theory#^p38991292-structure|Hyland 2024]] A single cross-sectional survey shows that predicted associations hold, not that identity disturbance causes symptoms, and the theory comes from developers of the diagnosis. [[P38991292 Hyland 2024 Testing memory and identity theory#^p38991292-caution-crosssectional|Appraisal: Hyland 2024]] [[P37338431 Hyland 2023 Memory and identity theory of CPTSD#^p37338431-caution-untested|Appraisal: Hyland 2023]]

**Treatment can change self-concept.** Across 25 randomised trials, psychological treatments for PTSD improved negative self-concept with a moderate-to-large effect. [[P36325255 Banz 2022 PTSD treatment and negative self-concept#^p36325255-effect|Banz 2022]] Heterogeneity was large and was not explained by whether a therapy was trauma-focused or included cognitive restructuring. [[P36325255 Banz 2022 PTSD treatment and negative self-concept#^p36325255-moderators|Banz 2022]] Self-concept was measured by self-report alongside symptoms, so part of the change may mirror general relief. [[P36325255 Banz 2022 PTSD treatment and negative self-concept#^p36325255-caution-measure|Appraisal: Banz 2022]]

**Borderline personality disorder: identity disturbance.** Research on identity disturbance leans on the SCID-II interview and the Identity Disturbance Questionnaire, draws mostly on Erikson's theory, and describes types with Wilkinson-Ryan and Westen's four-factor model, linking disturbance to goal-directed functioning and affect regulation. [[P42731903 Mullins 2026 Identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder#^p42731903-measures|Mullins 2026]] [[P42731903 Mullins 2026 Identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder#^p42731903-types|Mullins 2026]] Where the disturbance is a diagnostic criterion, finding it in diagnosed people is partly built in. [[P42731903 Mullins 2026 Identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder#^p42731903-caution-circular|Appraisal: Mullins 2026]]

**ADHD: self-esteem.** Most studies with comparison groups found lower self-esteem in adults with ADHD, higher symptom levels went with lower self-esteem, and self-esteem mediated several ADHD-linked outcomes. [[P38491855 Pedersen 2024 Self-esteem in adults with ADHD#^p38491855-lower|Pedersen 2024]] [[P38491855 Pedersen 2024 Self-esteem in adults with ADHD#^p38491855-mediation|Pedersen 2024]] Without control for co-occurring depression and anxiety, the deficit cannot be assigned to ADHD itself. [[P38491855 Pedersen 2024 Self-esteem in adults with ADHD#^p38491855-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Pedersen 2024]]

**Autism: identity costs of camouflaging.** A systematic review of camouflaging adds identity-related consequences to its known mental-health correlates, with the direction of those links still untested. [[P39370528 Klein 2025 Social camouflaging systematic review#^p39370528-direction|Klein 2025]] [[P39370528 Klein 2025 Social camouflaging systematic review#^p39370528-caution-direction|Appraisal: Klein 2025]]

**A narrative self in the brain.** One synthesis proposes that the default mode network combines remembered, linguistic and semantic content into an internal narrative that underpins the sense of self, which is a theory to test rather than a demonstrated mechanism. [[P37167968 Menon 2023 Twenty years of the default mode network#^p37167968-narrative|Menon 2023]] [[P37167968 Menon 2023 Twenty years of the default mode network#^p37167968-caution-theory|Appraisal: Menon 2023]]

**Reading rule.** Separate the content of a self-view (how negative) from its structure (how stable and coherent), name the instrument, and check whether the self-measure shares items or method with the symptoms it is related to. [[P36325255 Banz 2022 PTSD treatment and negative self-concept#^p36325255-caution-measure|Appraisal: Banz 2022]] [[P42731903 Mullins 2026 Identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder#^p42731903-caution-circular|Appraisal: Mullins 2026]]

## Evidence and status

Most of the evidence is conceptual or cross-sectional: a scoping review of definitions and measures in borderline personality disorder, a theory and one structural test in complex PTSD, and a systematic review of self-esteem in adult ADHD. [[P42731903 Mullins 2026 Identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder#^p42731903-limit|Mullins 2026]] [[P38991292 Hyland 2024 Testing memory and identity theory#^p38991292-limit|Hyland 2024]] [[P38491855 Pedersen 2024 Self-esteem in adults with ADHD#^p38491855-limit|Pedersen 2024]] The strongest design is the treatment meta-analysis, in which randomised trials show that PTSD treatments improve negative self-concept, with large unexplained heterogeneity. [[P36325255 Banz 2022 PTSD treatment and negative self-concept#^p36325255-effect|Banz 2022]] [[P36325255 Banz 2022 PTSD treatment and negative self-concept#^p36325255-moderators|Banz 2022]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether negative self-concept in complex PTSD and identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder are distinct processes or two measurements of one. [[P38753372 Atkinson 2024 CPTSD versus borderline personality disorder#^p38753372-caution-distinct|Appraisal: Atkinson 2024]]
- Whether the memory and identity theory's causal paths hold in prospective, independent tests. [[P37338431 Hyland 2023 Memory and identity theory of CPTSD#^p37338431-caution-untested|Appraisal: Hyland 2023]]
- Whether lower self-esteem in adult ADHD belongs to ADHD or to co-occurring depression and anxiety. [[P38491855 Pedersen 2024 Self-esteem in adults with ADHD#^p38491855-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Pedersen 2024]]
- No source on self-concept or identity in bipolar disorder is in this library yet.

## Recent research

- **2026 · Scoping review (Journal of psychiatric research).** Mapped how identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder is defined and measured, showing a central construct with diverse, partly unintegrated conceptualisations. [[P42731903 Mullins 2026 Identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder#^p42731903-types|Mullins 2026]] [[P42731903 Mullins 2026 Identity disturbance in borderline personality disorder#^p42731903-limit|Mullins 2026]]
- **2024 · Cross-sectional survey with structural equation modelling (Journal of anxiety disorders).** Gave initial support to the memory and identity theory of complex PTSD with newly developed measures. [[P38991292 Hyland 2024 Testing memory and identity theory#^p38991292-structure|Hyland 2024]]
- **2024 · Systematic review (Journal of attention disorders).** Found a robust association between adult ADHD and lower self-esteem while noting weak control of confounders. [[P38491855 Pedersen 2024 Self-esteem in adults with ADHD#^p38491855-lower|Pedersen 2024]] [[P38491855 Pedersen 2024 Self-esteem in adults with ADHD#^p38491855-limit|Pedersen 2024]]

- [[Personality constructs and traits]] - the borderline personality construct whose identity-disturbance criterion this note sets beside the complex PTSD self-concept cluster.
- [[Co-occurrence and differential reasoning]] - the method needed when negative self-concept and identity disturbance appear together and the complex PTSD versus borderline question arises.

- [[Shame and guilt]] - self-conscious emotions that attach to a negative view of the self; external shame is defined through how the self is seen by others.
- [[Episodic memory]] - autobiographical recall is the raw material of a continuous self-narrative, the continuity that identity-disturbance models describe as broken.
