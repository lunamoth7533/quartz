# Rumination and repetitive negative thinking

## Definition

Repetitive negative thinking (RNT) is the umbrella term for repeated thinking about negative content, with rumination and worry as its best-known forms, and it is studied as a transdiagnostic process tied to the start, persistence and relapse of several mental disorders. [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-definition|Stenzel 2025]] In this library it matters because rumination tracks intrusive re-experiencing in PTSD and is the regulation strategy most closely tied to both depressive and (hypo)manic symptoms in bipolar disorder. [[P28398085 Szabo 2017 Rumination and posttraumatic stress symptoms#^p28398085-clusters|Szabo 2017]] [[P39631486 Oliva 2025 Emotion regulation strategies in bipolar disorder#^p39631486-rumination|Oliva 2025]]

## How it works

**One process, several names.** Treating RNT as transdiagnostic means asking the same questions about it in every diagnosis rather than assigning it to depression or anxiety alone. [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-definition|Stenzel 2025]] The trial evidence supports lumping the forms together: CBT reduced rumination, worry and general repetitive thinking by amounts that did not differ significantly. [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-effect|Stenzel 2025]]

**Trauma.** Across 64 samples of trauma-exposed adults, rumination correlated with posttraumatic stress symptoms at about r = .50, and time since trauma, gender, earlier trauma and measure type did not change the link. [[P28398085 Szabo 2017 Rumination and posttraumatic stress symptoms#^p28398085-association|Szabo 2017]] The association was strongest with intrusive re-experiencing, and rumination about the trauma itself gave smaller effects than rumination as a general habit. [[P28398085 Szabo 2017 Rumination and posttraumatic stress symptoms#^p28398085-clusters|Szabo 2017]] Intrusions and rumination are both repeated thoughts about the event, so part of their overlap may lie in how questionnaires are worded. [[P28398085 Szabo 2017 Rumination and posttraumatic stress symptoms#^p28398085-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Szabo 2017]]

**Bipolar disorder: both poles.** Among the regulation strategies compared in a network meta-analysis, rumination had the strongest association with depressive symptoms and also with (hypo)manic symptoms, whereas other strategies related mainly to depression. [[P39631486 Oliva 2025 Emotion regulation strategies in bipolar disorder#^p39631486-rumination|Oliva 2025]] [[P39631486 Oliva 2025 Emotion regulation strategies in bipolar disorder#^p39631486-depression|Oliva 2025]] Compared with major depression, bipolar disorder differed on few regulation strategies, one of them dwelling on positive mood, which is a reminder that repetitive thinking can be about positive states too. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-mdd|De Prisco 2023]] Both findings come from correlations with current symptoms, which cannot separate rumination as a lasting habit from rumination as part of the episode. [[P39631486 Oliva 2025 Emotion regulation strategies in bipolar disorder#^p39631486-caution-state|Appraisal: Oliva 2025]] [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-caution-state|Appraisal: De Prisco 2023]]

**Candidate brain substrate.** Thinking about oneself and spontaneous mind wandering are among the functions tied to the default mode network, which makes that network a natural candidate for repetitive self-focused thought. [[P37167968 Menon 2023 Twenty years of the default mode network#^p37167968-functions|Menon 2023]] No rumination-specific brain source is in this library, and the network's internal-narrative account is itself a synthesis still to be tested. [[P37167968 Menon 2023 Twenty years of the default mode network#^p37167968-caution-theory|Appraisal: Menon 2023]]

**Reducing it.** Across 55 randomised trials, CBT lowered repetitive negative thinking after treatment with a moderate effect, and treatments built to target it did better than broader CBT. [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-effect|Stenzel 2025]] [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-specific|Stenzel 2025]] Those outcomes are questionnaire scores for the very process the therapy works on, which is where expectancy effects are strongest. [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-caution-outcome|Appraisal: Stenzel 2025]]

**Reading rule.** Name the construct (rumination, worry or a general RNT scale), the mood state at testing and whether the design is cross-sectional; a correlation with current symptoms cannot show that rumination comes before an episode or keeps it going. [[P39631486 Oliva 2025 Emotion regulation strategies in bipolar disorder#^p39631486-caution-state|Appraisal: Oliva 2025]] [[P28398085 Szabo 2017 Rumination and posttraumatic stress symptoms#^p28398085-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Szabo 2017]]

## Evidence and status

The associations are consistent and moderate: rumination correlates with PTSD symptoms across many samples, and in bipolar disorder it is the regulation strategy most tied to both poles of mood. [[P28398085 Szabo 2017 Rumination and posttraumatic stress symptoms#^p28398085-association|Szabo 2017]] [[P39631486 Oliva 2025 Emotion regulation strategies in bipolar disorder#^p39631486-rumination|Oliva 2025]] That evidence is correlational, largely cross-sectional and self-reported, and the bipolar authors themselves call for longitudinal work. [[P39631486 Oliva 2025 Emotion regulation strategies in bipolar disorder#^p39631486-limit|Oliva 2025]] [[P28398085 Szabo 2017 Rumination and posttraumatic stress symptoms#^p28398085-limit|Szabo 2017]] The treatment evidence is stronger in design, 55 randomised trials across diagnoses, but it measures change in the targeted process at the end of treatment rather than in the disorders that process is linked to. [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-limit|Stenzel 2025]] [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-caution-outcome|Appraisal: Stenzel 2025]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether rumination precedes and predicts mood episodes in bipolar disorder or only accompanies them. [[P39631486 Oliva 2025 Emotion regulation strategies in bipolar disorder#^p39631486-limit|Oliva 2025]]
- Whether the rumination-PTSD association reflects a maintaining process or overlapping item content. [[P28398085 Szabo 2017 Rumination and posttraumatic stress symptoms#^p28398085-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Szabo 2017]]
- Whether lowering repetitive thinking lowers the disorders it is linked to, beyond the repetitive-thinking scores themselves. [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-caution-outcome|Appraisal: Stenzel 2025]]
- No source on repetitive negative thinking in ADHD or autism is in this library yet.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis (Neuroscience and biobehavioral reviews).** Put rumination first among regulation strategies linked to bipolar symptoms, and the only one clearly tied to (hypo)mania as well as depression. [[P39631486 Oliva 2025 Emotion regulation strategies in bipolar disorder#^p39631486-rumination|Oliva 2025]]
- **2025 · Transdiagnostic meta-analysis (Psychological medicine).** Pooled CBT trials across diagnoses and found that treatments aimed at repetitive thinking reduce it most. [[P39916353 Stenzel 2025 CBT for repetitive negative thinking#^p39916353-specific|Stenzel 2025]]
- **2023 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Psychological medicine).** Found bipolar disorder close to major depression on most regulation strategies, differing in dwelling on positive mood and in risk-taking. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-mdd|De Prisco 2023]]
