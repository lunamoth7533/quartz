# Perception

## Definition

Sensation is detection of a stimulus by a sensory receptor; perception is the organisation and interpretation of that information into a coherent scene. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-sensation|Sensation versus Perception]]

## How it works

**Thresholds.** A perceptual report combines both stages, sensation and perception, which is why the distinction matters. Absolute thresholds describe the minimum stimulus that can be detected, and signal detection methods separate sensory sensitivity from the decision criterion, or response bias, a person uses. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-sensation|Sensation versus Perception]] [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-absolute|Sensation versus Perception]] Even at the sensory edge, a response reflects both the observer's sensitivity and their willingness to call an ambiguous event a signal, so thresholds are properties of a decision process, not of an eye alone. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception]]

**Perception is constructive.** Illusions are stable, repeatable demonstrations that the same sensory input supports different interpreted scenes depending on context and expectation - the empirical basis for treating perception as inference rather than as a passive readout or recording. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception#^f37-illusions|Sensation versus Perception]] The constructive account is not a metaphor: perception combines input with assumptions the system carries - about lighting, distance, object constancy - and those assumptions are normally so well matched to the environment that their contribution only shows when the environment is arranged to violate them. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception]]

**What bottom-up and top-down actually divide.** Bottom-up descriptions cover the chain from receptor to cortex; top-down descriptions cover the effects of expectation, context and attention on what is perceived. The division is analytical rather than anatomical - real perception runs both at once - but it earns its keep in interpretation: an effect of expectation on perception is a top-down claim that needs its own evidence, and cannot be read off a sensory pathway diagram. [[F37 OpenStax Psychology 2e sensation versus perception]]

**Cortical processing.** Visual cortex is the best-mapped example: visual signals reach cortex through the thalamic relay in the lateral geniculate nucleus and arrive first in striate cortex, and cortical neurons are described by properties such as orientation, direction and motion sensitivity that emerge in successive areas - the hierarchical account of how features are extracted. [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-pathway|Visual Processing: Cortical Pathways]] [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-properties|Visual Processing: Cortical Pathways]]

**Somatosensory detail.** Somatosensory processing divides into a discriminative-touch and proprioceptive channel and a separate pain and temperature pathway, which is why they can dissociate clinically, and which shows that the principle generalises across modalities. [[F103 Neuroscience Online somatosensory systems#^f103-division|Somatosensory systems]]

**The inferential version of the story.** Predictive-coding algorithms all fit a generative model to sensory data, sharing the idea that perception involves inference about hidden causes; they differ substantially in their generative models and optimisation methods, so evidence for one is not evidence for another. [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-common|Predictive coding algorithms]] [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-difference|Predictive coding algorithms]]

## Evidence and status

Psychophysics is among the most quantitatively precise areas of psychology; the mapping from perceptual performance to neural mechanism still requires models. [[P26809759 Spratling 2017 Predictive coding algorithms#^p26809759-common|Predictive coding algorithms]] Precision still needs reading with effect sizes: with large samples a significant difference can accompany a trivial one, and effect-size conventions are heuristics that ignore measurement accuracy and population diversity, so a perceptual threshold difference should be translated into the outcome's own units before it is interpreted. [[P23997866 Sullivan 2012 Using effect size#^p23997866-significance|Using effect size]] [[P23997866 Sullivan 2012 Using effect size#^p23997866-magnitude|Using effect size]]

## Connections

Perception is the input side of [[Attention]], and it is where the computational accounts in [[Bayesian inference and predictive processing]] are usually tested.

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Computational neuroscience's predictive-processing account is the formal version of the constructive claim - perception as inference combining prior and input - and the Bayesian article develops what that would require and what would count as testing it. ## Uncertainties

- Whether perception should be described as inference is a modelling claim; the behavioural regularities themselves are better established.
