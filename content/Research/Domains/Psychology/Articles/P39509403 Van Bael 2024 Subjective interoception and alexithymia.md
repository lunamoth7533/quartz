# A systematic review and meta-analysis of the relationship between subjective interoception and alexithymia: Implications for construct definitions and measurement.

**Authors.** Van Bael K, Scarfo J, Suleyman E, Katherveloo J, Grimble N, Ball M (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39509403/)

**Published.** 2024 in PloS one.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0310411](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0310411) · PMID [39509403](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39509403/) · PMCID [PMC11542822](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11542822/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Overall alexithymia was positively associated with self-reported interoceptive confusion, autonomic reactivity and heightened interoceptive attention, and negatively associated with self-reported interoceptive accuracy and adaptive interoception, especially trusting, self-regulation and attention regulation.
^p39509403-direction

The pattern held for the alexithymia facets, more strongly for difficulty identifying and difficulty describing feelings than for externally oriented thinking, and the association depended on which interoception questionnaire was used.
^p39509403-facets

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional studies only, with independent screening, extraction and risk-of-bias assessment by three reviewers; the authors highlight problems of construct definition and operationalisation, noting that interoception questionnaires capture sensing, attention, interpretation and memory.
^p39509403-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Both constructs were measured by self-report, so shared method variance and overlapping item content can produce correlations without a shared mechanism; the synthesis describes how questionnaires relate, not how bodily signals are processed.
^p39509403-caution-method
