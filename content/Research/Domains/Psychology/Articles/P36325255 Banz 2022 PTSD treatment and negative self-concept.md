# Effects of current treatments for trauma survivors with posttraumatic stress disorder on reducing a negative self-concept: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Banz L, Stefanovic M, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36325255/)

**Published.** 2022 in European journal of psychotraumatology (first online 2022-10-25).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1080/20008066.2022.2122528](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008066.2022.2122528) · PMID [36325255](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36325255/) · PMCID [PMC9621279](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9621279/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The authors define negative self-concept as dysfunctional beliefs about oneself, proposed as a factor in how PTSD develops and persists, and note that it features in DSM-5 PTSD and ICD-11 complex PTSD criteria.
^p36325255-definition

Psychological treatments improved negative self-concept after treatment with a moderate-to-large controlled effect (g = 0.67; 30 comparisons).
^p36325255-effect

Heterogeneity was large and was not explained by treatment type, including whether a therapy was trauma-focused or contained cognitive restructuring.
^p36325255-moderators

## Scope as reported

Five databases searched to February 2021 for randomised trials in adults; risk of bias assessed with the Cochrane tool; effects reported at post-treatment.
^p36325255-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Self-concept was measured by the same kind of self-report that tracks symptom change, so part of the improvement may mirror general relief, and the trials targeted PTSD rather than an ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis.
^p36325255-caution-measure
