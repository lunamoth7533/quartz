# Evidence of emotion dysregulation as a core symptom of adult ADHD: A systematic review.

**Authors.** Soler-Gutiérrez AM, Pérez-González JC, Mayas J (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36608036/)

**Published.** 2023 in PloS one.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across the included studies, adults with ADHD used non-adaptive emotion regulation strategies more often than comparison groups without ADHD symptoms.
^p36608036-strategies

Emotion dysregulation was associated with ADHD symptom severity, executive functioning, co-occurring psychiatric conditions and, in some samples, criminal conviction; brain-activity patterns also differed between adults with and without ADHD.
^p36608036-correlates

## Scope as reported

A PRISMA-guided qualitative synthesis of 22 English-language empirical studies, grouped into measurement, neural and psychophysiological activity, and treatment. The authors describe the eligible studies as scarce and very heterogeneous in aims and samples, and no pooled estimate is reported.
^p36608036-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A narrative synthesis of mostly cross-sectional studies can show that emotion dysregulation co-occurs with adult ADHD; it cannot establish that dysregulation is a core symptom rather than a correlate of severity, executive difficulty or comorbidity.
^p36608036-caution-core
