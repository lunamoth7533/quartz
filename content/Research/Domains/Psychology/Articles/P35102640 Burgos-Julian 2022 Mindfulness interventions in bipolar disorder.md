# Mindfulness-based and mindfulness-informed interventions in bipolar disorder: A meta-analysis based on Becker's method.

**Authors.** Burgos-Julián FA, Ruiz-Íñiguez R, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35102640/)

**Published.** 2022 in Clinical psychology & psychotherapy (first online 2022-02-10).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/cpp.2717](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.2717) · PMID [35102640](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35102640/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Relative to control groups, mindfulness interventions showed no significant effect on depression (g = 0.21) or on mania (g = -0.13).
^p35102640-mood

The one significant result was a moderate effect on anxiety (g = 0.53) over the pre-post period.
^p35102640-anxiety

## Scope as reported

Included both mindfulness-based and mindfulness-informed programmes and single-group designs, with a procedure that imputes comparisons where no control group exists; the authors name the small number of studies and the lack of follow-up as the main limits.
^p35102640-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Imputed comparisons and short pre-post windows make the anxiety effect provisional, and a null result on mood symptoms says nothing about relapse, which the abstract does not report.
^p35102640-caution-design
