**Authors.** Pedersen AB, Edvardsen BV, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38491855/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of attention disorders (first online 2024-03-16).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/10870547241237245](https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547241237245) · PMID [38491855](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38491855/) · PMCID [PMC11016209](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11016209/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Five of the six studies with healthy comparison groups found lower self-esteem in adults with ADHD, and higher ADHD symptom levels went with lower self-esteem.
^p38491855-lower

No gender difference appeared, and self-esteem mediated several outcomes associated with ADHD.
^p38491855-mediation

## Scope as reported

PRISMA-guided search of four databases; few studies examined mechanisms or controlled for confounders, and the authors call for longitudinal designs.
^p38491855-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Without control for co-occurring depression and anxiety, lower self-esteem may reflect those conditions as much as ADHD, and one global scale says little about which parts of the self-concept differ.
^p38491855-caution-confounding
