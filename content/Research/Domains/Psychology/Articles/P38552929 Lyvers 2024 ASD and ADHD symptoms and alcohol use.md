# Adult symptoms of ASD and ADHD in relation to alcohol use: Potential roles of transdiagnostic features.

**Authors.** Lyvers M, Dark S, Jaguru I, Thorberg FA (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38552929/)

**Published.** 2024 in Alcohol (Fayetteville, N.Y.).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.alcohol.2024.03.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.alcohol.2024.03.011) · PMID [38552929](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38552929/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Self-reported autism and ADHD symptoms were positively correlated, and path analysis found that association fully mediated by alexithymia, impulsivity and negative mood.
^p38552929-mediation

Higher ADHD symptom scores went with heavier drinking, and that association ran entirely through the same features, mainly alexithymia and impulsivity; autism symptoms instead had a negative direct association with drinking alongside positive indirect paths.
^p38552929-alcohol

## Scope as reported

Non-clinical young adults completing validated self-report measures; symptom scores were normally distributed, which the authors take to support viewing the disorders as extreme ends of population distributions.
^p38552929-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Symptom scales in a non-clinical sample are not diagnoses, and cross-sectional mediation cannot place alexithymia on a causal path; the study shows co-variation of traits rather than alexithymia in diagnosed ADHD.
^p38552929-caution-design
