# The relationship of trauma-related guilt with PTSD symptoms in adult trauma survivors: a meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Kip A, Diele J, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35781354/)

**Published.** 2022 in Psychological medicine (first online 2022-07-04).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1017/S0033291722001866](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291722001866) · PMID [35781354](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35781354/) · PMCID [PMC9527673](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9527673/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Trauma-related guilt correlated with PTSD symptoms at the same time point (r = 0.38) and, more weakly, with later symptoms (r = 0.21); every symptom cluster was involved and the association looked stable over time.
^p35781354-association

Associations were smaller in higher-quality studies and larger with DSM-5-based instruments; they peaked after war-related trauma (r = 0.44) and were lowest after motor-vehicle accidents (r = 0.18), while military and civilian, and clinical and non-clinical, samples did not differ.
^p35781354-moderators

## Scope as reported

Four databases searched; most data points were cross-sectional, heterogeneity was high (I² about 90% cross-sectionally) and the type of guilt measure moderated results.
^p35781354-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The predictive correlation rests on 19 longitudinal data points and does not exclude shared causes, so guilt is a candidate maintaining factor rather than an established cause.
^p35781354-caution-longitudinal
