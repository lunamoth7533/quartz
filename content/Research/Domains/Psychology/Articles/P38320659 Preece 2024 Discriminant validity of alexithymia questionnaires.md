# Alexithymia or general psychological distress? Discriminant validity of the Toronto Alexithymia Scale and the Perth Alexithymia Questionnaire.

**Authors.** Preece DA, Petrova K, Mehta A, Sikka P, Gross JJ (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38320659/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of affective disorders.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2024.01.271](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.01.271) · PMID [38320659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38320659/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Exploratory factor analysis pulled the scores apart into two broad factors, one for alexithymia and one for general distress (depression, anxiety and stress).
^p38320659-factors

All Perth Alexithymia Questionnaire scores loaded only on the alexithymia factor, whereas the TAS-20 difficulty-identifying-feelings facet cross-loaded heavily on distress, which the authors interpret as a discriminant-validity problem for that scale.
^p38320659-dif

## Scope as reported

General-community adults; the authors note that work in clinical settings is still needed.
^p38320659-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

One cross-sectional factor analysis cannot show whether current distress contaminates alexithymia scores or genuinely co-varies with alexithymia; replacing one scale with another needs longitudinal and clinical tests, and any study relating TAS-20 totals to distress-heavy outcomes should be read with this overlap in mind.
^p38320659-caution-overlap
