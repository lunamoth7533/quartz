# Shame, guilt, and depressive symptoms: a meta-analytic review.

**Authors.** Kim S, Thibodeau R, et al. (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21219057/)

**Published.** 2011 in Psychological bulletin.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1037/a0021466](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021466) · PMID [21219057](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21219057/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Shame was more strongly associated with depressive symptoms (r = .43) than guilt was (r = .28).
^p21219057-shame

Two maladaptive forms of guilt, an exaggerated sense of responsibility for events one could not control and free-floating guilt detached from any situation, were as closely tied to depressive symptoms as shame (r = .39 and .42).
^p21219057-guilt-variants

External shame, a negative view of oneself as others are imagined to see it, showed larger associations (r = .56) than internal shame (r = .42), and depression scales that mention guilt inflated guilt associations.
^p21219057-external

## Scope as reported

Correlational studies only; age, sex and ethnicity did not moderate the effects, and the authors state that the data leave the causal direction open.
^p21219057-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Several depression scales count guilt as a symptom, so part of the guilt-depression link is shared item content, as the moderator result shows; the samples were not drawn from this library's four conditions.
^p21219057-caution-content
