# Effects of acceptance and commitment therapy on trauma-related symptoms: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Rowe-Johnson MK, Browning B, et al. (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39374151/)

**Published.** 2025 in Psychological trauma : theory, research, practice and policy (first online 2024-10-07).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1037/tra0001785](https://doi.org/10.1037/tra0001785) · PMID [39374151](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39374151/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The authors describe ACT as a third-wave cognitive behavioural therapy with promise for trauma-related symptoms, and note that no meta-analysis of it in adults with such symptoms existed before theirs.
^p39374151-third-wave

Across 11 studies, adults who received ACT reported fewer trauma symptoms at the first post-treatment assessment than comparison groups, a moderate and significant effect (Hedges' g = -0.42) with low heterogeneity.
^p39374151-effect

## Scope as reported

Random-effects synthesis at immediate post-test only, with subgroup analyses by research design, trauma type and comparator; the abstract does not report risk of bias, diagnostic status or follow-up.
^p39374151-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The comparison groups were mixed, so the pooled effect does not show how ACT performs against established trauma-focused treatments, and trauma symptoms after one or more events are not an ICD-11 complex PTSD diagnosis.
^p39374151-caution-comparators
