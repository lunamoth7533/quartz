# Highest correlations between emotion regulation strategies and mood symptoms in bipolar disorder: A systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Oliva V, De Prisco M, et al. (13 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39631486/)

**Published.** 2025 in Neuroscience and biobehavioral reviews (first online 2024-12-02).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2024.105967](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2024.105967) · PMID [39631486](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39631486/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Of the regulation strategies compared, rumination had the strongest association with both depressive symptoms (effect size 0.43) and (hypo)manic symptoms (0.26).
^p39631486-rumination

Other strategies were associated mainly with depressive symptoms, and the network showed no significant heterogeneity or inconsistency.
^p39631486-depression

## Scope as reported

Searches to November 2023; every included study was cross-sectional, which the authors say limits causal interpretation, and they call for longitudinal work on how symptoms and regulation change together.
^p39631486-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Correlations with current symptoms cannot separate rumination as a trait that precedes episodes from rumination as part of being depressed or (hypo)manic at the time of testing.
