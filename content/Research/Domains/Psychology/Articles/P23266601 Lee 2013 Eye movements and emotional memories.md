# A meta-analysis of the contribution of eye movements in processing emotional memories.

**Authors.** Lee CW, Cuijpers P (2 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23266601/)

**Published.** 2013 in Journal of behavior therapy and experimental psychiatry (first online 2012-11-20).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jbtep.2012.11.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbtep.2012.11.001) · PMID [23266601](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23266601/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In treatment studies, EMDR with eye movements outperformed the same procedure without them by a moderate margin (d = 0.41), and treatment fidelity moderated that difference.
^p23266601-therapy

In laboratory studies where people recalled a distressing memory, adding eye movements had a large effect (d = 0.74), largest on how vivid the memory felt (d = 0.91).
^p23266601-lab

## Scope as reported

Two pooled groups of studies, dismantling trials and non-therapy experiments; the authors wrote in response to an earlier meta-analysis that had found no eye-movement effect, and they discuss the results as pointing to processes that differ from other exposure-based therapies.
^p23266601-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Vividness ratings in laboratory tasks stand in for clinical change, and dismantling trials are usually small, so an eye-movement contribution is plausible rather than settled; the search also predates later trials.
