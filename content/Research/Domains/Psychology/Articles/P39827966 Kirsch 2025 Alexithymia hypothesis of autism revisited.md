# The Alexithymia Hypothesis of Autism Revisited: Alexithymia Modulates Social Brain Activity During Facial Affect Recognition in Autistic Adults.

**Authors.** Kirsch S, Maier S, Lin M, Guendelman S, Kaufmann C, Dziobek I, Tebartz van Elst L (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39827966/)

**Published.** 2025 in Biological psychiatry. Cognitive neuroscience and neuroimaging.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.bpsc.2025.01.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpsc.2025.01.007) · PMID [39827966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39827966/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Autistic participants recognised facial affect less accurately than controls whether or not they had alexithymia, which the authors state does not support the alexithymia hypothesis at the behavioural level.
^p39827966-behaviour

Within the autistic group, alexithymia status was linked to reduced activation in three cortical clusters during explicit recognition and increased activation in three clusters during implicit recognition, a dissociation between behaviour and brain response.
^p39827966-brain

## Scope as reported

The study tests the alexithymia hypothesis - that emotion-processing difficulties in autism can be attributed to co-occurring alexithymia - at the level of brain function, which the authors describe as previously unexplored; autistic participants were categorised by alexithymia status.
^p39827966-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Imaging contrasts between alexithymia subgroups within 60 autistic participants are modest in size, and a behavioural null for alexithymia on one task sits alongside positive findings on others, so the result narrows the hypothesis rather than settling it.
^p39827966-caution-subgroup
