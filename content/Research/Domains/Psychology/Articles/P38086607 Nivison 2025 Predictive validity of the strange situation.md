# The predictive validity of the strange situation procedure: Evidence from registered analyses of two landmark longitudinal studies.

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

After adjustment for four demographic covariates, partial correlations between infant attachment security and later socioemotional outcomes were no larger than about .10 to .15 in either cohort.
^p38086607-socioemotional

Combining Strange Situations at 12 and 18 months did not substantively change prediction in one cohort, while a composite of three attachment measures raised it in the other; associations with academic skills were comparable to or larger than those with socioemotional outcomes.
^p38086607-composites

## Scope as reported

Registered analyses designed to address publication bias and incomplete outcome coverage in earlier meta-analyses; outcomes were across-time composites of teacher, mother and self-report.
^p38086607-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Small adjusted associations in two well-characterised cohorts are evidence against deterministic readings of an infant classification, but two cohorts from one country cannot say how large the association would be in other populations or with other attachment measures.
^p38086607-caution-scope
