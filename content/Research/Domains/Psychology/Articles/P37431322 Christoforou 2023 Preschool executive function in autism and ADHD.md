# Executive function profiles of preschool children with autism spectrum disorder and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: A systematic review.

**Authors.** Christoforou M, Jones EJH, White P, Charman T (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37431322/)

**Published.** 2023 in JCPP advances.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/jcv2.12123](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcv2.12123) · PMID [37431322](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37431322/) · PMCID [PMC10241451](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10241451/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Preschool autism studies consistently reported shifting difficulties and, in most cases, inhibition difficulties; ADHD studies consistently reported inhibition and planning difficulties and, in most cases, working-memory difficulties.
^p37431322-profiles

The authors describe overlap with some specificity between the two conditions, and note that informant-based measures showed more robust executive differences than laboratory-based tasks.
^p37431322-measures

## Scope as reported

Five databases searched to May 2022; preschool ages only. Findings were mixed for sustained attention and shifting in ADHD and for working memory and planning in autism, and the authors point to methodological differences and outcome measurement as possible reasons.
^p37431322-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The ADHD side rests on ten studies, and the stronger effects on informant ratings may partly reflect raters who know the child's diagnosis; group-level component profiles describe samples and do not classify an individual child.
^p37431322-caution-rater
