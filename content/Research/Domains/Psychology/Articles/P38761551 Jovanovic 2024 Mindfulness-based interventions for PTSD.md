# Can mindfulness-based interventions reduce PTSD symptoms? An umbrella review.

**Authors.** Jovanovic B, Garfin DR (2 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38761551/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of anxiety disorders (first online 2024-03-16).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.janxdis.2024.102859](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.janxdis.2024.102859) · PMID [38761551](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38761551/) · PMCID [PMC12092160](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12092160/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Judged with AMSTAR 2, 65.2% of the reviews were non-rigorous and only 7.2% rigorous; frequent gaps were weak risk-of-bias assessment, data extraction by a single person and no preregistration.
^p38761551-quality

Pooling 22 meta-analyses with replicable data (35 unique studies) gave a medium reduction in PTSD symptoms (SMD 0.41), consistent across control conditions and between established and broader programme types, which the authors compare with second-line treatments such as medication.
^p38761551-effect

## Scope as reported

Umbrella review of published systematic reviews with a meta-meta-analysis restricted to those whose data could be reproduced; findings were also synthesised narratively.
^p38761551-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A meta-meta-analysis inherits the weaknesses of mostly non-rigorous reviews and of the trials beneath them, and PTSD samples are not complex PTSD samples.
