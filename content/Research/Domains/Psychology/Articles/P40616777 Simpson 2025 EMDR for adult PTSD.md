# Clinical and cost-effectiveness of eye movement desensitization and reprocessing for treatment and prevention of post-traumatic stress disorder in adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Simpson E, Carroll C, et al. (8 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40616777/)

**Published.** 2025 in British journal of psychology (London, England : 1953) (first online 2025-07-05).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/bjop.70005](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjop.70005) · PMID [40616777](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40616777/) · PMCID [PMC12514334](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12514334/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

EMDR reduced PTSD symptoms significantly more than waitlist or usual care, and it did not differ significantly from trauma-focused CBT; both treatments improved symptoms.
^p40616777-comparators

EMDR courses were generally shorter and took less of patients' time, and the one modelling study ranked EMDR the most cost-effective of the eleven interventions it compared.
^p40616777-duration

Most trials were small, and all but one were judged at moderate or high risk of bias.
^p40616777-bias

## Scope as reported

Six databases searched in September 2023 for trials published after the 2018 NICE guideline; Bayesian pooling of standardised mean differences only where at least three trials were available.
^p40616777-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

No significant difference from trauma-focused CBT is a null contrast drawn from small, mostly biased trials rather than a designed non-inferiority result, and the cost-effectiveness ranking rests on a single model.
