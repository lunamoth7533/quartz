# Rumination and posttraumatic stress symptoms in trauma-exposed adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Szabo YZ, Warnecke AJ, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28398085/)

**Published.** 2017 in Anxiety, stress, and coping (first online 2017-04-11).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1080/10615806.2017.1313835](https://doi.org/10.1080/10615806.2017.1313835) · PMID [28398085](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28398085/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Rumination correlated positively with posttraumatic stress symptoms (r = .50), and the link held regardless of time since trauma, gender, earlier trauma, whether events met Criterion A, rumination type, symptom measure or setting.
^p28398085-association

The correlation was stronger for intrusive re-experiencing than for avoidance or hyperarousal, and rumination about the trauma itself gave smaller effects than trait rumination.
^p28398085-clusters

## Scope as reported

Correlational synthesis of studies found in several databases and dissertation records; the authors call for work on mechanisms and on whether the link is specific to particular symptom clusters.
^p28398085-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Correlations do not show direction, and intrusions and rumination are both repeated thoughts about the event, so part of their overlap may sit in how the questionnaires are worded.
^p28398085-caution-overlap
