# Investigating alexithymia in autism: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Kinnaird E, Stewart C, Tchanturia K (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30399531/)

**Published.** 2019 in European psychiatry (first online 2018-11-03).
**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.eurpsy.2018.09.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eurpsy.2018.09.004) · PMID [30399531](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30399531/) · PMCID [PMC6331035](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6331035/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Autistic groups scored significantly higher than neurotypical groups on all Toronto Alexithymia Scale scores.
^p30399531-scores

Estimated prevalence of alexithymia was 49.93% in autistic samples against 4.89% in neurotypical samples, with a significantly increased risk in autistic participants; the authors conclude alexithymia is common, not universal, in autism.
^p30399531-prevalence

## Scope as reported

All included studies used the Toronto Alexithymia Scale; the authors suggest co-occurring autism and alexithymia may mark a subgroup with specific clinical needs and call for research on its nature and implications.
^p30399531-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Prevalence here depends on a questionnaire cut-off whose measurement invariance across autistic and non-autistic groups this design does not establish, so the figures describe scale scores more securely than an underlying trait.
^p30399531-caution-cutoff
