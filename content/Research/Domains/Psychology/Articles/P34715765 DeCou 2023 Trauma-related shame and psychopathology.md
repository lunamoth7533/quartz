# On the Association Between Trauma-Related Shame and Symptoms of Psychopathology: A Meta-Analysis.

**Authors.** DeCou CR, Lynch SM, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34715765/)

**Published.** 2023 in Trauma, violence & abuse (first online 2021-10-29).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/15248380211053617](https://doi.org/10.1177/15248380211053617) · PMID [34715765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34715765/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Trauma-related shame correlated moderately with overall psychopathology (r = 0.44), with trauma-related distress (r = 0.49) and with depression (r = 0.35).
^p34715765-correlations

Heterogeneity was significant, yet neither study quality nor sample characteristics moderated the associations; the authors recommend assessing trauma-related shame as a routine part of care for trauma survivors.
^p34715765-heterogeneity

## Scope as reported

Random-effects meta-analysis of 25 studies from seven databases that used any measure of trauma-related shame; the authors present the result as a preliminary conclusion.
^p34715765-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Correlations cannot show whether shame drives symptoms or symptoms deepen shame, and a small set of studies with mixed measures cannot separate trauma-related shame from general negative self-evaluation.
^p34715765-caution-direction
