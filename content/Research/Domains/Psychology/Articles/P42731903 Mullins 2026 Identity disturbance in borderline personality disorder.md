## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Identity disturbance is described as a lasting instability in how people see themselves, and DSM-5 was the definition most often cited, stressing unstable self-image, values and relationships.
^p42731903-definition

The SCID-II interview and the Identity Disturbance Questionnaire were the most used instruments, and Erikson's theory dominated how identity was conceptualised.
^p42731903-measures

Types of disturbance were usually described with Wilkinson-Ryan and Westen's four-factor model or other multidimensional and developmental models, which stress breaks in the coherence, stability and continuity of the self and link them to goal-directed functioning and affect regulation.
^p42731903-types

## Scope as reported

Scoping review of four databases plus grey literature, with no date limits; it maps definitions, types and measures rather than pooling effects, and the authors call for integrated theory and better instruments.
^p42731903-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Where identity disturbance is a diagnostic criterion, finding it in people diagnosed with the disorder is partly built in, so its specificity has to be tested against neighbouring conditions such as complex PTSD.
^p42731903-caution-circular
