# Recognition of facial expressions in autism: Effects of face masks and alexithymia.

**Authors.** Gehdu BK, Tsantani M, Press C, Gray KL, Cook R (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36872641/)

**Published.** 2023 in Quarterly journal of experimental psychology.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1177/17470218231163007](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218231163007) · PMID [36872641](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36872641/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Autistic participants with high alexithymia categorised fewer facial expressions correctly than non-autistic controls, whereas autistic participants with low alexithymia were not impaired.
^p36872641-subgroups

The same pattern held when the lower face was covered by a surgical mask, so forcing judgements onto the eye region did not reveal an expression-recognition deficit attributable to autism alone.
^p36872641-masks

## Scope as reported

A laboratory expression-categorisation task framed as a test of whether reported recognition difficulties in autism reflect co-occurring alexithymia rather than autism itself; group sizes are not given in the abstract.
^p36872641-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Splitting autistic participants by a questionnaire cut-off creates small subgroups, and the absence of a difference in the low-alexithymia group is weaker evidence than a positive finding; the result supports the alexithymia account for this task without ruling out autism-related effects on other tasks.
