# Qualitative designs

## Definition

Qualitative research investigates meaning, process and experience using interviews, observation, documents and other data. It answers questions about how and why something happens in context rather than how much of it there is.

## How it works

**Sampling is purposive, and that is a strength when declared.** Qualitative designs usually sample cases to characterise a process, meaning or mechanism rather than to estimate a population parameter. Samples are selected for information richness - typical cases, extreme cases, maximum variation - rather than to represent a population, so the inference is conceptual (what is this and how does it work) rather than statistical (how many, how much). Analysis is typically iterative, moving between data and interpretation rather than fixing a model in advance. The inference-type difference is why qualitative findings integrate with trials in reviews as complementary rather than as a second-best trial. [[Functional outcomes and measurement]] [[Intervention outcomes and autistic perspectives]]

**The disagreement about criteria.** The methodological literature reviews disagreement about how qualitative research should be evaluated, including whether quantitative criteria such as generalisability transfer at all: because the designs are not sampling to estimate a parameter, asking for a confidence interval would answer a different question than the one posed. [[P18626033 Qualitative research criteria#^p18626033-controversy|Qualitative research criteria]] [[P18626033 Qualitative research criteria#^p18626033-limit|Qualitative research criteria]]

**How appraisal works instead.** Evaluation focuses on transparency, coherence between question, method and analysis, reflexivity about the researchers' role and influence, and how conclusions were generated, so a qualitative claim is judged by the quality of its reasoning and evidence rather than by statistical significance. Reflexivity means making the researcher's position and its effect on the data visible rather than pretending it is absent. [[P18626033 Qualitative research criteria#^p18626033-controversy|Qualitative research criteria]] [[P18626033 Qualitative research criteria#^p18626033-criteria|Qualitative research criteria]] [[P18626033 Qualitative research criteria#^p18626033-reading|Qualitative research criteria]] The credibility of an account rests on thick description, an audit trail and participant validation, and its transferability on description thick enough for readers to judge applicability. Applying trial criteria - power, blinding - is a category error; the designs answer questions trials do not ask, with their own failure modes. [[Qualitative designs]] [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-outcomes|Autistic adults' views on early interventions]]

**Contribution.** Qualitative work identifies mechanisms, generates hypotheses, documents lived experience and surfaces outcomes that existing instruments omit - which is how measures are sometimes built in the first place. [[P29942800 Boateng 2018 Developing and validating scales#^p29942800-content|Developing and validating scales]]

**Where it enters this library with force.** In autism research, a systematic review of autistic adults' retrospective views of early interventions drew on qualitative and mixed-method studies to identify which outcomes matter to participants - well-being, adult support, accessibility and reducing harmful behaviours rather than reducing autistic traits. That is exactly the kind of question a trial cannot answer, and the review's own limitation section notes that retrospective accounts and varied methods shape what was reported. [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-outcomes|Autistic adults' views on early interventions]] [[P42411151 Nosova 2026 Autistic adults views on early interventions#^p42411151-limit|Autistic adults' views on early interventions]]

**Combination.** Mixed-methods designs use qualitative work to explain or contextualise quantitative findings; the two strands answer different questions rather than validating each other's numbers. [[P18626033 Qualitative research criteria#^p18626033-reading|Qualitative research criteria]]

**What it cannot do.** A qualitative study does not estimate prevalence, does not test whether an intervention works, and does not license extrapolation to populations it did not study. Its findings are about the studied cases, and their transferability is an argument a reader makes rather than a property the design guarantees. ## Evidence and status

Criteria are debated within the field, and there is no single checklist; the practical standard is whether another researcher can follow how the findings were produced.

## Uncertainties

- Findings are not intended to be statistically generalisable, and asking them to be is a category error rather than a limitation.
