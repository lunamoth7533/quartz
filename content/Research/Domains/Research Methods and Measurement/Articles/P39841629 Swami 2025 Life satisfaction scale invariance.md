# Life satisfaction around the world: Measurement invariance of the Satisfaction With Life Scale (SWLS) across 65 nations, 40 languages, gender identities, and age groups.

**Authors.** Swami V, Stieger S, et al. (253 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39841629/)

**Published.** 2025 in PloS one (volume 20, issue 1, e0313107; PubMed date 2025-01-22).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0313107](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0313107) · PMID [39841629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39841629/) · PMCID [PMC11753666](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11753666/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Configural and metric invariance held across all nations, languages, gender identities and age groups, and full scalar invariance held across gender identities and age groups.
^p39841629-levels

Across nations and languages only partial scalar invariance was reached, using alignment methods, and it was not reached for three national groups; latent means differed widely between nations and languages but little by gender identity or age.
^p39841629-partial

## Scope as reported

Survey data collected under largely uniform conditions between 2020 and 2022; one short, unidimensional well-being scale was tested.
^p39841629-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Partial scalar invariance supports cautious latent-mean comparison under the chosen model; it does not make raw sum-score comparisons between countries safe, and it says nothing about clinical scales.
^p39841629-caution-alignment
