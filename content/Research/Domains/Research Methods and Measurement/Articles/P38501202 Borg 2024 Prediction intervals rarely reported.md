# Meta-analysis prediction intervals are under reported in sport and exercise medicine.

**Authors.** Borg DN, Impellizzeri FM, et al. (14 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38501202/)

**Published.** 2024 in Scandinavian journal of medicine & science in sports (volume 34, issue 3, e14603).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/sms.14603](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14603) · PMID [38501202](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38501202/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Among 866 random-effects meta-analyses, the estimated probability that a prediction interval was reported was 1.7% in sports medicine and 3.9% in general medicine.
^p38501202-reporting

For the 220 sports-medicine meta-analyses where a prediction interval could be calculated, its reading disagreed with the conclusion drawn from the reported confidence interval in 60%, and prediction intervals were about 3.4 times wider.
^p38501202-discrepancy

## Scope as reported

One meta-analysis was sampled per article from highly ranked journals published 2012-2022; the prediction-interval recalculation covered sports-medicine articles only.
^p38501202-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The rates describe sports medicine and general medical journals; reporting practice in psychiatric meta-analyses was not measured here and needs its own check.
^p38501202-caution-transfer
