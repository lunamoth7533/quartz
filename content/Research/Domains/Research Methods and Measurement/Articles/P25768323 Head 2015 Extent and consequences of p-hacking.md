## Reported findings (checked abstract)

p-hacking is defined as collecting or selecting data or analyses until non-significant results become significant, one form of bias created by a focus on novel, confirmatory and significant findings.
^p25768323-definition

Text-mining of reported P values indicated that p-hacking is widespread across science.
^p25768323-extent

Tests inside meta-analyses suggested its effect is weak relative to the true effects being estimated, so it probably does not drastically change meta-analytic conclusions.
^p25768323-meta

## Scope as reported

Inference about p-hacking from the distribution of text-mined P values across disciplines, with a worked method for testing for it within meta-analyses.
^p25768323-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Distribution-based tests detect p-hacking only in aggregate and can be confounded by publication bias; a reassuring meta-analytic result does not certify any single study.
^p25768323-caution-aggregate
