## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Sensitivity is the proportion of people who truly have the outcome whom a test calls positive; specificity is the proportion of people without it whom the test calls negative.
^p34065637-definitions

Positive predictive value is the share of positive results that are true positives, and negative predictive value the share of negative results that are true negatives.
^p34065637-predictive

Sensitivity and specificity trade off against each other but are usually treated as properties of the test, whereas predictive values shift with pre-test probability, such as the prevalence in the population tested.
^p34065637-prevalence

## Scope as reported

A teaching review that defines four accuracy metrics and illustrates them with one real-world example from the medical literature; it evaluates no particular test.
^p34065637-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Treating sensitivity and specificity as fixed is an approximation: they also shift with the mix of people tested, for example a referral clinic compared with the community, which is why accuracy studies must state their setting.
^p34065637-caution-stability
