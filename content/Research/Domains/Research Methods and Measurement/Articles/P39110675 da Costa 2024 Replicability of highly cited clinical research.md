# Estimating the replicability of highly cited clinical research (2004-2018).

**Authors.** da Costa GG, Neves K, Amaral O (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39110675/)

**Published.** 2024 in PloS one (volume 19, issue 8, e0307145; PubMed date 2024-08-07).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0307145](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0307145) · PMID [39110675](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39110675/) · PMCID [PMC11305584](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11305584/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Of 89 eligible highly cited intervention studies, 24 could be checked against a valid replication (17 meta-analyses, 7 primary studies); 20 of those 24 (83%) held up when both a significant same-direction effect and overlapping confidence intervals were required.
^p39110675-rate

The authors found no evidence of systematic effect-size inflation in these studies, with a mean ratio of 1.03 between original and later effects.
^p39110675-inflation

## Scope as reported

A very select sample: most highly cited studies had no eligible replication, and so few results were contradicted that the analysis had little power to find predictors of replicability. The authors caution that the estimate may not generalise to clinical research in general.
^p39110675-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Most of the replications were meta-analyses, which may pool the original trial, so agreement is not necessarily independent confirmation; the abstract does not say how this was handled.
^p39110675-caution-selection
