# Publication bias in psychology: A closer look at the correlation between sample size and effect size.

**Authors.** Linden AH, Pollet TV, Hönekopp J (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38359021/)

**Published.** 2024 in PloS one (volume 19, issue 2, e0297075; PubMed date 2024-02-15).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0297075](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0297075) · PMID [38359021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38359021/) · PMCID [PMC10868788](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10868788/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Correlations between sample size and effect size looked unremarkable in 150 meta-analyses, judged against bias-free multiple replications, and did not suggest a strong effect of publication bias on psychology meta-analyses.
^p38359021-metaanalyses

In 160 randomly sampled papers, the negative correlation was much larger for each paper's focal effects than for effects picked at random from the same papers; how far this reflects bias was left open.
^p38359021-focal

## Scope as reported

Two observational meta-research studies asking whether negative correlations between sample size and effect size reflect publication bias or ordinary planning of larger samples for smaller expected effects.
^p38359021-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A sample-size and effect-size correlation is an indirect indicator: its absence does not rule out publication bias, and its presence does not identify the mechanism.
^p38359021-caution-indicator
