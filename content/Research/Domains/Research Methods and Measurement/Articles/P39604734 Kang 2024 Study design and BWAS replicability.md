# Study design features increase replicability in brain-wide association studies.

**Authors.** Kang K, Seidlitz J, et al. (20 author entries) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39604734/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In a meta-analysis of brain volume and age across 63 MRI studies (77,695 scans), studies with a wider spread of the covariate, and longitudinal studies, reported larger standardised effect sizes.
^p39604734-design

Reanalyses of UK Biobank and ADNI showed that changing the sampling scheme raised standardised effects and replicability; the longitudinal schemes were also tested on cognitive, psychopathology and demographic associations in ABCD.
^p39604734-sampling

Common longitudinal models that treat between-person and within-person change as equal could lower standardised effects and replicability; modelling the two separately avoided the problem.
^p39604734-longitudinal

## Scope as reported

Analyses and meta-analyses of existing consortium and cohort MRI data using a robust effect size index. The design guidance rests mainly on age effects, with a generalisation check in one adolescent cohort.
^p39604734-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A standardised effect grows when the sampled covariate range widens, so a larger effect under an optimised design describes that design's sample; it is not a stronger brain-behaviour link for any one person.
^p39604734-caution-standardised
