# Diagnostic Accuracy and Acceptability of the Primary Care Posttraumatic Stress Disorder Screen for the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (Fifth Edition) Among US Veterans.

**Authors.** Bovin MJ, Kimerling R, et al. (7 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33538826/)

**Published.** 2021 in JAMA network open (first online 2021-02-01).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.36733](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.36733) · PMID [33538826](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33538826/) · PMCID [PMC7862990](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7862990/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Against a blinded structured interview, the PC-PTSD-5 discriminated well: area under the curve 0.927 overall, 0.932 in men and 0.899 in women.
^p33538826-accuracy

A cut point of 4 best balanced false negatives and false positives overall and in men, but among women it produced many false negatives (33.3%); a cut point of 3 suited women better at the cost of more false positives.
^p33538826-cutpoint

Veterans rated the screen as highly acceptable, and the authors advise choosing cut points by sample and screening purpose.
^p33538826-acceptability

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional diagnostic accuracy study in primary care clinics at two VA medical centres: 31% of those approached took part and 396 completed all measures (mean age 61, mostly male and White).
^p33538826-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Accuracy in an older, mostly male veteran sample may not carry over to civilian, younger or complex-trauma populations, and the women's subgroup was small.
^p33538826-caution-transfer
