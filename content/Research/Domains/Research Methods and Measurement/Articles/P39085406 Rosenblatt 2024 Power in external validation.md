# Power and reproducibility in the external validation of brain-phenotype predictions.

**Authors.** Rosenblatt M, Tejavibulya L, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39085406/)

**Published.** 2024 in Nature human behaviour (volume 8, issue 10, pages 2018-2033; PubMed date 2024-07-31).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41562-024-01931-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01931-7) · PMID [39085406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39085406/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In more than 900 million resampling simulations, predictions of large effects reached adequate power with a few hundred participants in both the training and the external sample, whereas small and medium effects needed hundreds to thousands in each.
^p39085406-power

Earlier external validation studies were mostly too small to have adequate power, and the usual theoretical power curves have to be corrected for the size of the training sample.
^p39085406-prior

Internal validation performance often anticipated external performance (Pearson r differing by less than 0.2), especially across well-harmonised datasets.
^p39085406-internal

## Scope as reported

Simulation study reusing existing research cohorts; the phenotypes were research measures (age, body mass index, reasoning, working memory, attention and anxiety or depression symptom scores), not clinical diagnoses.
^p39085406-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Enough power to confirm that a model generalises is a minimum for credibility, not a demonstration of individual usefulness; calibration and decision value remain separate questions.
^p39085406-caution-generalise
