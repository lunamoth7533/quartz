## Reported findings (checked abstract)

P values appear in almost every medical paper, yet they sit outside any formal system of statistical inference and have been widely misconstrued since at least the 1940s.
^p18582619-status

The commentary sets out twelve common misreadings, explains why each is wrong and describes their consequences.
^p18582619-misconceptions

It contrasts the P value with the Bayes factor, presented as having the interpretability that an evidential measure needs.
^p18582619-bayes

It names as the most serious error the belief that one experiment's data can give the probability that a conclusion is wrong without reference to external evidence or mechanistic plausibility.
^p18582619-error

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Replacing P values with Bayes factors moves judgement into the choice of prior and alternative hypothesis; it changes what has to be justified rather than removing the need for judgement.
^p18582619-caution-bayes

The definition this library works from: a P value is the probability, calculated under a test model that includes the null hypothesis and every analysis assumption, of a result at least as extreme as the one observed. Anything more that is commonly read into it - the chance the hypothesis is true, the chance the result arose by chance alone, the size of the effect, or proof of no effect when P is large - goes beyond that definition.
^p18582619-caution-definition
