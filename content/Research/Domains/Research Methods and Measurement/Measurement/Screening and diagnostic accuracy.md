# Screening and diagnostic accuracy

## Definition

Diagnostic accuracy describes how well a test's result agrees with a reference standard, summarised by sensitivity, specificity and the two predictive values. [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-definitions|Monaghan 2021]] [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-predictive|Monaghan 2021]] A screening instrument uses that result to decide who should be assessed further, not who has the condition: in adult autism guidance, for example, a threshold score leads to assessment rather than diagnosis. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-screening|NICE CG142]] [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-caution|Appraisal: NICE CG142]]

## How it works

**Four numbers, two directions.** Sensitivity is the share of people with the condition whom the test calls positive, and specificity the share without it whom the test calls negative; positive predictive value is the share of positive results that are correct, and negative predictive value the share of negative results that are correct. [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-definitions|Monaghan 2021]] [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-predictive|Monaghan 2021]] Sensitivity and specificity start from the true state and ask about the result; predictive values start from the result and ask about the true state, which is the question a person holding a result actually has. [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-predictive|Monaghan 2021]]

**Base rates change what a positive means.** Sensitivity and specificity trade off against each other and are usually treated as properties of the test, but predictive values shift with pre-test probability, such as the prevalence in the population tested. [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-prevalence|Monaghan 2021]] A hypothetical illustration: take a screen with 80% sensitivity and 80% specificity. In a specialist clinic where 30 of every 100 people have the condition, 240 of 1,000 people screen true-positive and 140 false-positive, so about 63% of positives are correct. In the community, where 3 in 100 have it, the same screen gives 24 true and 194 false positives, so only about 11% of positives are correct, while about 99% of negatives are right. None of these figures describes a real instrument or person.

**Accuracy belongs to a setting.** Treating sensitivity and specificity as fixed is itself an approximation, because they also shift with the mix of people tested. [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-caution-stability|Appraisal: Monaghan 2021]] The Mood Disorder Questionnaire had 66% sensitivity and 79% specificity in psychiatric services but 43% and 95% in primary care or general-population samples. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-accuracy|Carvalho 2015]] [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-primary|Carvalho 2015]] Among adults referred for autism assessment, the Autism-Spectrum Quotient barely separated those later diagnosed from those not (area under the curve 0.527; sensitivity 70%, specificity 35%), as expected where most referred people already show autistic-like features. [[P37160157 Sappok 2023 Adult autism differentials and AQ utility#^p37160157-aq|Sappok 2023]] [[P37160157 Sappok 2023 Adult autism differentials and AQ utility#^p37160157-caution-spectrum|Appraisal: Sappok 2023]]

**Cut-offs are choices.** Among US veterans in primary care, the PC-PTSD-5 discriminated well against a blinded structured interview (area under the curve 0.927), but the cut point that best balanced errors overall and in men produced many false negatives among women, for whom a lower cut point worked better at the cost of more false positives. [[P33538826 Bovin 2021 PC-PTSD-5 accuracy in veterans#^p33538826-accuracy|Bovin 2021]] [[P33538826 Bovin 2021 PC-PTSD-5 accuracy in veterans#^p33538826-cutpoint|Bovin 2021]] The authors advise choosing cut points by sample and screening purpose. [[P33538826 Bovin 2021 PC-PTSD-5 accuracy in veterans#^p33538826-acceptability|Bovin 2021]]

**Hypomania screens.** For bipolar disorder, three questionnaires were meta-analysed against structured interviews: at recommended cut-offs in psychiatric services the HCL-32 had 81% sensitivity and 67% specificity, the MDQ 66% and 79%, and the BSDS 69% and 86%, and the HCL-32 outperformed the MDQ for bipolar II disorder. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-accuracy|Carvalho 2015]] [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-type2|Carvalho 2015]] Most studies came from mental health settings and several had a high risk of bias. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-limit|Carvalho 2015]] In low-prevalence settings even high specificity leaves many false positives. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-caution-screen|Appraisal: Carvalho 2015]]

**ADHD screens and overlapping symptoms.** In 618 adults with anxiety disorders, some hyperactivity items of the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale loaded more strongly on anxiety than on ADHD, so a positive screen there can partly reflect anxiety. [[P38288573 Alarachi 2024 ADHD screener validity in anxiety#^p38288573-overlap|Alarachi 2024]] [[P38288573 Alarachi 2024 ADHD screener validity in anxiety#^p38288573-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Alarachi 2024]] In post-9/11 veterans, ADHD measures agreed poorly: one retrospective scale had low sensitivity against a historical diagnosis, and sensitivity and specificity against the self-report screener were both about 61%. [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-measures|Knight 2025]]

**Screening is not diagnosis.** Screens produce false positives and false negatives, so a score neither confirms nor excludes a condition; it opens an assessment. [[F24 NICE CG142 autism in adults diagnosis and management#^f24-caution|Appraisal: NICE CG142]]

**Reading rule.** For any accuracy figure, ask against which reference standard, in which setting and at what prevalence, at which cut-off and in whom (sex, age, co-occurring conditions), and then turn sensitivity and specificity into predictive values for the population where the test will be used. [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-prevalence|Monaghan 2021]] [[P33538826 Bovin 2021 PC-PTSD-5 accuracy in veterans#^p33538826-cutpoint|Bovin 2021]] [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-caution-stability|Appraisal: Monaghan 2021]]

## Evidence and status

The definitions, and the dependence of predictive values on prevalence, are settled methodology. [[P34065637 Monaghan 2021 Sensitivity specificity and predictive values#^p34065637-prevalence|Monaghan 2021]] Accuracy data for this library's conditions are uneven: a meta-analysis for hypomania screens, one large veteran study for a PTSD screen, and single-clinic studies for adult ADHD and autism screens. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-limit|Carvalho 2015]] [[P33538826 Bovin 2021 PC-PTSD-5 accuracy in veterans#^p33538826-limit|Bovin 2021]] [[P38288573 Alarachi 2024 ADHD screener validity in anxiety#^p38288573-limit|Alarachi 2024]] [[P37160157 Sappok 2023 Adult autism differentials and AQ utility#^p37160157-limit|Sappok 2023]] Across them, accuracy falls where symptoms overlap with co-occurring conditions and where a referral setting has already filtered the population. [[P38288573 Alarachi 2024 ADHD screener validity in anxiety#^p38288573-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Alarachi 2024]] [[P37160157 Sappok 2023 Adult autism differentials and AQ utility#^p37160157-caution-spectrum|Appraisal: Sappok 2023]]

## Uncertainties

- How the PC-PTSD-5 performs in civilian, younger and complex-trauma populations, and in women more generally. [[P33538826 Bovin 2021 PC-PTSD-5 accuracy in veterans#^p33538826-caution-transfer|Appraisal: Bovin 2021]]
- Whether hypomania screens work in primary care, where few studies were done. [[P25451435 Carvalho 2015 Screening for bipolar spectrum disorders#^p25451435-limit|Carvalho 2015]]
- How to separate ADHD from anxiety- or trauma-related concentration problems on self-report screens. [[P38288573 Alarachi 2024 ADHD screener validity in anxiety#^p38288573-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Alarachi 2024]] [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Knight 2025]]
- The accuracy of autism screens in community rather than referral samples is not yet sourced in this library. [[P37160157 Sappok 2023 Adult autism differentials and AQ utility#^p37160157-caution-spectrum|Appraisal: Sappok 2023]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Cohort study (Military Medicine).** In combat-deployed veterans, ADHD measures agreed poorly with one another and ADHD was more common with PTSD, a reminder that the reference standard can itself be uncertain. [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-measures|Knight 2025]] [[P39607449 Knight 2025 ADHD and PTSD in veterans#^p39607449-ptsd|Knight 2025]]
- **2024 · Psychometric study (Assessment).** In an anxiety clinic, some ADHD-screen items tracked anxiety, showing how a co-occurring condition can blur what a positive screen means. [[P38288573 Alarachi 2024 ADHD screener validity in anxiety#^p38288573-overlap|Alarachi 2024]]
- **2023 · Clinical cohort study (Psychiatrische Praxis).** In referred adults the AQ hardly discriminated autism from its differentials, confirming that accuracy depends on the setting. [[P37160157 Sappok 2023 Adult autism differentials and AQ utility#^p37160157-aq|Sappok 2023]]
