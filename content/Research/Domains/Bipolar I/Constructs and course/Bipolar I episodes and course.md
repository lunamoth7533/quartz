# Bipolar I episodes and course

**Definition.** Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania, with depressive episodes common across the lifetime course. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar Disorder]]

## Supported claims

- The diagnosis is defined by mania; a depressive episode is common but not required. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar Disorder]]
- Clinical care considers acute symptoms and long-term stability together. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar Disorder]]
- Guideline recommendations are organised by illness phase. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

## How it works

**The defining episode and the lifetime frame.** Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania; depressive episodes are common but not required for the diagnosis. The clinical picture combines episodes with a lifetime course, so acute treatment and long-term management answer different questions, and treatment planning considers acute symptoms and long-term stability together. That structure is why an assessment cannot be an episode inventory alone. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]] [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar disorder]]

**Episode-level description is standardised.** ICD-11 gives separate descriptions for depressive, manic, mixed and hypomanic episodes before the disorder categories, with bipolar type I disorder numbered 6A60 and each entry stating essential features, boundaries and course qualifiers. Episode-level description is therefore separable from diagnosis, and a claim about the episode does not automatically describe the disorder, or the reverse. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-use|ICD-11 CDDR]]

**Assessment covers risk and co-occurring conditions.** National (UK) guidance sets out assessment expectations for people with suspected or diagnosed bipolar disorder, including risk assessment and attention to coexisting conditions, and recommends long-term management with both pharmacological and structured psychological components, with explicit cautions for particular medicines in pregnancy and for younger people. The assessment is a structured process rather than a checklist for the presenting episode. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-assessment|Bipolar disorder CG185]] [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-longterm|Bipolar disorder CG185]]

**Course and treatment evidence are phase-specific.** Because the guideline evidence is organised by phase, a statement about treatment applies to the phase in which it was tested; the CANMAT and ISBD synthesis translates evidence into phase-specific lines of recommendation and carries no individual dosing guidance. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]] [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-caution-individual|AI appraisal: CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

**Course descriptions need longitudinal data.** How to define recovery, and how much subthreshold symptoms matter for outcome, remain debated, which is why course descriptions rely on longitudinal cohorts rather than single assessments.

**Reading rule.** Name the episode, the phase, the course pattern and the source of the diagnostic criteria. Longitudinal course is the part of this picture that a single assessment cannot supply, and retrospective recall is an imperfect substitute. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-limit|Bipolar disorder CG185]] [[P18509902 Shiffman 2008 Ecological momentary assessment#^p18509902-rationale|Ecological momentary assessment]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** The episode/disorder distinction this article develops is what lets the pharmacology and clinical-psychiatry domains talk about phase-specific treatment evidence coherently - a recommendation that names its phase inherits this article's framing, and a recommendation that does not cannot be read against the evidence without ambiguity. [[Phase-specific bipolar treatment evidence]] [[Bipolar depression and mixed features]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Descriptions of course are population averages; individual trajectories, triggers and comorbidities vary.
