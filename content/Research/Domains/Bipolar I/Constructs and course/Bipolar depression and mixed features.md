# Bipolar depression and mixed features

## Definition

Depressive episodes are common in bipolar I disorder even though mania defines the diagnosis. Mixed features describe episodes in which depressive and manic or hypomanic symptoms co-occur.

## How it works

**Episode and disorder are separate levels of description.** ICD-11's clinical descriptions give separate entries for depressive, manic, mixed and hypomanic episodes before the disorder categories - bipolar type I disorder is numbered 6A60 - and each entry states essential features, boundaries with other categories and course qualifiers, so episode-level description is separable from the disorder label. ICD-11's rules differ from DSM-5-TR in places, so an episode label means something specific to the system it comes from, and a mixed episode is not simply severe depression. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-purpose|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-use|ICD-11 CDDR]]

**Mania defines the category; depression dominates the time.** Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania, and depressive episodes are common but not required for the diagnosis. Clinical pictures combine episodes with a lifetime course, which is why treatment planning addresses acute symptoms and long-term stability together. Because depressive episodes occupy much of the time a person is unwell, bipolar disorder is frequently first recognised as depression, which is the practical reason history-taking covers past activation rather than relying on the presenting state. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]] [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar disorder]]

**Why the distinction from unipolar depression matters.** NIMH separates bipolar depression from major depressive disorder and from persistent depressive disorder explicitly because treatment implications differ. That is a classification statement rather than a treatment recommendation: the point is that the same depressive syndrome sits in two different longitudinal contexts. [[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-patterns|Depression]]

**Mixed features.** Mixed presentations are episodes in which depressive and manic or hypomanic symptoms co-occur, and the clinical literature associates co-occurring activation and depression with greater severity and risk - which is why they are specified rather than averaged into a label that would hide the activation. Episode-level description in a classification manual does not resolve where mixed features end and rapid cycling begins. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar disorder]]

## Evidence and status

Episode definitions are reliably applied; the treatment evidence for bipolar depression is smaller than for mania, and mixed states are less well studied. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-balance|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

The CANMAT and ISBD guidance synthesises efficacy, safety and tolerability evidence into first-, second- and third-line recommendations for different phases of the illness, with a 2023 update adding new evidence; it is a summary of a guideline rather than a trial and it carries no prescribing or dose guidance. The reading rule that follows is that a bipolar-depression claim should name the phase being treated, because evidence is not pooled across phases and a recommendation that applies to bipolar depression may not transfer to acute mania or to maintenance. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]] [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-balance|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]] [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-caution-individual|AI appraisal: CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]] Separating episode from disorder is what keeps such claims testable: a study reporting on "bipolar depression" can be checked for which episode type was actually measured, and the phase-specific treatment literature depends on that clarity being preserved. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]]

Subtype and ascertainment are part of the evidence base. A 2025 multi-ancestry genomic meta-analysis reports differences in genetic architecture depending on the source of patient ascertainment and on bipolar subtype, type I or type II. Study populations defined by different subtype and ascertainment rules are therefore not interchangeable, and a finding in one cannot be read straight across to the other. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|Genomics of bipolar disorder]]
