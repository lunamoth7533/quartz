# Bipolar I Map

Bipolar I is defined by mania, then treated across acute and maintenance phases. This map connects the condition to the five foundation domains and marks where the biology is group-level rather than personal.

**Contents.** [[#Reference overview|Overview]], [[#Reference spine|Reference spine]], [[#Concept register|Concept register]], [[#Where this domain connects|Cross-domain links]], [[#Evidence boundaries|Evidence boundaries]], [[#Learning route|Learning route]], [[#Domains|Domains]]

## Reference overview

Bipolar I disorder is defined by the occurrence of mania, with depressive episodes typical and
course highly variable. The domain's reference value is that it shows how a condition construct,
an evidence base and a set of mechanistic hypotheses fit together - and how often the hypotheses
are stated more confidently than the evidence warrants.

**The construct.** Episode phenomenology separates mania, hypomania, depression and mixed features,
and the longitudinal course (age of onset, episode frequency, inter-episode function) is what makes
the diagnosis a life-course description rather than a state. Diagnostic thresholds differ between
classification systems, which matters for who is counted in research samples. [[Bipolar I episodes and course]] [[Bipolar depression and mixed features]] [[ICD-11 versus DSM-5 classification]]

**Differential reasoning is a base-rate exercise.** Psychotic features, substance effects, medical
conditions and neurodevelopmental presentations can mimic episodes; co-occurrence with anxiety,
ADHD and substance use is common rather than exceptional. [[Co-occurrence and differential reasoning]]

**Hypotheses and their status.** Circadian and social-rhythm accounts connect timing instability to
episode onset and are among the better-supported mechanistically testable proposals; dopamine
findings are state-dependent and partly contradictory; cellular-energy and inflammatory proposals
are supported by measurable differences whose causal direction is unresolved; and imaging findings
are group-level and confounded by medication and course. [[Circadian and social rhythm hypotheses]] [[Dopamine hypothesis in bipolar disorder]] [[Cellular energy and mitochondrial hypotheses]] [[Inflammation and immune hypotheses in bipolar disorder]] [[Bipolar MRI findings and their limits]] [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-medication|ENIGMA cortical MRI findings in bipolar disorder]]

**Genetics describes architecture, not destiny.** Common-variant contribution is polygenic and
aggregated into scores whose individual interpretation requires calibration; rare variants of
larger effect exist but explain a minority of cases. [[Bipolar genetics and polygenic risk]]
[[Genes, environment and polygenic risk]]

**Treatment evidence is phase-specific.** Acute mania, acute bipolar depression and maintenance
each have their own evidence base, and conclusions from one phase do not transfer to another;
lithium's clinical effect is well established while its recurrence-prevention mechanism remains
only partially understood; and adjunctive psychotherapy has component-level evidence for
recurrence and depressive symptoms. [[Phase-specific bipolar treatment evidence]] [[Lithium mechanisms and uncertainty]] [[Bipolar psychotherapy evidence]] [[Psychoeducation and social rhythms]]

**Evidence boundaries.** Longitudinal data are observational and confounded; RCTs are short
relative to the course; certainty is often low or very low; and this hub describes research rather
than personal treatment. [[Longitudinal and within-person inference]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-limit|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

**Where to start.** [[Bipolar I episodes and course]] for the construct, [[Phase-specific bipolar treatment evidence]] for what is established, and [[Circadian and social rhythm hypotheses]] for the leading mechanistic family. The full article list for this domain is in the concept register below; [[Reference Index]] carries A-Z, concept-kind, domain, condition and evidence routes over the whole reference layer.

## Reference spine

Bipolar I disorder is defined by mania and described across a lifetime course. The reference literature separates three questions that are often conflated: what an episode looks like, what the longitudinal pattern is, and which mechanisms are proposed.

Episode description is standardised. ICD-11 describes depressive, manic, mixed and hypomanic episodes before the disorder categories, and bipolar I is defined by the occurrence of mania with depressive episodes common but not required. [[F93 WHO ICD-11 CDDR#^f93-structure|ICD-11 CDDR]] [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-mania|Bipolar disorder]] Because depressive time often dominates, first recognition frequently happens in a depressive episode, which is why history-taking covers past activation. [[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-patterns|Depression]]

Mechanistic hypotheses are multiple and none is established. Dopamine findings are state-dependent - elevated D2/3 availability with mania, increased transporter levels with depression - and both agonists and antidopaminergic drugs have shown effects in bipolar depression, which resists a single-direction account. [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-state|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]] [[P28289283 Ashok 2017 Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder#^p28289283-pharmacology|Dopamine hypothesis of bipolar disorder]] Circadian accounts propose that life events disrupt rhythms, and the evaluating review reports limited direct support while considering an internal pacemaker alternative. [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-theory|The social zeitgeber theory]] [[P16904251 Grandin 2006 Social zeitgeber theory#^p16904251-evaluation|The social zeitgeber theory]] Immune findings are group-level marker differences with substantial confounds. [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-finding|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]] [[P22749156 Munkholm 2013 Cytokines in bipolar disorder#^p22749156-caution|Cytokines in bipolar disorder]]

Genetic architecture is polygenic with substantial sharing with other conditions, which informs aetiological research rather than diagnosis. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-loci|Genomics of bipolar disorder]] [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|Genomics of bipolar disorder]] Imaging findings are reproducible group differences with overlap between groups and confounds that cannot be removed. [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-thickness|ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder]] [[P28461699 Hibar 2018 ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder#^p28461699-medication|ENIGMA cortical abnormalities in bipolar disorder]]

Treatment evidence is organised by phase, and guidelines attach recommendation strength to the underlying evidence review rather than to mechanism. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-longterm|Bipolar disorder CG185]] [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

## Evidence gaps

- Most treatment evidence is short-term relative to a lifetime condition; maintenance data is thinner than acute-phase data.
- Imaging findings are group averages with medication confounds and do not support individual diagnosis.
- Genomic findings describe risk architecture in populations and do not predict individual course.
