# Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs

## Definition

Benzodiazepines are positive modulators of GABA-A receptors: they strengthen the inhibitory signal that GABA produces rather than activating the receptor on their own. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-mechanism|Griffin 2013]] The Z-drugs - zolpidem, zopiclone, eszopiclone and zaleplon - are insomnia medicines introduced as alternatives to benzodiazepines, and like them they act on GABA receptors, which orexin receptor antagonists do not. [[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-motive|Treves 2018]] [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-mechanism|Muehlan 2023]] The European insomnia guideline endorses benzodiazepines and benzodiazepine receptor agonists only for short-term use. [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-shortterm|Riemann 2023]]

## How it works

**Members.** The benzodiazepines span drugs of different potency and duration of action. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-profiles|Griffin 2013]] In the antiseizure classification they form part of the family that enhances GABA inhibition, alongside tiagabine and vigabatrin. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-families|Sills 2020]]

**Mechanism.** Because they modulate rather than activate the receptor, benzodiazepines amplify inhibition where GABA is already acting. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-mechanism|Griffin 2013]] Their sedative, anxiolytic, anticonvulsant and muscle-relaxant effects depend on receptor subtype and dose, which is why one mechanism yields several clinical uses. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-effects|Griffin 2013]] Orexin receptor antagonists make the contrast: they leave GABA receptors alone and instead block an arousal-promoting signal. [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-mechanism|Muehlan 2023]]

**What it is used for.** In insomnia disorder, benzodiazepines, zolpidem, zopiclone and eszopiclone improved self-rated sleep quality more than placebo in acute trials, with moderate-to-high certainty, but among them only eszopiclone beat placebo long term, on very low-certainty evidence. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-acute|De Crescenzo 2022]] [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-longterm|De Crescenzo 2022]] The European guideline places cognitive behavioural therapy first and medication after it. [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-cbti|Riemann 2023]] In generalised anxiety disorder, benzodiazepines were effective against placebo but poorly tolerated. [[P30712879 Slee 2019 Drug treatments for generalised anxiety disorder#^p30712879-tolerability|Slee 2019]] In PTSD they neither treated nor prevented the disorder across 18 studies, and the reviewers regard them as relatively contraindicated after recent trauma. [[P26164054 Guina 2015 Benzodiazepines for PTSD#^p26164054-ineffective|Guina 2015]] [[P26164054 Guina 2015 Benzodiazepines for PTSD#^p26164054-conclusion|Guina 2015]]

**Class-level adverse effects and serious risks.** Tolerance, dependence and withdrawal, sedation and falls in older people are the characteristic risks, and older people, those with lung, liver or kidney disease and those taking other medication classes are most vulnerable to toxicity. [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-risks|Griffin 2013]] [[P23789008 Griffin 2013 Benzodiazepine pharmacology#^p23789008-profiles|Griffin 2013]] The Z-drugs share the fall risk: in older adults their use was associated with higher odds of fracture (odds ratio 1.63), and zolpidem with more injuries, against the common view that they are safer. [[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-fractures|Treves 2018]] [[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-falls|Treves 2018]] A regulatory case series found 20 deaths and 46 serious injuries after complex sleep behaviours on Z-drugs, a third of them after an earlier episode, and a boxed warning followed. [[P32323442 Harbourt 2020 Z-drugs and complex sleep behaviours#^p32323442-cases|Harbourt 2020]] [[P32323442 Harbourt 2020 Z-drugs and complex sleep behaviours#^p32323442-recurrence|Harbourt 2020]] [[P32323442 Harbourt 2020 Z-drugs and complex sleep behaviours#^p32323442-regulatory|Harbourt 2020]] In PTSD, reported harms included worse severity, a higher chance of developing PTSD when the drugs were given soon after trauma, poorer psychotherapy outcomes, aggression, depression and substance use. [[P26164054 Guina 2015 Benzodiazepines for PTSD#^p26164054-harms|Guina 2015]] Once physical dependence is likely, abrupt stopping carries withdrawal risk, and a 2025 joint guideline advises against it and recommends shared decisions and psychosocial support; this library reproduces none of its schedules. [[P40526204 Brunner 2025 Benzodiazepine tapering guideline#^p40526204-abrupt|Brunner 2025]] [[P40526204 Brunner 2025 Benzodiazepine tapering guideline#^p40526204-review|Brunner 2025]] [[P40526204 Brunner 2025 Benzodiazepine tapering guideline#^p40526204-support|Brunner 2025]]

**Interactions.** Opioids are the dangerous partner: among 315,428 insured adults, overlapping benzodiazepine and opioid prescriptions went with roughly doubled odds of an emergency visit or admission for opioid overdose. [[P28292769 Sun 2017 Opioid and benzodiazepine co-use and overdose#^p28292769-overdose|Sun 2017]] Co-use rose from 9% to 17% of opioid users between 2001 and 2013, and the authors estimate that, if the link is causal, it accounted for about 15% of overdose-related visits and admissions. [[P28292769 Sun 2017 Opioid and benzodiazepine co-use and overdose#^p28292769-trend|Sun 2017]] [[P28292769 Sun 2017 Opioid and benzodiazepine co-use and overdose#^p28292769-attributable|Sun 2017]]

**Reading rule.** Separate short-term relief from what accumulates with use: efficacy is measured over weeks on self-rated sleep or anxiety scales, whereas dependence, falls, complex behaviours and overdose appear in observational data with their own confounding, so neither kind of evidence answers the other's question. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-caution-outcomes|Appraisal: De Crescenzo 2022]] [[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Treves 2018]] [[P28292769 Sun 2017 Opioid and benzodiazepine co-use and overdose#^p28292769-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Sun 2017]]

## Evidence and status

Acute efficacy for insomnia rests on a large network meta-analysis of randomised trials with rated certainty, while long-term comparisons rest on few trials. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-limit|De Crescenzo 2022]] The harm evidence is mostly observational: the fracture analysis pooled observational studies, the complex-behaviour series has no denominator, and the overdose association comes from claims data. [[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-limit|Treves 2018]] [[P32323442 Harbourt 2020 Z-drugs and complex sleep behaviours#^p32323442-caution-denominator|Appraisal: Harbourt 2020]] [[P28292769 Sun 2017 Opioid and benzodiazepine co-use and overdose#^p28292769-limit|Sun 2017]] The PTSD review mixes designs and populations. [[P26164054 Guina 2015 Benzodiazepines for PTSD#^p26164054-limit|Guina 2015]] Tapering recommendations are graded partly by consensus, reflecting expert judgement where trials are few. [[P40526204 Brunner 2025 Benzodiazepine tapering guideline#^p40526204-caution-consensus|Appraisal: Brunner 2025]]

## Uncertainties

- How benzodiazepines and Z-drugs compare head to head on falls and fractures, since the Z-drug analysis compared users with non-users. [[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Treves 2018]]
- How often complex sleep behaviours occur, which spontaneous reports cannot show. [[P32323442 Harbourt 2020 Z-drugs and complex sleep behaviours#^p32323442-caution-denominator|Appraisal: Harbourt 2020]]
- Whether the harm signal in PTSD reflects the drugs or the greater distress of the people given them. [[P26164054 Guina 2015 Benzodiazepines for PTSD#^p26164054-caution-confounding|Appraisal: Guina 2015]]
- How benefit and harm balance beyond the few weeks most trials last. [[P30712879 Slee 2019 Drug treatments for generalised anxiety disorder#^p30712879-caution-group|Appraisal: Slee 2019]] [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-longterm|De Crescenzo 2022]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Clinical practice guideline (Journal of General Internal Medicine).** A joint guideline led by addiction medicine on tapering once risks outweigh benefits, centred on repeated review and against abrupt stopping in people likely to be dependent. [[P40526204 Brunner 2025 Benzodiazepine tapering guideline#^p40526204-review|Brunner 2025]] [[P40526204 Brunner 2025 Benzodiazepine tapering guideline#^p40526204-abrupt|Brunner 2025]]
- **2023 · Guideline update (Journal of Sleep Research).** Keeps cognitive behavioural therapy first for chronic insomnia and limits benzodiazepines and benzodiazepine receptor agonists to short-term use. [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-cbti|Riemann 2023]] [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-shortterm|Riemann 2023]]
- **2022 · Systematic review and network meta-analysis (Lancet).** Confirms acute benefit for sleep quality but finds benzodiazepines and most Z-drugs left more people with side effects than placebo. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-tolerability|De Crescenzo 2022]]
