# Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors

## Definition

Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) are antidepressants that differ in chemical structure and kinetics but share one action: they enhance the effect of serotonin. [[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-mechanism|Bruggeman 2023]] They do so by blocking reuptake, the step that would otherwise end a transmitter's action in the synaptic cleft. [[F06 OpenStax Cells of the nervous system#^f06-receptor|Cells of the Nervous System]] Guidelines rank them ahead of the tricyclics for major depression, and they are also used for anxiety, obsessive-compulsive, panic and eating disorders. [[P32491723 Moraczewski 2023 Tricyclic antidepressants overview#^p32491723-secondline|Moraczewski 2023]] [[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-uses|Bruggeman 2023]]

## How it works

**Members.** Citalopram, escitalopram, fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, paroxetine and sertraline are the SSRIs named in the largest network meta-analysis of acute treatment for adult depression. [[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-drugs|Cipriani 2018]] Paroxetine and sertraline are the only two drugs with US approval for PTSD. [[P39955464 Singh 2025 MDMA-assisted therapy regulatory challenges#^p39955464-approved|Singh 2025]]

**Mechanism.** Transporter blockade is immediate, but clinical change in depression unfolds over weeks, and monoamine-based accounts explain that mismatch poorly. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|Harmer 2017]] The leading proposals are early shifts in emotional and cognitive processing and slower changes in cellular plasticity and stress signalling, and both remain hypotheses under test. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-processing|Harmer 2017]] [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-plasticity|Harmer 2017]] [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-limit|Harmer 2017]] Because one drug target sits in many tissues, a drug's effects are rarely single, which is the frame for reading the side effects below. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-agonism|How Do Medicines Work?]]

**What it is used for.** In adults with major depression, every drug in the 2018 network meta-analysis, SSRIs included, produced more responders than placebo, but differences between drugs shrank once placebo-controlled trials were counted and most comparisons carried wide credible intervals. [[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-placebo|Cipriani 2018]] [[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-comparisons|Cipriani 2018]] Fluoxetine was one of only two drugs with fewer dropouts than placebo. [[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-acceptability|Cipriani 2018]] That ranking describes acute treatment in trials that left out bipolar, psychotic and treatment-resistant depression, and it is not a guide to choosing a drug. [[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-caution-ranking|Appraisal: Cipriani 2018]]

**Class-level adverse effects and serious risks.** The toxicity literature describes SSRIs as less toxic than the monoamine oxidase inhibitors and tricyclics that preceded them. [[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-uses|Bruggeman 2023]] Reported adverse effects include low sodium, loss of orgasm, sedation or activation, stomach irritation, uncommon seizures and impaired platelet function. [[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-adverse|Bruggeman 2023]] Near therapeutic concentrations SSRIs can also block sodium, calcium and potassium channels, and citalopram and escitalopram lengthen the QTc interval. [[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-mechanism|Bruggeman 2023]] Stopping matters too: at least one discontinuation symptom followed about 31% of antidepressant discontinuations against 17% after placebo, severe symptoms affected about 3%, and rates differed between drugs. [[P38851198 Henssler 2024 Antidepressant discontinuation symptom incidence#^p38851198-incidence|Henssler 2024]] [[P38851198 Henssler 2024 Antidepressant discontinuation symptom incidence#^p38851198-severe|Henssler 2024]] [[P38851198 Henssler 2024 Antidepressant discontinuation symptom incidence#^p38851198-drugs|Henssler 2024]] A list of possible harms is not a frequency estimate for ordinary use. [[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-caution-frequency|Appraisal: Bruggeman 2023]]

**Interactions.** Interactions can be drug-drug, drug-food or drug-condition, and individual decisions belong to product labelling and clinicians. [[F19 FDA Drug interactions what you should know#^f19-types|Drug interactions: what you should know]] [[F19 FDA Drug interactions what you should know#^f19-advice|Drug interactions: what you should know]] For SSRIs two class properties set the agenda: added serotonergic effect, whose extreme is now described as serotonin toxicity, a graded spectrum rather than a single syndrome, and impaired platelet function. [[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-serotonin|Bruggeman 2023]] [[P30521236 Bruggeman 2023 Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor toxicity#^p30521236-adverse|Bruggeman 2023]] Monoamine oxidase inhibitors carry clinically important interactions with many medicines, which is why that class is handled separately. [[P29083800 Easton-Carr 2026 Monoamine oxidase inhibitor toxicity#^p29083800-interactions|Easton-Carr 2026]]

**PTSD: the firmest evidence outside depression.** SSRIs beat placebo on response in [[Post-traumatic stress disorder|PTSD]] (about 58% against 35%, moderate certainty), and the Cochrane authors call them first-line. [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-ssri|Williams 2026]] [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-conclusion|Williams 2026]] Nearly all PTSD guidelines name SSRIs as the first-line drug class. [[P34575284 Martin 2021 Treatment guidelines for PTSD#^p34575284-firstline|Martin 2021]] Withdrawal ran higher than on placebo, although only about 9% stopped because of adverse events. [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-withdrawal|Williams 2026]] The trials enrolled PTSD, not ICD-11 complex PTSD, so transfer to [[CPTSD and disturbances in self-organization|complex PTSD]] is untested. [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-caution-cptsd|Appraisal: Williams 2026]]

**Bipolar depression: a switch concern.** An ISBD task force found antidepressants widely used in bipolar disorder on weak evidence, with insufficient support for adding them to mood stabilisers. [[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-evidence|Pacchiarotti 2013]] Switching into [[Hypomania|hypomania]], [[Mania|mania]] or mixed states was the chief concern; SSRIs may switch less often than tricyclics and SNRIs, and for bipolar I the task force confined antidepressants to use alongside mood stabilisers. [[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-switch|Pacchiarotti 2013]] [[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-bipolar-i|Pacchiarotti 2013]] In Swedish registers, antidepressant-only maintenance periods went with more depression- and mania-related admissions, an association open to confounding by why treatment started. [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-classes|Ermis 2025]] [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-mania|Ermis 2025]] [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-caution-register|Appraisal: Ermis 2025]] About 7% of people first diagnosed with major depression were rediagnosed with bipolar disorder after mood elevation during antidepressant treatment, a design that cannot separate drug from course. [[P38922810 Tondo 2024 Diagnostic change from depression to bipolar disorder#^p38922810-rate|Tondo 2024]] [[P38922810 Tondo 2024 Diagnostic change from depression to bipolar disorder#^p38922810-caution-attribution|Appraisal: Tondo 2024]] The phase logic is in [[Bipolar depression and mixed features]] and [[Phase-specific bipolar treatment evidence]].

**Autism: small and uncertain.** In pooled placebo-controlled trials in autistic participants, antidepressants had a small effect on restricted and repetitive behaviours, clomipramine did better than SSRIs, and adults responded better than children. [[P35948343 Liang 2022 Antidepressants for autism symptoms#^p35948343-effects|Liang 2022]] [[P35948343 Liang 2022 Antidepressants for autism symptoms#^p35948343-moderators|Liang 2022]] Those contrasts come from few trials compared across studies. [[P35948343 Liang 2022 Antidepressants for autism symptoms#^p35948343-caution-subgroups|Appraisal: Liang 2022]]

**Reading rule.** Keep the transporter action, the condition, the phase or age group and the outcome apart: one SSRI mechanism has moderate-certainty support in PTSD and adult depression, a doubtful effect on autistic repetitive behaviour, and a switch concern in bipolar I. [[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-caution-ranking|Appraisal: Cipriani 2018]] [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-ssri|Williams 2026]] [[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-switch|Pacchiarotti 2013]]

## Evidence and status

Efficacy in adult depression rests on hundreds of double-blind trials whose certainty ranges from moderate to very low. [[P29477251 Cipriani 2018 Comparative efficacy of 21 antidepressants#^p29477251-limit|Cipriani 2018]] PTSD evidence is moderate certainty for the class, from a Cochrane review last searched in 2020. [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-limit|Williams 2026]] The bipolar statements are expert consensus and register associations rather than randomised estimates, and the autism evidence comes from 16 small trials. [[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-caution-consensus|Appraisal: Pacchiarotti 2013]] [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-limit|Ermis 2025]] [[P35948343 Liang 2022 Antidepressants for autism symptoms#^p35948343-limit|Liang 2022]]

## Uncertainties

- Why one person responds and another does not is not predicted by the shared mechanism.
- Suicidality in young people is not yet covered by a source checked in this library.
- Whether the PTSD results hold for ICD-11 complex PTSD is untested. [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-caution-cptsd|Appraisal: Williams 2026]]
- No source on SSRIs for core ADHD symptoms is checked here; the drug evidence in [[ADHD medication evidence]] concerns catecholamine drugs.

## Recent research

- **2026 · Cochrane systematic review (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews).** Keeps SSRIs as the first-line drug class for PTSD on moderate-certainty evidence, while recording more withdrawals than on placebo and a need for more effective drugs. [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-ssri|Williams 2026]] [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-withdrawal|Williams 2026]] [[P42206608 Williams 2026 Pharmacotherapy for PTSD#^p42206608-conclusion|Williams 2026]]
- **2025 · Narrative review (CNS Drugs).** Notes that paroxetine and sertraline are still the only US-approved PTSD drugs, the backdrop to the 2024 MDMA decision covered in [[Psychedelics and MDMA-assisted therapy]]. [[P39955464 Singh 2025 MDMA-assisted therapy regulatory challenges#^p39955464-approved|Singh 2025]] [[P39955464 Singh 2025 MDMA-assisted therapy regulatory challenges#^p39955464-rejection|Singh 2025]]
- **2024 · Systematic review and meta-analysis (Lancet Psychiatry).** Puts discontinuation in proportion: after allowing for placebo, about one in six to seven people who stop have symptoms, and the placebo rate shows how much is non-specific. [[P38851198 Henssler 2024 Antidepressant discontinuation symptom incidence#^p38851198-severe|Henssler 2024]] [[P38851198 Henssler 2024 Antidepressant discontinuation symptom incidence#^p38851198-caution-incidence|Appraisal: Henssler 2024]]

## Connections

- [[Serotonin signalling]] - the transmitter system whose receptor diversity explains why one transporter block changes mood, gut, sleep and sexual function at once.
- [[Monoamine reuptake and degradation]] - the clearance step SSRIs block, and the timescale argument for why acute transporter occupancy is not the clinical effect.
- [[Antidepressant mechanisms]] - the processing and plasticity accounts proposed to bridge the weeks between transporter block and clinical change in depression.
- [[Receptor adaptation tolerance and dependence]] - the adaptation that follows sustained transporter block and sits behind discontinuation symptoms when an SSRI is stopped.
- [[Serotonin-noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors]] - the sibling class that adds noradrenaline transporter block, with a different adverse-effect profile and a higher bipolar switch concern.
- [[Chemical imbalance framing]] - why the class's clinical use does not show that depression is a serotonin deficit.
- [[Psychological interventions for complex trauma]] - the psychological treatments that most PTSD guidelines rank as first-line alongside SSRIs.

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** SSRIs tie neurochemistry's serotonin and reuptake notes to three condition domains that read the same drugs differently: first-line in PTSD, doubtful for autistic repetitive behaviour, and a switch concern in bipolar I.
