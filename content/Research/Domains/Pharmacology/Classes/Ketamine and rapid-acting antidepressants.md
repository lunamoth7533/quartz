# Ketamine and rapid-acting antidepressants

## Definition

Rapid-acting antidepressants are treatments that act on depressive symptoms faster than monoamine antidepressants: ketamine infusion, intranasal esketamine and dextromethorphan-bupropion all acted sooner, though with similar effect sizes. [[P40282849 Gao 2025 Pharmacological monotherapy for depressive disorders#^p40282849-rapid|Gao 2025]] Ketamine and esketamine can relieve treatment-resistant depression quickly, with effects that outlast the drug's presence in the body. [[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-rapid|Brown 2024]] Ketamine itself is an NMDA receptor blocker, a class one review proposes admitting among the antidepressants. [[P31211282 Fasipe 2019 Novel antidepressants and agomelatine#^p31211282-classes|Fasipe 2019]]

## How it works

**Members.** Ketamine and esketamine are the best-studied members; in trials, ketamine was mostly given as one intravenous administration and esketamine as repeated, mostly intranasal, administration. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-limit|Dean 2021]] Dextromethorphan-bupropion and the neurosteroids brexanolone and zuranolone are newer rapid-acting approaches. [[P40282849 Gao 2025 Pharmacological monotherapy for depressive disorders#^p40282849-rapid|Gao 2025]] Other glutamate receptor modulators have very little evidence. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-gaps|Dean 2021]]

**Mechanism: different first targets, shared downstream change.** The initiating mechanisms discussed for rapid-acting compounds include effects at NMDA receptor subtypes, metabotropic glutamate 2/3 antagonism, AMPA receptor potentiation and, for psychedelics, serotonin receptor activation. [[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-limit|Brown 2024]] Despite those different starting points, one synthesis argues that they converge on stronger excitatory synapses and a changed capacity for later plasticity, called metaplasticity, and proposes that process as a treatment target. [[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-convergence|Brown 2024]] [[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-proposal|Brown 2024]] The account rests largely on preclinical work and does not show that conventional antidepressants work the same way. [[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-caution-scope|Appraisal: Brown 2024]] It speaks to the delay problem of monoamine drugs, whose acute pharmacology explains their weeks-long course poorly. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|Harmer 2017]]

**What it is used for.** In unipolar depression, ketamine may raise response and remission and lower depression scores at 24 hours, but only on very low-certainty evidence. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-ketamine|Dean 2021]] Esketamine probably increases remission and lowers scores at 24 hours on moderate-certainty evidence from about 800 to 900 participants. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-esketamine|Dean 2021]] The neurosteroids brexanolone and zuranolone work in postpartum depression, whereas zuranolone's effect in major depression was very small. [[P40282849 Gao 2025 Pharmacological monotherapy for depressive disorders#^p40282849-rapid|Gao 2025]] Faster onset has so far come without a larger effect than monoamine drugs. [[P40282849 Gao 2025 Pharmacological monotherapy for depressive disorders#^p40282849-rapid|Gao 2025]]

**Class-level adverse effects and serious risks.** People on esketamine did not clearly drop out of trials more often than people on placebo. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-esketamine|Dean 2021]] Ketamine's psychotomimetic effects are noticeable enough that they may have broken blinding in trials. [[P34623633 Dean 2021 Ketamine for bipolar depression#^p34623633-blinding|Dean 2021]] Long-term safety and benefit are not established, and the Cochrane authors call for long-term comparative trials and real-world monitoring of repeated ketamine and esketamine. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-gaps|Dean 2021]] A 24-hour outcome measures speed, not durability. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-caution-blinding|Appraisal: Dean 2021]]

**Bipolar depression: thinner, shorter evidence.** Of the glutamate modulators tested in bipolar depression, only ketamine beat placebo, on response 24 hours after a single infusion added to mood stabilisers, in two trials with 33 participants and low-certainty evidence. [[P34623633 Dean 2021 Ketamine for bipolar depression#^p34623633-response|Dean 2021]] Ketamine showed no clear advantage for remission, and its effect was described as rapid but transient. [[P34623633 Dean 2021 Ketamine for bipolar depression#^p34623633-remission|Dean 2021]] Data in bipolar depression are far thinner than in unipolar depression. [[P34623633 Dean 2021 Ketamine for bipolar depression#^p34623633-blinding|Dean 2021]] Two small trials say nothing about maintenance, switching over time or repeated treatment, the questions set out in [[Phase-specific bipolar treatment evidence]]. [[P34623633 Dean 2021 Ketamine for bipolar depression#^p34623633-caution-phase|Appraisal: Dean 2021]]

**Reading rule.** Name the drug, the route and the time point. A 24-hour response after one infusion, a remission after repeated intranasal administration and a durable benefit are three different claims, and only the first two have been measured with any certainty. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-limit|Dean 2021]] [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-caution-blinding|Appraisal: Dean 2021]]

## Evidence and status

Esketamine's short-term effect in unipolar depression carries moderate certainty, ketamine's very low certainty, and ketamine in bipolar depression low to very low certainty. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-esketamine|Dean 2021]] [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-ketamine|Dean 2021]] [[P34623633 Dean 2021 Ketamine for bipolar depression#^p34623633-limit|Dean 2021]] Both Cochrane reviews were searched to July 2020, so later trials are not included. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-limit|Dean 2021]] The newer rapid-acting approaches are summarised here from a narrative review rather than a pooled analysis. [[P40282849 Gao 2025 Pharmacological monotherapy for depressive disorders#^p40282849-limit|Gao 2025]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether benefit persists with repeated ketamine or esketamine, and at what long-term cost, is unknown. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-gaps|Dean 2021]]
- Misuse potential and effects outside trial settings are not covered by a source checked in this library.
- Ketamine evidence in PTSD, ADHD or autism is not yet sourced here.
- Whether the metaplasticity account extends to conventional antidepressants is untested. [[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-caution-scope|Appraisal: Brown 2024]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Narrative review (Medicina).** Rapid-acting approaches, including dextromethorphan-bupropion and the neurosteroids, act sooner than monoamine drugs but not more strongly, and zuranolone's effect in major depression was very small. [[P40282849 Gao 2025 Pharmacological monotherapy for depressive disorders#^p40282849-rapid|Gao 2025]]
- **2024 · Narrative review (Molecular Psychiatry).** Proposes that diverse rapid-acting compounds converge on metaplasticity, a testable mechanism that remains mostly preclinical. [[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-convergence|Brown 2024]] [[P38177353 Brown 2024 Metaplasticity and sustained antidepressant action#^p38177353-caution-scope|Appraisal: Brown 2024]]
- **2021 · Cochrane systematic reviews (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews).** Moderate-certainty 24-hour benefit for esketamine in unipolar depression, and low-certainty, transient evidence for ketamine in bipolar depression. [[P34510411 Dean 2021 Ketamine for unipolar depression#^p34510411-esketamine|Dean 2021]] [[P34623633 Dean 2021 Ketamine for bipolar depression#^p34623633-remission|Dean 2021]]
