# Antiseizure medications

## Definition

Antiseizure medications prevent seizures by lowering neuronal excitability, and they are grouped by mechanism into ion-channel modulators, enhancers of GABA inhibition, dampers of glutamate excitation and drugs acting on transmitter release. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-families|Sills 2020]] They suppress seizures without a demonstrated effect on the disease process behind them. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-symptomatic|Sills 2020]] The same drugs are widely used outside epilepsy, in mood disorders, migraine, neuropathic pain and anxiety. [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-uses|Rogawski 2004]] [[P30712879 Slee 2019 Drug treatments for generalised anxiety disorder#^p30712879-efficacy|Slee 2019]]

## How it works

**Members.** In the mechanism classification, carbamazepine, lamotrigine and phenytoin act on sodium channels, ethosuximide on calcium channels and retigabine on potassium channels; tiagabine, vigabatrin and the benzodiazepines strengthen GABA inhibition; perampanel dampens glutamate excitation; and gabapentin, pregabalin, levetiracetam and brivaracetam act presynaptically on transmitter release. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-families|Sills 2020]] Valproate fits no single family: besides strengthening GABA transmission it acts on several signalling pathways and on gene expression. [[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-actions|Rosenberg 2007]] [[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-genomic|Rosenberg 2007]]

**Mechanism.** Seizures are episodes of abnormal brain activity. [[F73 MedlinePlus Epilepsy#^f73-definition|Epilepsy]] Glutamate carries most fast excitation in the brain and GABA most fast inhibition, so the families either quiet excitation, strengthen inhibition or reshape the channels and release machinery that set firing. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-excitatory|Amino acid transmitters]] [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-inhibitory|Amino acid transmitters]] [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-families|Sills 2020]] Their target is the general excitability that lets abnormal firing start and spread, not the defect behind a particular epilepsy, which is usually unknown. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-symptomatic|Sills 2020]] Sodium-channel block is use-dependent, so it may damp abnormally fast firing selectively, and gabapentin binds a calcium-channel subunit that injured sensory neurons overexpress - the two accounts offered for their use in neuropathic pain. [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-pain|Rogawski 2004]] A family label names the best-characterised action; several drugs have more than one target, and family does not predict which drug helps which person. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-caution-families|Appraisal: Sills 2020]]

**What it is used for: epilepsy.** National guidance organises drug choice by seizure type and syndrome. [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-treatment|Epilepsies NG217]] Of 26 drugs approved in the United States, 24 have similar average efficacy in focal epilepsy and 9 in generalised epilepsy, so selection turns on the epilepsy, age, sex, co-occurring conditions and interactions, and about 60-70% of people become seizure-free. [[P35380580 Kanner 2022 Antiseizure medications for adults with epilepsy#^p35380580-options|Kanner 2022]] [[P35380580 Kanner 2022 Antiseizure medications for adults with epilepsy#^p35380580-outcome|Kanner 2022]] Similar averages do not mean every drug works equally well for one person. [[P35380580 Kanner 2022 Antiseizure medications for adults with epilepsy#^p35380580-caution-equivalence|Appraisal: Kanner 2022]]

**What it is used for: beyond epilepsy.** In many non-epileptic uses the drugs are thought to act as they do in epilepsy, through sodium and calcium channels or GABA-A inhibition, with effects on plasticity signalling proposed to explain why benefit in bipolar disorder is delayed. [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-mechanism|Rogawski 2004]] [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-delayed|Rogawski 2004]] A 2026 umbrella review lists lamotrigine and valproate among effective treatments for adult bipolar depression, and valproate and carbamazepine for adult mania; the phase-by-phase picture is in [[Anticonvulsant mood stabilisers]]. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-depression|De Prisco 2026]] [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-mania|De Prisco 2026]] In generalised anxiety disorder, pregabalin lowered anxiety scores more than placebo and was relatively well accepted in a network meta-analysis of 89 trials. [[P30712879 Slee 2019 Drug treatments for generalised anxiety disorder#^p30712879-efficacy|Slee 2019]] That a drug controls seizures and also helps mood, anxiety or pain does not show that one action does both. [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-caution-transfer|Appraisal: Rogawski 2004]] [[P30712879 Slee 2019 Drug treatments for generalised anxiety disorder#^p30712879-caution-pregabalin|Appraisal: Slee 2019]]

**Class-level adverse effects and serious risks.** Pregnancy is the sharpest risk: about one in ten valproate-exposed children had a major malformation, against 2-3% of children of women without epilepsy; carbamazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin and topiramate also raised risk, while lamotrigine and levetiracetam showed no clear increase against untreated epilepsy. [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-valproate|Bromley 2023]] [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-others|Bromley 2023]] [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-lamotrigine|Bromley 2023]] Serious skin reactions come second: HLA-B*15:02 is strongly associated with Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis on carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine, and more weakly on lamotrigine and phenytoin. [[P38527170 Tham 2024 HLA-B and antiseizure drug skin reactions#^p38527170-association|Tham 2024]] Psychiatric and behavioural side effects differ more than tenfold between drugs, with levetiracetam and perampanel at the high end, mainly through irritability and aggression, and carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine and pregabalin among those with the least liability. [[P41432787 Lyndon 2025 Psychiatric side effects of antiseizure medications#^p41432787-spread|Lyndon 2025]] [[P41432787 Lyndon 2025 Psychiatric side effects of antiseizure medications#^p41432787-low|Lyndon 2025]] Risk rose with polytherapy, pre-existing psychiatric illness, intellectual disability and social deprivation, and with faster and higher dosing. [[P41432787 Lyndon 2025 Psychiatric side effects of antiseizure medications#^p41432787-risk|Lyndon 2025]] Enzyme-inducing drugs can also raise blood lipids and promote bone loss. [[P35380580 Kanner 2022 Antiseizure medications for adults with epilepsy#^p35380580-inducers|Kanner 2022]]

**Interactions.** Carbamazepine and phenytoin induce drug-metabolising enzymes and so speed the breakdown of other medicines. [[P35380580 Kanner 2022 Antiseizure medications for adults with epilepsy#^p35380580-inducers|Kanner 2022]] Because a quarter to a half of people with epilepsy also live with psychiatric, cognitive or other conditions, co-prescription is common and interactions are a routine question rather than an edge case. [[P35380580 Kanner 2022 Antiseizure medications for adults with epilepsy#^p35380580-outcome|Kanner 2022]]

**Reading rule.** Name the drug and the indication, not only the class: a mechanism family does not predict which drug suits which seizure type or person, and a benefit or harm measured in epilepsy reaches psychiatric use only by argument. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-caution-families|Appraisal: Sills 2020]] [[P41432787 Lyndon 2025 Psychiatric side effects of antiseizure medications#^p41432787-caution-indication|Appraisal: Lyndon 2025]] [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-caution-indication|Appraisal: Bromley 2023]]

## Evidence and status

The mechanism classification comes from a narrative review and names best-characterised actions rather than full profiles. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-limit|Sills 2020]] Figures on seizure freedom come from a narrative review rather than new data, and national guidance lists research recommendations where evidence was insufficient. [[P35380580 Kanner 2022 Antiseizure medications for adults with epilepsy#^p35380580-limit|Kanner 2022]] [[F95 NICE Epilepsies NG217#^f95-research|Epilepsies NG217]] Pregnancy risks rest on observational studies with a high risk of some biases, which the Cochrane authors judge unlikely to explain the differences between drugs. [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-limit|Bromley 2023]] The skin-reaction evidence is strongest for carbamazepine, and the behavioural-risk tiers are a structured judgement rather than a pooled estimate. [[P38527170 Tham 2024 HLA-B and antiseizure drug skin reactions#^p38527170-limit|Tham 2024]] [[P41432787 Lyndon 2025 Psychiatric side effects of antiseizure medications#^p41432787-caution-tiers|Appraisal: Lyndon 2025]] Psychiatric uses are graded drug by drug and phase by phase, mostly at moderate or low certainty. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-limit|De Prisco 2026]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether the action that controls seizures is the one that stabilises mood or eases pain, for any drug used in both. [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-caution-transfer|Appraisal: Rogawski 2004]]
- How far behavioural-risk rankings drawn from epilepsy apply to people taking the same drugs for psychiatric conditions. [[P41432787 Lyndon 2025 Psychiatric side effects of antiseizure medications#^p41432787-caution-indication|Appraisal: Lyndon 2025]]
- Why about a third of people with epilepsy do not become seizure-free, when the drugs act on excitability rather than on the cause. [[P35380580 Kanner 2022 Antiseizure medications for adults with epilepsy#^p35380580-outcome|Kanner 2022]] [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-symptomatic|Sills 2020]]
- What a negative HLA test means, since serious skin reactions also occur in people without the allele. [[P38527170 Tham 2024 HLA-B and antiseizure drug skin reactions#^p38527170-caution-negative|Appraisal: Tham 2024]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Narrative review (Journal of Neurology).** Places 28 drugs on a behavioural-risk spectrum, turning "mood side effects" into a drug-specific question. [[P41432787 Lyndon 2025 Psychiatric side effects of antiseizure medications#^p41432787-spread|Lyndon 2025]] [[P41432787 Lyndon 2025 Psychiatric side effects of antiseizure medications#^p41432787-risk|Lyndon 2025]] The tiers are the authors' own classification. [[P41432787 Lyndon 2025 Psychiatric side effects of antiseizure medications#^p41432787-caution-tiers|Appraisal: Lyndon 2025]]
- **2024 · Umbrella review (Pharmacogenetics and Genomics).** Confirms a strong HLA-B*15:02 association for carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine skin reactions and a weaker one for lamotrigine and phenytoin. [[P38527170 Tham 2024 HLA-B and antiseizure drug skin reactions#^p38527170-association|Tham 2024]]
- **2023 · Cochrane systematic review (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews).** Updates malformation risks drug by drug, with valproate highest. [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-valproate|Bromley 2023]] [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-lamotrigine|Bromley 2023]]

## Connections

- [[Seizures and epilepsy]] - the condition these drugs were developed for, where seizure type and syndrome organise treatment in guidance.
- [[Anticonvulsant mood stabilisers]] - the psychiatric branch of the class: valproate, carbamazepine and lamotrigine in bipolar disorder, with phase- and polarity-specific evidence.
- [[Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs]] - the GABA-enhancing members, used across seizures, anxiety and sleep, with their own dependence and withdrawal profile.
- [[Glutamate and GABA]] - the excitatory and inhibitory transmitters that most mechanism families act on, directly or through the channels that shape firing.
- [[Excitation and inhibition balance]] - the circuit concept these drugs act on clinically, with the caution that balance claims need a named level and mechanism.
- [[Pharmacogenomics]] - HLA-B*15:02 and carbamazepine skin reactions, the clearest case of a genetic marker tied to a serious adverse reaction.
- [[Pharmacokinetics and ADME]] - enzyme induction by carbamazepine and phenytoin is a metabolism effect that changes exposure to other drugs.
- [[Headache and pain]] - migraine and neuropathic pain are among the main non-epileptic uses, with use-dependent sodium-channel block and gabapentin's calcium-channel binding as the proposed mechanisms.

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Antiseizure medications join the neurology domain's epilepsy material to the psychiatric domains through shared molecules: the same drugs appear in bipolar treatment, anxiety and pain, and carry their pregnancy, skin and behavioural risks into each. The neurochemistry domain supplies the excitatory and inhibitory targets, and the methods domain's rule that an effect does not establish a mechanism governs every cross-indication claim.
