# Stimulant and non-stimulant mechanisms

## Definition

Stimulant and non-stimulant medications used in ADHD increase noradrenaline and dopamine signalling, by different molecular routes and with different time courses.

## How it works

**Molecular routes.** Both classes raise noradrenaline and dopamine signalling in prefrontal circuits. Reuptake blockade raises extracellular transmitter concentrations, and agents differ in selectivity and in additional actions such as vesicular action: the classical contrast pairs amphetamine-type release with methylphenidate-type reuptake blockade, and the selective noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor atomoxetine with both. [[F12 NIGMS What happens to medicine in your body#^f12-adme|What happens to medicine in your body]] Pharmacodynamics generally acts on receptors, enzymes, transporters or ion channels, and the same target appears in many tissues, which is why effects are rarely single. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How do medicines work]] [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-agonism|How do medicines work]]

**Why the prefrontal cortex is the target of interest.** Prefrontal function depends on noradrenaline and dopamine in an inverted-U relationship to arousal, with benefit attributed to postsynaptic alpha-2A receptors and moderate D1 stimulation. Too little and too much signalling both impair performance, which is the mechanistic reason dose matters and the reason an agent's identity does not by itself predict a person's response. [[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-catecholamines|Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD]] [[P19621976 Arnsten 2009 Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD#^p19621976-mechanisms|Prefrontal catecholamines and ADHD]]

**Reading rule.** Keep the mechanism claim, the group-level efficacy claim and the individual decision in separate compartments. Mechanism explains why a class of drugs could work; the trials describe average short-term effects on named raters' scales; neither establishes what will happen for a particular person. ## Evidence and status

Short-term randomised evidence for symptom reduction is well established; longer-term functional and safety questions rest on weaker designs and are the focus of ongoing research. [[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-caution-individual|AI appraisal: ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]]

**What the trials show.** In the 2018 network meta-analysis, drug effects on clinician-rated symptoms were present for all agents in children and adolescents while teacher-rated effects were limited to two, which shows how rater and setting shape the result. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] In adults, stimulants and atomoxetine had higher efficacy than placebo for reducing core symptoms at 12 weeks on both self-reported and clinician-reported scales, and most therapeutic components were similar to placebo on all-cause discontinuation, though atomoxetine and guanfacine had lower acceptability. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-stimulants|Comparative interventions for ADHD]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-acceptability|Comparative interventions for ADHD]] The same adult synthesis reports that ADHD medications were not efficacious on additional outcomes such as quality of life and that longer-term evidence is under-investigated - a limit of the evidence base rather than a demonstration of no effect. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-longterm|Comparative interventions for ADHD]] [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-caution-absence|AI appraisal: Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

**Tolerability is agent-specific rather than class-wide.** In the 2018 network meta-analysis, amphetamines were less well tolerated than placebo in both age groups, guanfacine only in children, and atomoxetine, methylphenidate and modafinil only in adults. Head-to-head comparisons favoured amphetamines on efficacy, and the authors found insufficient data at 26 and 52 weeks. Group-level rankings and head-to-head averages are not prescribing guidance for one person. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-tolerability|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-caution-choice|AI appraisal: ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

**Dose-effect patterns differ by class and age.** A 2026 synthesis found that median efficacy plateaued above study-specific doses for methylphenidate, amphetamines and guanfacine in children, and for amphetamines in adults, while discontinuation for adverse events rose with dose for amphetamines and methylphenidate above study-specific ranges and no dose pattern appeared for atomoxetine in fixed-dose studies. The authors read this as challenging both therapeutic inertia and uncritical dose escalation. Aggregate dose-response curves cannot set a dose for an individual, and this library reproduces no titration or dosing guidance.
