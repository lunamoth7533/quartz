# First-generation antipsychotics

## Definition

First-generation, or typical, antipsychotics are the older drugs of the class, which begins with chlorpromazine, and they act mainly by blocking dopamine D2 receptors. [[P39350614 Leucht 2024 Antipsychotic drugs concise review#^p39350614-limit|Leucht 2024]] [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Kaar 2020]] The label is contested: a 2024 review judges the split into first- and second-generation drugs neither valid nor useful and proposes naming each drug by its pharmacology instead. [[P39350614 Leucht 2024 Antipsychotic drugs concise review#^p39350614-classification|Leucht 2024]]

## How it works

**Members.** Chlorpromazine, the first antipsychotic, and haloperidol are the reference members, and haloperidol is the comparator against which most newer drugs were tested; low-potency first-generation drugs form a separate comparison group because their harms differ. [[P39350614 Leucht 2024 Antipsychotic drugs concise review#^p39350614-limit|Leucht 2024]] [[P19058842 Leucht 2009 Second-generation versus first-generation antipsychotics#^p19058842-eps|Leucht 2009]] Network meta-analyses place them beside other drugs of their era, among them pimozide, zuclopenthixol and levomepromazine, each with its own harm profile. [[P31303314 Huhn 2019 Comparative efficacy of 32 antipsychotics#^p31303314-sideeffects|Huhn 2019]] [[P31303314 Huhn 2019 Comparative efficacy of 32 antipsychotics#^p31303314-efficacy|Huhn 2019]]

**Mechanism.** Blocking striatal D2 receptors is the shared basis of clinical response and of motor and endocrine side effects, so benefit and harm share one target and the drugs work within a therapeutic window. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Kaar 2020]] Binding at serotonergic, histaminergic, cholinergic and adrenergic receptors adds effects such as weight gain, sedation and dysphoria that differ from drug to drug. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-offtarget|Kaar 2020]] When the 2024 review was written, every available antipsychotic either blocked dopamine receptors or partially stimulated them, so the older drugs set the dopamine-blocking template that later drugs modified. [[P39350614 Leucht 2024 Antipsychotic drugs concise review#^p39350614-mechanism|Leucht 2024]] That statement is dated: a muscarinic combination without direct D2 blockade received US approval in September 2024. [[P39350614 Leucht 2024 Antipsychotic drugs concise review#^p39350614-caution-date|Appraisal: Leucht 2024]] [[F126 FDA Drug Trials Snapshot COBENFY#^f126-approval|Drug Trials Snapshot: COBENFY]]

**Potency, not generation, predicts much of the harm.** Against haloperidol, even at low doses, second-generation drugs produced fewer extrapyramidal effects, but against low-potency first-generation drugs only a few did, and low-potency drugs caused as much weight gain as most newer drugs. [[P19058842 Leucht 2009 Second-generation versus first-generation antipsychotics#^p19058842-eps|Leucht 2009]] [[P19058842 Leucht 2009 Second-generation versus first-generation antipsychotics#^p19058842-weight|Leucht 2009]] The older drugs' reputation for movement effects is therefore partly the reputation of a high-potency comparator. [[P19058842 Leucht 2009 Second-generation versus first-generation antipsychotics#^p19058842-caution-haloperidol|Appraisal: Leucht 2009]]

**What it is used for.** Antipsychotics reduce psychosis whatever the underlying diagnosis and do more than sedate, and in schizophrenia their relapse-prevention effects are larger than their acute effects. [[P39350614 Leucht 2024 Antipsychotic drugs concise review#^p39350614-mechanism|Leucht 2024]] [[P39350614 Leucht 2024 Antipsychotic drugs concise review#^p39350614-effects|Leucht 2024]] In acute schizophrenia almost every drug in a 32-drug network beat placebo on point estimates, with levomepromazine at the weakest end of the range. [[P31303314 Huhn 2019 Comparative efficacy of 32 antipsychotics#^p31303314-efficacy|Huhn 2019]] In adult mania, haloperidol is among the treatments a 2026 umbrella review judged effective. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-mania|De Prisco 2026]]

**Class-level adverse effects and serious risks.** Extrapyramidal signs are the clinical face of basal-ganglia dysfunction, the motor circuitry through which striatal D2 blockade acts. [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal Ganglia]] In the network, pimozide needed the most antiparkinson medication and zuclopenthixol caused the most sedation. [[P31303314 Huhn 2019 Comparative efficacy of 32 antipsychotics#^p31303314-sideeffects|Huhn 2019]] Dopamine released by hypothalamic neurons holds back prolactin secretion, so blocking its receptors raises prolactin. [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-prolactin|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Kaar 2020]] Tardive dyskinesia met rating-scale criteria in about a quarter of treated people across 41 studies, more often during first-generation (30.0%) than second-generation treatment (20.7%), and older age, longer illness and parkinsonism went with higher prevalence. [[P28146614 Carbon 2017 Tardive dyskinesia prevalence meta-analysis#^p28146614-prevalence|Carbon 2017]] [[P28146614 Carbon 2017 Tardive dyskinesia prevalence meta-analysis#^p28146614-class|Carbon 2017]] [[P28146614 Carbon 2017 Tardive dyskinesia prevalence meta-analysis#^p28146614-moderators|Carbon 2017]] Treatment arms never exposed to first-generation drugs had the lowest rates, which is why the class contrast partly describes treatment eras. [[P28146614 Carbon 2017 Tardive dyskinesia prevalence meta-analysis#^p28146614-naive|Carbon 2017]] [[P28146614 Carbon 2017 Tardive dyskinesia prevalence meta-analysis#^p28146614-caution-crosssectional|Appraisal: Carbon 2017]]

**Reading rule.** Before accepting that older antipsychotics are simply worse, name the drug and its potency, the comparator, the outcome and the exposure history: movement-effect contrasts shrink against low-potency comparators, and tardive dyskinesia contrasts mix treatment eras. [[P19058842 Leucht 2009 Second-generation versus first-generation antipsychotics#^p19058842-caution-haloperidol|Appraisal: Leucht 2009]] [[P28146614 Carbon 2017 Tardive dyskinesia prevalence meta-analysis#^p28146614-caution-crosssectional|Appraisal: Carbon 2017]]

## Evidence and status

The comparative base is large but short-term: 150 mostly short double-blind trials compared the generations, and a 402-trial network found efficacy differences mostly gradual, with confidence often low or very low. [[P19058842 Leucht 2009 Second-generation versus first-generation antipsychotics#^p19058842-limit|Leucht 2009]] [[P31303314 Huhn 2019 Comparative efficacy of 32 antipsychotics#^p31303314-interpretation|Huhn 2019]] Only four newer drugs beat first-generation comparators on overall efficacy, so efficacy does not separate the generations cleanly. [[P19058842 Leucht 2009 Second-generation versus first-generation antipsychotics#^p19058842-efficacy|Leucht 2009]] The tardive dyskinesia contrast rests on cross-sectional rating-scale data whose severity reporting was too poor to pool. [[P28146614 Carbon 2017 Tardive dyskinesia prevalence meta-analysis#^p28146614-limit|Carbon 2017]]

## Uncertainties

- How much of the lower tardive dyskinesia prevalence under newer drugs reflects the drugs rather than less cumulative exposure to older ones. [[P28146614 Carbon 2017 Tardive dyskinesia prevalence meta-analysis#^p28146614-caution-crosssectional|Appraisal: Carbon 2017]]
- Whether extrapyramidal differences between the generations survive comparison against low-potency older drugs. [[P19058842 Leucht 2009 Second-generation versus first-generation antipsychotics#^p19058842-eps|Leucht 2009]]
- How much rating-scale tardive dyskinesia burdens the people counted, since severity was rarely reported. [[P28146614 Carbon 2017 Tardive dyskinesia prevalence meta-analysis#^p28146614-caution-severity|Appraisal: Carbon 2017]]
- Why tardive dyskinesia can persist after treatment stops is not explained by any source in this library yet.

## Recent research

- **2024 · Narrative review (The American Journal of Psychiatry).** Argues that the first/second-generation split is neither valid nor useful and that drugs should be described by the Neuroscience-based Nomenclature, which turns this note's title into a historical grouping rather than a mechanism class. [[P39350614 Leucht 2024 Antipsychotic drugs concise review#^p39350614-classification|Leucht 2024]] Its statement that every available antipsychotic acts on dopamine was overtaken by a muscarinic approval as the issue appeared. [[P39350614 Leucht 2024 Antipsychotic drugs concise review#^p39350614-caution-date|Appraisal: Leucht 2024]]
- **2026 · Living umbrella review (BMJ).** Lists haloperidol among the options judged effective for adult mania, alongside lithium, valproate, carbamazepine and several newer antipsychotics. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-mania|De Prisco 2026]] Appearing on such a list says nothing about effect size or harms. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-caution-lists|Appraisal: De Prisco 2026]]
