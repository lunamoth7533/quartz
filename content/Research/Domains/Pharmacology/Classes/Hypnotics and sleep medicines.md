# Hypnotics and sleep medicines

## Definition

Hypnotics and sleep medicines are drugs used to bring on or maintain sleep, and the ones trialled in insomnia disorder include benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, orexin receptor antagonists, melatonin and the melatonin-receptor agonist ramelteon, the antihistamine doxylamine and sedating antidepressants. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-acute|De Crescenzo 2022]] [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-limited|De Crescenzo 2022]] They reach sleep by different routes: orexin receptor antagonists, for example, do not act on the GABA receptors that benzodiazepines and Z-drugs target. [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-mechanism|Muehlan 2023]] For chronic insomnia in adults, the European guideline places cognitive behavioural therapy first and medication after it. [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-cbti|Riemann 2023]]

## How it works

**Members.** The European guideline grades benzodiazepines, benzodiazepine receptor agonists, orexin receptor antagonists such as daridorexant, low-dose sedating antidepressants, melatonin and ramelteon, and antihistamines. [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-shortterm|Riemann 2023]] [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-notrecommended|Riemann 2023]] Trials add the orexin antagonists lemborexant, seltorexant and suvorexant and the antidepressants doxepin and trazodone. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-acute|De Crescenzo 2022]] [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-limited|De Crescenzo 2022]] [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-tolerability|De Crescenzo 2022]]

**Mechanism: GABA and orexin.** Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs act on GABA receptors, as set out in [[Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs]]. [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-mechanism|Muehlan 2023]] Orexin antagonists take the opposite approach: insomnia is framed as hyperarousal, orexin as a key promoter of arousal and vigilance, and the drugs block orexin signalling for a time rather than acting on GABA receptors. [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-arousal|Muehlan 2023]] [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-mechanism|Muehlan 2023]]

**Mechanism: melatonin and histamine.** Melatonin is a pineal hormone that regulates sleep-wake timing through the suprachiasmatic nucleus and the retina, and its sleep-promoting role is attributed to MT1 and MT2 receptors. [[P30521244 Savage 2024 Melatonin pharmacology and uses#^p30521244-source|Savage 2024]] [[P30521244 Savage 2024 Melatonin pharmacology and uses#^p30521244-receptors|Savage 2024]] In the United States melatonin has no approved indication and is sold as a dietary supplement. [[P30521244 Savage 2024 Melatonin pharmacology and uses#^p30521244-status|Savage 2024]] The antidepressant agomelatine is also an MT1 and MT2 agonist. [[P31211282 Fasipe 2019 Novel antidepressants and agomelatine#^p31211282-agomelatine|Fasipe 2019]] Histaminergic neurons are active in waking and support wakefulness, and their H1 and H2 receptor actions are mostly excitatory, so drugs that block H1 receptors remove an arousal input. [[P18626069 Haas 2008 Histamine in the nervous system#^p18626069-waking|Haas 2008]] [[P18626069 Haas 2008 Histamine in the nervous system#^p18626069-receptors|Haas 2008]] Trazodone blocks histamine, serotonin type 2 and alpha-1 receptors, and mirtazapine's receptor profile is tied to its sedative effect and its off-label use in insomnia. [[P29262060 Shin 2024 Trazodone pharmacology and uses#^p29262060-mechanism|Shin 2024]] [[P30085601 Jilani 2024 Mirtazapine pharmacology and uses#^p30085601-mechanism|Jilani 2024]] [[P30085601 Jilani 2024 Mirtazapine pharmacology and uses#^p30085601-uses|Jilani 2024]]

**What it is used for: insomnia disorder.** In acute trials, the Z-drugs zolpidem, zopiclone and eszopiclone, benzodiazepines, lemborexant, seltorexant and doxylamine improved self-rated sleep quality more than placebo, with moderate-to-high certainty. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-acute|De Crescenzo 2022]] Melatonin, ramelteon and unlicensed drugs showed no material overall benefit, while daridorexant, suvorexant and trazodone helped acutely but were poorly tolerated or lacked long-term data, and only eszopiclone and lemborexant beat placebo long term, on very low-certainty evidence. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-limited|De Crescenzo 2022]] [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-longterm|De Crescenzo 2022]] The European guideline endorses orexin antagonists for longer than benzodiazepines, allows prolonged-release melatonin from age 55, and does not recommend antihistamines, fast-release melatonin or ramelteon. [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-shortterm|Riemann 2023]] [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-notrecommended|Riemann 2023]]

**What it is used for: timing and neurodevelopment.** Outside insomnia disorder, melatonin is used to shift timing: it is listed with bright light and chronotherapy for delayed sleep-wake phase disorder, the commonest circadian disorder of adolescence. [[P38054481 Narala 2024 Delayed sleep-wake phase disorder in adolescents#^p38054481-assessment|Narala 2024]] In autistic children and adolescents, a clinical review describes melatonin as effective alongside behavioural interventions and parent education. [[P38302207 Johnson 2024 Autism spectrum disorder and sleep#^p38302207-treatment|Johnson 2024]] In children with neurodisabilities it lengthened diary-reported sleep by about half an hour against placebo, was well tolerated, and may help autistic children most, though nearly every trial carried a high or unclear risk of bias. [[P30710339 Parker 2019 Melatonin for sleep in children with neurodisabilities#^p30710339-sleep|Parker 2019]] [[P30710339 Parker 2019 Melatonin for sleep in children with neurodisabilities#^p30710339-safety|Parker 2019]] [[P30710339 Parker 2019 Melatonin for sleep in children with neurodisabilities#^p30710339-bias|Parker 2019]]

**What it is used for: trauma-related nightmares.** Pooled across ten trials, the alpha-1 blocker prazosin improved nightmares and insomnia in PTSD but not overall symptoms, whereas the largest single trial found no benefit. [[P39828080 Mendes 2025 Prazosin for PTSD sleep meta-regression#^p39828080-sleep|Mendes 2025]] [[P29414272 Raskind 2018 Prazosin trial in veterans with PTSD#^p29414272-null|Raskind 2018]] The evidence is followed in [[Adrenergic blockers in psychiatry]].

**Class-level adverse effects and serious risks: how the groups differ.** Benzodiazepines and most Z-drugs left more people with side effects than placebo, doxepin, seltorexant or zaleplon. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-tolerability|De Crescenzo 2022]] Z-drug use was associated with fractures in older adults, and complex sleep behaviours on Z-drugs have ended in deaths. [[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-fractures|Treves 2018]] [[P32323442 Harbourt 2020 Z-drugs and complex sleep behaviours#^p32323442-cases|Harbourt 2020]] Benzodiazepine dependence makes abrupt stopping risky. [[P40526204 Brunner 2025 Benzodiazepine tapering guideline#^p40526204-abrupt|Brunner 2025]] Orexin antagonists are linked to fewer next-morning effects and less motor and cognitive impairment, and appear free of dependence and tolerance, though real-world data are still needed. [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-mechanism|Muehlan 2023]] [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-dependence|Muehlan 2023]]

**Reading rule.** Name the medicine, the outcome and the population: this family includes drugs that add inhibition, drugs that block arousal signals and drugs that shift the clock, and a result for self-rated sleep quality in adults with insomnia disorder does not speak to circadian disorders, children or next-day function. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-caution-outcomes|Appraisal: De Crescenzo 2022]] [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-caution-melatonin|Appraisal: De Crescenzo 2022]] [[P30521244 Savage 2024 Melatonin pharmacology and uses#^p30521244-caution-roles|Appraisal: Savage 2024]]

## Evidence and status

The strongest comparative evidence is a network meta-analysis of double-blind monotherapy trials in adults with insomnia disorder, measuring self-rated sleep quality and resting on few trials for long-term comparisons. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-limit|De Crescenzo 2022]] Guideline grades describe typical adults and leave out children and circadian disorders. [[P38016484 Riemann 2023 European insomnia guideline#^p38016484-caution-scope|Appraisal: Riemann 2023]] Harms of the GABA-acting drugs come largely from observational data and case series. [[P29077902 Treves 2018 Z-drugs falls and fractures#^p29077902-limit|Treves 2018]] [[P32323442 Harbourt 2020 Z-drugs and complex sleep behaviours#^p32323442-limit|Harbourt 2020]] The advantages claimed for orexin antagonists compare separate studies of different classes. [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Muehlan 2023]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether orexin antagonists keep their tolerability and dependence advantages in long-term real-world use. [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-caution-comparison|Appraisal: Muehlan 2023]]
- Which children benefit from melatonin, given very high heterogeneity and parent-kept diaries. [[P30710339 Parker 2019 Melatonin for sleep in children with neurodisabilities#^p30710339-caution-diary|Appraisal: Parker 2019]] [[P30710339 Parker 2019 Melatonin for sleep in children with neurodisabilities#^p30710339-caution-grouping|Appraisal: Parker 2019]]
- Whether prazosin's pooled benefit on nightmares holds outside veteran samples and in complex trauma. [[P29414272 Raskind 2018 Prazosin trial in veterans with PTSD#^p29414272-caution-population|Appraisal: Raskind 2018]] [[P39828080 Mendes 2025 Prazosin for PTSD sleep meta-regression#^p39828080-caution-discordance|Appraisal: Mendes 2025]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Systematic review and meta-regression (Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology and Biological Psychiatry).** Ten pooled prazosin trials show benefit on nightmares and insomnia but not on overall PTSD symptoms. [[P39828080 Mendes 2025 Prazosin for PTSD sleep meta-regression#^p39828080-sleep|Mendes 2025]] Its trial-level moderators do not describe individuals. [[P39828080 Mendes 2025 Prazosin for PTSD sleep meta-regression#^p39828080-caution-ecological|Appraisal: Mendes 2025]]
- **2023 · Narrative review (Journal of Sleep Research).** Presents orexin antagonism as a non-GABA route to sleep with fewer next-day effects. [[P37086045 Muehlan 2023 Orexin receptor antagonists for insomnia#^p37086045-mechanism|Muehlan 2023]]
- **2022 · Systematic review and network meta-analysis (Lancet).** Ranks 36 drug treatments for insomnia disorder, separating acute benefit from long-term evidence and from tolerability. [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-acute|De Crescenzo 2022]] [[P35843245 De Crescenzo 2022 Drugs for insomnia disorder#^p35843245-longterm|De Crescenzo 2022]]
