# Drug classes and mechanisms overview

## Definition

Psychotropic drugs are grouped by their principal target or clinical use: antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilisers, anxiolytics and hypnotics, and stimulants and non-stimulants.

## How it works

**Two axes describe every drug.** Pharmacodynamics describes what a drug does to the body, usually by acting on receptors, enzymes, transporters or ion channels; pharmacokinetics describes what the body does to a drug - absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How do medicines work]] [[F12 NIGMS What happens to medicine in your body#^f12-adme|What happens to medicine in your body]]

**Class logic.** Class names describe a target (monoamine reuptake), an approved or historical use (antidepressant, antipsychotic, mood stabiliser) or an effect (stimulant), and the name does not always correspond to a single mechanism: the same class can contain agents with different molecular targets. "Antidepressant" names a use and contains reuptake inhibitors, enzyme inhibitors and receptor modulators; "antipsychotic" contains a dopamine-blocking majority, a partial agonist subset and a 2024 muscarinic combination; "mood stabiliser" is a use label whose members share no mechanism; "stimulant" names an effect whose members differ in transporter selectivity. The labels are navigation aids for clinicians and regulators; they are not mechanism claims, and mechanism claims made from class names inherit every exception in the class. [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-targets|How Do Medicines Work?]] [[Antipsychotic mechanisms]]

**Antidepressants.** Monoamine-based actions are acute while clinical change takes weeks, and the antidepressant literature notes that monoamine-based accounts explain acute pharmacological action poorly relative to that weeks-long time course. This has motivated emotional-processing and plasticity accounts as complements: antidepressants are proposed to shift emotional and cognitive processing early, while cellular plasticity and stress-related signalling unfold more slowly. [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-monoamine|How do antidepressants work]] [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-processing|How do antidepressants work?]]

**Antipsychotics, for the dopamine-blocking subclass.** Response and several side effects converge on D2 receptor blockade: it is the mechanism identified for clinical response and for the motor and endocrine side effects that bound the usable dose range. The endocrine and motor effects are not attributed to a single structure - the pathways differ by effect: basal-ganglia motor circuitry for movement, and the hypothalamic-pituitary route that dopamine tonically inhibits for endocrine effects such as prolactin elevation. Off-target binding at serotonergic, histaminergic, cholinergic and adrenergic receptors contributes further effects such as weight gain, sedation and dysphoria and much of the difference between agents. The class also now contains a muscarinic treatment, so this class-wide account is time-scoped to the dopamine-blocking drugs. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]] [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-offtarget|Antipsychotics mechanisms]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-prolactin|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[F127 FDA prescribing information COBENFY#^f127-mechanism|COBENFY prescribing information]]

**Why the time-scoped reading of class mechanisms matters.** The dopamine-blocking account of antipsychotic action is the best-supported class mechanism in psychopharmacology, and it still carries a date and a subclass because the class has since admitted drugs without it. That pattern - strong mechanism, named boundaries - is the template this article applies to every class summary, and the class-specific articles carry the details. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]] [[F128 DailyMed label NUPLAZID pimavanserin#^f128-mechanism|NUPLAZID approved label]]

**Mood stabilisers.** Lithium's pharmacokinetics and monitoring requirements are well described and its actions span several signalling nodes, but its mechanism of mood stabilisation is not settled: the recurrence-preventing mechanism remains only partially understood. [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-unknown|Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics]]

**Stimulants and non-stimulants.** These increase catecholamine signalling by different routes. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

**Regulatory and clinical categories differ too.** Approval decisions weigh the submitted evidence against a regulatory standard, and that judgement is separate from the fact that a compound was tested. A class label can also be applied by marketing or habit rather than by mechanism, which is one reason this library cites the mechanism review rather than the label whenever a mechanism claim is made. [[F90 FDA Drug review#^f90-decision|Drug review]] [[F13 NIGMS How do medicines work#^f13-limit|How do medicines work]]

## Evidence and status

Mechanisms for the main classes are reasonably well characterised; class membership predicts side-effect patterns far better than it predicts individual response. Class-level statements hide response variability: the same antipsychotic mechanism that produces benefit also produces the endocrine and motor side effects that define a therapeutic window, and the review notes that these drugs are ineffective for some patients and that response varies. A statement of the form 'this class works by X' therefore describes a mechanism shared across a group, not a prediction about a person. [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-limits|Antipsychotics mechanisms]] [[P28153641 Harmer 2017 How do antidepressants work#^p28153641-plasticity|How do antidepressants work]]

For stimulants and non-stimulants, the evidence base is concentrated on short-term symptom outcomes. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]
