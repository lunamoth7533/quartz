# Anticonvulsant mood stabilisers

## Definition

Anticonvulsant mood stabilisers are antiseizure drugs - valproate, carbamazepine and lamotrigine - used to treat or prevent mood episodes in bipolar disorder. [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-uses|Rogawski 2004]] [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-depression|De Prisco 2026]] [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-mania|De Prisco 2026]] The group is defined by clinical use rather than by one mechanism, and each member has a different profile across mania, depression and maintenance. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-families|Sills 2020]] [[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-actions|Rosenberg 2007]] [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-polarity|Kishi 2021]] Lithium, which is not an anticonvulsant, is the usual comparator: in maintenance it protected against both poles, which none of the three did alone. [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-polarity|Kishi 2021]] [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-any|Kishi 2021]]

## How it works

**Members.** Valproate, carbamazepine and lamotrigine are the three members with bipolar evidence in the syntheses used here. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-mania|De Prisco 2026]] [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-polarity|Kishi 2021]]

**Mechanism.** In the antiseizure classification, carbamazepine and lamotrigine modulate voltage-gated sodium channels. [[P32120063 Sills 2020 Mechanisms of antiseizure drugs#^p32120063-families|Sills 2020]] Valproate's actions are broader and incompletely understood: it strengthens GABA-mediated transmission, acts on Wnt/beta-catenin and ERK signalling and on inositol metabolism, and changes gene expression partly by inhibiting histone deacetylases. [[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-unknown|Rosenberg 2007]] [[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-actions|Rosenberg 2007]] [[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-genomic|Rosenberg 2007]] In mood disorders these drugs are thought to act much as they do in epilepsy, through sodium and calcium channels or GABA-A inhibition, with slower effects on plasticity and cell-survival signalling offered as the reason benefit is delayed. [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-mechanism|Rogawski 2004]] [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-delayed|Rogawski 2004]] Which of these actions prevents a mood episode has not been shown for any member. [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-caution-transfer|Appraisal: Rogawski 2004]] [[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-limit|Rosenberg 2007]]

**The lithium comparator.** Lithium also acts at several signalling nodes, including GSK3, and its recurrence-preventing mechanism is likewise unsettled. [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-signalling|Alda 2015]] [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-unknown|Alda 2015]] Two of valproate's candidate pathways, inositol metabolism and Wnt/beta-catenin signalling, also feature in lithium hypotheses, which makes shared targets a candidate explanation for the drugs' overlapping uses rather than a demonstrated one. [[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-caution-lithium|Appraisal: Rosenberg 2007]]

**What it is used for: phase and polarity.** A 2026 living umbrella review judged lamotrigine and valproate effective in adult bipolar depression, valproate and carbamazepine (with lithium) in adult mania, and valproate (with lithium) in adult maintenance, while listing lamotrigine among treatments effective across phases. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-depression|De Prisco 2026]] [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-mania|De Prisco 2026]] [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-maintenance|De Prisco 2026]] In a maintenance network meta-analysis, lamotrigine lowered depressive but not manic recurrence, valproate lowered manic but not depressive recurrence, lithium lowered both, and carbamazepine did not beat placebo for recurrence of any mood episode. [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-polarity|Kishi 2021]] [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-any|Kishi 2021]] Guidelines therefore rank options phase by phase, weighing efficacy with safety and tolerability. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|Keramatian 2023]] In register data, periods on a mood stabiliser alone were associated with fewer depression-related admissions, and lithium was the only single drug with that association. [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-classes|Ermis 2025]] [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-lithium|Ermis 2025]]

**Class-level adverse effects and serious risks.** Pregnancy exposure is the defining risk for valproate: about one in ten exposed children had a major congenital malformation, against 2-3% of children of women without epilepsy, with an excess over other antiseizure drugs that was dose-dependent in several studies. [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-valproate|Bromley 2023]] Carbamazepine carried a smaller raised risk, and lamotrigine showed no clear increase against children of women with untreated epilepsy. [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-others|Bromley 2023]] [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-lamotrigine|Bromley 2023]] These figures come from epilepsy, and their transfer to bipolar use rests on pharmacology. [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-caution-indication|Appraisal: Bromley 2023]] UK bipolar guidance carries explicit cautions about particular medicines in pregnancy and for younger people. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-longterm|Bipolar disorder CG185]] Serious skin reactions are the second class risk: HLA-B*15:02 is strongly associated with carbamazepine-induced Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis, and more weakly with lamotrigine-induced reactions. [[P38527170 Tham 2024 HLA-B and antiseizure drug skin reactions#^p38527170-association|Tham 2024]] A negative allele test does not remove that risk. [[P38527170 Tham 2024 HLA-B and antiseizure drug skin reactions#^p38527170-caution-negative|Appraisal: Tham 2024]]

**Reading rule.** Name the drug, the phase and the polarity: "mood stabiliser" covers drugs whose evidence points in different directions - lamotrigine toward depression, valproate and carbamazepine toward mania - so a maintenance claim must say which recurrences it prevents. [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-polarity|Kishi 2021]] [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-caution-lists|Appraisal: De Prisco 2026]]

## Evidence and status

Phase-specific efficacy rests on meta-analyses whose certainty is mostly moderate or low. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-limit|De Prisco 2026]] Maintenance comparisons come from long trials pooled in one network, and a non-significant comparison there is not proof of no benefit. [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-limit|Kishi 2021]] [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-caution-null|Appraisal: Kishi 2021]] Mechanisms remain candidates for every member, as they do for lithium. [[P17514356 Rosenberg 2007 Mechanisms of action of valproate#^p17514356-unknown|Rosenberg 2007]] [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-unknown|Alda 2015]] Pregnancy risks rest on observational data whose biases the Cochrane authors judge unlikely to explain the differences between drugs. [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-limit|Bromley 2023]]

## Uncertainties

- Which action produces mood stabilisation for any member. [[P15229516 Rogawski 2004 Antiepileptic drugs in nonepileptic conditions#^p15229516-caution-transfer|Appraisal: Rogawski 2004]]
- Whether carbamazepine's weak maintenance showing reflects the drug or too few trials. [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-caution-null|Appraisal: Kishi 2021]]
- How much maintenance trials that enrol prior responders favour the study drug. [[P33177610 Kishi 2021 Maintenance treatment of bipolar disorder#^p33177610-caution-enrichment|Appraisal: Kishi 2021]]
- Whether genetic markers can predict response; for lithium, pharmacogenetic findings remain limited by small samples. [[P25687772 Alda 2015 Lithium pharmacology and pharmacogenetics#^p25687772-pharmacogenetics|Alda 2015]]

## Recent research

- **2026 · Living umbrella review (BMJ).** Grades the evidence by phase and age group, placing valproate across depression, mania and maintenance, lamotrigine mainly on the depressive side and carbamazepine in mania. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-depression|De Prisco 2026]] [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-mania|De Prisco 2026]] [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-maintenance|De Prisco 2026]] As a living review, its lists carry a version date. [[P42580772 De Prisco 2026 Umbrella review of bipolar treatments#^p42580772-caution-living|Appraisal: De Prisco 2026]]
- **2024 · Umbrella review (Pharmacogenetics and Genomics).** Confirms a strong HLA-B*15:02 link for carbamazepine and a milder one for lamotrigine. [[P38527170 Tham 2024 HLA-B and antiseizure drug skin reactions#^p38527170-association|Tham 2024]]
- **2023 · Cochrane systematic review (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews).** Updates malformation risks by drug, with valproate highest and lamotrigine without a clear excess against untreated epilepsy. [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-valproate|Bromley 2023]] [[P37647086 Bromley 2023 Antiseizure monotherapy in pregnancy#^p37647086-lamotrigine|Bromley 2023]]
