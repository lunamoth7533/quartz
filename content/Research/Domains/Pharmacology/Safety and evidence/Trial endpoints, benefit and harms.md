# Trial endpoints, benefit and harms

## Definition

An endpoint is the outcome a trial is designed to measure. Primary endpoints determine the main conclusion; secondary and safety endpoints supply context. Endpoint choice encodes a judgement about what matters.

## How it works

**Endpoints are choices.** A trial's primary endpoint decides what counts as success, and different choices answer different questions: symptom scales, functional measures, quality of life and adverse-event discontinuation are different endpoints, and improvement in one does not imply improvement in another. A symptom scale measures symptoms, a functioning instrument measures activity and participation, and a quality-of-life measure measures something else again. [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-domains|International Classification of Functioning]] WHODAS 2.0, for example, covers cognition, mobility, self-care, getting along, life activities and participation, and a functioning score summarises reported activity rather than symptom severity. [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-domains|International Classification of Functioning]] [[F23 WHO International Classification of Functioning#^f23-caution-impairment|AI appraisal: International Classification of Functioning]]

**Duration.** Short-term symptom trials dominate the evidence base; long-term questions about function and safety usually rest on observational data with different limitations. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-longterm|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]]

**Rater and setting define the measurement.** Self-report, clinician rating and informant rating produce systematically different estimates, which is why trials report them separately: rater choice changes the answer within the same randomised trials. In the ADHD medication literature every drug beat placebo on clinicians' ratings in children while only two did on teachers' ratings, a change in result produced by a change in informant rather than in treatment, so any benefit claim should name who rated it. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-efficacy|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P29566425 Lopez 2018 Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD#^p29566425-caution-rater|AI appraisal: Cognitive behavioural interventions for ADHD]]

**Harms are part of the effect.** Amphetamines were less well tolerated than placebo in both age groups, and dose-related discontinuation rose above study-specific ranges for amphetamines and methylphenidate, while the antipsychotic literature shows benefit and side effects sharing one mechanism. Tolerability is not a footnote to efficacy; it is the other half of the same question. [[P30097390 Cortese 2018 ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability#^p30097390-tolerability|ADHD medication efficacy and tolerability]] [[P42134365 Nourredine 2026 ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis#^p42134365-tolerability|ADHD medication dose-effect synthesis]] [[P31299229 Kaar 2020 Antipsychotics mechanisms#^p31299229-d2|Antipsychotics mechanisms]]

**Certainty and recommendation are separate outputs.** Guidelines rate evidence per outcome, so a recommendation can be strong for one outcome and weak for another. GRADE separates the certainty of evidence from the strength of a recommendation, and certainty is rated down for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. A strong recommendation on low-certainty evidence is legitimate where values and context justify it - which is exactly why the two columns must be read together. [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-domains|GRADE]]

**Non-significance.** A trial that fails to detect a difference has not shown equivalence, and absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-caution-absence|AI appraisal: Comparative interventions for ADHD]]

**Reading rule.** For each benefit claim, find the paired harm claim and the certainty grade, and check whether the endpoint was chosen before or after the analysis. A trial report that supplies all three is readable; one that supplies a headline is not. [[P39701638 Ostinelli 2025 Comparative interventions for ADHD#^p39701638-acceptability|Comparative interventions for ADHD]] [[P23997866 Sullivan 2012 Using effect size#^p23997866-example|Using effect size]]

## Evidence and status

Endpoint and rater effects are well documented in the meta-analytic literature; their interpretation requires knowing the decision the trial was designed to support.
