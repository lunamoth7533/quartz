# Therapeutic index, monitoring and interactions

**Definition.** The therapeutic window is the concentration range between too little and too much drug; a narrow window makes small exposure changes consequential. [[F18 FDA Narrow therapeutic index drugs#^f18-window|Setting and Implementing Standards for Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs]]

## Supported claims

- Every medicine sits between a concentration that does too little and one that produces toxicity. [[F18 FDA Narrow therapeutic index drugs#^f18-window|Setting and Implementing Standards for Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs]]
- Narrow therapeutic index drugs need tighter exposure control and often biological monitoring. [[F18 FDA Narrow therapeutic index drugs#^f18-nti|Setting and Implementing Standards for Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs]]
- Interactions include drug-drug, drug-food and drug-condition types and can change how much drug reaches its target. [[F19 FDA Drug interactions what you should know#^f19-types|Drug Interactions: What You Should Know]]

## How it works

**Every drug has a window.** The range between a concentration that does too little and one that produces toxicity is the therapeutic window, and the width of that range is the therapeutic index; the window is a property of the drug-tissue system rather than of the drug alone. A narrow window means small differences in exposure produce large differences in effect. [[F18 FDA Narrow therapeutic index drugs#^f18-window|Setting and Implementing Standards for Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs]]

**Monitoring follows from kinetics.** Narrow-index drugs need tighter control of exposure and often biological monitoring because small changes in absorption, metabolism or interactions move patients across the window, and individuals vary; monitoring is therefore a kinetic precaution rather than a comment on the drug's potency. [[F18 FDA Narrow therapeutic index drugs#^f18-nti|Setting and Implementing Standards for Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs]] [[Half-life and steady state]] Lithium is the standard example in this library, with serum sampling under stable conditions. [[F27 DailyMed lithium carbonate labelling#^f27-monitoring|Lithium carbonate labelling]]

**What monitoring actually monitors.** Biological monitoring for a narrow-index drug tracks a surrogate - a blood concentration, a renal marker, an electrolyte - chosen because it tracks the danger before symptoms do. Each surrogate has its own validity question: how well it reflects tissue exposure, how much it swings with non-drug factors, and how quickly it changes. A monitoring programme is therefore a measurement system with the same validity and timing questions the methods domain applies to any instrument. [[F18 FDA Narrow therapeutic index drugs#^f18-nti|Setting and Implementing Standards for Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs]] [[Measurement validity and reliability]]

**Interactions are pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic, and the difference matters.** Interactions arise when one agent changes the absorption, metabolism or protein binding of another, or when two agents act on the same physiological system, so they can change how much drug is present or how it acts. A kinetic interaction changes exposure - one agent speeds or slows another's metabolism, absorption or binding - so the effect looks like a dose change; a dynamic interaction changes effect at the same exposure, as two agents acting on the same physiological system add or oppose. Predicting them requires the kinetic and dynamic profile rather than the class name: kinetic interactions live in metabolism and transport, dynamic ones in receptor and circuit overlap. [[F19 FDA Drug interactions what you should know#^f19-types|Drug Interactions: What You Should Know]]

**The lithium case ties the three concepts together.** Narrow index, renal clearance sensitive to salt and fluid balance, and dynamic interactions with systems it shares - lithium is the standard worked example because every concept in this article is load-bearing for it at once, which the lithium article develops without turning the account into personal guidance. [[Lithium mechanisms and uncertainty]] [[Half-life and steady state]]

**Where decisions belong.** The FDA directs individual decisions to product labelling and qualified clinicians or pharmacists rather than to general guidance. [[F19 FDA Drug interactions what you should know#^f19-advice|Drug interactions: what you should know]] This note is a mechanism and safety-principle reference; it deliberately contains no dosing, titration or personal recommendations.

**Reading rule.** Monitoring claims should state what is monitored, on what timescale and why; general statements that a drug 'needs monitoring' without those specifics cannot be evaluated. [[F18 FDA Narrow therapeutic index drugs#^f18-limit|Setting and Implementing Standards for Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** The regulatory framing is the honest boundary with clinical psychiatry: the FDA's own material routes individual decisions to labelling and qualified clinicians, so this reference explains why monitoring exists and leaves the decisions where the source puts them. ## Limitation or common misconception

Monitoring manages concentration rather than the whole clinical picture; individual decisions belong with clinicians and product labelling.
