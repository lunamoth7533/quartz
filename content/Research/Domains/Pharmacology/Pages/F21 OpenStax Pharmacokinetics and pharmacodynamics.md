## Key points (as published)

Elimination half-life is the time for the amount of drug in the body to fall by half, and repeated dosing moves a drug toward a steady-state concentration.
^f21-halflife

Concentration, half-life and steady state are models that describe exposure over time; they are not dosing instructions and do not by themselves predict clinical response.
^f21-model

## Clarification: tolerance and dosing intervals (checked 2026-09-24)

The blocks below were appended during the staged learning-layer build from the same checked OpenStax section
2.2 page. The original note above is unchanged and its reading status remains `queued`.

Drug tolerance occurs when a client requires more drug, or higher concentrations, to achieve the desired
effect: the body becomes used to the medication, so more may be needed for the same effect, and tolerance need
not develop for every effect of a drug.
^f21-tolerance

In this educational exposure model, medicines with a short half-life may need short dosing intervals because
concentration falls below the minimum effective concentration, while medicines with long half-lives clear more
slowly and can accumulate. The page presents these as general model statements, not as instructions for any
product or person.
^f21-interval
