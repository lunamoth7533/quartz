# Factors impacting prazosin efficacy for nightmares and insomnia in PTSD patients - a systematic review and meta-regression analysis.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review describes prazosin as an alpha-1 adrenergic antagonist whose results for PTSD-related sleep problems have been mixed.
^p39828080-framing

Pooled across the trials, prazosin improved insomnia and nightmares more than placebo (standardised mean differences about -0.65 and -0.64), whereas its effect on overall PTSD symptoms fell short of significance.
^p39828080-sleep

In meta-regression, trials with more benzodiazepine co-prescription showed larger gains in insomnia and PTSD severity, a finding the authors did not anticipate.
^p39828080-moderator

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis and meta-regression of randomised placebo-controlled trials found in five databases, testing age, sex, military or civilian status, dose, duration, baseline severity, co-medication, depression and alcohol use as moderators; its suggestions about dosing are not carried into this library.
^p39828080-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Moderators measured at trial level describe study populations, not people: that trials with more benzodiazepine use showed bigger effects does not show that combining the drugs helps anyone, and ten trials give meta-regression little power.
^p39828080-caution-ecological

The pooled benefit on nightmares coexists with a null result in the largest single trial; the two answer different questions about which populations respond, so neither settles the matter alone.
^p39828080-caution-discordance
