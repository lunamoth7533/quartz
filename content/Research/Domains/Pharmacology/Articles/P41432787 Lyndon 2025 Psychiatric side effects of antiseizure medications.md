# Psychiatric and behavioural side effects of antiseizure medications in epilepsy.

**Authors.** Lyndon S, Dworetzky BA, Baslet G · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41432787/)

**Published.** 2025 in Journal of neurology (first online 2025-12-23).

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Psychiatric and behavioural side-effect burden differed more than tenfold across 28 drugs; levetiracetam, perampanel, felbamate and stiripentol sat at the high end, mainly through irritability and aggression.
^p41432787-spread

Carbamazepine, ethosuximide, lacosamide, oxcarbazepine, phenytoin, pregabalin and primidone carried the least behavioural liability, and some also have mood-stabilising properties.
^p41432787-low

Polytherapy, fast titration, higher doses, prior psychiatric illness, intellectual disability and social deprivation went with higher risk; proposed mechanisms include strong AMPA-receptor modulation, broad GABA potentiation, NMDA antagonism, folate depletion and drug interactions.
^p41432787-risk

## Scope as reported

Narrative review of five databases and regulatory documents from 1990 to September 2025, integrating evidence qualitatively; the risk tiers are the authors' own composite classification, and the selection, monitoring and switching guidance it offers is not reproduced here.
^p41432787-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Tiers assembled from trials, cohorts and spontaneous reports are a structured judgement rather than a pooled estimate; a drug's tier describes average liability in epilepsy populations, not what a given person will experience.
^p41432787-caution-tiers

The evidence comes from people with epilepsy, among whom mood and anxiety disorders are common; how far the tiers apply when the same drugs are used for psychiatric indications is not addressed.
^p41432787-caution-indication
