# Ketamine and other glutamate receptor modulators for depression in adults with unipolar major depressive disorder.

**Authors.** Dean RL, Hurducas C, et al. (12 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34510411/)

**Published.** 2021 in The Cochrane database of systematic reviews (first online 2021-09-12).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/14651858.CD011612.pub3](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011612.pub3) · PMID [34510411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34510411/) · PMCID [PMC8434915](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8434915/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Against placebo, ketamine may raise response and remission and lower depression scores at 24 hours, but only on very low-certainty evidence from a handful of small trials.
^p34510411-ketamine

Esketamine probably increases remission and lowers depression scores at 24 hours, on moderate-certainty evidence from about 800 to 900 participants, and people on it did not clearly drop out more often than those on placebo.
^p34510411-esketamine

Evidence for the other glutamate receptor modulators was very sparse, and the authors call for long-term comparative trials and real-world monitoring of repeated ketamine and esketamine.
^p34510411-gaps

## Scope as reported

Cochrane review searched to July 2020; most ketamine trials used one intravenous administration and all esketamine trials used repeated administration, mostly intranasal; unclear masking of treatment lowered certainty for every outcome.
^p34510411-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A 24-hour outcome measures speed, not durability, and ketamine's noticeable acute effects make masking hard, which the certainty ratings only partly capture; the review does not establish long-term benefit or safety.
^p34510411-caution-blinding
