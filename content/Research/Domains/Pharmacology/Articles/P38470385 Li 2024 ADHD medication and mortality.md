# ADHD Pharmacotherapy and Mortality in Individuals With ADHD.

**Authors.** Li L, Zhu N, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38470385/)

**Published.** 2024 in JAMA.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jama.2024.0851](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.0851) · PMID [38470385](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38470385/) · PMCID [PMC10936112](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10936112/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Starting ADHD medication within three months of diagnosis was associated with lower two-year all-cause mortality than not starting (hazard ratio 0.79).
^p38470385-allcause

The difference came from unnatural causes such as unintentional injuries, suicide and accidental poisoning; natural-cause mortality did not differ significantly.
^p38470385-causes

## Scope as reported

Observational register study of 148,578 people in Sweden that emulated a target trial of medication initiation versus non-initiation; follow-up ended two years after diagnosis, and absolute two-year risks were low (39.1 versus 48.1 deaths per 10,000 people).
^p38470385-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Target trial emulation makes the causal question explicit but cannot remove confounding by indication: people who start medication may differ in unmeasured ways, so the estimate is an association, and a two-year window says nothing about decades of use.
^p38470385-caution-confounding
