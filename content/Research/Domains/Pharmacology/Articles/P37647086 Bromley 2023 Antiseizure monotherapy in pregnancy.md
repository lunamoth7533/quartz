# Monotherapy treatment of epilepsy in pregnancy: congenital malformation outcomes in the child.

**Authors.** Bromley R, Adab N, et al. (14 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37647086/)

**Published.** 2023 in The Cochrane database of systematic reviews (first online 2023-08-29).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/14651858.CD010224.pub3](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010224.pub3) · PMID [37647086](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37647086/) · PMCID [PMC10463554](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10463554/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Major congenital malformations affected about 9.8% of valproate-exposed children in cohort studies and 9.7% in health-record studies, against 2.1-3.3% in children of women without epilepsy; the excess over other monotherapies was 5-9 percentage points and dose-dependent in several studies.
^p37647086-valproate

Carbamazepine (about 4.7%), phenobarbital, phenytoin and topiramate were also linked to raised risk, topiramate specifically to oro-facial clefts.
^p37647086-others

For lamotrigine and levetiracetam, comparisons with children of women with untreated epilepsy showed no clear increase.
^p37647086-lamotrigine

## Scope as reported

Cochrane review of 49 observational studies (prospective cohorts, pregnancy registries and routine health records) searched to February 2022; all carried a high risk of some biases, which the authors judge unlikely to explain the differences between drugs.
^p37647086-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

These data come from women with epilepsy; the same malformation risks are expected when the drugs are used for bipolar disorder, but that transfer rests on pharmacology rather than on this review.
^p37647086-caution-indication

A relative risk needs its baseline: the same ratio means a different absolute change at different base rates, and no figure here is guidance for an individual pregnancy.
^p37647086-caution-absolute
