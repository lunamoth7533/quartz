# Unraveling the genetic link: an umbrella review on HLA-B*15:02 and antiepileptic drug-induced Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis.

**Authors.** Tham KM, Yek JJL, Liu CWY · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38527170/)

**Published.** 2024 in Pharmacogenetics and genomics (first online 2024-03-22).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1097/FPC.0000000000000531](https://doi.org/10.1097/FPC.0000000000000531) · PMID [38527170](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38527170/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

HLA-B*15:02 was strongly associated with Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis induced by carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine, and more weakly with those induced by lamotrigine and phenytoin.
^p38527170-association

The authors suggest that screening could reduce these reactions, possibly also before starting lamotrigine or phenytoin, and call for study of other alleles and of genome-wide approaches.
^p38527170-screening

## Scope as reported

Umbrella review searched in May 2023, appraising meta-analyses with AMSTAR 2 and case-control studies with the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale; evidence for drugs other than carbamazepine rests on fewer studies.
^p38527170-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A strong association is not a strong individual prediction: the abstract gives no positive predictive value, and the allele's frequency varies widely between ancestries, so a result's meaning depends on the population tested.
^p38527170-caution-prediction

A negative result does not remove the risk of serious skin reactions, which also occur in people without the allele.
^p38527170-caution-negative
