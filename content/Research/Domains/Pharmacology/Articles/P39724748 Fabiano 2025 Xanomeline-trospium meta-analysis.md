# Efficacy, tolerability, and safety of xanomeline-trospium chloride for schizophrenia: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Fabiano N, Wong S, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39724748/)

**Published.** 2025 in European neuropsychopharmacology : the journal of the European College of Neuropsychopharmacology (first online 2024-12-25).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.euroneuro.2024.11.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2024.11.013) · PMID [39724748](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39724748/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across three five-week trials, xanomeline-trospium beat placebo on total PANSS (SMD -0.56) and on positive, negative and global-severity measures with moderate-certainty evidence, roughly doubling the response rate.
^p39724748-efficacy

Vomiting, nausea, dry mouth, dyspepsia, constipation and hypertension were more frequent than on placebo, as were raised triglycerides and supine heart rate, but these pro- and anticholinergic effects were mostly mild to moderate and transient, and serious adverse events and all-cause discontinuation did not increase.
^p39724748-safety

Clinically relevant weight gain was less likely than on placebo, and LDL and HDL cholesterol fell.
^p39724748-weight

## Scope as reported

PRISMA 2020 review with random-effects meta-analysis, Cochrane risk-of-bias 2 and GRADE; all three trials were at low risk of bias, five weeks long and in acutely ill adults, and subgroup findings on negative and cognitive symptoms were post hoc.
^p39724748-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The three trials share one design (five weeks, acute exacerbation, placebo comparator), so pooling adds precision without adding different settings, durations or active comparators.
