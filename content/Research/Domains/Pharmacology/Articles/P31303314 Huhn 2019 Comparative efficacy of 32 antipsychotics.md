## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Every drug's point estimate favoured it over placebo for overall symptoms, although six were not statistically significant; standardised effects spanned roughly 0.89 for clozapine down to 0.03 for levomepromazine.
^p31303314-efficacy

Harms separated the drugs more sharply: pimozide had the most antiparkinson-medication use, zuclopenthixol the most sedation, zotepine the most weight gain, paliperidone the largest prolactin rise and sertindole the longest QTc prolongation.
^p31303314-sideeffects

The authors judge most efficacy differences gradual rather than categorical, and confidence in the estimates was often low or very low.
^p31303314-interpretation

## Scope as reported

Network meta-analysis of acute-phase trials in adults with multi-episode schizophrenia; studies of treatment resistance, first episodes, predominant negative or depressive symptoms and relapse prevention were excluded, and certainty was rated with CINeMA.
^p31303314-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Rankings from short acute trials in multi-episode adults say little about first-episode, treatment-resistant or maintenance use, and a drug's position is not a forecast for one person.
^p31303314-caution-ranking

Within each generation the network shows a wide spread of harms, which is why grouping these drugs by generation hides more than it reveals.
^p31303314-caution-generations
