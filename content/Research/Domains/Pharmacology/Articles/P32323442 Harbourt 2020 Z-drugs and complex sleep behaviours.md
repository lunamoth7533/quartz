# Association of eszopiclone, zaleplon, or zolpidem with complex sleep behaviors resulting in serious injuries, including death.

**Authors.** Harbourt K, Nevo ON, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32323442/)

**Published.** 2020 in Pharmacoepidemiology and drug safety (first online 2020-04-22).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/pds.5004](https://doi.org/10.1002/pds.5004) · PMID [32323442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32323442/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Sixty-six cases qualified: 20 deaths and 46 serious injuries after complex sleep behaviours on a Z-drug, from carbon monoxide poisoning, drowning, falls, hypothermia and vehicle collisions to burns, overdoses and self-injury.
^p32323442-cases

Twenty-two cases had an earlier complex sleep behaviour on a Z-drug before the reported event.
^p32323442-recurrence

Regulators responded with a boxed warning, a contraindication after a prior episode, updated warnings and a public safety communication.
^p32323442-regulatory

## Scope as reported

Retrospective descriptive analysis of the U.S. FDA Adverse Event Reporting System and of published case reports, using random sampling and descriptive statistics.
^p32323442-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Spontaneous reports lack a denominator and depend on reporting habits, so the series shows that these events occur and can be fatal, not how often they occur.
