# Trial of Prazosin for Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder in Military Veterans.

**Authors.** Raskind MA, Peskind ER, et al. (18 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29414272/)

**Published.** 2018 in The New England journal of medicine.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1056/NEJMoa1507598](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1507598) · PMID [29414272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29414272/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Earlier randomised trials in military veterans had found prazosin, an alpha-1 adrenoceptor antagonist, effective against nightmares associated with PTSD.
^p29414272-rationale

At 10 weeks prazosin did not differ from placebo on distressing dreams (CAPS item B2), sleep quality (PSQI) or global clinical change, and no differences emerged at 26 weeks or on secondary outcomes.
^p29414272-null

Supine systolic blood pressure fell 6.7 mm Hg more on prazosin, and suicidal ideation that was new or worse was reported by 8% on prazosin against 15% on placebo.
^p29414272-safety

## Scope as reported

Double-blind trial in 304 veterans with chronic PTSD and frequent nightmares at 13 centres; the three primary outcomes were assessed at 10 weeks and blinded treatment continued to 26 weeks. The dose-escalation schedule is not reproduced here.
^p29414272-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Veterans with chronic PTSD recruited at specialist centres differ from civilian and complex-trauma samples, so a null result here does not settle the question for other trauma populations.
^p29414272-caution-population

A large null trial following smaller positive ones leaves the evidence discordant; which result generalises depends on population, placebo response and outcome measure rather than on this trial alone.
^p29414272-caution-discordance
