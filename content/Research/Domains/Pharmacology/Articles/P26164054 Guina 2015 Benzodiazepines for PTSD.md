# Benzodiazepines for PTSD: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

**Authors.** Guina J, Rossetter SR, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26164054/)

**Published.** 2015 in Journal of psychiatric practice.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1097/PRA.0000000000000091](https://doi.org/10.1097/PRA.0000000000000091) · PMID [26164054](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26164054/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across 18 studies, benzodiazepines neither treated nor prevented PTSD, and their risks tended to outweigh any short-lived benefit.
^p26164054-ineffective

Problems specific to PTSD included worse overall severity, a higher chance of developing PTSD when the drugs were used soon after trauma, poorer psychotherapy outcomes, aggression, depression and substance use.
^p26164054-harms

The authors conclude that the drugs should be regarded as relatively contraindicated in PTSD or after recent trauma, and that evidence-based PTSD treatments should be preferred.
^p26164054-conclusion

## Scope as reported

PROSPERO-registered review following PRISMA, with qualitative and quantitative synthesis of trials and observational studies of mixed designs; the biological and psychosocial explanations it offers are proposals, not tested findings.
^p26164054-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Much of the harm signal rests on observational comparisons, where people given benzodiazepines after trauma may already have been more distressed, so severity can masquerade as a drug effect.
^p26164054-caution-confounding

The samples are described as PTSD populations; none is identified as ICD-11 complex PTSD, so applying the findings to complex trauma is an inference.
^p26164054-caution-cptsd
