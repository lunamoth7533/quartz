# Comparative efficacy and acceptability of 21 antidepressant drugs for the acute treatment of adults with major depressive disorder: a systematic review and network meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Cipriani A, Furukawa TA, et al. (18 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29477251/)

**Published.** 2018 in Lancet (London, England) (first online 2018-02-21).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32802-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32802-7) · PMID [29477251](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29477251/) · PMCID [PMC5889788](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5889788/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

All 21 antidepressants produced more responders than placebo in adult major depression, with odds ratios from about 1.4 (reboxetine) to about 2.1 (amitriptyline).
^p29477251-placebo

On dropout for any reason, only agomelatine and fluoxetine did better than placebo and clomipramine did worse; head-to-head, agomelatine, vortioxetine and several SSRIs were among the more tolerable, while the highest dropout rates included the tricyclics amitriptyline and clomipramine, the SNRIs duloxetine and venlafaxine, and trazodone.
^p29477251-acceptability

Between-drug differences shrank once placebo-controlled trials entered the network, and most comparative estimates carried wide credible intervals.
^p29477251-comparisons

## Scope as reported

Network meta-analysis of published and unpublished double-blind trials searched to January 2016, covering acute treatment only; trials with 20% or more participants with bipolar, psychotic or treatment-resistant depression were excluded. Of 522 trials, 9% were rated at high and 73% at moderate risk of bias, and certainty ranged from moderate to very low.
^p29477251-limit

Drugs named in the abstract include six SSRIs (citalopram, escitalopram, fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, paroxetine, sertraline), the SNRIs duloxetine and venlafaxine, the tricyclics amitriptyline and clomipramine, and agomelatine, mirtazapine, reboxetine, trazodone and vortioxetine.
^p29477251-drugs

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The ranking summarises average acute effects in trial populations that left out bipolar, psychotic and treatment-resistant depression, so it does not transfer to those groups or to long-term use, and it is not a guide to choosing a drug for a person.
^p29477251-caution-ranking
