# Efficacy and Safety of Xanomeline-Trospium Chloride in Schizophrenia: A Randomized Clinical Trial.

**Published.** 2024 in JAMA psychiatry.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Xanomeline-trospium, which pairs an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist without direct D2 blocking activity with a peripheral muscarinic antagonist, reduced PANSS total scores more than placebo at week 5 (effect size about 0.6).
^p38691387-efficacy

The most common adverse events were gastrointestinal (nausea, dyspepsia, vomiting, constipation), while extrapyramidal symptoms, weight gain, somnolence and adverse-event discontinuation were similar to placebo.
^p38691387-safety

## Scope as reported

Five-week inpatient trial in people with acute psychosis; the authors present the results as consistent with the earlier EMERGENT-1 and EMERGENT-2 trials.
^p38691387-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A five-week placebo-controlled trial in acutely ill inpatients shows short-term efficacy for a non-D2 mechanism; it does not compare the combination with dopamine-blocking antipsychotics or show long-term effects.
