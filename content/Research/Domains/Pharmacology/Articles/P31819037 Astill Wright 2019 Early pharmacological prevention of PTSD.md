## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Among drugs started within three months of trauma, only hydrocortisone in adults outperformed placebo, in three small trials of people with severe physical illness.
^p31819037-hydrocortisone

Propranolol, gabapentin, oxytocin, escitalopram, imipramine, dexamethasone, chloral hydrate and two fish-oil preparations showed no significant effect.
^p31819037-null

Most trials were of low methodological quality, and the authors conclude that evidence is lacking that any drug other than hydrocortisone is likely to help.
^p31819037-quality

## Scope as reported

Review using Cochrane methods of randomised trials of early pharmacotherapy in children and adults, pooling risk ratios for PTSD presence and standardised mean differences for severity; the hydrocortisone dosing questions it raises are not carried into this library.
^p31819037-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A null pooled result from a few low-quality trials cannot rule out a modest preventive effect of propranolol; it shows only that none has been demonstrated.
^p31819037-caution-power

Prevention trials test drugs given soon after trauma, which is a different question from treating established or complex PTSD.
^p31819037-caution-question
