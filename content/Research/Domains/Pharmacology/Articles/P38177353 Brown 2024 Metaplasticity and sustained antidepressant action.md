# Targeting metaplasticity mechanisms to promote sustained antidepressant actions.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review notes that ketamine and esketamine can produce rapid antidepressant effects in treatment-resistant depression that persist after the drug has been cleared.
^p38177353-rapid

It argues that pharmacologically diverse rapid-acting compounds, despite different initial targets, converge on shared downstream mediators that strengthen excitatory synapses and change the later capacity for synaptic plasticity, a process called metaplasticity.
^p38177353-convergence

The authors propose metaplastic mechanisms as a target for future treatments.
^p38177353-proposal

## Scope as reported

Narrative review of human and preclinical work; the initiating mechanisms discussed include NMDA receptor subtype effects, metabotropic glutamate 2/3 receptor antagonism, AMPA receptor potentiation and serotonin receptor activation by psychedelics.
^p38177353-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The convergence account is a synthesis of largely preclinical mechanism work about rapid-acting compounds; it does not show that conventional monoamine antidepressants work the same way, and it does not predict individual response.
^p38177353-caution-scope
