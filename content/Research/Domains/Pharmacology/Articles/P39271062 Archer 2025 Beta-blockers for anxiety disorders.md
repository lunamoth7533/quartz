# Beta-blockers for the treatment of anxiety disorders: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Archer C, Wiles N, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39271062/)

**Published.** 2025 in Journal of affective disorders (first online 2024-09-11).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2024.09.068](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.09.068) · PMID [39271062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39271062/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Pooled analyses showed no benefit of beta-blockers over placebo or over benzodiazepines for social phobia or for panic disorder with or without agoraphobia.
^p39271062-null

Beta-blocker prescribing for anxiety rose substantially between 2003 and 2018 without clinical guidance, and an earlier review of the beta-blocker propranolol had judged the evidence insufficient.
^p39271062-prescribing

## Scope as reported

Ten included studies, five pooled (179 participants); many were small and incompletely reported, their risk of bias was high or unclear, and the authors call for a large randomised trial.
^p39271062-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A null pooled result from 179 participants in small, biased studies is absence of evidence of benefit rather than evidence of no benefit, and situational anxiety outside the diagnoses pooled was not assessed.
^p39271062-caution-absence
