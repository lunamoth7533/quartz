# Pharmacogenomic Testing and Depressive Symptom Remission: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Prospective, Controlled Clinical Trials.

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Across 13 trials, patients whose antidepressant treatment was guided by pharmacogenomic testing were more likely to reach remission than those treated without it (risk ratio 1.41); the ratio was 1.46 in randomised trials and not significant in open-label ones.
^p36111494-remission

The authors describe the gain as modest and attribute remaining uncertainty to differences in which genes and alleles the tests covered and in the prescribing advice attached to them.
^p36111494-modest

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis of 13 prospective controlled trials published in English up to July 2022 (4767 patients), with risk-of-bias assessment and random-effects pooling.
^p36111494-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Clinicians in the guided arm necessarily see the test report, so trials cannot fully blind them; part of the effect may reflect closer medication review rather than genotype information, and a relative gain of this size changes the outcome for a minority of those tested.
^p36111494-caution-blinding

This meta-analysis and the CPIC antidepressant guideline in this library share an author (Bousman CA), so they are related rather than independent lines of evidence.
^p36111494-caution-authorship
