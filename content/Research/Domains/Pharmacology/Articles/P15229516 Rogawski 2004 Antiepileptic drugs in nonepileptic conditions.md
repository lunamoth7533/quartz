## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The drugs are commonly used beyond epilepsy, for mood disorders, migraine, chronic neuropathic pain, neuromuscular syndromes and schizophrenia.
^p15229516-uses

In many of these conditions they are thought to act as in epilepsy, altering nerve or muscle excitability through sodium and calcium channels or by strengthening GABA-A inhibition.
^p15229516-mechanism

In neuropathic pain, use-dependent sodium-channel block may selectively damp abnormally fast firing, and gabapentin binds a calcium-channel subunit that injured sensory neurons overexpress.
^p15229516-pain

Effects on signalling that governs plasticity and cell survival may help explain why benefit is delayed in some neuropsychiatric uses, including bipolar disorder.
^p15229516-delayed

## Scope as reported

Narrative review from 2004 concentrating on mechanisms, especially in neuropathic pain; it does not grade clinical efficacy for any nonepileptic use.
^p15229516-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

That a drug controls seizures and also helps a mood or pain condition does not show that the same action is responsible in both; the shared-mechanism account is a hypothesis for the psychiatric uses.
^p15229516-caution-transfer

Written in 2004, before several newer drugs and before phase-specific bipolar evidence matured; its list of uses is historical context, not current guidance.
^p15229516-caution-age
