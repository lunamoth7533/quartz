## Reported findings (checked abstract)

For chronic insomnia at any adult age, cognitive-behavioural therapy delivered in person or digitally comes first, with medication offered when it does not help enough.
^p38016484-cbti

Benzodiazepines, benzodiazepine receptor agonists, daridorexant and low-dose sedating antidepressants are endorsed for short-term use of up to four weeks, and orexin receptor antagonists for up to three months, sometimes longer.
^p38016484-shortterm

Prolonged-release melatonin may be used for up to three months from age 55, whereas antihistamines, antipsychotics, fast-release melatonin, ramelteon and herbal remedies are not recommended.
^p38016484-notrecommended

## Scope as reported

Update of the 2017 European guideline with recommendations graded A to C on diagnosis and treatment; the abstract states recommendations, not the effect sizes behind them.
^p38016484-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Grades describe typical adults with insomnia disorder; the abstract does not address children or circadian rhythm disorders, where melatonin plays a different role, and a grade is not a recommendation for a particular person.
^p38016484-caution-scope
