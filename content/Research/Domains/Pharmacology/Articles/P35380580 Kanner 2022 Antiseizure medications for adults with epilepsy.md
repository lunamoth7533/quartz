## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Of 26 antiseizure drugs approved in the United States, 24 have similar efficacy in focal epilepsy and 9 in generalised epilepsy, so selection turns on seizure and epilepsy type, syndrome, age, sex, co-occurring conditions and interactions.
^p35380580-options

About 60-70% of patients become seizure-free, and a quarter to a half also live with psychiatric, cognitive, neurological or medical conditions, including mood, anxiety and attention disorders and migraine.
^p35380580-outcome

Enzyme-inducing drugs such as carbamazepine and phenytoin speed the metabolism of other medicines, raise blood lipids and can promote bone loss.
^p35380580-inducers

## Scope as reported

Narrative clinical review for general medical readers that reports no new data; its drug-by-drug selection advice is not carried into this library.
^p35380580-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Similar average efficacy across many drugs moves the choice onto adverse effects, interactions and co-occurring conditions; it does not mean that every drug will work equally well for a given person.
^p35380580-caution-equivalence

Written for adults with epilepsy in the United States; approval counts change over time, and children and pregnancy have their own evidence bases.
^p35380580-caution-scope
