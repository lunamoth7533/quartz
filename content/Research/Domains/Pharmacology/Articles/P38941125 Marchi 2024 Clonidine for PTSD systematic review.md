# Clonidine for post-traumatic stress disorder: a systematic review of the current evidence.

**Authors.** Marchi M, Grenzi P, Boks MP · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38941125/)

**Published.** 2024 in European journal of psychotraumatology (first online 2024-06-28).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1080/20008066.2024.2366049](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008066.2024.2366049) · PMID [38941125](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38941125/) · PMCID [PMC11216257](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11216257/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Ten reports with 569 adults were found, mostly case reports and observational studies with one small crossover randomised trial; the quality of evidence was low to very low, with marked heterogeneity and low power.
^p38941125-evidence

Many reports described better sleep, fewer nightmares and milder PTSD symptoms on clonidine, but pooling the two studies that compared it with prazosin or terazosin on nightmares showed no difference.
^p38941125-effects

The authors describe clonidine as a centrally acting anti-adrenergic drug and argue that hyperarousal and sleep problems in PTSD reflect adrenergic involvement, which makes anti-adrenergic drugs a rational target.
^p38941125-framing

## Scope as reported

Searches of five databases and a trial registry to April 2023; narrative synthesis, with meta-analysis possible for two studies only; the dose information it reports is not reproduced here.
^p38941125-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

No difference in two small studies is not evidence that clonidine matches prazosin; the comparison was neither designed nor powered to show equivalence.
^p38941125-caution-equivalence
