## Reported findings (checked abstract)

On clinicians' ratings at 12 weeks, all drugs beat placebo in children and adolescents; on teachers' ratings only methylphenidate and modafinil did; in adults, amphetamines, methylphenidate, bupropion and atomoxetine beat placebo, while modafinil did not.
^p30097390-efficacy

Amphetamines were less well tolerated than placebo in both age groups, guanfacine only in children, and atomoxetine, methylphenidate and modafinil only in adults; head-to-head comparisons favoured amphetamines.
^p30097390-tolerability

The authors found insufficient data at 26 and 52 weeks and call for urgent research on long-term effects.
^p30097390-longterm

## Scope as reported

Short-term randomized evidence; most indirect comparisons carried low or very low confidence, while some direct comparisons were high or moderate.
^p30097390-limit

## Clarification: what the tolerability block does and does not establish (staged 2026-09-25)

This append preserves the original block above, which ends with an unspecific statement that head-to-head
comparisons favoured amphetamines. The indexed primary abstract assigns the head-to-head differences to
clinician-rated efficacy, comparing amphetamines with modafinil, atomoxetine and methylphenidate; it does not
establish a head-to-head tolerability advantage. Read the earlier clauses about adverse-event discontinuation
versus placebo as the tolerability finding, and treat the final clause as an efficacy statement.
