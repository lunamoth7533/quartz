## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Endogenous melatonin is a hormone made and released mainly by the pineal gland, and it regulates sleep-wake timing through actions involving the suprachiasmatic nucleus and the retina.
^p30521244-source

Its best-known role, promoting sleep and damping wake-promoting signals, is attributed to MT1 and MT2 receptors.
^p30521244-receptors

In the United States melatonin has no FDA-approved indication; synthetic melatonin sold as a dietary supplement mimics the hormone's regulatory actions.
^p30521244-status

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The chapter joins two roles that trials measure differently - shifting the timing of the body clock and promoting sleep - so a claim about melatonin should say which one it tests.
^p30521244-caution-roles

'Not approved' describes the United States only and is not a statement about efficacy; regulatory status differs between countries.
