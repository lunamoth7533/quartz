# Comparative Tolerability of Dopamine D2/3 Receptor Partial Agonists for Schizophrenia.

**Published.** 2020 in CNS drugs.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1007/s40263-020-00718-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40263-020-00718-4) · PMID [32246399](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32246399/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The three drugs are set apart from other second-generation antipsychotics by partial agonism at D2 and D3 receptors; brexpiprazole has weaker intrinsic D2 activity, and cariprazine the strongest D3 affinity and longest half-life.
^p32246399-pharmacology

Akathisia, mostly mild to moderate and early in treatment, is the main adverse effect, probably least common with brexpiprazole and most common with cariprazine.
^p32246399-akathisia

Sedation and prolactin rise are uncommon; brexpiprazole carries a moderate weight-gain risk while aripiprazole and cariprazine carry a low one, and aripiprazole has been linked to gambling and other impulse-control problems.
^p32246399-profile

## Scope as reported

Narrative review without pooled analysis; the authors state that firm comparisons between the three drugs need head-to-head trials, and that diabetes and tardive dyskinesia risks are unknown though probably low.
^p32246399-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The link with impulse-control problems is a safety signal; the abstract gives no frequency, so the size of the risk cannot be read from it.
^p32246399-caution-impulse

The closing suggestion that these drugs be first-line options rests on tolerability alone and is the authors' judgement, not a comparative-effectiveness finding.
^p32246399-caution-firstline
