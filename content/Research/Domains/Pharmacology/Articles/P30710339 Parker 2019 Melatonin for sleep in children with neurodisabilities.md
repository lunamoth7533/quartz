# Oral melatonin for non-respiratory sleep disturbance in children with neurodisabilities: systematic review and meta-analyses.

**Authors.** Parker A, Beresford B, et al. (11 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30710339/)

**Published.** 2019 in Developmental medicine and child neurology (first online 2019-02-01).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/dmcn.14157](https://doi.org/10.1111/dmcn.14157) · PMID [30710339](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30710339/) · PMCID [PMC6617775](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6617775/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

All 13 eligible trials tested oral melatonin; pooled, it lengthened diary-reported total sleep by about half an hour against placebo (29.6 minutes), with very high heterogeneity.
^p30710339-sleep

All trials but one carried a high or unclear risk of bias; in the one low-risk trial the gain was smaller and significant only after adjusting for baseline sleep.
^p30710339-bias

Melatonin was well tolerated, and the authors suggest benefit may be greatest in autistic children while urging caution about that observation.
^p30710339-safety

## Scope as reported

Search of 16 databases, grey literature and reference lists to February 2017; the review sought any drug treatment but found only melatonin trials, and total sleep time came mainly from sleep diaries.
^p30710339-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Sleep diaries are usually kept by parents who may expect benefit, and an average half-hour gain amid 97% heterogeneity leaves wide uncertainty about which children benefit.
^p30710339-caution-diary

'Neurodisability' is a broad grouping, so the pooled estimate may hide differences between diagnoses; the autism signal is one the authors themselves call tentative.
^p30710339-caution-grouping
