# Z-drugs and risk for falls and fractures in older adults-a systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Treves N, Perlman A, et al. (5 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29077902/)

**Published.** 2018 in Age and ageing.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1093/ageing/afx167](https://doi.org/10.1093/ageing/afx167) · PMID [29077902](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29077902/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Z-drug use was associated with higher odds of fracture (OR 1.63), with considerable heterogeneity between studies.
^p29077902-fractures

The odds of falls were about doubled on the point estimate (OR 2.40) but not significantly, and zolpidem exposure was associated with more injuries (OR 2.05) without heterogeneity.
^p29077902-falls

Zolpidem, zopiclone, eszopiclone and zaleplon are used for insomnia as alternatives to benzodiazepines, and the review set out to test the common perception that they are safer.
^p29077902-motive

## Scope as reported

Meta-analysis of observational studies only, open to confounding; results held in sensitivity analyses limited to higher-quality studies, to control groups with insomnia and to individual Z-drugs.
^p29077902-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

People prescribed hypnotics differ from non-users in frailty, illness and sleep itself, and poor sleep can raise fall risk on its own; the insomnia-control analysis narrows but does not remove that explanation.
^p29077902-caution-confounding

The analysis compares Z-drug users with non-users, not with benzodiazepine users, so it undercuts the idea that Z-drugs are free of this risk without ranking them against benzodiazepines.
^p29077902-caution-comparison
