# Antidepressants for pain management in adults with chronic pain: a network meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Birkinshaw H, Friedrich CM, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37160297/)

**Published.** 2023 in The Cochrane database of systematic reviews (first online 2023-05-10).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/14651858.CD014682.pub2](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD014682.pub2) · PMID [37160297](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37160297/) · PMCID [PMC10169288](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10169288/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Duloxetine ranked first on every efficacy outcome, with moderate- to high-certainty evidence of a small to moderate benefit for substantial pain relief and pain intensity; milnacipran ranked next, on less certain evidence.
^p37160297-duloxetine

For the other antidepressants studied, evidence was of low certainty and too thin for firm conclusions.
^p37160297-others

Safety evidence was very low certainty for every drug, no drug had reliable long-term evidence, and because trials excluded people with low mood the effect in people with both pain and depression is unknown.
^p37160297-gaps

## Scope as reported

Bayesian network and pairwise meta-analyses of trials searched to January 2022; trials lasted about ten weeks on average and mostly reported short-term outcomes; certainty was rated with CINeMA or GRADE.
^p37160297-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Because the trials enrolled people without clinically low mood, a pain benefit here says nothing about antidepressant action in depression, and the reverse; the two uses of the same drug need separate evidence.
^p37160297-caution-mood
