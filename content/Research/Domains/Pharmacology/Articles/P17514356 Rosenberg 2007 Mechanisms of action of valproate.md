## Reported findings (checked abstract)

After four decades of clinical use, how valproate works in epilepsy, bipolar disorder and migraine remains incompletely understood.
^p17514356-unknown

Besides strengthening GABA-mediated transmission, valproate acts on Wnt/beta-catenin and ERK signalling and on inositol and arachidonate metabolism, though how much these effects matter clinically is uncertain.
^p17514356-actions

It alters the expression of many genes, via increased AP-1 binding to DNA and by directly inhibiting histone deacetylases, and the author proposes that fast biochemical and slower genomic effects together underlie all three uses.
^p17514356-genomic

## Scope as reported

Single-author narrative review from 2007 drawing mainly on cellular and animal work; it does not test which action produces mood stabilisation.
^p17514356-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Two of the pathways named here, inositol metabolism and Wnt/beta-catenin signalling (which runs through GSK3), also feature in lithium hypotheses, so shared targets are a candidate explanation for the drugs' overlapping uses, not a demonstrated one.
^p17514356-caution-lithium

The review predates later genomic and imaging work, so its list of candidate actions is a snapshot rather than a current consensus.
^p17514356-caution-age
