# Effect of Pharmacogenomic Testing for Drug-Gene Interactions on Medication Selection and Remission of Symptoms in Major Depressive Disorder: The PRIME Care Randomized Clinical Trial.

**Authors.** Oslin DW, Lynch KG, et al. (57 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819423/)

**Published.** 2022 in JAMA.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jama.2022.9805](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.9805) · PMID [35819423](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819423/) · PMCID [PMC9277497](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9277497/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

With commercial test results in hand, clinicians prescribed fewer drugs with a predicted drug-gene interaction: 59.3% of prescriptions had none, against 25.7% under usual care.
^p35819423-prescribing

Remission over 24 weeks was modestly more likely with test-guided care (odds ratio 1.28; risk difference 2.8 percentage points), but the difference at week 24 itself was not significant.
^p35819423-remission

The authors call the effect on remission small and not persistent.
^p35819423-summary

## Scope as reported

Pragmatic trial with blinded raters, remission defined by the PHQ-9, and exclusion of active substance use disorder, mania and psychosis; a quarter of patients were women, and the usual-care group received results after 24 weeks.
^p35819423-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Testing clearly changed what was prescribed; how much that changes outcomes is the harder question, and a risk difference of about three points means few patients do better than they would under usual care.
^p35819423-caution-outcome

Veterans, mostly men, in one health system; transfer to other settings, and to bipolar or ADHD medicines, is untested here.
^p35819423-caution-population
