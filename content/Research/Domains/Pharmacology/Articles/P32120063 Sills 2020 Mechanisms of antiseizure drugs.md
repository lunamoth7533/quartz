## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Mechanisms fall into four broad families: modulating voltage-gated sodium channels (carbamazepine, lamotrigine, phenytoin), calcium channels (ethosuximide) or potassium channels (retigabine); enhancing GABA-mediated inhibition (tiagabine, vigabatrin, benzodiazepines); dampening glutamate-mediated excitation (perampanel); and acting presynaptically on transmitter release (gabapentin, pregabalin, levetiracetam, brivaracetam).
^p32120063-families

The drugs prevent seizures but have no demonstrated disease-modifying effect: they act on general excitability to curb the generation and spread of hyperexcitability rather than correcting the defect behind a given epilepsy, which is usually unknown.
^p32120063-symptomatic

## Scope as reported

Narrative review in a special issue on new epilepsy therapies; it classifies mechanisms rather than comparing clinical efficacy, and anticipates small-molecule, antisense and gene therapies aimed at the causes of some rare genetic epilepsies.
^p32120063-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Placing a drug in one family names its best-characterised action; some drugs act on several targets, and family membership does not predict effectiveness for a given seizure type or person.
