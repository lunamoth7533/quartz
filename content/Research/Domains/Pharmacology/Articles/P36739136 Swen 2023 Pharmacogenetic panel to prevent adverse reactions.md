# A 12-gene pharmacogenetic panel to prevent adverse drug reactions: an open-label, multicentre, controlled, cluster-randomised crossover implementation study.

**Authors.** Swen JJ, van der Wouden CH, et al. (41 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36739136/)

**Published.** 2023 in Lancet (London, England).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/S0140-6736(22)01841-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(22)01841-4) · PMID [36739136](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36739136/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Before a first prescription, people were tested for 50 variants across 12 genes; when a result was actionable, treatment followed the working group's recommendations.
^p36739136-design

Clinically relevant adverse drug reactions within 12 weeks fell from 27.7% to 21.0% among people with an actionable result (odds ratio 0.70), and from 28.6% to 21.5% across all participants.
^p36739136-adr

## Scope as reported

Open-label implementation study in which sites crossed between standard and genotype-guided care; 97.7% of participants reported European, Mediterranean or Middle Eastern ethnicity, and more were lost to follow-up in the genotyped group (11.0%) than among controls (7.9%).
^p36739136-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Patients and clinicians knew which care they received, and adverse reactions depend partly on reporting, so expectation may have shaped the result; the panel spanned many drug classes rather than psychiatric drugs specifically.
^p36739136-caution-openlabel

With nearly all participants of European or neighbouring ancestry, the share of people with an actionable result, and so the benefit, may differ elsewhere.
^p36739136-caution-ancestry
