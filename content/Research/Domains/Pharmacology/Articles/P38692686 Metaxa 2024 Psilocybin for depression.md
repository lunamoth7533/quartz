# Efficacy of psilocybin for treating symptoms of depression: systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Metaxa AM, Clarke M (2 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38692686/)

**Published.** 2024 in BMJ (Clinical research ed.) (first online 2024-05-01).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1136/bmj-2023-078084](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078084) · PMID [38692686](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38692686/) · PMCID [PMC11062320](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11062320/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Psilocybin given as a standalone treatment improved depression scores more than comparators, with a pooled Hedges' g of 0.66 and moderate-certainty evidence.
^p38692686-effect

Effects were larger in secondary depression, on self-report scales, in older participants and in people who had used psychedelics before.
^p38692686-moderators

The authors call for research on expectancy, moderating factors and treatment delivery before the size of the effect can be interpreted.
^p38692686-expectancy

## Scope as reported

Random-effects meta-analysis of randomised trials comparing psilocybin with placebo or non-psychoactive drugs, with psychotherapy allowed only when both arms received it; every study had a moderate risk of bias.
^p38692686-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Larger effects on self-report scales and in people with previous psychedelic use are what unblinding and expectancy would predict, so the pooled effect may overstate the drug's specific contribution.
^p38692686-caution-unblinding
