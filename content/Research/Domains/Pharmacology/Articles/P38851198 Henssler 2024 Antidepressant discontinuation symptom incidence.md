# Incidence of antidepressant discontinuation symptoms: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Henssler J, Schmidt Y, et al. (6 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38851198/)

**Published.** 2024 in The lancet. Psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/S2215-0366(24)00133-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(24)00133-0) · PMID [38851198](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38851198/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

At least one discontinuation symptom occurred in about 31% of patients after stopping an antidepressant and in about 17% after stopping placebo.
^p38851198-incidence

Allowing for these non-specific effects, the authors estimate that about one in six to seven people who stop an antidepressant have discontinuation symptoms; severe symptoms occurred in about 3%, against under 1% after placebo.
^p38851198-severe

Frequency and severity differed between drugs, and heterogeneity was substantial.
^p38851198-drugs

## Scope as reported

Pooled incidence from 79 randomised and observational studies (searched to October 2022) of people with mental, behavioural or neurodevelopmental disorders; the authors note that residual or re-emerging symptoms of the underlying condition complicate interpretation.
^p38851198-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Studies differ in how long people had taken the drug and how it was stopped, so the pooled incidence is an average that cannot predict one person's experience; the placebo-group rate shows how much of what follows stopping is non-specific.
^p38851198-caution-incidence
