## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The authors reject the split into first-generation (typical) and second-generation (atypical) drugs as neither valid nor useful, and propose naming drugs by the Neuroscience-based Nomenclature instead.
^p39350614-classification

At the time of writing, every available antipsychotic either blocked dopamine receptors or acted as a dopamine partial agonist; the drugs reduce psychosis across diagnoses and do more than sedate.
^p39350614-mechanism

In schizophrenia, relapse-prevention effects exceed acute effects; objectively measured side-effect differences such as weight gain are clearer than rating-scale efficacy differences, and the rise in trial placebo response over decades is unexplained.
^p39350614-effects

## Scope as reported

A narrative review tracing the class from chlorpromazine, the first antipsychotic, through its naming, uses, pharmacology, benefits, harms and practical prescribing; it presents no new data, and its dosing, initiation and switching material is not carried into this library.
^p39350614-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The statement that every available antipsychotic acts on dopamine receptors describes the market when the review was written: a muscarinic combination without direct D2 blockade was approved in the United States in late September 2024, days before the issue date.
^p39350614-caution-date

Rejecting the generation labels is the authors' position; the labels remain in regulatory and clinical use, so this library keeps them as navigation terms while making mechanism claims drug by drug.
^p39350614-caution-labels
