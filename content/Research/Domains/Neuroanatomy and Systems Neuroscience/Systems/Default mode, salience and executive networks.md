# Default mode, salience and executive networks

## Definition

These are three large-scale sets of brain regions whose activity rises and falls together. The default mode network is tied to thinking about oneself and others, recalling personal events, stored meaning and mind wandering; the salience network, anchored in anterior insula and anterior cingulate cortex, picks out the most relevant internal and external events; and an executive network spanning frontal and parietal cortex supports top-down control, including working memory. [[P37167968 Menon 2023 Twenty years of the default mode network#^p37167968-functions|Menon 2023]] [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-salience|Menon 2010]] [[P34881727 Jones 2021 Executive dysfunction and the prefrontal cortex#^p34881727-networks|Jones 2021]] The triple network model proposes that aberrant mapping of salience, and difficulty engaging and disengaging these networks, underlie cognitive dysfunction across psychiatric and neurological disorders. [[P21908230 Menon 2011 Triple network model of psychopathology#^p21908230-model|Menon 2011]] [[P21908230 Menon 2011 Triple network model of psychopathology#^p21908230-disorders|Menon 2011]]

This note covers the three named networks and the clinical model built on them; [[Association cortex and networks]] covers the cortex they are drawn from, and [[Network and connectome models]] the general graph methods used for any brain network.

## How it works

**Three networks, three roles.** A two-decade review proposes that the default mode network weaves self-reference, memory and meaning into an internal narrative, a proposal to test rather than a demonstrated mechanism. [[P37167968 Menon 2023 Twenty years of the default mode network#^p37167968-narrative|Menon 2023]] [[P37167968 Menon 2023 Twenty years of the default mode network#^p37167968-caution-theory|Appraisal: Menon 2023]] The salience network detects salient events and marks them for further processing, and executive functions depend on control distributed across a parietal-temporal-frontal system rather than on frontal cortex alone. [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-switching|Menon 2010]] [[P34881727 Jones 2021 Executive dysfunction and the prefrontal cortex#^p34881727-networks|Jones 2021]]

**The switching idea.** In the model, the anterior insula sits between networks for outward-directed attention and for self-related thought, and after a salient event it shifts activity towards the networks that supply attention and working memory. [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-hub|Menon 2010]] [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-switching|Menon 2010]] The switching role is inferred mainly from correlational imaging, and a model built to fit many disorders explains what they share rather than what distinguishes them. [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-caution-model|Appraisal: Menon 2010]] [[P21908230 Menon 2011 Triple network model of psychopathology#^p21908230-caution-scope|Appraisal: Menon 2011]]

**How the networks are measured.** Functional MRI tracks an activity-related haemodynamic signal over time; resting-state studies measure how closely that signal rises and falls together between regions when no task is set. [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-fmri|Brain imaging]] Networks are defined either by placing seeds and assigning them to networks from an a priori atlas, or by clustering connectivity measures from the data. [[P32259712 Wang 2020 Network dysfunction across bipolar mood states#^p32259712-limit|Wang 2020]] [[P36912839 Vandewouw 2023 Replicable transdiagnostic brain subgroups#^p36912839-subgroups|Vandewouw 2023]] Quality depends on design: prediction accuracy rises with total scan time (people multiplied by minutes scanned), longitudinal designs and wider sampling of the studied variable raise replicability, and analytic flexibility with low power exaggerates published effects. [[P40670782 Ooi 2025 Longer scans boost BWAS prediction#^p40670782-model|Ooi 2025]] [[P39604734 Kang 2024 Study design and BWAS replicability#^p39604734-design|Kang 2024]] [[P39604734 Kang 2024 Study design and BWAS replicability#^p39604734-sampling|Kang 2024]] [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]] [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-power|Neuroimaging reproducibility]]

**ADHD.** In a mega-analysis pooling several large cohorts, children with ADHD showed weaker anticorrelation between the default mode network and the salience/ventral attention, somatomotor and dorsal attention networks, and ADHD traits showed the same pattern, but the effects were small (Cohen's d about 0.14-0.17). [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-limit|Norman 2023]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-anticorrelation|Norman 2023]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-traits|Norman 2023]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-size|Norman 2023]] Resting-state data cannot show that weaker segregation causes attentional lapses. [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-caution-effect|Appraisal: Norman 2023]] A second mega-analysis, of 1,696 youths with ADHD and 6,737 controls, found stronger resting coupling of the striatum with fronto-insular and supplementary motor cortex, with the largest effect near d = 0.15. [[P38476041 Norman 2024 Subcortico-cortical connectivity in ADHD#^p38476041-limit|Norman 2024]] [[P38476041 Norman 2024 Subcortico-cortical connectivity in ADHD#^p38476041-striatal|Norman 2024]] [[P38476041 Norman 2024 Subcortico-cortical connectivity in ADHD#^p38476041-effect|Norman 2024]] Connectivity-based subgroups in young people with neurodevelopmental conditions replicated across two data sets but tracked intelligence and hyperactivity-impulsivity rather than diagnosis. [[P36912839 Vandewouw 2023 Replicable transdiagnostic brain subgroups#^p36912839-subgroups|Vandewouw 2023]] [[P36912839 Vandewouw 2023 Replicable transdiagnostic brain subgroups#^p36912839-transdiagnostic|Vandewouw 2023]]

**Bipolar disorder.** A meta-analysis of 39 resting-state data sets found lower connectivity within an affective network and within the default mode network during acute mood episodes; in remission the affective reduction was absent and default mode connectivity was higher than in controls. [[P32259712 Wang 2020 Network dysfunction across bipolar mood states#^p32259712-limit|Wang 2020]] [[P32259712 Wang 2020 Network dysfunction across bipolar mood states#^p32259712-acute|Wang 2020]] [[P32259712 Wang 2020 Network dysfunction across bipolar mood states#^p32259712-remitted|Wang 2020]] A finding that reverses with mood state can cancel out in mixed samples, and the states were compared across studies rather than within people. [[P32259712 Wang 2020 Network dysfunction across bipolar mood states#^p32259712-caution-state|Appraisal: Wang 2020]]

**Autism.** Autism is among the conditions in which the triple network model describes problems engaging and disengaging networks. [[P21908230 Menon 2011 Triple network model of psychopathology#^p21908230-disorders|Menon 2011]] Across six diagnoses including autism, ADHD and bipolar disorder, any given region's grey-matter deviation was shared by fewer than 7% of people with the same diagnosis, yet the deviations converged on common networks, with the salience-ventral attention system involved across diagnoses. [[P37580620 Segal 2023 Heterogeneity of brain abnormalities#^p37580620-regional|Segal 2023]] [[P37580620 Segal 2023 Heterogeneity of brain abnormalities#^p37580620-circuits|Segal 2023]] [[P37580620 Segal 2023 Heterogeneity of brain abnormalities#^p37580620-diagnoses|Segal 2023]]

**PTSD.** In adult resting-state studies, weaker connectivity within the default mode network, and between an affective network and the default mode network, was tied to trauma exposure rather than to PTSD, while stronger coupling of the somatomotor network with both was specific to PTSD. [[P34688728 Bao 2021 Large-scale networks in adult PTSD#^p34688728-trauma|Bao 2021]] [[P34688728 Bao 2021 Large-scale networks in adult PTSD#^p34688728-ptsd|Bao 2021]] A comparison against non-exposed controls therefore mixes trauma with disorder, and a decade review judges PTSD imaging not ready for clinical practice. [[P34688728 Bao 2021 Large-scale networks in adult PTSD#^p34688728-caution-controls|Appraisal: Bao 2021]] [[P38632413 Hinojosa 2024 PTSD neuroimaging progress#^p38632413-status|Hinojosa 2024]]

**Reading rule.** Ask which network definition was used (atlas seeds or data-driven clusters), how many minutes of data each person gave, which comparison group was chosen, what mood or clinical state the sample was in and how large the effect was; even a difference that survives them describes a group, not an individual. [[P40670782 Ooi 2025 Longer scans boost BWAS prediction#^p40670782-caution-prediction|Appraisal: Ooi 2025]] [[P37580620 Segal 2023 Heterogeneity of brain abnormalities#^p37580620-caution-average|Appraisal: Segal 2023]] [[P38476041 Norman 2024 Subcortico-cortical connectivity in ADHD#^p38476041-caution-effect|Appraisal: Norman 2024]]

## Evidence and status

What the three networks do is described in narrative and theoretical reviews, and the switching mechanism is a proposed model rather than the result of causal tests. [[P37167968 Menon 2023 Twenty years of the default mode network#^p37167968-limit|Menon 2023]] [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-limit|Menon 2010]] [[P21908230 Menon 2011 Triple network model of psychopathology#^p21908230-limit|Menon 2011]] Clinical findings are group-level, small, shared across diagnoses and sensitive to design, and none is ready to diagnose, subtype or choose treatment for an individual. [[P36912839 Vandewouw 2023 Replicable transdiagnostic brain subgroups#^p36912839-caution-subgroups|Appraisal: Vandewouw 2023]] [[P38632413 Hinojosa 2024 PTSD neuroimaging progress#^p38632413-caution-clinical|Appraisal: Hinojosa 2024]] [[P40670782 Ooi 2025 Longer scans boost BWAS prediction#^p40670782-caution-prediction|Appraisal: Ooi 2025]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether the anterior insula causes switches between networks or only changes alongside them. [[P20512370 Menon 2010 Salience network model of insula function#^p20512370-caution-model|Appraisal: Menon 2010]]
- Whether any network pattern is specific to one diagnosis, given convergence on shared systems across diagnoses. [[P37580620 Segal 2023 Heterogeneity of brain abnormalities#^p37580620-circuits|Segal 2023]]
- Whether bipolar state effects hold within the same people followed across episodes and remission. [[P32259712 Wang 2020 Network dysfunction across bipolar mood states#^p32259712-caution-state|Appraisal: Wang 2020]]
- Whether adult PTSD network findings apply to complex PTSD, which the sourced studies did not examine. [[P34688728 Bao 2021 Large-scale networks in adult PTSD#^p34688728-caution-controls|Appraisal: Bao 2021]]
- A resting-state meta-analysis focused on autism has not yet been sourced in this library.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Modelling and reanalysis across nine data sets (Nature).** Prediction from resting-state and task fMRI improved with total scan time, and 30-minute scans were the most cost-effective on average, which bears on how much data a network measure needs. [[P40670782 Ooi 2025 Longer scans boost BWAS prediction#^p40670782-model|Ooi 2025]] [[P40670782 Ooi 2025 Longer scans boost BWAS prediction#^p40670782-tradeoff|Ooi 2025]]
- **2024 · Meta-analysis and reanalysis of consortium MRI (Nature).** Longitudinal designs and wider sampling raised replicability, and modelling within-person and between-person change separately avoided losing effects. [[P39604734 Kang 2024 Study design and BWAS replicability#^p39604734-design|Kang 2024]] [[P39604734 Kang 2024 Study design and BWAS replicability#^p39604734-longitudinal|Kang 2024]]
- **2023 · Mega-analysis of resting-state fMRI (Neuropsychopharmacology).** Tests the default-mode account of ADHD directly: weaker segregation of the default mode network from task networks, found in diagnosed children and along ADHD traits, with small effects. [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-anticorrelation|Norman 2023]] [[P36100657 Norman 2023 Default-mode hypothesis of ADHD#^p36100657-size|Norman 2023]]
