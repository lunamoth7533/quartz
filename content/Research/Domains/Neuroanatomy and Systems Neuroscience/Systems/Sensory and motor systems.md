# Sensory and motor systems

## Definition

Sensory and motor systems are the input and output arms of the nervous system. Each is organised as a chain of processing stages rather than a single relay, and both converge on the thalamus and cortex.

## How it works

**Sensory pathways are parallel chains.** Each modality travels through a dedicated series of relay neurons with characteristic crossing points and thalamic relays before reaching cortex, and the modality determines the receptor type, the adaptation rate and the cortical target. The parallelism is functional: fine touch and pain are carried by different pathways precisely because they require different speeds and different central processing. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]] Somatosensory processing divides into a discriminative-touch and proprioceptive channel, whose three-neuron chain - primary afferent, second-order neuron crossing the midline, and thalamic neuron projecting to the postcentral gyrus - reaches the thalamus before cortex, and a separate pain and temperature channel with different receptors and dynamics. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] [[F103 Neuroscience Online somatosensory systems#^f103-division|Somatosensory Systems]] [[F111 Neuroscience Online pain principles#^f111-nociceptors|Pain Principles]]

**Pain is the pathway where parallelism pays off most visibly.** Nociceptors feed a channel that crosses early, ascends separately from discriminative touch, and is modulated at spinal and supraspinal levels before it reaches cortical targets. Pain and sensory processing are therefore modulated at several levels rather than being a fixed ascending signal, which is why attention and context change what is felt, why pain intensity can dissociate from injury severity, and why pain has both a sensory and an affective dimension in the clinical literature. The modulation finding is not a psychological gloss; it is built into the pathway's anatomy. [[F111 Neuroscience Online pain principles#^f111-nociceptors|Pain Principles]] [[F111 Neuroscience Online pain principles#^f111-transmission|Pain Principles]]

**Crossing matters.** Because most pathways cross, a deficit on one side usually indicates a lesion on the opposite side above the crossing point, which is one of the most useful localising rules in neurology. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]]

**Motor chain.** Motor output runs the other way: descending commands originate in cortex and brain stem and converge on lower motor neurons, and the exam distinguishes upper from lower motor neuron lesions by the pattern of tone, reflex and wasting changes. [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-descending|Motor Responses]] [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]]

**The descending motor systems differ in what they control.** Lateral systems project to distal limb circuitry controlling fine, fractionated movements; medial systems project to proximal and axial circuitry controlling posture and orientation. The split predicts the clinical patterns: lateral-system damage costs skilled hand use, medial-system damage costs balance and truncal control. [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-descending|Motor Responses]]

**Motor control is hierarchical and recursive.** Motor cortex and brainstem issue commands that are refined at the spinal level by reflex circuits and sensory feedback, while the basal ganglia modulate rather than generate movement, shaping selection, and the cerebellum adjusts and calibrates it. Because each level both receives and returns information, the system is a set of nested loops rather than a descending chain, and motor control becomes a division of labour that can be lesioned part by part. [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-descending|Motor Responses]] [[Basal ganglia]] [[Cerebellum and brainstem]] A motor deficit can therefore arise at the cortical, pyramidal, extrapyramidal, cerebellar or lower motor neuron level, which is why the exam's motor domain is interpreted jointly with coordination and reflex findings. [[F56 Neuroscience Online basal ganglia#^f56-pathways|Basal Ganglia]] [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-mapping|Motor Disorders]] [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-layers|Cerebellum]]

**Feedback is not optional.** Proprioceptive and cutaneous input reaches every level of the motor hierarchy, so a movement is continuously adjusted rather than executed open-loop. That is why deafferentation produces deficits in accuracy and timing, not paralysis - the command still exists, but its calibration does not. [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-reflex|Motor Responses]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-neuron|Nervous Tissue]]

**Reading rule.** Sensory and motor claims should specify the level (receptor, pathway, nucleus, cortical area, loop) and the modality or muscle group; descriptions that stay at the level of 'sensory' or 'motor' cannot be tested. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-limit|Central Processing]] [[F53 OpenStax Motor responses#^f53-limit|Motor Responses]]

## Evidence and status

Pathway organisation is well established from anatomy and clinical correlation; the simplified chain omits parallel channels, feedback and convergence. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-limit|Central Processing]]
