# Cerebellum and brainstem

## Definition

The cerebellum is a highly structured cortical structure attached to the brain stem, and the brain stem contains the cranial nerve nuclei, long ascending and descending tracts, and the core autonomic and arousal systems.

## How it works

**Cerebellar architecture.** The cerebellar cortex has a stereotyped three-layer organisation - molecular, Purkinje cell and granule cell layers - with a single output cell type, the Purkinje cell, whose single-cell-thick layer projects inhibitory output to the deep nuclei. There are two main input systems: mossy fibres relayed through granule cells, and climbing fibres that wrap Purkinje cells directly. Convergence is massive: each parallel fibre contacts many Purkinje cells and each Purkinje cell receives enormous input. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-layers|Cerebellum]] [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-circuit|Cerebellum]]

**The uniform microcircuit is the theoretical clue.** The cerebellum repeats the same three-layer module with the same two input systems across a huge number of very similar units. That regularity is why it is described as a computational structure rather than a collection of nuclei and why it became a model system for circuit computation, and it suggests a single computation applied to many domains rather than many specialisations in one structure. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-circuit|Cerebellum]]

**What it computes.** The classical function is error-based adjustment of ongoing movement: climbing-fibre input carries a teaching signal that adjusts Purkinje output and the gain of the circuit, which is why cerebellar learning is described as supervised rather than reinforcement-based. Climbing-fibre and parallel-fibre input support forms of plasticity that develop over days, so the cerebellum is treated as a learning structure as well as a coordinator. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-learning|Cerebellum]] [[P27069377 Schultz 2016 Reward prediction error#^p27069377-scope|Dopamine reward prediction error coding]] The same architecture serves eye movements, vestibular calibration, gait and, in imaging work, cognitive sequences, so the structure is best described by its computation rather than by a single output domain. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-circuit|Cerebellum]] [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-learning|Cerebellum]]

**Brainstem organisation.** The brainstem carries long ascending and descending tracts between cord and forebrain and contains the cranial nerve nuclei, respiratory and cardiovascular centres, and the nuclei of the ascending arousal system that generate and gate arousal and autonomic outflow; reflexes and autonomic integrations happen there before information reaches cortex. [[F05 OpenStax The central nervous system#^f05-parts|The Central Nervous System]] [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-brainstem|Central Processing]] [[F31 OpenStax The peripheral nervous system#^f31-cranial|The Peripheral Nervous System]] Central autonomic control originates in interconnected brain-stem and hypothalamic cell groups that also connect with somatic, endocrine and limbic circuitry. [[F63 Neuroscience Online central autonomic control#^f63-network|Central Control of the Autonomic Nervous System]]

**Brainstem lesions localise by combination.** The organising principle is vertical: functions are arranged along the neuraxis from spinal cord to midbrain rather than in one map. Because cranial nerve nuclei sit alongside long tracts in a small volume, small lesions produce combinations of cranial nerve, long-tract and autonomic findings, including crossed patterns - cranial nerve findings on one side, body findings on the other - whose combination identifies the level. That combination logic is the brainstem's signature contribution to neurological localisation, the clearest example in the domain where anatomical knowledge directly constrains clinical inference, and the reason brainstem anatomy is taught vertical-column-first. [[F05 OpenStax The central nervous system#^f05-parts|The Central Nervous System]] [[F112 Neuroscience Online motor disorders#^f112-extrapyramidal|Disorders of the Motor System]] [[Differential reasoning in neurology]]

**Two structures, one examination page.** The neurological exam tests coordination separately from motor strength and localises cranial nerve findings to brain-stem levels, so the two structures appear in the same examination domains while subserving unrelated functions. Introductory diagrams simplify: parallel channels, feedback and convergence are not represented. [[F33 OpenStax Overview of the neurological exam#^f33-domains|Overview of the Neurological Exam]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-limit|Central Processing]]

**Reading rule.** Cerebellar and brainstem claims should distinguish the structure's intrinsic computation from the function of the loop it participates in; a finding about one loop does not transfer to others. [[F57 Neuroscience Online cerebellum#^f57-limit|Cerebellum]]

## Evidence and status

Cerebellar microcircuitry is among the best-described in neuroscience, largely from animal physiology; brain-stem anatomy is established from clinical and anatomical correlation. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Anatomy of the Spinal Cord]]
