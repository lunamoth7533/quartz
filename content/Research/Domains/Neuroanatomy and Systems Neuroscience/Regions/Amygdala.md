# Amygdala

## Definition

The amygdala is a group of nuclei in the medial temporal lobe connected with cortical, thalamic, hypothalamic and brain-stem structures. Those connections explain why it is treated as a hub for emotional salience.

## How it works

**A hub, not a single-function centre.** The amygdala's connections with cortical, thalamic, hypothalamic and brain-stem structures make it a convergence point: sensory information arrives through thalamic and cortical routes, autonomic and endocrine outputs leave through hypothalamic and brain-stem targets, and the structure's contribution is the coordination between them. It is also not one nucleus - the basolateral group receives sensory input from cortex and thalamus while the central nucleus projects to the hypothalamic and brain-stem targets that generate autonomic and behavioural outputs - so lesions produce several kinds of change depending on which subregion is damaged, and every claim that "the amygdala does X" needs a pathway. [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-connections|Amygdala]] [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-limit|Amygdala]]

**Stimulation and lesion evidence.** Stimulation produces intense emotional responses including fear and aggression, and irritative lesions in temporal-lobe epilepsy can produce panic-like episodes - observational evidence that motivated the structure's association with emotion without identifying a mechanism; the conditioning paradigm supplied the controlled version of the question. [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-emotion|Amygdala]]

**Fear learning and extinction.** Fear conditioning and extinction are the experimental models of how emotional associations form and change, with autonomic measures as readouts; they are the paradigm where the circuit logic is most completely worked out - cue and outcome converge and autonomic expression is read out - and the basis for translational work on exposure-based treatment. They do not measure appraisal, memory content or the meaning a person assigns to a cue, so laboratory effects are treated as mechanisms rather than as the phenomenon of fear. [[F60 Neuroscience Online amygdala#^f60-fear|Amygdala]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-model|Fear extinction]]

**The return of fear is part of the model.** Extinction is new learning that competes with the original memory rather than erasing it, and fear can return through spontaneous recovery, reinstatement or renewal - the model's own predictions, demonstrated in animals and partly reproduced in humans. The model therefore explains relapse as well as acquisition: relapse of conditioned fear after successful treatment is within its expectations rather than a treatment failure, and a treatment claim that promises erasure claims something the experimental literature does not support. [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-newlearning|Fear extinction]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-model|Fear extinction]]

**Translation is the weak link.** Consolidation and reconsolidation findings were expected to improve treatment design, and the review that surveys them reports translation is incomplete, so the model constrains what a treatment mechanism could look like without predicting individual outcomes. [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-translation|Fear extinction]] [[P22129456 Milad 2012 Fear extinction#^p22129456-limit|Fear extinction]]

## Evidence and status

Conditioning evidence is strong in animals and healthy humans, and direct evidence comes largely from that animal work and from human conditioning in healthy participants; human imaging findings localise group-level responses rather than individual states. Amygdala findings in clinical samples come from group designs with the analytic flexibility and power problems documented for neuroimaging generally, so an average difference is a sample-level description rather than a test for an individual. ## Uncertainties

- The 'fear centre' label over-simplifies a structure involved in salience, social processing and reward as well as threat.
