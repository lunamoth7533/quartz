# Cortical layers and columns

## Definition

Neocortex is organised in six layers distinguished by cell type and density, and in vertical columns in which cells share response properties and connections. Laminar structure is the physical basis for the separation of input and output pathways.

## How it works

**Layers.** Cell types are distributed non-randomly across the layers, and the orderly arrays of microtubules and organelles in dendrites support this compartmental structure. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-compartments|Organization of Cell Types]]

**Lamination is a wiring diagram, not just texture.** Cortical tissue repeats a laminar motif whose usefulness is that it predicts where axons terminate and where they originate: some layers receive thalamic input while others project to subcortical targets or to the opposite hemisphere. Thalamic input arrives in the middle layers, corticocortical projections concentrate superficially and deeply, with the superficial layers exchanging cortico-cortical connections, and subcortical outputs leave from the deep layers, whose pyramidal cells project onward. Each layer therefore has a characteristic set of inputs and outputs, so a lesion or recording can be described by layer, a stain that reveals layers reveals something about a region's connections, and a region's layer profile summarises its connectivity: heavy middle layers mean strong thalamic drive, prominent deep layers mean strong subcortical output, and differences between cortical areas in layer thickness are statements about their different jobs. [[F05 OpenStax The central nervous system#^f05-parts|The Central Nervous System]] [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure and Function]]

**Development builds the pattern.** Lamination and the layer-specific wiring appear during development through inside-out migration as neurons migrate and differentiate, so the adult wiring plan and the developmental sequence are two views of one structure. The receptors expressed at a given layer change with age, so the same microcircuit can have different molecular properties at different ages, which is why developmental claims and adult circuit claims need to be stated separately. [[P21042938 Stiles 2010 Basics of brain development#^p21042938-processes|The basics of brain development]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-receptors|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]]

**Columns as a functional motif.** Cells stacked vertically tend to share response preferences and input sources, which supports the description of cortex as a repeating unit. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-dendrites|Organization of Cell Types]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-receptors|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]] This is how orientation and direction selectivity were described in visual cortex: visual signals reach cortex through the lateral geniculate nucleus and arrive first in striate cortex, and cortical neurons are described by properties such as orientation and direction sensitivity - the findings that established ordered mapping from a layered circuit. [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-pathway|Visual Cortex]] [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-properties|Visual Processing: Cortical Pathways]]

**Circuit logic.** Excitatory and inhibitory inputs are placed differently on cortical cells, and that placement determines how each input influences the cell's output. [[F106 Neuroscience Online amino acid transmitters#^f106-spatial|Amino acid transmitters]]

**Beyond primary areas.** Association areas extend the same architecture across territory, linking primary and unimodal regions into anterior, posterior and limbic groups. Grey matter carries cell bodies and local processing while white matter carries long-range connections, though real circuits do not observe a neat split. [[F109 Neuroscience Online association and executive processing#^f109-areas|Association and Executive Processing]] [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure and Function]]

**Reading rule.** A claim that 'cortex is organised in columns' should specify the area, the species and the method. Generalised column claims are one of the places where a textbook motif has been overextended into a theory of cortical computation. [[F102 Neuroscience Online cell types#^f102-limit|Organization of Cell Types]]

## Evidence and status

Cortical lamination and cell-type distribution are established histologically. [[F54 Neuroscience Online nervous system overview#^f54-sections|Overview of the Nervous System]] Cortical thickness, lamination and connectivity vary between regions and species, so a layer index from one preparation is not automatically the same layer in another; the motif is a hypothesis about organisation rather than a fixed coordinate system. [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-limit|Visual Cortex]]

Columnar organisation is established in some systems and remains less clear in others. In primary sensory cortex, vertically stacked cells share response properties strongly enough that columns function as units; outside primary areas, the evidence for discrete, boundary-defined columns weakens and the column becomes a tendency rather than a module. The distinction matters because a modular claim carries predictions - sharp boundaries, reproducible maps - that a tendency claim does not, and the mapping methods themselves shape what boundaries appear, so the motif is a regularity to be used carefully rather than a universal module.
