# White matter and tracts

## Definition

White matter is composed of myelinated axons grouped into tracts that connect cortical areas to each other, to subcortical structures and to the periphery.

## How it works

**Tissue logic and composition.** Grey matter is dominated by cell bodies and local processing, white matter by long-range connections, though the distinction describes the dominant tissue rather than an absolute rule. White matter is made of myelinated axons, oligodendrocytes and supporting glia, and the sheaths that give it its appearance increase conduction speed along the axons they enclose, so tissue colour, histology and function are linked. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure]] [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]]

**Three tract families, three lesion geometries.** Tracts are grouped by origin, destination and crossing pattern. Projection fibres connect cortex with subcortical and spinal targets, so their damage produces long-tract deficits below the lesion; commissural fibres cross the midline to join the hemispheres, so their damage produces disconnection between hemispheres; association fibres join cortical areas within a hemisphere, so their damage produces network-level disconnections without primary sensory or motor loss. The classification is anatomical and predictive: knowing the group predicts where a lesion's effects appear, and predicting which geometry a lesion will produce is the practical payoff. [[F01 OpenStax Nervous system structure and function#^f01-matter|Nervous System Structure]] [[F54 Neuroscience Online nervous system overview#^f54-sections|Overview of the Nervous System]]

**Disconnection.** Because tracts carry information between areas, damage can produce deficits that look like a lost function when the area itself is intact - the logic behind disconnection syndromes. [[F110 Neuroscience Online language#^f110-model|Language]] [[F54 Neuroscience Online nervous system overview#^f54-sections|Overview of the Nervous System]] In the spinal cord, ascending and descending tracts occupy characteristic positions, which is why the level and pattern of a deficit carries localising information, and the sensory pathway's three-neuron chain with a midline crossing explains why some lesions produce contralateral deficits. [[F59 Neuroscience Online spinal cord#^f59-tracts|Spinal Cord]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]]

**Conduction and timing.** Myelination raises conduction velocity by restricting current to nodes and reducing the membrane area that must be repolarised, so tracts differ in speed as well as in route and tract integrity affects timing relationships between regions. Timing differences between tracts are part of how signals converge, which makes white matter a timing structure as much as a routing structure. [[F02 OpenStax Nervous tissue#^f02-myelin|Nervous Tissue]] [[Myelin and saltatory conduction]] [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]]

**Development and clinical relevance.** Myelination continues into adulthood and after synapse numbers peak, with different tracts maturing on different schedules, so white-matter development is one basis for changing capabilities across development and one reason age at injury changes outcome. [[P19794405 Tau 2010 Normal development of brain circuits#^p19794405-timing|Normal development of brain circuits]] [[Synaptic pruning and myelination]] Where myelin is attacked - as in multiple sclerosis - the resulting deficits follow the tracts involved rather than a single functional system. [[Demyelination and multiple sclerosis]] [[F75 MedlinePlus Multiple sclerosis#^f75-course|Multiple Sclerosis]]

**Reading rule.** 'Connectivity' is used for at least three different things: anatomical axons, statistical correlation between regions, and effective influence in a model. Only the first is a tract, and the others must carry their method label. [[White matter and tracts]] [[Structural versus functional measures]]

## Evidence and status

Tract anatomy is established by dissection and tracer work in animals: tracer methods establish the direction of a connection, and staining and dissection establish gross organisation. [[Neuroanatomical methods]] [[F101 Neuroscience Online synapse formation and elimination#^f101-elimination|Synapse Formation, Survival, and Elimination]] Human estimates use diffusion imaging, which measures indirect properties of tissue and carries modelling assumptions: it estimates the preferred orientation of water movement, from which tractography reconstructs plausible continuations of fibre orientation and continuity. The reconstructions resolve major bundles well but are limited at crossings and in complex geometry, are biased by parameter choices, do not establish direction, and inherit the analytic flexibility and power constraints of other imaging methods. [[P28053326 Poldrack 2017 Neuroimaging reproducibility#^p28053326-flexibility|Neuroimaging reproducibility]] [[F28 OpenStax Brain imaging Psychology 2e#^f28-mri|Brain imaging]] Human connectional claims are therefore estimates subject to method-specific biases rather than wiring diagrams - stronger than guesses, weaker than the tracer literature that anchors them in animals. [[Neuroanatomical methods]] Connectivity descriptions are also degenerate - different parameters can produce similar outputs - so a tract or connectome constrains function rather than determining it. [[P23040802 Marder 2012 Neuromodulation#^p23040802-degeneracy|Neuromodulation]]
