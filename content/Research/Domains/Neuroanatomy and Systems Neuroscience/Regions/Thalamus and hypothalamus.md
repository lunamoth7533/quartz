# Thalamus and hypothalamus

## Definition

The thalamus is a relay and integration structure that sits between sensory pathways and cortex and participates in cortical loops. The hypothalamus is a small structure with broad regulatory roles in endocrine, autonomic and behavioural function.

## How it works

**Thalamic relay.** Sensory pathways converge on thalamic relays before reaching cortex: the three-neuron chain for discriminative touch and proprioception ends with a thalamic neuron projecting to the postcentral gyrus, and in the visual system the lateral geniculate nucleus carries the signal to striate cortex. [[F104 Neuroscience Online visual cortex#^f104-pathway|Visual Processing: Cortical Pathways]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] Thalamic lesions can therefore mimic cortical deficits.

**Relay is active: participation in cortical loops.** The thalamus receives sensory and motor information and projects to cortex, and it also receives massive return projections from cortex: every major thalamic nucleus receives reciprocal cortex-to-thalamus projections that usually outnumber its feedforward input. Because of that reciprocal arrangement, thalamic nuclei are better described as participants in cortical loops than as gateways that merely pass signals through. Relay nuclei are distinguished by their cortical targets, which is why naming the nucleus is more informative than naming 'the thalamus'. [[F05 OpenStax The central nervous system#^f05-parts|The Central Nervous System]] [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]]

**Gating as a function.** Because thalamic activity determines whether cortical areas are engaged, the structure is described as gating and routing the flow of information to cortex; arousal state and attentional state both change that gating, which is the mechanistic connection between this structure and the sleep-wake and modulation literature. [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]] [[P23040802 Marder 2012 Neuromodulation#^p23040802-state|Neuromodulation]] During sleep, thalamic cells shift into burst modes that block pass-through traffic - the cellular form of the observation that sensory signals fail to reach cortex effectively in deep sleep. [[F52 OpenStax Central processing#^f52-pathway|Central Processing]] [[F14 NINDS Brain basics understanding sleep#^f14-stages|Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep]]

**Hypothalamic zones and nuclei.** The hypothalamus is described in longitudinal zones containing named nuclei with distinct functions; the periventricular zone contains the arcuate and paraventricular nuclei and is central to neuroendocrine and autonomic control. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-zones|Hypothalamus]] It contains the releasing-hormone neurons that drive the pituitary, the suprachiasmatic nucleus that times daily rhythms, and nuclei that control temperature, feeding and arousal, so its nuclei map onto distinct regulatory functions such as feeding, thermoregulation and biological timing. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-nuclei|Hypothalamus]] [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-timing|Hypothalamus]]

**Small tissue with system-level reach.** From a small amount of tissue the hypothalamus coordinates three output systems: its nuclei command the pituitary through releasing factors, the autonomic system through descending projections, and behaviour through integrated drives, while the suprachiasmatic nucleus sets the timing for all of it. That concentration of command in one small structure is why hypothalamic damage produces combinations - endocrine, autonomic, thermoregulatory, circadian - rather than one deficit. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-nuclei|Hypothalamus]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-connection|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]]

**Endocrine output and feedback.** The hypothalamus links the nervous system to endocrine output: it produces releasing and inhibiting factors for the anterior pituitary and sends neural projections to the posterior pituitary, which releases oxytocin and antidiuretic hormone made in the hypothalamus. [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-connection|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-lobes|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] Hormone release is regulated by feedback from target-gland products back to hypothalamus and pituitary, which is why the axis is described as a loop rather than a chain - and why a single blood level samples a pulsatile signal shaped by binding proteins and tissue-level effects the textbook presentation omits. [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-feedback|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[F34 OpenStax Pituitary gland and hypothalamus#^f34-limit|Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus]] [[F64 OpenStax The adrenal glands#^f64-limit|The Adrenal Glands]]

**Timing and coupling.** Because the same structure contains the circadian clock and the hormonal command neurons, it is the anatomical place where timing and endocrine control are coupled - which is why rhythm disruption and hormonal measures appear together in clinical research. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-timing|Hypothalamus]] [[P27990019 Takahashi 2017 Circadian clock architecture#^p27990019-loop|Circadian clock architecture]]

**Reading rule.** 'Thalamus' and 'hypothalamus' are collection labels covering nuclei with different connections and functions; a mechanism claim should name the nucleus and the output it controls. [[F58 Neuroscience Online hypothalamus#^f58-limit|Hypothalamus]]

## Evidence and status

Anatomy and hormonal control are well established in animals and humans; functional assignment of individual nuclei relies heavily on animal lesion and stimulation work. [[F119 Neuroscience Online pituitary control#^f119-feedback|Hypothalamic control of pituitary hormone]]

## Uncertainties

- Thalamic function is best described as participation in cortico-thalamic loops, which makes simple relay metaphors misleading.
