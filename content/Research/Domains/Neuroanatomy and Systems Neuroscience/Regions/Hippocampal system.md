# Hippocampal system

## Definition

The hippocampal formation comprises the dentate gyrus, the hippocampus proper and the subicular cortex, in the medial temporal lobe. Its orderly circuit has made it one of the most studied structures in neuroscience.

## How it works

**The circuit, step by step.** Information reaches the hippocampal formation from the entorhinal cortex through the perforant pathway, which terminates on granule cells of the dentate gyrus. Dentate granule cells project by mossy fibres to the pyramidal cells of CA3, and CA3 projects by Schaffer collaterals to CA1. CA1 output leaves through the subiculum into the alveus, fimbria and fornix, and the subiculum also projects back to the entorhinal cortex and to the amygdala; afferents and efferents of the hippocampus travel in the same bundles, so the same anatomy describes traffic in both directions. [[F61 Neuroscience Online hippocampus#^f61-circuit|Hippocampus]] [[F61 Neuroscience Online hippocampus#^f61-output|Hippocampus]]

**The classical sketch and the full system.** The three-step loop - perforant pathway, mossy fibres, Schaffer collaterals - is the trisynaptic circuit, and it is a sketch of one dominant route through the formation rather than a complete wiring diagram: the entorhinal cortex also projects directly to CA1 and the subiculum, connections run in both directions, and the formation exchanges fibres with the parahippocampal region, the amygdala and the diencephalon through the fornix. The sketch earns its place because of the structure's laminar, single-layer principal-cell organisation and unusually well-mapped internal circuit: the transverse slice preserves those three pathways in a preparation where stimulating and recording electrodes can be placed on identified fibre bundles, which is why the structure became the model system in which synaptic plasticity was characterised rather than merely a well-described one. [[F61 Neuroscience Online hippocampus#^f61-slices|Hippocampus]] [[F61 Neuroscience Online hippocampus#^f61-circuit|Hippocampus]] [[F61 Neuroscience Online hippocampus#^f61-formation|Hippocampus]]

**Memory, context and place.** Patient and animal evidence links hippocampal damage to memory impairment, particularly for new episodic learning, and special properties of its circuits are used to explain why the system supports rapid, one-trial encoding. [[F61 Neuroscience Online hippocampus#^f61-memory|Hippocampus]] [[F61 Neuroscience Online hippocampus#^f61-limit|Hippocampus]] The structural signature corresponds to a function: the system binds the elements of an event together including where and when it happened, which is why context appears in both memory retrieval and extinction experiments. Retrieval depends on cues and context, so availability is not the same as accessibility. [[F61 Neuroscience Online hippocampus#^f61-memory|Hippocampus]] [[F11 Noba Memory encoding storage retrieval#^f11-cues|Memory encoding storage retrieval]]

**Plasticity mechanisms were worked out here.** The structure is a focus of plasticity research because its synapses are unusually tractable to record. Long-term potentiation is an activity-dependent persistent increase in synaptic strength; it is long-lasting, input-specific and associative, and its durable forms need gene expression. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-properties|Long-term potentiation and depression]] Where pre- and postsynaptic spike timing has been manipulated in pairing protocols, pre-before-post pairing within tens of milliseconds has commonly produced potentiation and the reversed order produced depression; the exact window and even the polarity depend on protocol and preparation, so this describes a common experimental pattern in reduced preparations rather than a universal rule, and single-spike effects, where they have been reported, are preparation- and protocol-specific. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-timing|Long-term potentiation and depression]]

**Development and aging.** Hippocampal and temporal regions are among those affected early in degenerative disease, and age-related structural change is measured through these structures. [[F113 Neuroscience Online CNS aging and Alzheimer's disease#^f113-ad|CNS aging and Alzheimer's disease]]

## Evidence and status

Anatomy and the memory association are well established; mechanistic claims usually come from animal preparations and are extrapolated to humans. [[P21779718 Bliss 2011 Long-term potentiation and depression#^p21779718-translation|Long-term potentiation and depression]]

**What the model does not carry.** Slice and animal preparations establish mechanism; they do not measure human memory, and the transposition from a cellular phenomenon to a memory claim requires behavioural evidence that the same manipulation changes recall. The plasticity review also notes that human non-invasive effects described as LTP-like have not demonstrated the defining features of LTP. ## Uncertainties

- Reported hippocampal volume differences carry measurement error from segmentation and protocol choices alongside biological variation; without reliability data for a specific protocol, a group difference or an individual value cannot be decomposed into those components.
