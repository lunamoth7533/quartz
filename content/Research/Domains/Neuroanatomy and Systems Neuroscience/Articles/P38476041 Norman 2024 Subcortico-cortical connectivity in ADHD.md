# Subcortico-Cortical Dysconnectivity in ADHD: A Voxel-Wise Mega-Analysis Across Multiple Cohorts.

**Authors.** Norman LJ, Sudre G, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38476041/)

**Published.** 2024 in The American journal of psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1176/appi.ajp.20230026](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.20230026) · PMID [38476041](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38476041/) · PMCID [PMC11486346](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11486346/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Compared with controls, youths with ADHD showed stronger resting-state coupling of striatal seeds with supplementary motor, fronto-insular and temporal cortex, and of the amygdala with the dorsal anterior cingulate.
^p38476041-striatal

The strongest associations centred on the caudate in both hemispheres, and similar patterns appeared for ADHD traits and with alternative seed definitions.
^p38476041-caudate

Results were not driven by head motion or shared with commonly co-occurring internalising or other externalising problems, but effect sizes were small, with the largest peak near d = 0.15.
^p38476041-effect

## Scope as reported

Voxel-wise mega-analysis of six neuroimaging datasets examining four subcortical seeds in 1,696 youths with ADHD and 6,737 controls, plus trait analyses in 9,890 youths, with sensitivity analyses for co-occurring problems, motion-matched subsamples and adjustment for estimated intelligence.
^p38476041-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

With a largest effect near d = 0.15 the two groups' distributions overlap almost entirely, so the difference describes populations and cannot classify or characterise an individual. Resting-state correlation also does not show the direction of influence around the cortico-striatal loop.
^p38476041-caution-effect
