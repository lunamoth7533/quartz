# Change in striatal functional connectivity networks across 2 years due to stimulant exposure in childhood ADHD: results from the ABCD sample.

**Authors.** Kaminski A, Xie H, et al. (4 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39505862/)

**Published.** 2024 in Translational psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41398-024-03165-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-024-03165-7) · PMID [39505862](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39505862/) · PMCID [PMC11541585](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11541585/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Over two years, real-world stimulant exposure was associated with change in resting connectivity between six striatal regions (caudate, putamen and nucleus accumbens on each side) and cortical networks linked to executive and visuomotor control, not accounted for by psychotropic medication in general.
^p39505862-networks

Three changes were specific to stimulant-exposed compared with stimulant-naive children: lower caudate-frontoparietal connectivity, and lower putamen connectivity with frontoparietal and visual networks.
^p39505862-specific

The authors read some changes as moving towards values seen in typically developing children, and a putamen-visual change distinguished the 14% of exposed children with reliable symptom reduction.
^p39505862-normalising

## Scope as reported

Observational analysis of the ABCD cohort using Bayesian hierarchical regression of two-year change; dosage and adherence were not controlled, which the authors present as reflecting real-world exposure.
^p39505862-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Exposure was not randomised, so children who received stimulants may differ in ways that also shape connectivity change; the study cannot show that the medication caused the change, and it gives no basis for any individual treatment decision. Its main lesson for anatomy is that medication history must be modelled when striatal findings are read.
^p39505862-caution-observational
