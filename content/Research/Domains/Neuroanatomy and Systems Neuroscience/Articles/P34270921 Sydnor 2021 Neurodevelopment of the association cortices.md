## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review describes human cortical development as lasting several decades, moving through childhood and adolescence from primary sensory and motor areas towards transmodal association cortex, which serves executive, social-emotional and mentalising functions.
^p34270921-decades

This hierarchical timing follows a sensorimotor-to-association axis that the authors regard as evolutionarily old, is tied to an ordered series of plasticity-related cellular events, and sharpens the differences between lower-order and higher-order regions.
^p34270921-axis

The long-lasting plasticity of late-maturing association cortex is presented as a distinctively human feature that also raises vulnerability to several developmental forms of psychopathology.
^p34270921-risk

## Scope as reported

Narrative review synthesising multimodal neuroimaging with cellular and circuit findings; no systematic search or pooled estimate is reported, and the link to psychopathology is framed as accumulating evidence.
^p34270921-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A developmental programme that runs for decades has no single finishing line. The popular claim that the brain, or the prefrontal cortex, is mature at 25 compresses gradual, region-specific group trajectories into one round number that no individual scan can confirm.
^p34270921-caution-age
