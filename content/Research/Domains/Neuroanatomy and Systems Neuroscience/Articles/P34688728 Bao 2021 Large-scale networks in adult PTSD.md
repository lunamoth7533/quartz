# Alterations in large-scale functional networks in adult posttraumatic stress disorder: A systematic review and meta-analysis of resting-state functional connectivity studies.

**Authors.** Bao W, Gao Y, et al. (11 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34688728/)

**Published.** 2021 in Neuroscience and biobehavioral reviews (first online 2021-10-22).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.10.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.10.017) · PMID [34688728](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34688728/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Weaker connectivity within the default mode network, and between an affective network and the default mode network, was tied to having experienced trauma rather than to PTSD as such.
^p34688728-trauma

Stronger coupling of the somatomotor network with the affective network and with the default mode network was specific to PTSD.
^p34688728-ptsd

The review compared PTSD with controls overall and separately with trauma-exposed and non-exposed controls, pooling studies quantitatively only where enough were available.
^p34688728-design

## Scope as reported

Systematic review of seed-based resting-state connectivity studies in adults with PTSD, with meta-analysis where study numbers allowed; the abstract reports no study or participant counts.
^p34688728-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The result turns on the comparison group: a difference against non-exposed controls mixes the effects of trauma with those of the disorder. Adult PTSD samples also do not establish network patterns for complex PTSD.
^p34688728-caution-controls
