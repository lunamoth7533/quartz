## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The review gives each prefrontal subregion a different emphasis: dorsolateral cortex for working memory, goal-driven attention, set switching, planning and problem solving; ventrolateral cortex for inhibition, selecting responses and monitoring them; medial cortex for self-knowledge, motivation, regulating emotion and revising goals; orbitofrontal cortex for personality, inhibition and social and emotional reasoning.
^p34881727-subdivisions

Executive function is described as working memory, flexibility and inhibition exercised through top-down control over processes spread across the brain. Prefrontal cortex works within large-scale networks, so executive failure can follow damage to a wider parietal-temporal-frontal system, as in an atypical dysexecutive form of Alzheimer disease.
^p34881727-networks

## Scope as reported

Narrative review written for clinical neurologists, built mainly on lesion syndromes and clinical observation with support from neuroimaging; no systematic search or pooled estimate is reported.
^p34881727-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The subregion list summarises tendencies from lesion and imaging work. The same review stresses network participation, so reading the list as one region, one function overstates it, and none of it describes how a particular person's prefrontal cortex works.
