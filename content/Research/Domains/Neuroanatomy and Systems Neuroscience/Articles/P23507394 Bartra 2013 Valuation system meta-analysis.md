# The valuation system: a coordinate-based meta-analysis of BOLD fMRI experiments examining neural correlates of subjective value.

**Authors.** Bartra O, McGuire JT, et al. (3 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23507394/)

**Published.** 2013 in NeuroImage (first online 2013-03-15).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.02.063](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.02.063) · PMID [23507394](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23507394/) · PMCID [PMC3756836](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3756836/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In ventromedial prefrontal cortex and anterior ventral striatum, signal mostly rose with the subjective value of an option, both when a choice was faced and when an outcome arrived, for money and for primary rewards alike. The authors call these regions a valuation system that carries one value signal across domains.
^p23507394-valuation

The thalamus, anterior insula, dorsomedial prefrontal cortex and the dorsal and posterior striatum showed value effects in both directions, which the authors read as a U-shaped signal reflecting arousal or salience rather than value itself.
^p23507394-salience

## Scope as reported

Quantitative coordinate-based meta-analysis of peak locations reported by 206 published fMRI studies of subjective value; it pools reported coordinates rather than individual data.
^p23507394-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Consistent value-related signal in a region shows where value tracks activity across laboratory tasks; it does not show that the region computes value or that value is its only role, so inferring valuation from activity there in a new study is reverse inference.
^p23507394-caution-reverse
