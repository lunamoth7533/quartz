# Intrinsic activity development unfolds along a sensorimotor-association cortical axis in youth.

**Authors.** Sydnor VJ, Larsen B, et al. (17 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36973514/)

**Published.** 2023 in Nature neuroscience.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41593-023-01282-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01282-y) · PMID [36973514](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36973514/) · PMCID [PMC10406167](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10406167/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The amplitude of intrinsic fMRI activity declined with age, mirroring animal work in which cortical activity becomes sparser as plasticity wanes, and the decline began at different ages in different regions.
^p36973514-refinement

The regional timing was coupled to maturation of intracortical myelin and was ordered along a hierarchical axis running from sensorimotor to association cortex between ages 8 and 18.
^p36973514-axis

Associations between neighbourhood disadvantage and intrinsic activity also varied along this axis, diverging most in mid-adolescence.
^p36973514-environment

## Scope as reported

Resting-state functional MRI in 1,033 youths aged 8 to 23 years, with age-related change in the amplitude of intrinsic activity modelled region by region and compared with intracortical myelin and neighbourhood environment.
^p36973514-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Age trajectories modelled across many young people describe how the group changes on average, not individual developmental curves. The fMRI amplitude is an indirect index, and reading its decline as falling plasticity rests on the analogy with animal recordings that the authors draw.
^p36973514-caution-inference
