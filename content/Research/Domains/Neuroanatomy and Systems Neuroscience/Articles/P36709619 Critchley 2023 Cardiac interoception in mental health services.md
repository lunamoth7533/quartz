# Cardiac interoception in patients accessing secondary mental health services: A transdiagnostic study.

**Authors.** Critchley HD, Sherrill SP, et al. (10 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36709619/)

**Published.** 2023 in Autonomic neuroscience : basic & clinical.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.autneu.2023.103072](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autneu.2023.103072) · PMID [36709619](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36709619/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Patients were less accurate and less confident than comparison participants on heartbeat discrimination, and less confident on heartbeat tracking.
^p36709619-accuracy

Among patients, more anxiety and depression went with higher self-reported interoceptive sensibility and a wider gap between that self-report and task accuracy; comparison participants did not show this pattern.
^p36709619-mismatch

Heart rate variability differed between diagnostic groups, but post hoc contrasts did not survive correction for multiple comparisons; heartbeat tracking accuracy was lowest in schizophrenia.
^p36709619-hrv

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional study of 258 patients and 67 non-clinical comparison participants, combining cardiac physiology, heartbeat discrimination and tracking tasks, and a self-report measure, followed by correlations with affective symptoms and exploratory comparisons across recorded diagnoses.
^p36709619-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Because task accuracy, confidence and self-report pulled apart, a single 'interoception' score would have hidden the main result. The design is cross-sectional, so it cannot say whether altered interoception precedes, accompanies or follows symptoms.
^p36709619-caution-measures
