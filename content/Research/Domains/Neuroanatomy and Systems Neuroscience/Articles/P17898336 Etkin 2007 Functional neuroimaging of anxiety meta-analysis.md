# Functional neuroimaging of anxiety: a meta-analysis of emotional processing in PTSD, social anxiety disorder, and specific phobia.

**Authors.** Etkin A, Wager TD (2 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17898336/)

**Published.** 2007 in The American journal of psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1176/appi.ajp.2007.07030504](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2007.07030504) · PMID [17898336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17898336/) · PMCID [PMC3318959](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3318959/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In all three disorders, patients processing negative emotional material showed more amygdala and insula activity than comparison groups, resembling the pattern seen in healthy people during fear conditioning.
^p17898336-hyper

That over-activation was found less consistently in PTSD than in the other two disorders.
^p17898336-frequency

Only the PTSD studies showed reduced activity in dorsal and rostral anterior cingulate and in ventromedial prefrontal cortex, regions the authors associate with experiencing and regulating emotion; they suggest this points to emotional dysregulation that goes beyond exaggerated fear.
^p17898336-ptsd

## Scope as reported

Quantitative meta-analysis of fMRI and PET studies comparing negative emotional processing with baseline, neutral or positive conditions, with fear-conditioning studies in healthy participants as a reference.
^p17898336-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

These are group-level task findings from studies published before 2008. The PTSD samples predate the ICD-11 complex PTSD category, so the prefrontal under-activation cannot be relabelled as a complex PTSD mechanism, and it is not a marker that identifies any individual.
^p17898336-caution-cptsd
