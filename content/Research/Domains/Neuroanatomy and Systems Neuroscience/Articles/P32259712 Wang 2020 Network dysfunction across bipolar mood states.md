# Large-scale network dysfunction in the acute state compared to the remitted state of bipolar disorder: A meta-analysis of resting-state functional connectivity.

**Authors.** Wang Y, Gao Y, et al. (14 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32259712/)

**Published.** 2020 in EBioMedicine (first online 2020-04-04).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.ebiom.2020.102742](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2020.102742) · PMID [32259712](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32259712/) · PMCID [PMC7136605](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7136605/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

During acute mood episodes, connectivity within an affective network and within the default mode network was lower than in controls.
^p32259712-acute

In remission the affective-network reduction was not seen, default mode connectivity was higher than in controls rather than lower, and the pattern of connectivity between networks also differed from the acute state.
^p32259712-remitted

The authors suggest that this shift between states may relate to different emotional and cognitive difficulties and could guide the search for state-specific biomarkers.
^p32259712-interpretation

## Scope as reported

Seed-based d mapping meta-analysis of 39 voxel-wise resting-state datasets; each seed was assigned to a network by its location within a priori functional networks.
^p32259712-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The acute and remitted groups come from different study samples rather than the same people followed over time, and medication and seed choice vary between studies, so the state difference is a between-study contrast. No person's mood state can be read from a connectivity map.
^p32259712-caution-state
