# Mood monitoring and early warning signs in bipolar disorder

## Definition

Mood monitoring is the repeated recording of mood, sleep and activity - by diaries, momentary sampling on a phone, or sensors - so that change can be seen as it happens rather than reconstructed later. [[P18509902 Shiffman 2008 Ecological momentary assessment#^p18509902-method|Ecological momentary assessment]] [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-patterns|Kutcher 2026]] Early warning signs are the prodromal changes that precede a mood episode; in one clinical sample most patients recalled sleep disturbance before their current episode. [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-prevalence|Basquin 2024]]

## How it works

**Warning signs are usually reconstructed after the episode.** Asked during an episode about the period before it, 59% of patients in one sample volunteered sleep disturbance and 83% reported it once asked about specific problems, with onset reported earlier before depression than before mania and insomnia the commonest complaint. [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-prevalence|Basquin 2024]] [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-timing|Basquin 2024]] The authors argue for systematic questions about sleep and for teaching patients their sleep prodromes. [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-education|Basquin 2024]] Reports gathered this way carry the weakness that motivated momentary methods - global retrospective accounts are shaped by recall and miss how states change across time and context - and the jump under prompting shows how much the estimate depends on the questions asked. [[P18509902 Shiffman 2008 Ecological momentary assessment#^p18509902-rationale|Ecological momentary assessment]] [[P38653574 Basquin 2024 Prodromal sleep disturbances in bipolar disorder#^p38653574-caution-recall|Appraisal: Basquin 2024]]

**Active monitoring samples the present repeatedly.** Ecological momentary assessment records current states many times in daily life, by diary, phone or sensor, at a cost in participant burden and adherence, and with a design choice between sampling at set intervals and sampling around events. [[P18509902 Shiffman 2008 Ecological momentary assessment#^p18509902-method|Ecological momentary assessment]] [[P18509902 Shiffman 2008 Ecological momentary assessment#^p18509902-tradeoffs|Ecological momentary assessment]] Dense sampling changes what the interval between episodes looks like: five prompts a day for four months in 20 patients resolved into neutral, elevated, mixed and lowered states with frequent switching, and mixed states sitting between elevated and lowered ones. [[P38829323 Mildiner Moraga 2024 Mood instability in intensive EMA data#^p38829323-states|Mildiner Moraga 2024]] [[P38829323 Mildiner Moraga 2024 Mood instability in intensive EMA data#^p38829323-instability|Mildiner Moraga 2024]] Model-derived momentary states are not diagnosed episodes, so such data describe instability rather than relapse. [[P38829323 Mildiner Moraga 2024 Mood instability in intensive EMA data#^p38829323-caution-states|Appraisal: Mildiner Moraga 2024]]

**Passive sensing reads behaviour without asking.** Across 57 studies of markers collected passively by wearables and phones, depression went with reduced mobility and social contact, later or more variable sleep and less daytime light, while mania and hypomania went with more and more variable activity, shorter or advanced sleep and more communication; circadian features derived from sleep and activity repeatedly helped prediction. [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-patterns|Kutcher 2026]] [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-circadian|Kutcher 2026]] These are the timing variables that the circadian hypotheses treat as mechanism, which is where the monitoring and mechanism literatures meet. [[Circadian and social rhythm hypotheses]]

**Personal patterns do not transfer automatically.** In a small wearable study, models built on movement, electrodermal and heart-rate signals tracked episode severity within individuals with moderate accuracy but generalised poorly to patients they had not been trained on. [[P36939345 Anmella 2023 Wearable digital biomarkers of mood episodes#^p36939345-accuracy|Anmella 2023]] [[P36939345 Anmella 2023 Wearable digital biomarkers of mood episodes#^p36939345-generalization|Anmella 2023]] A pattern that is informative inside one person's time series is a within-person regularity; turning it into a rule for a new person is a separate claim that needs its own evidence. [[P36939345 Anmella 2023 Wearable digital biomarkers of mood episodes#^p36939345-caution-sample|Appraisal: Anmella 2023]] [[Longitudinal and within-person inference]]

**Reading rule.** For any monitoring claim, ask what was recorded (self-report or sensor), against which clinical label and how often, whether the analysis forecast a future episode or classified the current state, and whether the model was tested on new people and new periods.

## Evidence and status

Prospective tests of warning signals come from small intensive cohorts. In a year of daily e-diaries from 29 patients, self-rated mood fell in the two weeks before depressive episodes but not before manic ones, and rising autocorrelation of mood - proposed as a generic sign of an approaching transition - did not anticipate depressive prodromes. [[P38284217 Ludwig 2024 Autocorrelation and upcoming affective episodes#^p38284217-selfreport|Ludwig 2024]] [[P38284217 Ludwig 2024 Autocorrelation and upcoming affective episodes#^p38284217-autocorrelation|Ludwig 2024]] [[P38284217 Ludwig 2024 Autocorrelation and upcoming affective episodes#^p38284217-limit|Ludwig 2024]] Passive-sensing studies are mostly small and short: 60% enrolled 50 or fewer participants and 81% monitored for a year or less. [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-limit|Kutcher 2026]] Group-level differences between prodromal periods and euthymia are consistent enough to pursue, but these sources establish no forecasting rule for an individual. [[P38284217 Ludwig 2024 Autocorrelation and upcoming affective episodes#^p38284217-caution-signal|Appraisal: Ludwig 2024]] [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-caution-validation|Appraisal: Kutcher 2026]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether prodromal sleep and activity change triggers an episode or is its first symptom; in adolescents with bipolar disorder, lagged links between sleep quality and mood ran in both directions. [[P38917889 Stepan 2024 Sleep quality and future mood in adolescents#^p38917889-lag|Stepan 2024]] [[P38917889 Stepan 2024 Sleep quality and future mood in adolescents#^p38917889-reverse|Stepan 2024]]
- How much sparse clinical labels, short monitoring windows and unmodelled missing data inflate reported accuracy, and whether any model holds for new people and later periods. [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-gaps|Kutcher 2026]]
- The burden, adherence and privacy costs of repeated self-report and continuous sensing. [[P18509902 Shiffman 2008 Ecological momentary assessment#^p18509902-tradeoffs|Ecological momentary assessment]] [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-priorities|Kutcher 2026]]
- Whether acting on a detected warning reduces relapse needs randomised tests of monitoring plus a planned response; the sources collected here test detection and association only.

## Recent research

- **2026 · Systematic review (JMIR Mental Health).** Beyond cataloguing consistent group-level patterns, the review sets out what clinical translation would need: longer within-person monitoring, clinical labels timed to match sensing, preregistration, external validation and minimal data collection to protect privacy. [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-priorities|Kutcher 2026]] Until that validation exists, reported accuracies are in-sample estimates. [[P42594348 Kutcher 2026 Passive digital markers in bipolar disorder#^p42594348-caution-validation|Appraisal: Kutcher 2026]]
- **2025 · Prospective daily sleep-log cohort (Bipolar Disorders).** In remitted patients logging sleep for a year, later waking marked the prodrome of depression and sleep-timing variability differed before depressive and before hypomanic or manic episodes - candidate self-monitoring targets from exploratory, group-averaged analyses. [[P40847383 Ulrichsen 2025 Sleep parameters and upcoming mood episodes#^p40847383-waking|Ulrichsen 2025]] [[P40847383 Ulrichsen 2025 Sleep parameters and upcoming mood episodes#^p40847383-variability|Ulrichsen 2025]] [[P40847383 Ulrichsen 2025 Sleep parameters and upcoming mood episodes#^p40847383-exploratory|Ulrichsen 2025]] [[P40847383 Ulrichsen 2025 Sleep parameters and upcoming mood episodes#^p40847383-caution-group|Appraisal: Ulrichsen 2025]]

## Connections

- [[Bipolar I episodes and course]] - the course being monitored: relapse is a change within a lifetime pattern, which is why detection needs repeated measures rather than one assessment.
- [[Psychoeducation and social rhythms]] - the teaching and routine-stabilising context into which early-warning education fits; its recurrence evidence comes from packaged protocols, not from monitoring alone.
- [[Circadian and social rhythm hypotheses]] - the rationale for tracking sleep and activity timing; prospective monitoring data are also how that hypothesis's direction-of-effect problem gets tested.
- [[Longitudinal and within-person inference]] - the method behind monitoring: repeated measures of the same person, where within-person and between-person effects can differ in size and even sign.
- [[Association versus individual prediction]] - why group-level links between sleep change and relapse do not yet amount to a forecast for any one person.
- [[Circadian rhythms and sleep]] - the physiology behind the sleep signals monitored here: timing, duration, continuity and stage composition are distinct measurements.
- [[Measurement validity and reliability]] - whether a diary item or sensor feature actually measures mood state, and does so consistently, before it is used as a warning sign.
- [[Autonomic measurement and heart rate variability]] - background for the heart-rate and electrodermal signals that wearables record, and for which physiological inferences from them are defensible.

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Mood monitoring is where the bipolar condition domain meets the methods domain's within-person inference and the neuroendocrine domain's clock biology: many of the signals are sleep and activity rhythms, the inference is longitudinal, and the clinical question is relapse. A monitoring claim needs all three - a physiological rationale, a design that separates within-person change from between-person difference, and a clinically defined episode label.
