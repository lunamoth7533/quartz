# Phase-specific bipolar treatment evidence

**Definition.** Bipolar treatment evidence is organized by illness phase, and options are ranked by efficacy, safety and tolerability together. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

## Supported claims

- Guideline recommendations are organized by phase and ranked first-, second- and third-line. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]
- Ranking weighs efficacy, safety and tolerability together rather than efficacy alone. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-balance|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]
- NIMH patient material frames treatment as combining medication and psychotherapy across the lifetime course. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-treatment|Bipolar Disorder]]

## How it works

**What a phase-specific guideline does.** The CANMAT and ISBD guidance synthesised efficacy, safety and tolerability evidence and translated it into first-, second- and third-line recommendations for different phases of the illness, with a 2023 update adding new evidence across the lifespan; its stated objective is to help clinicians navigate a rapidly changing treatment landscape. It also summarises maintenance evidence and flags where the literature is thin, particularly for complex presentations. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]] [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-balance|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

**What a guideline ranking is and is not.** A first-, second-, third-line ranking aggregates efficacy, safety and tolerability evidence into an ordered recommendation for a typical patient in a phase; it is not a prediction for an individual, and it is explicitly tied to the evidence quality behind each option, so a first-line option with strong evidence can rank above a newer one with thinner evidence. Reading the ranking without the evidence grades inverts the guideline's logic. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]] [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-balance|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

**Why phases are not interchangeable.** Treatment evidence is organised by episode type - mania, depression, mixed states - because a treatment that helps one phase may not help another: acute mania, bipolar depression and maintenance have different outcome definitions, different acceptable risk profiles and different trial designs, so an effect demonstrated in one phase does not transfer to another. NIMH frames the clinical picture as episodes plus a lifetime course, with treatment planning addressing acute symptoms and long-term stability together. [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-course|Bipolar disorder]]

**National guidance and its grades.** NICE sets out assessment expectations and recommends long-term pharmacological management alongside structured psychological approaches, with explicit cautions for particular medicines in pregnancy and for younger people. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-longterm|Bipolar disorder CG185]] Guidance products tie graded recommendations to the evidence review the committee used, which is why the text distinguishes stronger and weaker recommendations, and they reflect the funded service context as well as the literature. A recommendation is therefore a judgement about a body of evidence, not a measurement of effect. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-framing|Bipolar disorder CG185]] [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]]

**Endpoints.** Trials use different primary outcomes - symptom scores, relapse, functioning - and the choice changes what a result means. [[P18436948 Guyatt 2008 GRADE#^p18436948-certainty|GRADE]]

**Why maintenance evidence is the hardest to produce.** Maintenance trials run for months to years, recruitment and retention are hard, and the outcome - recurrence - depends on definitions, observation windows and what counts as an episode. The same medicine can show an acute effect and an unproven maintenance one, or the reverse, which is why phase-specific reading is not bureaucratic caution but the structure of the actual evidence. [[Phase-specific bipolar treatment evidence]] [[Short-term versus long-term outcomes]]

**What this note does not do.** It summarises research structure and guideline framing and carries no prescribing, dose, titration, monitoring or personal treatment guidance; the CANMAT/ISBD record in this library is a summary of a guideline rather than a trial. Where a specific drug decision is at issue, the full guideline and a clinician are the relevant sources. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-caution-individual|Appraisal: CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]] [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-limit|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

**Reading rule.** For any bipolar treatment claim, name the phase, the outcome, the comparator and the guideline version. Phase, outcome and version together are what make the claim usable rather than decorative. [[P38695002 Keramatian 2023 CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines#^p38695002-phases|CANMAT and ISBD bipolar treatment guidelines]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** Psychotherapy evidence lives under the same phase logic - adjunctive psychotherapy has its own recurrence and depressive-symptom evidence - so the pharmacology and psychology domains of this condition are parallel chapters of one evidence structure rather than competing accounts. [[Bipolar psychotherapy evidence]] [[Psychoeducation and social rhythms]]

## Limitation or common misconception

This record summarizes a guideline summary; medication choice, dose and monitoring belong to clinical care.

## Recent research

- **2025 · Nationwide register cohort with within-individual design (The Lancet Psychiatry).** Among 105,495 people in Swedish registers, maintenance periods on a mood stabiliser alone were associated with fewer depression-related hospitalisations, antidepressant-only periods with more depression- and mania-related admissions, and lithium was the only single medicine linked to lower risk. [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-classes|Ermis 2025]] [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-lithium|Ermis 2025]] [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-mania|Ermis 2025]] This is maintenance-phase effectiveness evidence for severe relapse, open to confounding by the reasons treatment starts, not a trial or a prescription. [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-limit|Ermis 2025]] [[P39922213 Ermis 2025 Real-world maintenance treatment of bipolar depression#^p39922213-caution-register|Appraisal: Ermis 2025]]
- **2024 · Comparative review of guidelines (BJPsych Open).** Comparing guidelines from five sources, the authors trace their differing medicine recommendations to consensus versus meta-analytic methods, timing, study selection, population differences in drug metabolism and health policy, with scarce high-quality trials underneath - which qualifies how any first-line ranking should be read. [[P38586960 Park 2024 International bipolar guideline comparison#^p38586960-sources|Park 2024]] [[P38586960 Park 2024 International bipolar guideline comparison#^p38586960-context|Park 2024]] [[P38586960 Park 2024 International bipolar guideline comparison#^p38586960-caution-consensus|Appraisal: Park 2024]]
