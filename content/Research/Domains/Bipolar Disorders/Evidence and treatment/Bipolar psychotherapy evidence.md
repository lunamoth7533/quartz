# Bipolar psychotherapy evidence

**Definition.** Psychological treatments for bipolar disorder are tested as additions to medication, usually as manualized protocols with named components. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-components|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

## Supported claims

- Adjunctive manualized psychotherapy was linked to lower recurrence than control (OR 0.56). [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]
- CBT was linked to stabilizing depressive symptoms (SMD -0.32), with family and interpersonal therapy pointing the same way less certainly. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-symptoms|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]
- Component analyses estimate average contributions; they do not identify what produced one person's improvement. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-caution-ingredients|Appraisal: Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

## How it works

**What is tested.** Psychological treatments are studied as additions to medication, usually as manualised protocols with named components, which is what makes component-level questions answerable at all. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-components|Adjunctive psychotherapy component network meta-analysis]]

**Where it sits in care.** National guidance recommends structured psychological and psychosocial approaches as part of longer-term management alongside long-term pharmacological management, so psychotherapy is an adjunct in this literature rather than a substitute; the trials all sit on top of medication. [[F94 NICE Bipolar disorder CG185#^f94-longterm|Bipolar disorder CG185]] [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-treatment|Bipolar disorder]]

**The pooled result.** Across 20 two-group trials, adding manualised psychotherapy to medication was associated with lower recurrence than control (OR 0.56), and CBT was linked to stabilising depressive symptoms (SMD -0.32), with family and interpersonal therapy pointing the same way less certainly. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-recurrence|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-symptoms|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]]

**Format mattered more than content in the component analysis.** A component analysis of 13 therapy elements found that psychoeducation with supervised skills practice delivered in family or group sessions was linked to fewer recurrences than the same content delivered individually (OR 0.12) - a large difference in a comparison of formats rather than of whole protocols. It says that how a therapy is delivered changed recurrence more than which named therapy was delivered: an uncomfortable result for brand-based comparisons and a productive one for design, because the delivery model is itself an ingredient, and component analyses are the tool that surfaced it. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-components|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]]

**Retention is an outcome, not housekeeping.** Retention was higher for family or conjoint therapy and for brief psychoeducation, and that finding is clinically load-bearing: a therapy that works but loses people delivers less than its effect size suggests. Trials that report retention beside effect sizes - as this review does - allow that computation; trials that do not, hide it. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-retention|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]] [[Efficacy versus tolerability]]

**The evidence base has stated limits.** The review is a systematic review and network meta-analysis of 39 randomised trials in outpatients, and its authors temper the conclusions for heterogeneity in populations, treatment duration and follow-up; reading the effect size without that sentence overstates the precision of the estimate. Component analyses estimate average contributions across these heterogeneous trials and protocols, and they cannot identify which ingredient produced one person's improvement. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-limit|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]] [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-caution-ingredients|Appraisal: Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]]

**Reading rule.** Name the comparator, the format, the outcome and the follow-up when quoting a psychotherapy effect; the format difference in the component analysis shows how much the delivery model matters. [[P33052390 Miklowitz 2021 Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder#^p33052390-components|Adjunctive psychotherapy for bipolar disorder]]

**Cross-domain connection (curation).** This article and the phase-specific treatment article are the two halves of bipolar intervention evidence - psychological and pharmacological - read against the same phase structure and the same maintenance-trial difficulties, which is why their limitations sections sound alike. [[Phase-specific bipolar treatment evidence]] [[Longitudinal and within-person inference]]

## Limitation or common misconception

Conclusions are tempered by heterogeneity in populations, treatment duration and follow-up, as the authors state.

## Recent research

- **2024 · Randomised clinical trial (JAMA Psychiatry).** In 305 euthymic adults at nine German centres, a structured CBT group and a supportive emotion-focused group added to usual care prevented relapse equally over 18 months, with nearly half relapsing in each arm and bipolar II predicting more relapse. [[P38837133 Hautzinger 2024 Group psychotherapies for bipolar relapse prevention#^p38837133-equivalence|Hautzinger 2024]] [[P38837133 Hautzinger 2024 Group psychotherapies for bipolar relapse prevention#^p38837133-relapse|Hautzinger 2024]] Without a usual-care-only arm, the trial compares formats rather than testing psychotherapy against none. [[P38837133 Hautzinger 2024 Group psychotherapies for bipolar relapse prevention#^p38837133-caution-comparator|Appraisal: Hautzinger 2024]]
- **2024 · Randomised clinical trial (JAMA Psychiatry).** In 100 adolescents with bipolar spectrum disorder, a year of dialectical behavior therapy reduced suicide attempts compared with standard psychotherapy while mood improved equally in both arms, partly through reduced emotion dysregulation - a target-specific effect rather than a change in course. [[P37703037 Goldstein 2024 DBT for adolescents with bipolar disorder#^p37703037-suicide|Goldstein 2024]] [[P37703037 Goldstein 2024 DBT for adolescents with bipolar disorder#^p37703037-mood|Goldstein 2024]] [[P37703037 Goldstein 2024 DBT for adolescents with bipolar disorder#^p37703037-caution-scope|Appraisal: Goldstein 2024]]
