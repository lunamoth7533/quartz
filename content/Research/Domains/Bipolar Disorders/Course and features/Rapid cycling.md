# Rapid cycling

## Definition

Rapid cycling is a course pattern in bipolar disorder in which four or more full mood episodes occur within 12 months, separated by two months of remission or by a switch to the opposite pole. [[P29630220 Dailey 2023 Mania reference chapter#^p29630220-rapid|Dailey 2023]] It is common: across reviews, pooled one-year prevalence was about 22% and lifetime prevalence about 36% of people with bipolar disorder. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-prevalence|Miola 2023]] It is associated with suicide attempts and with poor response to mood stabilisers. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-strong|Miola 2023]]

## How it works

**A course specifier, not a separate disorder.** Rapid cycling describes how episodes are spaced in time within bipolar I or II, so it is defined by a count over a window rather than by symptoms of its own. [[P29630220 Dailey 2023 Mania reference chapter#^p29630220-rapid|Dailey 2023]] The reviews behind the pooled estimates mostly counted at least four recurrences within the last year or within any single year, regardless of treatment response. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-limit|Miola 2023]] DSM-5 describes other course patterns, such as mixed symptoms, with specifiers too, whereas the ICD-11 draft kept a separate mixed episode, so course labels are specific to their system. [[P25450512 de Dios 2014 Bipolar disorders in DSM-5 and ICD-11#^p25450512-dsm5|de Dios 2014]] [[P25450512 de Dios 2014 Bipolar disorders in DSM-5 and ICD-11#^p25450512-icd11|de Dios 2014]]

**Who it affects.** Lifetime rapid cycling is more common in women, and female sex, bipolar II, childhood maltreatment and mixed features had moderate support as correlates. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-prevalence|Miola 2023]] [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-strong|Miola 2023]] Head-to-head comparisons likewise find more rapid cycling in bipolar II than in bipolar I. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-more|Hernandorena 2023]] Within bipolar cohorts, greater affective lability and intensity went with rapid cycling, suicide attempts and anxiety disorders. [[P40715078 Etain 2025 Polygenic scores and bipolar dimensions#^p40715078-lability|Etain 2025]]

**What it predicts.** The strongest evidence links rapid cycling with suicide attempts and with unsatisfactory response to mood stabilisers, and a clinical reference describes it as harder to treat with medication. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-strong|Miola 2023]] [[P29630220 Dailey 2023 Mania reference chapter#^p29630220-rapid|Dailey 2023]] Because rapid cycling is counted over a chosen window, these associations may partly reflect shared severity or treatment history. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-caution-state|Appraisal: Miola 2023]]

**Antidepressants.** Antidepressant exposure had only level-3 support as a correlate across reviews, alongside genetic predisposition, metabolic or thyroid disturbance and predominantly depressive polarity. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-weak|Miola 2023]] The one randomised comparison is small: among 68 patients on mood stabilisers whose depression had responded to an antidepressant, those with rapid cycling who continued it had about three times as many episodes a year as those without rapid cycling and spent less time in remission, a difference absent among patients randomised to stop. [[P26142612 El-Mallakh 2015 Antidepressants and rapid cycling in STEP-BD#^p26142612-episodes|El-Mallakh 2015]] [[P26142612 El-Mallakh 2015 Antidepressants and rapid cycling in STEP-BD#^p26142612-discontinue|El-Mallakh 2015]] That subgroup was small and preselected for antidepressant response. [[P26142612 El-Mallakh 2015 Antidepressants and rapid cycling in STEP-BD#^p26142612-caution-subgroup|Appraisal: El-Mallakh 2015]] Consensus guidance names switching into hypomania, mania or mixed states as the main antidepressant risk in bipolar disorder. [[P24030475 Pacchiarotti 2013 ISBD antidepressants in bipolar disorders#^p24030475-switch|Pacchiarotti 2013]]

**Faster than the definition.** Five self-reports a day over four months showed patients leaving a mood state within a day on average, with mixed states between elevated and lowered ones - switching far faster than an episode count can record. [[P38829323 Mildiner Moraga 2024 Mood instability in intensive EMA data#^p38829323-instability|Mildiner Moraga 2024]] These are model-derived states in 20 people, not diagnosed episodes, so how such instability relates to rapid cycling is open. [[P38829323 Mildiner Moraga 2024 Mood instability in intensive EMA data#^p38829323-caution-states|Appraisal: Mildiner Moraga 2024]]

**Reading rule.** Name the count, the window and whether episodes met full criteria, and ask whether associations were adjusted for severity and treatment history; a label defined over one year can apply in one year and not the next. [[P29630220 Dailey 2023 Mania reference chapter#^p29630220-rapid|Dailey 2023]] [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-caution-state|Appraisal: Miola 2023]]

## Evidence and status

Prevalence and correlates come from a meta-review of 22 reviews covering 13,698 patients, with graded evidence levels and heterogeneous lifetime estimates. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-limit|Miola 2023]] The antidepressant evidence is one small randomised subgroup analysis plus weaker observational support. [[P26142612 El-Mallakh 2015 Antidepressants and rapid cycling in STEP-BD#^p26142612-limit|El-Mallakh 2015]] [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-weak|Miola 2023]] Treatment trials designed for rapid cycling are not yet summarised in this library.

## Uncertainties

- Whether antidepressants provoke rapid cycling or are prescribed more to people prone to it. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-caution-state|Appraisal: Miola 2023]] [[P26142612 El-Mallakh 2015 Antidepressants and rapid cycling in STEP-BD#^p26142612-caution-subgroup|Appraisal: El-Mallakh 2015]]
- How momentary mood instability relates to episode-based rapid cycling. [[P38829323 Mildiner Moraga 2024 Mood instability in intensive EMA data#^p38829323-caution-states|Appraisal: Mildiner Moraga 2024]]
- Whether thyroid, metabolic and immune correlates are causes, consequences or markers of treatment. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-weak|Miola 2023]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Cross-sectional cohort analysis (Translational Psychiatry).** Affective lability went with rapid cycling in 852 people with bipolar disorder. [[P40715078 Etain 2025 Polygenic scores and bipolar dimensions#^p40715078-lability|Etain 2025]]
- **2024 · Intensive longitudinal study (Journal of Psychopathology and Clinical Science).** Momentary mood states switched within a day on average, with mixed states in between. [[P38829323 Mildiner Moraga 2024 Mood instability in intensive EMA data#^p38829323-instability|Mildiner Moraga 2024]]
- **2023 · Systematic meta-review (Journal of Psychiatric Research).** Rapid cycling affected about a fifth to a third of patients, with strong evidence for links to suicide attempts and poor mood-stabiliser response. [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-prevalence|Miola 2023]] [[P37429185 Miola 2023 Rapid cycling prevalence and outcomes#^p37429185-strong|Miola 2023]]
- **2023 · Systematic review with meta-analyses (Harvard Review of Psychiatry).** Rapid cycling was more frequent in bipolar II than in bipolar I. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-more|Hernandorena 2023]]
