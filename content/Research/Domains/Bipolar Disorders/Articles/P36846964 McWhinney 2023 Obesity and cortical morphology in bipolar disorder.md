# Mega-analysis of association between obesity and cortical morphology in bipolar disorders: ENIGMA study in 2832 participants.

**Authors.** McWhinney SR, Abé C, Alda M, Benedetti F, Bøen E, Del Mar Bonnin C, et al. (76 author entries) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36846964/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Higher body mass index and bipolar disorder were each associated with thinner cortex, but not with smaller surface area, and their effects added up in many of the same regions.
^p36846964-additive

In most regions the number of psychiatric medication classes used together remained associated with thinner cortex after controlling for BMI; in the fusiform gyrus about a third of that association ran through higher BMI.
^p36846964-medication

## Scope as reported

Mixed-effects mega-analysis testing interaction and mediation between diagnosis and BMI, and the influence of medication on BMI-related associations.
^p36846964-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Body mass and medication load both track the illness and its treatment, so a cross-sectional case-control difference in thickness mixes diagnosis with health and treatment variables the design cannot fully separate.
^p36846964-caution-confounds
