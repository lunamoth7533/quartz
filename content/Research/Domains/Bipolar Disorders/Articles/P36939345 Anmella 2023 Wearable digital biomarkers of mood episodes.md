# Exploring Digital Biomarkers of Illness Activity in Mood Episodes: Hypotheses Generating and Model Development Study.

**Authors.** Anmella G, Corponi F, Li BM, Mas A, Sanabra M, Pacchiarotti I, et al. (34 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36939345/)

**Published.** 2023 in JMIR mHealth and uHealth.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.2196/45405](https://doi.org/10.2196/45405) · PMID [36939345](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36939345/) · PMCID [PMC10196899](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10196899/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Deep-learning models on wearable signals predicted episode severity within individuals with moderate accuracy (62%-85%) and episode polarity with about 70% accuracy, with acceleration, electrodermal activity and heart rate contributing most.
^p36939345-accuracy

Models generalised poorly to patients they had not been trained on, except for intra-individual models.
^p36939345-generalization

## Scope as reported

Exploratory study recording patients at acute, response and remission time points and controls in single sessions of about 48 hours.
^p36939345-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

With 12 patients, accuracy figures are unstable, and poor transfer to unseen patients means the signals are personal patterns rather than markers that can be applied to a new person.
^p36939345-caution-sample
