# Canadian Network for Mood and Anxiety Treatments (CANMAT) and International Society for Bipolar Disorders (ISBD) 2018 guidelines for the management of patients with bipolar disorder.

**Authors.** Yatham LN, Kennedy SH, Parikh SV, Schaffer A, Bond DJ, Frey BN, et al. (29 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29536616/)

**Published.** 2018 in Bipolar disorders (first online 2018-03-14).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/bdi.12609](https://doi.org/10.1111/bdi.12609) · PMID [29536616](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29536616/) · PMCID [PMC5947163](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5947163/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Treatments are ranked first-, second- or third-line by weighing the strength of efficacy evidence and clinical experience together with agreed judgements of safety, tolerability and the likelihood of a mood switch during treatment.
^p29536616-lines

The first-line options listed for bipolar I depression are mood stabilisers and antipsychotics, alone or combined; no antidepressant is among them.
^p29536616-depression

Treatments that work in an acute phase of bipolar I should generally be carried into maintenance, and antidepressants are named as one exception.
^p29536616-maintenance

Bipolar II disorder gets its own overview and recommendations.
^p29536616-bipolar2

## Scope as reported

The first full CANMAT edition since 2005, produced with the ISBD after interim updates; recommendations combine graded evidence with clinical experience and consensus, and the new hierarchical rankings cover first- and second-line options for bipolar I.
^p29536616-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Because line assignments blend trial evidence with consensus about safety and switch risk, a drug class's position reflects judgement about harms as well as efficacy data, and the 2018 rankings predate later trials and network meta-analyses.
^p29536616-caution-consensus
