# Prevalence and outcomes of rapid cycling bipolar disorder: Mixed method systematic meta-review.

**Authors.** Miola A, Fountoulakis KN, Baldessarini RJ, Veldic M, Solmi M, Rasgon N, Ozerdem A, Perugi G, Frye MA, Preti A · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37429185/)

**Published.** 2023 in Journal of psychiatric research (first online 2023-06-25).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jpsychires.2023.06.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2023.06.021) · PMID [37429185](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37429185/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Pooled one-year prevalence of rapid cycling was about 22% and lifetime prevalence about 36%, with higher lifetime prevalence in women.
^p37429185-prevalence

Associations with suicide attempts and with unsatisfactory response to mood stabilisers had strong (level 1) support; childhood maltreatment, mixed features, female sex and bipolar II had moderate (level 2) support.
^p37429185-strong

Genetic predisposition, metabolic or thyroid disturbance, antidepressant exposure and predominantly depressive polarity had weaker (level 3) support, and longer illness and immune-inflammatory dysfunction weaker still (level 4).
^p37429185-weak

## Scope as reported

Searches to April 2022; study quality rated with the NIH Quality Assessment Tool and evidence levels with Oxford criteria; 14 reports defined rapid cycling as at least four recurrences within the last year or within any single year, regardless of treatment response, and lifetime estimates came from 18 heterogeneous reports.
^p37429185-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Rapid cycling is a count of episodes over a chosen window, so its associations may partly reflect shared severity or treatment history; level-3 factors such as antidepressant exposure remain hypotheses rather than established causes.
