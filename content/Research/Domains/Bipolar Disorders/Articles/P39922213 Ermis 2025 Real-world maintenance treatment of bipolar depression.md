# Real-world effectiveness of pharmacological maintenance treatment of bipolar depression: a within-subject analysis in a Swedish nationwide cohort.

**Authors.** Ermis C, Taipale H, Tanskanen A, Vieta E, Correll CU, Mittendorfer-Rutz E, Tiihonen J · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39922213/)

**Published.** 2025 in The lancet. Psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/S2215-0366(24)00411-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(24)00411-5) · PMID [39922213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39922213/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Comparing each person's periods on and off medication, antidepressant-only, antipsychotic-only and some combination periods were associated with more depression-related hospitalisation, and mood-stabiliser-only periods with less.
^p39922213-classes

Lithium was the only specific monotherapy associated with fewer depression-related hospitalisations, and in head-to-head within-person comparisons it was associated with lower risk than antidepressant or antipsychotic monotherapy.
^p39922213-lithium

Antidepressant-only periods were also associated with more mania-related hospitalisation.
^p39922213-mania

## Scope as reported

Within-individual stratified Cox models on register data; the authors note that medicines are usually started when symptoms return, so every treatment may look less effective than it is when compared with non-use.
^p39922213-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Register admissions capture severe relapse only, and a within-person design removes stable confounders but not time-varying reasons for starting or stopping a medicine; this is group-level effectiveness evidence, not a prescription.
^p39922213-caution-register
