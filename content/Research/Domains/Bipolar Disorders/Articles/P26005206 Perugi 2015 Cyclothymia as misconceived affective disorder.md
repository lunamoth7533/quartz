# Cyclothymia reloaded: A reappraisal of the most misconceived affective disorder.

**Authors.** Perugi G, Hantouche E, Vannucchi G, Pinto O · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26005206/)

**Published.** 2015 in Journal of affective disorders (first online 2015-05-13).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2015.05.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2015.05.004) · PMID [26005206](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26005206/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Data from academic and outpatient centres indicate that 20-50% of people seeking help for mood, anxiety, impulsive or addictive problems prove cyclothymic on careful screening, and more if DSM-5 rules are broadened.
^p26005206-prevalence

The authors define cyclothymia as an exaggerated cyclothymic temperament: early-onset mood instability with marked reactivity, interpersonal and separation sensitivity, mixed features in depression, several comorbidities and a raised risk of impulsive and suicidal acts.
^p26005206-temperament

They argue it is a distinct form of bipolarity rather than a softer one, and that misdiagnosis with repeated antidepressant or sedative exposure risks turning it into a severe, borderline-like course.
^p26005206-distinct

## Scope as reported

Narrative review drawing on published research and on the authors' own clinical expertise; no systematic search is described.
^p26005206-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Prevalence figures from specialist settings under a broadened definition do not transfer to the community, and the harm attributed to antidepressant exposure rests on clinical observation rather than controlled comparison.
^p26005206-caution-advocacy
