# Occurrence of Psychosis and Bipolar Disorder in Individuals With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Treated With Stimulants: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

**Authors.** Salazar de Pablo G, Aymerich C, Chart-Pascual JP, Solmi M, Torres-Cortes J, Abdelhafez N, et al. (12 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40900605/)

**Published.** 2025 in JAMA psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2025.2311](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2025.2311) · PMID [40900605](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40900605/) · PMCID [PMC12409658](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12409658/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Among people with ADHD prescribed stimulants, pooled proportions later developing psychotic symptoms, a psychotic disorder or bipolar disorder were about 2.8%, 2.3% and 3.7%, with wide intervals and very high heterogeneity.
^p40900605-occurrence

Psychosis was more frequent after amphetamines than after methylphenidate (odds ratio about 1.6), and more frequent in studies with higher stimulant doses, more female participants, smaller samples, longer follow-up or North American settings.
^p40900605-moderators

The authors state that the included studies cannot establish causation, and call for randomised and mirror-image designs comparing exposed with unexposed people.
^p40900605-causality

## Scope as reported

Random-effects meta-analysis with subgroup analyses and meta-regression of studies of any design in DSM- or ICD-defined ADHD, searched to October 2024; quality rated with the Newcastle-Ottawa scale and the Cochrane risk-of-bias-2 tool.
^p40900605-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Without an unexposed ADHD comparison group, these proportions cannot be separated from the background rate of psychosis and bipolar disorder in ADHD, or from early bipolar symptoms being read as ADHD before a first episode.
^p40900605-caution-baseline
