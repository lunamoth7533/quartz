# Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha and its association with bipolar disorder: a systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** McIntyre A, Mozafar D, Liang M, Kapczinski F, Wollenhaupt-Aguiar B · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41082830/)

**Published.** 2025 in Journal of psychiatric research.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jpsychires.2025.09.077](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2025.09.077) · PMID [41082830](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41082830/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

TNF-alpha and its soluble receptors sTNFR1 and sTNFR2 were higher in people with bipolar disorder than in healthy controls.
^p41082830-elevated

Subgroup analyses found the elevations in mania, depression, bipolar I and bipolar II, but not in euthymic individuals, which the authors read as immune dysregulation concentrated in mood episodes.
^p41082830-state

## Scope as reported

PRISMA-guided review of PubMed, PsycINFO and Embase including 97 studies of blood or post-mortem brain tissue.
^p41082830-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Elevation during episodes but not in euthymia fits a state marker as well as a cause, and pooled cross-sectional studies cannot tell whether the marker precedes, accompanies or follows the episode.
^p41082830-caution-state
