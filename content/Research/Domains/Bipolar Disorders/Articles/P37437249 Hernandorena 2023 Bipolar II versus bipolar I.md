# Status of Type II vs. Type I Bipolar Disorder: Systematic Review with Meta-Analyses.

**Authors.** Hernandorena CV, Baldessarini RJ, Tondo L, Vázquez GH · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37437249/)

**Published.** 2023 in Harvard review of psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1097/HRP.0000000000000371](https://doi.org/10.1097/HRP.0000000000000371) · PMID [37437249](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37437249/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Compared with bipolar I, bipolar II went with more additional psychiatric diagnoses, more depressive episodes per year, more rapid cycling, more family psychiatric history, a higher share of women and more antidepressant treatment.
^p37437249-more

Bipolar II had fewer hospitalisations and psychotic features, less lithium or antipsychotic treatment and lower unemployment.
^p37437249-less

The types did not differ in suicide-attempt risk, onset age, education, marital status, hypomanias or manias per year, substance use, medical comorbidity or access to psychotherapy.
^p37437249-same

Bipolar II remained diagnostically stable over many years; the authors call for better recognition and more treatment research.
^p37437249-stable

## Scope as reported

Head-to-head comparisons on 21 factors, with about 12 reports per factor; the authors note that heterogeneity limits the firmness of some comparisons.
^p37437249-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The comparisons come from clinical samples in which bipolar II had been recognised, so people whose bipolar II is still labelled as unipolar depression, possibly a different mix, are not represented.
^p37437249-caution-selection
