# Prevalence and correlates of bipolar spectrum disorder in the world mental health survey initiative.

**Authors.** Merikangas KR, Jin R, He JP, Kessler RC, Lee S, Sampson NA, et al. (17 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21383262/)

**Published.** 2011 in Archives of general psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2011.12](https://doi.org/10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2011.12) · PMID [21383262](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21383262/) · PMCID [PMC3486639](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3486639/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Lifetime prevalence was 0.6% for bipolar I, 0.4% for bipolar II and 1.4% for subthreshold bipolar disorder, 2.4% in all.
^p21383262-prevalence

Manic and depressive severity and suicidal behaviour rose step by step from subthreshold disorder to bipolar I, while role impairment was similar across subtypes and more often severe in depression than in mania.
^p21383262-gradient

Three-quarters had another disorder, most often anxiety, and fewer than half had received mental health treatment, about a quarter in low-income countries.
^p21383262-comorbidity

The authors read these consistent gradients across countries as support for the spectrum concept.
^p21383262-validity

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional, face-to-face household surveys using a fully structured interview given by lay interviewers and DSM-IV criteria; prevalence varied between sites.
^p21383262-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Lay interviewers working from a fully structured schedule can over- or under-count past hypomania, and a severity gradient across categories that are themselves defined by severity thresholds is partly built into the design.
^p21383262-caution-lay
