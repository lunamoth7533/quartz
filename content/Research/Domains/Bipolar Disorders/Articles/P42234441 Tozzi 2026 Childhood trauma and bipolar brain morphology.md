# Brain Morphology Mediators of the Association of Childhood Trauma With Bipolar Disorder: An International ENIGMA Bipolar Disorder Working Group Study.

**Published.** 2026 in JAMA psychiatry.

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

More severe reported childhood trauma was directly associated with a higher likelihood of a bipolar disorder diagnosis.
^p42234441-association

Brain morphology mediated less than 1% of that association; the significant mediators were hippocampal volume and cortical thickness in the medial orbitofrontal and superior frontal gyri.
^p42234441-mediation

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional analysis of 19 cohorts collected between 2010 and 2022, using 75 grey-matter measures and the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire, with a mediation pathway specified in advance, leave-one-site-out cross-validation and permutation testing.
^p42234441-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Mediation estimated from a single cross-sectional scan cannot show that trauma changed the brain before illness began; the very small mediated share is the informative result, showing how little of a risk association structural measures carry.
^p42234441-caution-mediation
