# Association Between Schizophrenia-Related Polygenic Liability and the Occurrence and Level of Mood-Incongruent Psychotic Symptoms in Bipolar Disorder.

**Authors.** Allardyce J, Leonenko G, Hamshere M, Pardiñas AF, Forty L, Knott S, et al. (19 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29167880/)

**Published.** 2018 in JAMA psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.3485](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.3485) · PMID [29167880](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29167880/) · PMCID [PMC5833541](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5833541/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Schizophrenia polygenic scores showed a graded association across diagnoses: strongest for schizophrenia, then schizoaffective bipolar disorder, then bipolar I, with no clear association for bipolar II.
^p29167880-gradient

Within bipolar disorder the scores followed the kind of psychosis: highest with prominent mood-incongruent features, lower with mood-congruent psychosis and lowest where psychosis had never occurred.
^p29167880-incongruent

## Scope as reported

Multinomial and ordinal logistic regression on standardised scores built from the second Psychiatric Genomics Consortium schizophrenia genome-wide study; subtypes set by Research Diagnostic Criteria and psychosis rated from a structured interview schedule and a dimensional scale; data collected 2000-2013.
^p29167880-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Risk ratios of roughly 1.1 to 1.5 per standard deviation of score are population-level shifts; the gradient supports shared, graded liability across diagnoses, not a genetic test that sorts an individual into one of them.
^p29167880-caution-scores
