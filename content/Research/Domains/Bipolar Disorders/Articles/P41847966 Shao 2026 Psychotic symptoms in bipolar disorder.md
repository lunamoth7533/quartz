# Comorbid Schizophrenia and Psychotic Symptoms in Patients With Bipolar Disorder: A Meta-Analysis of the Global Literature.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

A co-occurring schizophrenia diagnosis was uncommon, pooled at about 8% of people with bipolar disorder.
^p41847966-schizophrenia

Mood-incongruent psychosis, defined broadly, was common, pooled at about 47%.
^p41847966-incongruent

Psychotic symptoms were frequent, above all in bipolar I and during manic episodes, and delusions were more common than hallucinations or thought disorder.
^p41847966-type

The authors describe substantial overlap in phenomenology between bipolar disorder and schizophrenia and propose comparing patients who share symptoms, whatever their diagnosis.
^p41847966-overlap

## Scope as reported

Single-proportion meta-analysis with double-arcsine transformation of studies found through Medline, PsycINFO and Web of Science title and abstract searches plus forward and backward citation tracking.
^p41847966-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Pooling 285 studies mixes diagnostic systems, settings and definitions of mood incongruence, and the abstract reports no heterogeneity estimate, so the 47% shows that incongruent features are common rather than giving a precise rate.
^p41847966-caution-definitions
