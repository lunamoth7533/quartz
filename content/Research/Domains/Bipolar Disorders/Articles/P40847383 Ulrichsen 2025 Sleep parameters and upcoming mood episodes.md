# Can Sleep Parameters Predict Upcoming Mood Episodes in Bipolar Disorder?

**Authors.** Ulrichsen A, Mühlbauer E, Hartnagel LM, Severus E, Cleare A, Jauhar S, Bauer M, Ebner-Priemer U · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40847383/)

**Published.** 2025 in Bipolar disorders.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/bdi.70054](https://doi.org/10.1111/bdi.70054) · PMID [40847383](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40847383/) · PMCID [PMC12483305](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12483305/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Waking up later was associated with the prodromal stage of depression and was the only significant change in average sleep before an episode.
^p40847383-waking

Greater variability of sleep duration, time in bed and waking time went with prodromal depression, while less variable times of falling asleep and waking went with prodromal hypomania or mania.
^p40847383-variability

The authors describe these analyses as exploratory and call for further study.
^p40847383-exploratory

## Scope as reported

Person-centred multilevel logit models compared prodromal periods with euthymia, with Bonferroni-Holm correction for multiple tests.
^p40847383-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

These are group-averaged associations in 29 people: they show sleep features that differ before episodes on average, but cannot say whether the change triggers an episode or is its first symptom, nor set a threshold that forecasts one person's next episode.
^p40847383-caution-group
