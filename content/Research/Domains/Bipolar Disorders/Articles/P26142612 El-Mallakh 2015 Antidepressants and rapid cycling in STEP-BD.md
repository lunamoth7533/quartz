# Antidepressants worsen rapid-cycling course in bipolar depression: A STEP-BD randomized clinical trial.

**Authors.** El-Mallakh RS, Vöhringer PA, Ostacher MM, Baldassano CF, Holtzman NS, Whitham EA, Thommi SB, Goodwin FK, Ghaemi SN · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26142612/)

**Published.** 2015 in Journal of affective disorders (first online 2015-06-10).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2015.04.054](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2015.04.054) · PMID [26142612](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26142612/) · PMCID [PMC4519402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4519402/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Among patients who continued antidepressants, those with rapid cycling had roughly three times as many mood episodes and depressive episodes per year as those without, and spent about 29% less time in remission.
^p26142612-episodes

Among patients randomised to stop the antidepressant, rapid-cycling and non-rapid-cycling patients did not differ in this way.
^p26142612-discontinue

## Scope as reported

Rapid cycling was an a priori secondary predictor in a randomised continuation-versus-discontinuation trial; outcomes were percent time well and number of episodes, and analyses within the rapid-cycling subgroup were limited by sample size.
^p26142612-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A subgroup comparison in 68 people selected for a good antidepressant response is a signal to test, not a rule for rapid cycling in general; randomisation applied to continuing or stopping the drug, not to rapid-cycling status.
^p26142612-caution-subgroup
