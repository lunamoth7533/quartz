# Single-molecule DNA analysis implicates brain mitochondria pathology in bipolar disorder.

**Authors.** Ohtani H, Ichikawa R, Mori K, Kato T, Nishioka M · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40442452/)

**Published.** 2025 in Molecular psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41380-025-03071-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03071-4) · PMID [40442452](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40442452/) · PMCID [PMC12436149](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12436149/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Duplex barcoded sequencing at single-molecule resolution found ultra-rare heteroplasmic mitochondrial variants above 1% allele fraction enriched in bipolar disorder, including potentially pathogenic ones such as m.3243A>G, loss-of-function and rRNA variants.
^p40442452-rare

There was no general increase in low-level heteroplasmic variants in bipolar disorder, and the authors suggest that a subset of patients might be stratified by ultra-rare mitochondrial variants.
^p40442452-general

## Scope as reported

Case-control sequencing of post-mortem brain DNA with a schizophrenia comparison group, using duplex molecular barcoding.
^p40442452-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

A variant enrichment in a small post-mortem series points to a possible subgroup; it does not show that mitochondrial variants cause episodes, and it does not describe most people with the diagnosis.
^p40442452-caution-subset
