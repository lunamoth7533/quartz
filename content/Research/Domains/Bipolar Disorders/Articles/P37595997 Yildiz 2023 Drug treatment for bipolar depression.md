# Comparative efficacy and tolerability of pharmacological interventions for acute bipolar depression in adults: a systematic review and network meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Yildiz A, Siafis S, Mavridis D, Vieta E, Leucht S · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37595997/)

**Published.** 2023 in The lancet. Psychiatry.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/S2215-0366(23)00199-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(23)00199-2) · PMID [37595997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37595997/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

With moderate confidence, lamotrigine, cariprazine, lumateperone, lurasidone, olanzapine, quetiapine and the olanzapine-fluoxetine combination reduced depressive symptoms more than placebo, with standardised mean differences from about 0.16 (lamotrigine) to 0.41 (the combination).
^p37595997-efficacy

Antidepressants as a class appeared efficacious but carried a higher risk of switching into mania than antipsychotics.
^p37595997-antidepressants

Several other medicines might also work, but only with low or very low confidence, and side-effect profiles differed between medicines.
^p37595997-other

## Scope as reported

Masked randomised trials of 2-16 weeks, including monotherapy, add-on and combination designs; co-primary outcomes were depressive symptoms and manic switch; confidence rated with CINeMA; searches to April 2023.
^p37595997-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A class-level switch estimate pools very different antidepressants and trial designs over a few weeks; it ranks average short-term risk and cannot give an individual's risk or the long-term picture.
