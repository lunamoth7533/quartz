# Limited evidence of autocorrelation signaling upcoming affective episodes: a 12-month e-diary study in patients with bipolar disorder.

**Authors.** Ludwig VM, Reinhard I, Mühlbauer E, Hill H, Severus WE, Bauer M, Ritter P, Ebner-Priemer UW · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38284217/)

**Published.** 2024 in Psychological medicine.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1017/S0033291723003811](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291723003811) · PMID [38284217](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38284217/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Self-rated current mood fell during the two weeks before a depressive episode but did not change before hypomanic or manic episodes.
^p38284217-selfreport

Rising autocorrelation of daily mood, proposed as a sign of an approaching transition, did not predict prodromal phases of depression; it rose mainly during depressive episodes and fell in the late prodrome of hypomania or mania.
^p38284217-autocorrelation

## Scope as reported

Exploratory analyses in one cohort comparing prodromal phases and episodes with euthymia, with affective status set by expert interview every two weeks.
^p38284217-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Forty-nine episodes in 29 people is a small base for testing a transition metric, so the null result limits confidence in autocorrelation as a warning sign without ruling it out.
^p38284217-caution-signal
