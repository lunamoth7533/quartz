# Lithium and lamotrigine for the treatment of bipolar II disorder - a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials.

**Authors.** Fredskild MU, Bruun CF, Vinberg M, Faurholt-Jepsen M, Kessing LV, Munkholm K · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40288449/)

**Published.** 2025 in Journal of affective disorders (first online 2025-04-25).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2025.04.125](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2025.04.125) · PMID [40288449](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40288449/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Whether lithium reduces new mood episodes of either polarity in bipolar II compared with placebo was very uncertain (risk ratio 0.86, interval including no effect; four trials).
^p40288449-lithium

Lamotrigine-placebo trials could not be pooled, and lithium against lamotrigine for depressive severity was also very uncertain (two trials).
^p40288449-lamotrigine

Across 10 trials with 645 patients, certainty was graded very low for every outcome and most outcomes raised some risk-of-bias concern; the authors call for large, well-conducted trials in bipolar II.
^p40288449-certainty

## Scope as reported

Pairwise random-effects meta-analysis with risk-of-bias assessment and GRADE certainty ratings; PubMed, Embase, PsycINFO and grey literature searched to November 2024.
^p40288449-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Very low certainty means the trials can show neither benefit nor its absence; practice fills the gap by extrapolating from bipolar I, the very assumption these trials were too few to test.
^p40288449-caution-absence
