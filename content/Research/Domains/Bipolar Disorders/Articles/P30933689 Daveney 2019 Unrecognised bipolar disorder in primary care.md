# Unrecognized bipolar disorder in patients with depression managed in primary care: A systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Daveney J, Panagioti M, Waheed W, Esmail A · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30933689/)

**Published.** 2019 in General hospital psychiatry (first online 2019-03-27).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2019.03.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2019.03.006) · PMID [30933689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30933689/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

About 17% of primary-care patients with a depression diagnosis had bipolar disorder that had not been recognised.
^p30933689-prevalence

The estimate was 14% where bipolar disorder was assessed by questionnaire and 22% where it was assessed by clinical interview, a difference that was not significant; neither risk of bias nor publication bias explained the pooled figure.
^p30933689-method

## Scope as reported

Random-effects pooling of quantitative observational studies, with risk of bias rated on the Newcastle-Ottawa scale and searches run to January 2019.
^p30933689-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

A cross-sectional count shows how many cases were missed at one time point; it cannot say how many would have been recognised later in routine care, and questionnaire-defined cases include false positives.
