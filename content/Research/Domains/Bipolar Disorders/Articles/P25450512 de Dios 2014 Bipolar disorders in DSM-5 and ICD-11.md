# Bipolar disorders in the new DSM-5 and ICD-11 classifications.

**Authors.** de Dios C, Goikolea JM, Colom F, Moreno C, Vieta E · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25450512/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Both systems now require increased activity, not only elated or irritable mood, before hypomania or mania can be diagnosed, which makes the threshold more restrictive.
^p25450512-activity

The main novelties described for DSM-5 were dimensional symptom ratings, subthreshold states within the bipolar spectrum, course specifiers such as mixed symptoms, and removal of the mixed episode.
^p25450512-dsm5

Including bipolar II disorder in ICD-11 was a point of convergence; the draft diverged by keeping the mixed episode, keeping bereavement as an exclusion for depressive episodes and retaining a mixed anxiety-depression category.
^p25450512-icd11

## Scope as reported

Critical review written while ICD-11 was still being drafted; it compares published DSM-5 criteria with ICD-11 proposals, and the authors expect further changes to be needed for harmonisation.
^p25450512-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Statements about ICD-11 describe the draft, so the approved text should be checked before they are relied on; and because both systems draw the episode threshold by convention, a sample recruited under one set of rules is not interchangeable with one recruited under the other.
^p25450512-caution-draft
