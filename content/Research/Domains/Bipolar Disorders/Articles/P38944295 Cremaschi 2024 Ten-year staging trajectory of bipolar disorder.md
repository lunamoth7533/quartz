# The progression trajectory of Bipolar Disorder: results from the application of a staging model over a ten-year observation.

**Authors.** Cremaschi L, Macellaro M, Girone N, Bosi M, Cesana BM, Ambrogi F, Dell'Osso B · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38944295/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of affective disorders.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2024.06.094](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.06.094) · PMID [38944295](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38944295/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Applied to the same 100 patients over ten years, the staging model showed a high hazard of moving from stage 2 to stage 3: the probability of staying in stage 2 fell to 14% after three years.
^p38944295-transition

Bipolar II was associated with moving from stage 1 to stage 2, while more than three lifetime episodes and a predominantly elevated polarity were associated with moving from stage 3 to stage 4.
^p38944295-predictors

## Scope as reported

A retrospective application of one staging model to a previously described sample of 100 patients at four time points across ten years, analysed with multistate models and Markov models with stratified hazards.
^p38944295-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Transitions in one retrospectively staged clinic sample describe that sample's course under its care; staging rules are themselves contested, so the hazards are model-dependent rather than natural-history constants.
^p38944295-caution-staging
