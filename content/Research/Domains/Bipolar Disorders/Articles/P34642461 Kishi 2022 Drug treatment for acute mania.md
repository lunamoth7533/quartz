# Pharmacological treatment for bipolar mania: a systematic review and network meta-analysis of double-blind randomized controlled trials.

**Authors.** Kishi T, Ikuta T, Matsuda Y, Sakuma K, Okuya M, Nomura I, Hatano M, Iwata N · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34642461/)

**Published.** 2022 in Molecular psychiatry (first online 2021-10-12).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41380-021-01334-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-021-01334-4) · PMID [34642461](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34642461/) · PMCID [PMC9054678](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9054678/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Thirteen medicines produced more responders than placebo: nine antipsychotics (aripiprazole, asenapine, cariprazine, haloperidol, olanzapine, paliperidone, quetiapine, risperidone, ziprasidone), lithium, the anticonvulsants carbamazepine and valproate, and tamoxifen.
^p34642461-efficacy

Only aripiprazole, olanzapine, quetiapine and risperidone also had fewer all-cause dropouts than placebo, while topiramate had more.
^p34642461-acceptability

## Scope as reported

Random-effects network meta-analysis of oral monotherapy trials lasting at least 10 days, excluding trials that allowed rescue antipsychotics; databases searched to March 2021; primary outcomes were response and all-cause discontinuation.
^p34642461-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Trials averaging about four weeks answer the acute-phase question only: beating placebo on response says nothing about maintenance, and a ranking across drugs is a group-level summary that guidelines weigh against safety, not a choice for any one person.
^p34642461-caution-short
