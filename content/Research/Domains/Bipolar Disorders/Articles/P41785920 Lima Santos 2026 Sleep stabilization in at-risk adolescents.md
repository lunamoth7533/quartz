# Unstable sleep and rest-activity rhythms in adolescents at-risk for bipolar disorder: Links to mood symptoms and the effect of sleep stabilization.

**Authors.** Lima Santos JP, Keller L, Hartman AG, Chan S, Phillips ML, Soehner AM · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41785920/)

**Published.** 2026 in Journal of affective disorders.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2026.121558](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2026.121558) · PMID [41785920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41785920/) · PMCID [PMC13191431](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13191431/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Two weeks of actigraphy showed no group differences in sleep or rest-activity measures, but among high-risk adolescents only, more variable sleep duration went with more depressive and manic symptoms, and weaker circadian robustness with more mania symptoms.
^p41785920-groups

A two-week protocol targeting regular sleep timing and duration reduced sleep variability and improved the circadian function index, with only trend-level reductions in subthreshold mania symptoms.
^p41785920-stabilization

## Scope as reported

Cross-sectional actigraphy comparison of 50 adolescents plus a two-week manipulation in 11 high-risk participants, with depression and mania rating scales before and after.
^p41785920-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

The manipulation had 11 participants and no control arm, so it shows that a routine protocol can shift rhythm measures over two weeks, not that it prevents bipolar disorder.
^p41785920-caution-uncontrolled
