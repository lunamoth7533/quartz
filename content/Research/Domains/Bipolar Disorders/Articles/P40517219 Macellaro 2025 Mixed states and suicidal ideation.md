# The influence of depressive and manic symptoms on suicidal ideation in mixed mood states.

**Authors.** Macellaro M, Cafaro R, Kelly CM, Ostacher MJ, Dell'Osso B, Lyu J, et al. (14 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40517219/)

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Depressive symptom severity was by far the strongest correlate of suicidal ideation (odds ratio about 22); moderate and milder hypomanic or manic symptoms were also associated, more weakly.
^p40517219-depression

When mood states were categorised with clinical thresholds, hypomania or mania with mixed features showed the highest ideation; in models using continuous scores, only depressive severity predicted ideation.
^p40517219-mixed

The authors conclude that research on suicidality should combine dimensional and categorical classifications of mood state.
^p40517219-modelling

## Scope as reported

Generalized estimating equations on archived network data from 1995-2002; mood states came from clinician-rated depression and mania scales, and suicidal ideation from a single inventory item.
^p40517219-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Because the mixed-state signal appears or disappears with the way mood is coded, the study shows that classification choices shape the conclusion; it does not settle whether mixed features carry risk beyond depressive severity.
^p40517219-caution-coding
