# Switch to mania after acute antidepressant treatment for bipolar depression: a systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials.

**Authors.** Oliva V, De Prisco M, La Spina E, Paolucci S, Fico G, Anmella G, et al. (15 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40823496/)

**Published.** 2025 in EClinicalMedicine (first online 2025-08-07).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.eclinm.2025.103413](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2025.103413) · PMID [40823496](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40823496/) · PMCID [PMC12355415](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12355415/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

No antidepressant raised the rate of switching into mania significantly above placebo, although some estimates pointed toward more switching.
^p40823496-placebo

Venlafaxine had the highest, non-significant risk estimate (risk ratio about 4.5, very wide interval) and was the only drug with consistent switch signals across individual studies.
^p40823496-venlafaxine

Most evidence concerned add-on treatment rather than monotherapy; heterogeneity was low, sensitivity analyses agreed, and overall confidence was rated low.
^p40823496-regimen

The authors keep antidepressants as an option, mainly as add-ons, and call for studies of long-term safety.
^p40823496-interpretation

## Scope as reported

Frequentist network meta-analysis of randomised trials searched to February 2025 across six databases, with sensitivity analyses by regimen, severity, switch definition, setting, comorbidity, duration and sponsorship; CINeMA confidence ratings and a preregistered protocol.
^p40823496-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Thirteen short trials with few switches give wide intervals, so a risk that is not significantly higher than placebo is not ruled out; acute trials also say nothing about switching during longer use.
^p40823496-caution-acute
