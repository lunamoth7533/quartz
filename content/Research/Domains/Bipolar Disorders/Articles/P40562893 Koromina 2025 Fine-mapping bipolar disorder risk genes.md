# Fine-mapping genomic loci refines bipolar disorder risk genes.

**Authors.** Koromina M, Ravi A, Panagiotaropoulou G, Schilder BM, Humphrey J, Braun A, et al. (133 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40562893/)

**Published.** 2025 in Nature neuroscience.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1038/s41593-025-01998-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01998-z) · PMID [40562893](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40562893/) · PMCID [PMC12229890](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12229890/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Fine-mapping the 64 loci prioritised 17 likely causal SNPs, which were mapped to genes using variant annotations, brain cell-type epigenomics, brain quantitative trait loci and rare-variant exome results.
^p40562893-snps

Convergent evidence pointed to genes involved in neurotransmission and neurodevelopment, including SCN2A, TRANK1, CACNA1B, FURIN and THRA, which the authors present as candidates for functional experiments.
^p40562893-genes

Using the fine-mapped effect sizes made polygenic risk scores for bipolar disorder perform better in several ancestry groups.
^p40562893-prs

## Scope as reported

Runs several statistical and functional fine-mapping approaches over loci found in earlier studies and presents a high-throughput pipeline; the named genes are prioritised candidates rather than tested mechanisms.
^p40562893-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Resolving 17 likely causal SNPs from 64 loci shows how much of each locus remains unresolved; better score performance across ancestries is a group-level gain, not individual prediction.
