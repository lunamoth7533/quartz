# Schizoaffective Disorder in the DSM-5.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

DSM-IV schizoaffective disorder was unreliable, unstable over time and of doubtful validity, and was used far more often than its originators intended.
^p23707642-problem

In DSM-5, psychosis that occurs only during mood episodes points to a mood disorder with psychotic features; two weeks or more of psychosis without prominent mood symptoms points to schizoaffective disorder or schizophrenia.
^p23707642-rule

DSM-5 turns schizoaffective disorder from an episode diagnosis into a judgement about the whole illness: full mood episodes must be present for most of its active and residual course since psychosis began.
^p23707642-lifecourse

## Scope as reported

An account of the DSM-5 criteria change and its rationale; the expected gains - clearer separation from schizophrenia with mood symptoms, fewer diagnoses and greater stability once made - are stated as expectations rather than tested results.
^p23707642-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Judging whether mood episodes filled most of an illness's course relies on retrospective recall and records, so the rule replaces a vague boundary with a clearer one that is still hard to measure.
^p23707642-caution-duration
