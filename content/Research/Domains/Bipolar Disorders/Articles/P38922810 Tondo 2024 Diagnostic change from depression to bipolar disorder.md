# Antidepressant-associated diagnostic change from major depressive to bipolar disorder.

**Authors.** Tondo L, Miola A, Pinna M, Contu M, Baldessarini RJ · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38922810/)

**Published.** 2024 in Acta psychiatrica Scandinavica.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/acps.13721](https://doi.org/10.1111/acps.13721) · PMID [38922810](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38922810/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

About 6.7% of patients first diagnosed with major depressive disorder became hypomanic or manic during antidepressant treatment and were rediagnosed with bipolar disorder, most of them (about 77%) with bipolar II.
^p38922810-rate

Independent predictors of the change included a family history of bipolar disorder, more depressive episodes per year, cyclothymic temperament, suicidal ideation or behaviour, unemployment and having fewer children.
^p38922810-predictors

## Scope as reported

Comparison of 3212 initially depressed patients using bivariate and multivariate statistics; converters were also compared with cases of spontaneous bipolar disorder.
^p38922810-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Switching during antidepressant treatment is observed, not assigned, so the study cannot separate a drug-associated change from a bipolar course that would have declared itself anyway.
^p38922810-caution-attribution
