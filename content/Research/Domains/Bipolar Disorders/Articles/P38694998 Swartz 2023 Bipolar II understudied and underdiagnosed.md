## Reported findings (checked abstract)

People with bipolar II typically have symptoms for more than ten years before the correct diagnosis; the authors link misdiagnosis to overuse of monoaminergic antidepressants, poorer functioning and lower quality of life.
^p38694998-delay

Treatment studies specific to bipolar II are limited, and results from bipolar I studies are often applied to bipolar II without direct evidence.
^p38694998-extrapolation

The review finds bipolar I and II similar in suicide risk and in depression predominating over the course, but different in treatment response, for example to antidepressants.
^p38694998-similar

## Scope as reported

Narrative review; no systematic search or pooled estimates are described.
^p38694998-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

The decade-long delay and the harms attributed to misdiagnosis are summary claims from a narrative review; their size depends on cohorts that the abstract does not name.
