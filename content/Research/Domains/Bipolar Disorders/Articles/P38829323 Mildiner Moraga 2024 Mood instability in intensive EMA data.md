# Evidence for mood instability in patients with bipolar disorder: Applying multilevel hidden Markov modeling to intensive longitudinal ecological momentary assessment data.

**Authors.** Mildiner Moraga S, Bos FM, Doornbos B, Bruggeman R, van der Krieke L, Snippe E, Aarts E · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38829323/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of psychopathology and clinical science.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1037/abn0000915](https://doi.org/10.1037/abn0000915) · PMID [38829323](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38829323/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

A multilevel hidden Markov model fitted to the momentary reports recovered four mood states - neutral, elevated, mixed and lowered - that lined up with the weekly symptom scores.
^p38829323-states

Patients stayed in one state for less than a day on average, almost half showed mood instability, and elevated and lowered states were interspersed by mixed states; the authors read this as instability persisting even in relatively euthymic periods.
^p38829323-instability

## Scope as reported

Twenty patients with bipolar disorder answered five self-report prompts a day on manic and depressive constructs for four months (about 9,820 assessments), with weekly mania and depression questionnaires as the reference; states were derived statistically from the self-reports.
^p38829323-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Model-derived momentary states are not diagnosed episodes: with 20 participants and self-report alone, the study shows what dense sampling can reveal, not how common instability is across bipolar I.
^p38829323-caution-states
