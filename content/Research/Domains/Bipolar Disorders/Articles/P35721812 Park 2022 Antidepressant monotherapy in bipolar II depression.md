# Short Term Second-Generation Antidepressant Monotherapy in Acute Depressive Episodes of Bipolar II Disorder: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

**Authors.** Park JH, Nuñez NA, Gardea-Resendez M, Gerberi DJ, Breitinger S, Veldic M, Frye MA, Singh B · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35721812/)

**Published.** 2022 in Psychopharmacology bulletin.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.64719/pb.4433](https://doi.org/10.64719/pb.4433) · PMID [35721812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35721812/) · PMCID [PMC9172552](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9172552/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

In two double-blind trials, response to venlafaxine or sertraline monotherapy did not differ significantly from lithium monotherapy (risk ratio 1.44, interval including no difference).
^p35721812-response

Treatment-emergent affective switch rates did not differ significantly between antidepressant and lithium monotherapy.
^p35721812-switch

Fewer patients stopped antidepressants than lithium because of side effects, while all-cause dropout was similar; the authors call for short- and long-term trials in bipolar II.
^p35721812-tolerability

## Scope as reported

Mantel-Haenszel random-effects pooling of two small double-blind trials; searches to March 2021; outcomes were response, switch and discontinuation.
^p35721812-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Two trials and 223 patients cannot rule out a clinically relevant difference in switching, so the absence of a significant difference is absence of evidence rather than evidence of safety.
^p35721812-caution-power
