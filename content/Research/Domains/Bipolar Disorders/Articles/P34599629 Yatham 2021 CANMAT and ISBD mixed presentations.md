# Canadian Network for Mood and Anxiety Treatments (CANMAT) and International Society for Bipolar Disorders (ISBD) recommendations for the management of patients with bipolar disorder with mixed presentations.

**Authors.** Yatham LN, Chakrabarty T, Bond DJ, Schaffer A, Beaulieu S, Parikh SV, et al. (29 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34599629/)

**Published.** 2021 in Bipolar disorders (first online 2021-11-01).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/bdi.13135](https://doi.org/10.1111/bdi.13135) · PMID [34599629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34599629/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

For DSM-5 episodes with mixed features, whether manic or depressive, no treatment reached the threshold for a first-line recommendation.
^p34599629-firstline

For depression with mixed features, the options named are second-line: two atypical antipsychotics, cariprazine and lurasidone.
^p34599629-depression

DSM-IV mixed episodes, researched for longer, have first-line options; maintenance evidence after a DSM-5 mixed presentation is very limited, and its third-line options rest on expert opinion.
^p34599629-maintenance

## Scope as reported

An overview of research on mixed presentations, with recommendations rated by a modified CANMAT/ISBD method; the authors acknowledge that high-quality data are scarce and that much of the guidance depends on expert opinion. The 2018 guidelines had commented on mixed features without giving specific recommendations.
^p34599629-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

With no first-line option, every recommendation here rests on sparse, partly post hoc data and expert opinion, and an option's absence from the list is not, on its own, evidence that it causes harm.
