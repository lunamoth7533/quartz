# Sleep quality predicts future mood symptoms in adolescents with bipolar disorder.

**Authors.** Stepan ME, Franzen PL, Teresi GI, Rode N, Goldstein TR · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38917889/)

**Published.** 2024 in Journal of affective disorders.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2024.06.069](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2024.06.069) · PMID [38917889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38917889/) · PMCID [PMC12208217](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12208217/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Poorer sleep quality at the six-month point was followed a month later by more depression, more hypomania and more suicidal ideation, and sleep quality was worse before months with a suicide attempt.
^p38917889-lag

The reverse path also appeared: worse depression and suicidal ideation at several time points predicted worse later sleep quality.
^p38917889-reverse

## Scope as reported

Sleep quality was measured with the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index quarterly during treatment and twice in follow-up, and mood was rated retrospectively each week by an independent clinician; the authors note the retrospective ratings and that the index may miss relevant sleep dimensions.
^p38917889-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Lagged associations in both directions fit a reciprocal process, which is why sleep change cannot be read as a trigger from observational data alone.
^p38917889-caution-direction
