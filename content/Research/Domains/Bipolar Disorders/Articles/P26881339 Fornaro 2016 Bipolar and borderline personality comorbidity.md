# The prevalence and predictors of bipolar and borderline personality disorders comorbidity: Systematic review and meta-analysis.

**Authors.** Fornaro M, Orsolini L, Marini S, De Berardis D, Perna G, Valchera A, Ganança L, Solmi M, Veronese N, Stubbs B · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26881339/)

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jad.2016.01.040](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2016.01.040) · PMID [26881339](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26881339/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

After trim-and-fill adjustment, about 22% of people with bipolar disorder also had borderline personality disorder, and about 19% of people with borderline personality disorder had bipolar disorder.
^p26881339-prevalence

Comorbidity was higher in bipolar II samples (about 38%) and in North American studies, and lower where samples were older or included more men.
^p26881339-type2

The authors conclude that roughly one in five people carry both diagnoses, but that the qualitative and quantitative evidence underlines differences between the disorders more than similarities.
^p26881339-differences

## Scope as reported

Random-effects meta-analysis with meta-regression, searches to November 2015; the authors note a paucity of longitudinal or controlled studies and of accurate treatment records.
^p26881339-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Comorbidity rates depend on whether mood episodes and personality features were assessed separately; affective instability counted toward both diagnoses inflates overlap, so these rates bound the differential problem rather than settle it.
^p26881339-caution-overlap
