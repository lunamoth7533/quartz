# Pramipexole in addition to mood stabilisers for treatment-resistant bipolar depression: the PAX-BD randomised double-blind placebo-controlled trial.

**Authors.** McAllister-Williams H, Goudie N, Azim L, Bartle V, Berger M, Butcher C, et al. (36 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40455248/)

**Published.** 2025 in Health technology assessment (Winchester, England).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.3310/HBFC1953](https://doi.org/10.3310/HBFC1953) · PMID [40455248](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40455248/) · PMCID [PMC12146921](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12146921/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Only 39 of a planned 290 participants were randomised; at 12 weeks depressive symptoms fell more with pramipexole than placebo, a medium-sized difference that was not statistically significant.
^p40455248-primary

Some secondary outcomes favoured pramipexole, and it was associated with more hypomanic or manic symptoms, which appeared lower with a co-prescribed antipsychotic; the authors recommend no change in practice and call for a larger replication.
^p40455248-secondary

## Scope as reported

Multicentre trial across 21 NHS trusts and health boards; recruitment during the COVID-19 pandemic and early closure left a small sample and variable follow-up, limitations the authors state.
^p40455248-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

An underpowered trial that misses its primary outcome is inconclusive rather than negative, so the dopamine-agonist side of the bipolar depression evidence remains thinly tested.
^p40455248-caution-power
