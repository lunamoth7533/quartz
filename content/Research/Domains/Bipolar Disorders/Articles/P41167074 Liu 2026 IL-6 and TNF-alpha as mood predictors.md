# Interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor alpha as predictive biomarkers for depression and mania in young adults with depressive and bipolar disorder.

**Authors.** Liu S, Morgan-Fleming GM, Denenny DM, Frey SJ, Irwin MR, Miklowitz DJ, Ho TC · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41167074/)

**Published.** 2026 in Journal of psychiatric research (first online 2025-10-21).
**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1016/j.jpsychires.2025.10.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2025.10.035) · PMID [41167074](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41167074/) · PMCID [PMC13180435](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13180435/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Higher baseline IL-6 went with more severe and more persistent depressive symptoms, yet rises in IL-6 over the study went with falling depressive symptoms.
^p41167074-il6

Higher baseline TNF-alpha predicted declining mania symptoms, and rises in TNF-alpha went with lower mania severity; the authors present the study as exploratory and call for replication.
^p41167074-tnf

## Scope as reported

Linear mixed-effects models adjusted for age, BMI and gender over six months; several associations were reported with uncorrected p-values.
^p41167074-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Opposite-signed links for baseline levels and for change, in 55 people and two markers, are fragile; they argue against a simple 'more inflammation, more symptoms' reading rather than establishing a predictor.
^p41167074-caution-direction
