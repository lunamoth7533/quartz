# Ketamine and other glutamate receptor modulators for depression in adults with bipolar disorder.

**Authors.** Dean RL, Marquardt T, et al. (12 authors) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34623633/)

**Published.** 2021 in The Cochrane database of systematic reviews (first online 2021-10-08).

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1002/14651858.CD011611.pub3](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011611.pub3) · PMID [34623633](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34623633/) · PMCID [PMC8499740](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8499740/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Of the glutamate modulators studied, only ketamine beat placebo, on response 24 hours after a single infusion added to mood stabilisers, in two trials with 33 participants and low-certainty evidence.
^p34623633-response

Ketamine showed no clear advantage for remission, and the authors describe its antidepressant effect as rapid but transient.
^p34623633-remission

They warn that ketamine's psychotomimetic effects may have broken blinding, and that data in bipolar depression are far thinner than in unipolar depression.
^p34623633-blinding

## Scope as reported

Cochrane review searched to July 2020; ketamine trials used one intravenous administration, most modulators were tested as add-ons to mood stabilisers, and certainty was low to very low.
^p34623633-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Two small trials measured a 24-hour response in bipolar depression; they say nothing about maintenance, switching over time or repeated treatment, the questions that phase-specific bipolar evidence needs answered.
^p34623633-caution-phase
