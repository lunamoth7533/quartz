# Study of the different sleep disturbances during the prodromal phase of depression and mania in bipolar disorders.

**Authors.** Basquin L, Maruani J, Leseur J, Mauries S, Bazin B, Pineau G, Henry C, Lejoyeux M, Geoffroy PA · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38653574/)

**Published.** 2024 in Bipolar disorders.

**Identifiers.** DOI [10.1111/bdi.13429](https://doi.org/10.1111/bdi.13429) · PMID [38653574](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38653574/)

**Verification.** Abstract-level check via PubMed on 2026-09-27 (metadata matched by PMID). Full text was not read; read status is queued.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

Asked about the period before the current episode, 59% of patients spontaneously reported sleep disturbance; after structured questions about specific problems, 83% did.
^p38653574-prevalence

Reported onset came earlier before depression than before mania - about 90 versus 35 days in spontaneous reports - and insomnia complaints were the most frequent disturbance.
^p38653574-timing

The authors argue for systematic questioning about sleep and for educating patients about sleep prodromes to support early intervention.
^p38653574-education

## Scope as reported

Standardised questionnaires, structured interviews and self-reports collected during the current episode about the period before it, in 41 patients.
^p38653574-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the paper.

Prodromes reconstructed during an episode are open to recall bias, and the rise from 59% to 83% under prompting shows how much the estimate depends on the questions asked.
^p38653574-caution-recall
