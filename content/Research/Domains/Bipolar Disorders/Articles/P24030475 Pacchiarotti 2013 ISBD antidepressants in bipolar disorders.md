# The International Society for Bipolar Disorders (ISBD) task force report on antidepressant use in bipolar disorders.

## Reported findings (checked abstract)

The task force found antidepressants widely used in bipolar disorder on a weak base of efficacy and safety evidence, with few rigorous long-term prevention trials and insufficient evidence of benefit when they are added to mood stabilisers.
^p24030475-evidence

Switching into hypomania, mania or mixed states was the main safety concern; SSRIs and bupropion may carry lower switch rates than tricyclic and tetracyclic antidepressants and SNRIs.
^p24030475-switch

Antidepressant-associated mood elevation appeared more frequent and severe in bipolar I than in bipolar II, so for bipolar I the task force limited antidepressants to use alongside mood-stabilising medication.
^p24030475-bipolar-i

## Scope as reported

Delphi consensus of an expert task force, built on a systematic literature review; because data were limited the task force could not broadly endorse antidepressants, while accepting that some individual patients may benefit.
^p24030475-limit

## Library appraisal

These paragraphs are this library's own methodological appraisal, not statements from the source.

Consensus fills gaps where trials were lacking, so its class comparisons of switch risk carry expert judgement rather than randomised estimates, and the statement predates later trials and syntheses.
