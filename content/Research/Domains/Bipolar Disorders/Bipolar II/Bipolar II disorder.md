# Bipolar II disorder

## Definition

Bipolar II disorder is defined by hypomanic and major depressive episodes rather than mania, with hypomania less severe than the mania of bipolar I. [[P33278937 McIntyre 2020 Bipolar disorders seminar#^p33278937-definitions|McIntyre 2020]] [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-types|NIMH]] It is not simply a milder bipolar I: head-to-head comparisons of about 90,000 patients show more depressive episodes, comorbidity and rapid cycling, fewer hospitalisations and psychotic features, and a similar risk of suicide attempts. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-more|Hernandorena 2023]] [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-less|Hernandorena 2023]] [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-same|Hernandorena 2023]]

## How it works

**Depression dominates the course.** Across bipolar disorders, depressive episodes and symptoms fill more of the long-term course than elevated states and account for a disproportionate share of illness and deaths. [[P33278937 McIntyre 2020 Bipolar disorders seminar#^p33278937-course|McIntyre 2020]] Bipolar II has more depressive episodes per year than bipolar I, while the two do not differ in hypomanias or manias per year, onset age or substance use. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-more|Hernandorena 2023]] [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-same|Hernandorena 2023]] A review of the bipolar II literature likewise finds depression predominating over the course in both types, and the diagnosis stays stable over many years. [[P38694998 Swartz 2023 Bipolar II understudied and underdiagnosed#^p38694998-similar|Swartz 2023]] [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-stable|Hernandorena 2023]]

**Suicide risk.** Bipolar disorders shorten life by roughly 10-20 years, mainly through cardiovascular disease and suicide. [[P33278937 McIntyre 2020 Bipolar disorders seminar#^p33278937-mortality|McIntyre 2020]] In pooled head-to-head comparisons the risk of suicide attempts did not differ between bipolar II and bipolar I, and the review literature finds the two alike in suicide risk, so a milder elevated pole does not mean a safer illness. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-same|Hernandorena 2023]] [[P38694998 Swartz 2023 Bipolar II understudied and underdiagnosed#^p38694998-similar|Swartz 2023]]

**Distinct, not milder.** Bipolar II has fewer hospitalisations and psychotic features, lower unemployment and less lithium or antipsychotic treatment, but more co-occurring psychiatric diagnoses, family psychiatric history and antidepressant treatment. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-less|Hernandorena 2023]] [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-more|Hernandorena 2023]] Genetically, bipolar II sits closer to major depression and bipolar I closer to schizophrenia, and genome-wide architecture differs by subtype. [[P33278937 McIntyre 2020 Bipolar disorders seminar#^p33278937-genetics|McIntyre 2020]] [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|O'Connell 2025]] In one long wearable study, circadian-phase disturbance preceded mood symptoms in bipolar I and major depression but not in bipolar II. [[P38579366 Song 2024 Circadian phase and mood symptom dynamics#^p38579366-direction|Song 2024]] [[P38579366 Song 2024 Circadian phase and mood symptom dynamics#^p38579366-caution-precedence|Appraisal: Song 2024]] In a staging cohort bipolar II was associated with moving from the first to the second stage, and in a relapse-prevention trial it predicted more new episodes. [[P38944295 Cremaschi 2024 Ten-year staging trajectory of bipolar disorder#^p38944295-predictors|Cremaschi 2024]] [[P38837133 Hautzinger 2024 Group psychotherapies for bipolar relapse prevention#^p38837133-relapse|Hautzinger 2024]] These comparisons draw on people whose bipolar II was recognised, and unrecognised cases may differ. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-caution-selection|Appraisal: Hernandorena 2023]]

**Differential with unipolar depression.** People usually present in a depressive episode, so bipolar II is often treated first as unipolar depression: about 17% of primary-care patients treated for depression had unrecognised bipolar disorder, and bipolar II symptoms typically precede the correct diagnosis by more than ten years. [[P30933689 Daveney 2019 Unrecognised bipolar disorder in primary care#^p30933689-prevalence|Daveney 2019]] [[P38694998 Swartz 2023 Bipolar II understudied and underdiagnosed#^p38694998-delay|Swartz 2023]] When patients diagnosed with major depression became hypomanic or manic on antidepressants, about three-quarters were rediagnosed with bipolar II; a family history of bipolar disorder, frequent depressions and cyclothymic temperament were among the predictors. [[P38922810 Tondo 2024 Diagnostic change from depression to bipolar disorder#^p38922810-rate|Tondo 2024]] [[P38922810 Tondo 2024 Diagnostic change from depression to bipolar disorder#^p38922810-predictors|Tondo 2024]] NIMH separates bipolar depression from major depression because the treatment implications differ. [[F80 NIMH Depression#^f80-patterns|NIMH depression]]

**Differential with borderline personality disorder.** Borderline personality disorder is described through long-standing instability of mood, self-image and relationships with sensitivity to rejection, whereas bipolar II is defined by episodes. [[F84 NIMH Borderline personality disorder#^f84-pattern|NIMH borderline]] [[F16 NIMH Bipolar disorder#^f16-types|NIMH]] About 22% of people with bipolar disorder also meet criteria for borderline personality disorder, rising to about 38% in bipolar II samples, and the authors read the evidence as highlighting differences between the disorders more than similarities. [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-prevalence|Fornaro 2016]] [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-type2|Fornaro 2016]] [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-differences|Fornaro 2016]] The most useful discriminators reported are family history, developmental antecedents, illness course, how mood states are experienced, personality style and relationships, while biological measures are not yet good enough to guide diagnosis. [[P31749106 Bayes 2019 Bipolar II versus borderline personality disorder#^p31749106-discriminators|Bayes 2019]] [[P31749106 Bayes 2019 Bipolar II versus borderline personality disorder#^p31749106-biology|Bayes 2019]] People with borderline personality disorder report more emotion dysregulation and more maladaptive regulation strategies than people with bipolar disorder. [[P37842774 De Prisco 2023 Emotion dysregulation in bipolar disorder#^p37842774-bpd|De Prisco 2023]] Because the two can co-occur, and overlap counts depend on whether affective instability is credited to both, choosing one label is not always the right question. [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Fornaro 2016]] [[P31749106 Bayes 2019 Bipolar II versus borderline personality disorder#^p31749106-caution-rules|Appraisal: Bayes 2019]]

**Reading rule.** Check whether a bipolar sample reports types I and II separately, how bipolar II was ascertained, and whether mood episodes and personality features were assessed separately; a result pooled across types cannot be read straight across to bipolar II. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|O'Connell 2025]] [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-caution-selection|Appraisal: Hernandorena 2023]] [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Fornaro 2016]]

## Evidence and status

The distinct profile rests on a meta-analysis of 36 head-to-head reports whose heterogeneity limits some comparisons, and the suicide finding on the same pooled comparisons plus a narrative review. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-limit|Hernandorena 2023]] [[P38694998 Swartz 2023 Bipolar II understudied and underdiagnosed#^p38694998-limit|Swartz 2023]] Comorbidity with borderline personality disorder rests on mostly cross-sectional studies with few longitudinal or controlled designs, and the discriminators come from a narrative review. [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-limit|Fornaro 2016]] [[P31749106 Bayes 2019 Bipolar II versus borderline personality disorder#^p31749106-limit|Bayes 2019]] Treatment evidence specific to bipolar II is thin and often extrapolated from bipolar I. [[P38694998 Swartz 2023 Bipolar II understudied and underdiagnosed#^p38694998-extrapolation|Swartz 2023]]

## Uncertainties

- Whether bipolar II is a separate condition or a different position on a shared spectrum; community surveys show graded severity from subthreshold states to bipolar I. [[P21383262 Merikangas 2011 Bipolar spectrum in World Mental Health surveys#^p21383262-gradient|Merikangas 2011]] [[P33278937 McIntyre 2020 Bipolar disorders seminar#^p33278937-genetics|McIntyre 2020]]
- How much of the comorbidity with borderline personality disorder reflects true co-occurrence rather than shared criteria. [[P26881339 Fornaro 2016 Bipolar and borderline personality comorbidity#^p26881339-caution-overlap|Appraisal: Fornaro 2016]]
- Whether biological measures will ever separate bipolar II from borderline personality disorder in an individual. [[P31749106 Bayes 2019 Bipolar II versus borderline personality disorder#^p31749106-biology|Bayes 2019]]

## Recent research

- **2025 · Multi-ancestry genome-wide study (Nature).** Genetic architecture differed by bipolar subtype and by how patients were ascertained. [[P39843750 O'Connell 2025 Genomics of bipolar disorder#^p39843750-ascertainment|O'Connell 2025]]
- **2024 · Randomised trial (JAMA Psychiatry).** In group relapse prevention, patients with bipolar II relapsed most, especially in the CBT arm. [[P38837133 Hautzinger 2024 Group psychotherapies for bipolar relapse prevention#^p38837133-relapse|Hautzinger 2024]] [[P38837133 Hautzinger 2024 Group psychotherapies for bipolar relapse prevention#^p38837133-caution-comparator|Appraisal: Hautzinger 2024]]
- **2024 · Observational wearable study (EBioMedicine).** Circadian-phase disturbance preceded mood symptoms in bipolar I but not bipolar II. [[P38579366 Song 2024 Circadian phase and mood symptom dynamics#^p38579366-direction|Song 2024]]
- **2023 · Systematic review with meta-analyses (Harvard Review of Psychiatry).** Bipolar II differed from bipolar I on many clinical measures while matching it in suicide-attempt risk and staying diagnostically stable. [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-same|Hernandorena 2023]] [[P37437249 Hernandorena 2023 Bipolar II versus bipolar I#^p37437249-stable|Hernandorena 2023]]
